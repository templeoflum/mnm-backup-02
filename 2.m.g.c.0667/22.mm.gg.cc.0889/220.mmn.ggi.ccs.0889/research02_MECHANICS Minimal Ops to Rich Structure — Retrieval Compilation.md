## AREA 1: Minimal generating sets of transformation groups (build all from two ops)

### Point 1.1 — "mapping class group minimal generators two elements Dehn twist"

**Definitions.** The mapping class group Mod(Σ_g) of a closed oriented genus-g surface is Homeo⁺(Σ_g)/Homeo₀(Σ_g) (isotopy classes of orientation-preserving homeomorphisms). A Dehn twist is the mapping class obtained by cutting along a simple closed curve, twisting by 2π, and regluing. Element types via Nielsen–Thurston classification: periodic (torsion), reducible, pseudo-Anosov.

**Canonical/foundational results (verified precisely):**
- **Dehn (1938):** Mod(Σ_g) is generated by a finite set of Dehn twists.
- **Lickorish (1964):** 3g−1 Dehn twists generate Mod(Σ_g). ("A finite set of generators for the homeotopy group of a 2-manifold," Proc. Camb. Phil. Soc. 60, 769–778.)
- **Humphries (1979):** Stephen P. Humphries, "Generators for the mapping class group," in *Topology of Low-Dimensional Manifolds* (Proc. Second Sussex Conf., Chelwood Gate, 1977), Lecture Notes in Math. 722, pp. 44–47, Springer. Per Massuyeau's survey, Humphries showed Mod(Σ_g) is generated by 2g+1 twists "and that 2g+1 is the minimal number of twists needed to generate the mapping class group" (for g>1).
- **Wajnryb (1983):** "A simple presentation for the mapping class group of an orientable surface," Israel J. Math. — gives presentation on 2g+1 Humphries generators. Wajnryb later proved Mod(Σ_g) can be generated by TWO elements; since the group is non-abelian, two is minimal. One generator is a product of two Dehn twists (one right, one left), the other a product of 2g Dehn twists.
- **Korkmaz (2005):** "Generating the surface mapping class group by two elements," Trans. AMS 357, 3299–3310. Showed one of the two generators can be taken to be a single Dehn twist; the other a torsion element of order 4g+2. Also proved the extended mapping class group is generated by two elements (one a Dehn twist), and that Mod can be generated by two torsion elements of order 4g+2.

**Related precise results:**
- **Brendle & Farb (2004):** "Every mapping class group is generated by 3 torsion elements and by 6 involutions," J. Algebra 278 (2004) 187–198: "6 involutions (i.e. orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms of order two) suffice to generate Mod_{g,b} for every genus g ≥ 3, b = 0 and g ≥ 4, b = 1"; answers Feng Luo's question, with 3 torsion elements sufficing for Mod_{g,0}.
- **Kassabov:** Mod(Σ_g) generated by 4 involutions if g≥7, 5 if g≥5, 6 if g≥3.
- **Monden (2013):** "Generating the mapping class group by torsion elements of small order," Math. Proc. Camb. Phil. Soc. 154, 41–62. Mod(Σ_g) is generated by 3 elements of order 3 and by 4 elements of order 4 for g≥3.
- **Monden (2011):** mapping class group of a punctured surface generated by 3 elements (Hiroshima Math. J. 41, 1–9).
- **Baykur & Korkmaz (2021):** "The mapping class group is generated by two commutators," J. Algebra 574, 278–291. For genus ≥5, generated by 2 commutators; genus 3 or 4, by 3 commutators.
- **Yıldız (2022):** "Generating mapping class group by two torsion elements," Mediterr. J. Math. 19(2):59. Mod(Σ_g) generated by two elements of order g for g≥6.
- **Korkmaz (2020):** "Mapping class group is generated by three involutions," Math. Res. Lett. 27, 1095–1108.
- **Hirose & Monden (2023/2026):** "On generating mapping class groups by pseudo-Anosov elements" (arXiv:2310.13272; Bull. Braz. Math. Soc. 2026). Generated by two pseudo-Anosov elements; for genus ≥9 two conjugate pseudo-Anosov with arbitrarily large dilatations; genus ≥8 two conjugate reducible elements of infinite order.

**Frontier (2024–2026):**
- **Monden (2024):** "On minimal generating sets for the mapping class group of a punctured surface," Israel J. Math. 264(1):97. For g≥3, p≥0, Mod(Σ_{g,p}) and the extended group are generated by two elements.
- **Altunöz, Pamuk, Yildiz (Nov 2025):** "Minimal Generation of Mapping Class Groups: A Survey of the Orientable Case," arXiv:2511.16840. Comprehensive survey; includes new result that for even number of punctures p≥8, Mod(Σ_{13,p}) is generated by three involutions.
- **Altunöz, Pamuk, Yildiz (Nov 2025):** companion survey of the Nonorientable Case, arXiv:2511.16829. For sufficiently large genus g, both Mod(N_g) and the twist subgroup T_g generated by two elements (minimum possible).
- "On the Torsion Generators of the Mapping Class Groups," arXiv:2312.04058.

**Active researchers:** Mustafa Korkmaz, Naoyuki Monden, Susumu Hirose, Oğuz Yıldız, Tülin Altunöz, Mehmetcik Pamuk, R. İnanç Baykur, Tara Brendle, Benson Farb, Martin Kassabov, Feng Luo, Justin Lanier.

### Point 1.2 — "generating set group action composition rotation translation"

**Definitions/structure.** The special Euclidean group SE(n) of orientation-preserving rigid motions is the semidirect product SE(n) = ℝⁿ ⋊ SO(n) (translation subgroup ℝⁿ is normal; SO(n) is a non-normal subgroup). Group law: (x,R)·(y,S) = (x+Ry, RS); inverse (x,R)⁻¹ = (−Rᵀx, Rᵀ). Dimension n(n+1)/2 (SE(2):3, SE(3):6). Lie algebra 𝔰𝔢(3) elements are "twists."

**Canonical/foundational results:**
- **Euler's rotation theorem (1775, pub. 1776):** any displacement of a rigid body fixing one point equals a single rotation about an axis (the Euler axis). Linear-algebra form: a 3×3 R with RᵀR=I, det R=+1 has eigenvalue +1. Primary: L. Euler, "Formulae generales pro translatione quacunque corporum rigidorum," Novi Commentarii Acad. Sci. Petropolitanae 20 (1775/1776), ~pp. 189–207.
- **Chasles' theorem (Michel Chasles, 1830; precursor Giulio Mozzi, 1763):** every rigid-body displacement in 3D is a screw motion — a rotation about an axis (the screw axis / asse di Mozzi) combined with a translation along that same axis. Mozzi precursor: *Discorso matematico sopra il rotamento momentaneo dei corpi* (Naples, 1763); documented by M. Ceccarelli, Mech. Mach. Theory (2000). Augustin-Louis Cauchy among contemporaneous contributors.
- **Cartan–Dieudonné theorem (Élie Cartan 1938; Jean Dieudonné 1948):** every isometry of an n-dimensional Euclidean space that is not the identity is a composition of at most n reflections. Affine extension (Gallier): every isometry in Is(n) is a composition of ≤ n reflections if it has a fixed point, else ≤ n+2 reflections (a generalization of Chasles' screw-motion result). Composition of two reflections = a rotation (intersecting mirrors, by twice the angle) or a translation (parallel mirrors). SE(n) is generated by even numbers of reflections.
- **Screw theory — Robert Stawell Ball (1840–1913):** *A Treatise on the Theory of Screws*, Cambridge University Press, 1900 (xix+544 pp.). Definitive classical treatment of twists, wrenches, reciprocal screws, the cylindroid; builds on Poinsot.

**Lie-theoretic generation:** SO(3) is generated by its one-parameter subgroups (rotations about fixed axes) and is exponential; SE(3) is generated by one-parameter subgroups of screw motions (infinitesimal Chasles), underpinning the product-of-exponentials formula — R. W. Brockett, "Robotic manipulators and the product of exponentials formula," Proc. Int. Symp. Mathematical Theory of Networks and Systems, Beer Sheba, Israel (1983), pp. 120–129 (publ. Springer LNCIS vol. 58, 1984).

**Canonical references:** R. M. Murray, Z. Li, S. S. Sastry, *A Mathematical Introduction to Robotic Manipulation*, CRC Press, 1994; J. M. Selig, *Geometric Fundamentals of Robotics*, 2nd ed., Springer, 2005; K. M. Lynch & F. C. Park, *Modern Robotics*, Cambridge UP, 2017.

**Active researchers:** Kevin M. Lynch, Frank C. Park, J. M. Selig, J. Michael McCarthy, Richard M. Murray, Francesco Bullo, Zexiang Li, Jian S. Dai, Marco Ceccarelli, Melvin Leok, Maani Ghaffari.

**Caveat:** A crisp single theorem on a "minimal generating set of SE(2)/SE(3) as a topological/Lie group" was not located; verified results are the reflection-generation (Cartan–Dieudonné) and one-parameter-subgroup/exponential generation. The Chasles 1830 exact bibliographic line and the exact original publication years of Cartan (1938) / Dieudonné (1948) were confirmed by author/theorem content but not independently verified to the primary documents.

### Point 1.3 — "minimal generators rich structure orbit reachability"

**Definitions.** For a finitely generated group G = ⟨S⟩, d(G) is the minimum cardinality of a generating set (the "minimum generating set problem"). The orbit of x under G-action is {g·x : g∈G}; reachability concerns which configurations are attainable by words in the generators. The word problem asks whether a word in generators equals the identity. A Cayley graph encodes generation; it is connected iff S generates G.

**Canonical/foundational and frontier sources:**
- **Lucchini & Thakkar (2024):** "The Minimum Generating Set Problem," J. Algebra 640 (2024) 117–128 (arXiv:2306.07633): "if a chief series of G is known... At most |G|^{13/5} subsets must be tested. This implies that the minimum generating set problem for a finite group G can be solved in polynomial time." Arvind & Torán: problem is in DSPACE(log²n).
- **Word problem / growth:** Novikov–Boone (undecidability of word problem for some finitely presented groups). Gromov (word-hyperbolic groups → word problem solvable in linear time). "The word problem and growth of groups," arXiv:2403.11148 (2024): for groups of exponential growth, word problem not solvable in o(n²) by a single-tape Turing machine.
- **Whitehead minimization problem** (minimum size element in an automorphic orbit): Abdó Roig, Enric Ventura & Pascal Weil, "On the complexity of the Whitehead minimization problem" (Int. J. Algebra Comput.; arXiv:math/0608779): "We give the first fully polynomial algorithm to solve this problem, that is, an algorithm that is polynomial both in the length of the input word and in the rank of the free group."
- **Free monoids/semigroups & reachability:** A free monoid A* on generator set A has unique free generators; rank = cardinality of A. Reachability via composition is the orbit of a transformation semigroup: words in A* induce transformations giving a monoid morphism A* → T_S (full transformation monoid).
- **Growth (frontier):** Kambites, Nyberg-Brodda, Szakács, Webb (2024), "The growth of free inverse monoids," arXiv:2407.10489 — exponential growth rate of free inverse monoid of rank r is an algebraic number strictly between 2r−1 and 2r.
- **Cayley graph foundations:** rotation+reversal generators of dihedral groups produce connected Cayley graphs; rotations-only generators give disconnected graphs (Wolfram MathWorld).

**Active researchers:** Andrea Lucchini, Dhara Thakkar, V. Arvind, Jacobo Torán, Mark Kambites, Carl-Fredrik Nyberg-Brodda, Nóra Szakács, Richard Webb, Enric Ventura, Pascal Weil.

## AREA 2: Orbit spaces / forms as group orbits (the orbit IS the space of shapes)

### Point 2.1 — "group action orbit space of shapes computational anatomy deformable template"

**Definitions.** In Grenander's metric pattern theory, anatomy is modeled as a deformable template: given a template I_temp, the group of diffeomorphisms 𝒢 acts to generate an orbit ℐ = 𝒢·I_temp — a family of objects with similar structure. Typicality is encoded by the template, variability by transformations. This orbit is the "morphological space of forms."

**Canonical/foundational sources:**
- **Grenander & Miller (1997/1998):** first formalization of computational anatomy as an orbit of exemplar templates under diffeomorphism group action — original lecture by Grenander and Miller, May 1997, 50th Anniversary of the Division of Applied Mathematics, Brown University; "Computational Anatomy: An Emerging Discipline," Quarterly of Applied Mathematics, 1998.
- **Christensen, Joshi, Miller, Rabbitt:** earliest introduction of flows of diffeomorphisms for coordinate transformation in image analysis. "Deformable templates using large deformation kinematics," IEEE Trans. Image Process. 5:1435–47 (1996).
- **Dupuis, Grenander & Miller (1998):** "Variational problems on flows of diffeomorphisms for image matching," Quarterly of Applied Mathematics LVI:587–600 — established conditions guaranteeing existence of solutions in the space of flows of diffeomorphisms (Sobolev norm penalizing kinetic energy).
- **Trouvé (1995):** "Action de groupe de dimension infinie et reconnaissance de formes," C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 321:1031–34.
- **Miller, Trouvé & Younes (2002):** "On the metrics and Euler-Lagrange equations of computational anatomy," Annu. Rev. Biomed. Eng. 4:375–405. The metric space of anatomical images is constructed from geodesics connecting structures in the orbit.
- Antecedents: D'Arcy Thompson (comparative morphology / *On Growth and Form*); Ruzena Bajcsy and Fred Bookstein (earliest virtual mapping on CT/MRI); Jean Talairach (Talairach coordinates).

**LDDMM (the core algorithmic instantiation):**
- **Beg, Miller, Trouvé, Younes (2005):** "Computing Large Deformation Metric Mappings via Geodesic Flows of Diffeomorphisms," Int. J. Comput. Vis. 61(2):139–157. Large Deformation Diffeomorphic Metric Mapping: solves I₀∘φ⁻¹=I₁ where φ=Φ₁ is the endpoint of a flow Φ̇_t = v_t(Φ_t), minimizing a Sobolev kinetic energy plus image-matching term. Earliest dense diffeomorphic image-matching code (Beg PhD, Johns Hopkins).

**Frontier:** Bayesian template estimation, multiple-atlas deformable templates (Miller, Tward); diffeomorphometry and "geodesic positioning systems" (PMC4041578); "Computational Anatomy and Diffeomorphometry: A Dynamical Systems Model" (PMC6195456). Active code bases: ANTS, DARTEL, DEMONS, LDDMM, StationaryLDDMM, FastLDDMM.

**Active researchers:** Michael I. Miller, Alain Trouvé, Laurent Younes, Sarang Joshi, M. Faisal Beg, Daniel J. Tward, Ulf Grenander (founder, deceased 2016), Gary Christensen.

### Point 2.2 — "orbit-space canonicalization generative geometry 2025"

**Definitions.** Canonicalization attains invariance by mapping inputs to canonical forms (one representative per orbit, learning a distribution over X/G rather than X). Frame averaging averages over an input-dependent subset (frame) of the group. Orbit representative map c: X → X/G, c(x)=h(x)⁻¹x, with h an equivariant canonicalization function.

**Canonical/foundational sources:**
- **Puny et al. (2021):** "Frame Averaging for Invariant and Equivariant Network Design," arXiv:2110.03336. Group average replaced by average over a frame ℱ(X)⊂G with set-equivariance property.
- **Kaba, Mondal, Zhang, Bengio, Ravanbakhsh (2023):** "Equivariance with Learned Canonicalization Functions," ICML 2023 (PMLR 202:15546–15566; arXiv:2211.06489): "we propose an alternative that avoids this architectural constraint by learning to produce canonical representations of the data... competitive with existing techniques... across many tasks, including image classification, N-body dynamics prediction, point cloud classification and part segmentation."

**Frontier (2024–2026):**
- **Ma, Wang, Lim, Jegelka, Wang (NeurIPS 2024):** "A Canonicalization Perspective on Invariant and Equivariant Learning," arXiv:2405.18378. Connects frames and canonical forms; designs optimal frames for eigenvectors.
- **Dym, Lawrence, Siegel (ICML 2024):** "Equivariant Frames and the Impossibility of Continuous Canonicalization," arXiv:2402.16077. For commonly-used groups there is no efficiently computable frame preserving continuity; introduces weighted/robust frames.
- **"Symmetry-Aware Generative Modeling through Learned Canonicalization" (Jan 2025):** arXiv:2501.07773. Learns a group-equivariant canonicalization network mapping samples to canonical pose; trains non-equivariant diffusion model over canonicalized samples; improved sample quality, halved inference time on molecules.
- **"Rethinking Diffusion Models with Symmetries through Canonicalization" (2026):** arXiv:2602.15022. Quotient-space theory of canonical diffusion under S_n × SE(3); proves correctness, universality, superior expressivity; outperforms equivariant baselines in 3D molecule generation.
- **"Frame-based Equivariant Diffusion Models for 3D Molecular Generation":** arXiv:2509.19506.
- "Improved Canonicalization for Model Agnostic Equivariance," arXiv:2405.14089.

**Active researchers:** Sékou-Oumar Kaba, Siamak Ravanbakhsh, Arnab Kumar Mondal, Yoshua Bengio, Yan Zhang, George Ma, Yisen Wang, Derek Lim, Stefanie Jegelka, Nadav Dym, Hannah Lawrence, Jonathan Siegel, Omri Puny, Max Welling, Emiel Hoogeboom.

### Point 2.3 — "shape space invariants frame-free PCA signature"

**Definitions.** Kendall shape space: the shape of a configuration of k landmarks in m dimensions is what remains after removing translation, scale, and rotation. Pre-shape = after removing translation and scale; shape = after also removing rotation. Procrustes distance is the metric. For m=2, shape space identifies with complex projective space CP^{k−2}; for triangles it is the surface of a sphere (radius 1/2).

**Canonical/foundational sources:**
- **Kendall (1977, 1984, 1989):** D. G. Kendall, "Shape manifolds, Procrustean metrics, and complex projective spaces," Bull. London Math. Soc. 16(2):81–121 (1984); "The diffusion of shape" (1977); "A survey of the statistical theory of shape," Statistical Science 4(2):87–99 (1989).
- **Bookstein (1991):** *Morphometric Tools for Landmark Data*, Cambridge UP.
- **Dryden & Mardia (1998, 2016):** *Statistical Shape Analysis*, Wiley.
- **Gower; Procrustes analysis** (generalized Procrustes; Rohlf & Slice 1990).

**Invariant signatures (differential/integral):**
- **Calabi, Olver, Shakiban, Tannenbaum, Haker (1998):** "Differential and numerically invariant signature curves applied to object recognition," IJCV 26:107–135. Cartan's theorem: two curves are group-equivalent iff their signature curves coincide. Euclidean signature curve = set of (κ, κ_s) (curvature vs. its arc-length derivative).
- **Boutin (2000):** "Numerically Invariant Signature Curves," IJCV 40:235–248.
- **Manay et al. (2004):** "Integral Invariant Signatures" — functionals invariant under Euclidean/similarity group via integral operations; less noise-sensitive than differential invariants.
- **Fels & Olver (1999):** moving coframes / moving frame method (modern generalization of Cartan's méthode du repère mobile).

**Elastic shape analysis:**
- **Srivastava, Klassen, Joshi, Jermyn (2011):** "Shape analysis of elastic curves in Euclidean spaces," IEEE TPAMI 33(7):1415–1428. Square-root velocity (SRV) representation; elastic metric simplifies to L² metric; reparameterization group acts by isometries; shape space is quotient of the unit sphere modulo rotation and reparameterization. Michor & Mumford theory of curve spaces.
- **Srivastava & Klassen (2016):** *Functional and Shape Data Analysis*, Springer.

**Frontier (2024–2026):** iterated-integrals signature with Fels-Olver moving frames for rigid-motion invariants (arXiv:2012.05880); Morphomatics shape space tools; continued geometric morphometrics ("Walking on Kendall's Shape Space," Evol. Biol. 2021).

**Active researchers:** Peter J. Olver, Anuj Srivastava, Eric Klassen, Ian Dryden, Kanti Mardia, Sebastian Kurtek, Christian Klingenberg, Philipp Mitteroecker, Martin Bauer, Peter Michor, Tom Needham.

## AREA 3: Self-similarity / scale-invariance (same relation at a new scale)

### Point 3.1 — "self-similar scaling logarithmic spiral invariance"

**Definitions.** A logarithmic (equiangular, growth) spiral, r = a·e^{bθ}, is a self-similar curve cutting all radial lines at a constant angle. Scaling by e^{2πb} reproduces the spiral (scaling ≡ rotation). Self-similar: size increases in geometric progression while shape is unaltered. Distinct from the Archimedean spiral, whose turn spacing is constant (arithmetic).

**Canonical/foundational sources:**
- **Albrecht Dürer (1525):** first description ("eternal line," ewige Linie), in *Underweysung der Messung*.
- **René Descartes (1638):** discussed the curve; started from property s = a·r.
- **Evangelista Torricelli (d. 1647):** rectification; radii in geometric progression with uniform angular increase.
- **Jacob (Jakob) Bernoulli (1692):** "Spira mirabilis," Acta Eruditorum — derived the form from the equiangular property; named it the "marvelous spiral"; motto "Eadem mutata resurgo" ("Although changed, I shall arise the same"). Noted invariance under scaling, rotation, inversion, involute, pedal, caustic.
- Reference: E. H. Lockwood, *A Book of Curves* (1961).

**Note (source-quality flag):** Some descriptive material was retrieved from Grokipedia (an AI-generated encyclopedia) and Wolfram MathWorld; the Dürer 1525 / Descartes 1638 / Bernoulli 1692 attributions are independently corroborated across Wikipedia, Britannica, and Wolfram MathWorld.

**Occurrences cited:** nautilus shells, sunflower heads, hurricane/galaxy spiral arms; relation to Fibonacci numbers and the golden ratio (golden spiral).

### Point 3.2 — "normalization scale-invariance ratio preservation form"

**Definitions.** Scale invariance: features preserved under scaling and selected scale levels transform in accordance with the scaling amount. Normalization of local neighborhoods by detected scale produces scale-invariant descriptors. Ratios (cross-ratios, length ratios) are preserved under similarity/affine transforms.

**Canonical/foundational sources:**
- **Lindeberg (1994, 1998):** Tony Lindeberg, scale-space theory; showed the scale-normalized Laplacian σ²∇²G is required for true scale invariance ("Scale-space theory in computer vision," 1994; "Feature detection with automatic scale selection," IJCV 1998). Scale-space extrema are scale-covariant.
- **Lowe (1999, 2004):** David G. Lowe, "Distinctive image features from scale-invariant keypoints," IJCV 60:91–110 (2004). SIFT: difference-of-Gaussians approximates σ²∇²G; keypoints normalized for scale and dominant orientation; descriptor histograms normalized (and clamped at 0.2) for illumination invariance.
- **Koenderink & van Doorn (1987, 1992):** Gaussian-derivative receptive fields as canonical model.
- **Mikolajczyk (2002):** σ²∇²G extrema most stable.
- **Morel & Yu (2009):** ASIFT, "A New Framework for Fully Affine Invariant Image Comparison," SIAM J. Imaging Sci.
- Cross-ratio / projective area invariants: Nielsen & Sparr (1991).

**Active researchers:** Tony Lindeberg, David Lowe, Krystian Mikolajczyk, Cordelia Schmid, Jean-Michel Morel.

### Point 3.3 — "fractal self-affine recurrence iterated function system"

**Definitions.** An iterated function system (IFS) is a finite collection of contraction mappings on a complete metric space. The Hutchinson operator F(B) = ⋃_i w_i(B) has a unique compact attractor (fractal) A with F(A)=A, by the Banach fixed-point theorem. Self-similar sets use similarity contractions; self-affine sets use affine maps. A fractal (Mandelbrot's definition): a set whose Hausdorff dimension strictly exceeds its topological dimension.

**Canonical/foundational sources:**
- **Hutchinson (1981):** John E. Hutchinson, "Fractals and self-similarity," Indiana Univ. Math. J. 30(5):713–747. Established contraction-mapping framework, Hutchinson operator, existence/uniqueness of attractors.
- **Mandelbrot (1982):** Benoit B. Mandelbrot, *The Fractal Geometry of Nature*.
- **Barnsley & Demko (1985):** coined the term "Iterated Function Systems"; Barnsley, *Fractals Everywhere* (1988) — popularized IFS, the Collage Theorem, fractal image compression, the "chaos game."
- **Moran (1946):** dimension formula for self-similar sets.
- **McMullen (1984); Bedford:** self-affine set dimension.
- **Falconer:** *Fractal Geometry: Mathematical Foundations and Applications* (1990).
- **Peres & Solomyak (2000):** survey on self-affine dimension. Georges de Rham (1957): de Rham curves.

**Frontier (2024–2026):**
- Pant & Nashine (2024): "Fractals of FG-Hutchinson Barnsley operator in metric spaces," Filomat 38(27):9711–9725.
- "The Hutchinson-Barnsley theory for iterated function systems with general measures," arXiv:2505.09560 (2025).
- Pearse: "Canonical self-affine tilings by iterated function systems" (and complex-dimensions theory with Lapidus).
- Barnsley & Vince: "The Chaos Game on a General Iterated Function System," Ergodic Theory Dynam. Systems.

**Active researchers:** Michael Barnsley, Kenneth Falconer, Yuval Peres, Boris Solomyak, Michel Lapidus, Erin Pearse, Andrew Vince, Mark Pollicott, Károly Simon.
