# Compilation: Dive 3 — The Shadow (Unconstrained Relation)
### A source-compilation in semiotics, history of religions, and the sociology of knowledge

*Compilation only. This reports what rigorous sources SAY, attributed precisely. Pseudoscientific/practitioner sources are excluded or explicitly flagged. No synthesis across targets, no recommendations.*

## TL;DR
- Eco names the failure mode "hermetic semiosis"/"hermetic drift" (everything resembles everything; unlimited semiosis without brakes) and bounds it with the **Augustinian coherence criterion** and **intentio operis** vs. **intentio lectoris**; he treats the "paranoiac" over-reader who overestimates clues as the aberrant case.
- Gematria, notarikon, and temurah are described by Scholem and recent peer-reviewed work as **structural combinatorial techniques**, not revelations; the gold-standard statistical refutation in this family (McKay, Bar-Natan, Bar-Hillel & Kalai 1999) targets the *Bible Code*/ELS, not classical gematria — a scope distinction that matters.
- Weber (routinization of charisma), Peirce/Ricoeur/Turner (living vs. dead/conventional sign), and Couliano (morphodynamics; systems as recombinant "mind games") supply the vocabulary for why borrowed symbolic systems mutate or go dead. **The named "Gematria: A Comprehensive Survey" (ed. Boccaccini, 2024) could not be verified and appears not to exist.**

## TARGET 3A — Eco's "hermetic semiosis" and unlimited drift

### 3A.1 — Definitions: hermetic semiosis / hermetic drift / unlimited semiosis
**Primary texts.** Umberto Eco, *The Limits of Interpretation* (Bloomington: Indiana University Press, 1990; the chapter "Unlimited Semiosis and Drift" derives from his Indiana University Institute for Advanced Study lecture "Drift and Unlimited Semiosis," delivered July 19, 1989); and Umberto Eco, *Interpretation and Overinterpretation* (Cambridge University Press, 1992), ed. Stefan Collini, with replies by Richard Rorty, Jonathan Culler, and Christine Brooke-Rose. The relevant Eco lectures in the latter are "Interpretation and history" (ch. 1) and "Overinterpreting texts" (ch. 2, pp. 45–66; DOI 10.1017/CBO9780511627408.003).

**Eco's own definition (verbatim, *Interpretation and Overinterpretation*, ch. 2).** "Both a metaphysic and a physic of universal sympathy must stand upon a semiotics (explicit or implicit) of similarity… My hypothesis is historically more comprehensive and is intended to highlight an interpretive criterion (which I call Hermetic semiosis) the survival of which can be traced through the centuries." Eco adds that the criterion of similarity "displayed an over-indulgent generality and flexibility," subsuming "not only those phenomena that today we would list under the heading of morphological resemblance or proportional analogy, but every kind of possible substitution permitted by the rhetoric tradition, that is, contiguity, pars pro toto, action or actor, and so on."

**Source of the associative list.** Eco draws his catalogue of associative criteria "not from a treatise on magic but from a sixteenth-century mnemonics or *ars memoriae*," because that author had codified "associative automatisms commonly accepted as effective."

**The core principle and "false transitivity."** As Bernard Williams summarizes in "The Riddle of Umberto Eco," *The New York Review of Books*, February 2, 1995 (verbatim): "since, as Eco says, 'from a certain point of view, everything bears relationships of analogy, contiguity and similarity to everything else,' by exploiting 'a false transitivity' you can get anywhere from anywhere." Williams gives Eco's examples — orchis → testicles (similarity of shape), crow → Ethiopians (similarity of color), ant → Providence (hieroglyphic relation).

**Lineage and result.** Eco explicitly relates his "paradigm of similarity" to Michel Foucault's treatment in *Les mots et les choses*, but says his own hypothesis is "historically more comprehensive." The reviewer Denis Dutton ("Umberto Eco on Interpretation") summarizes the upshot: under the hermetic outlook "interpretation is indefinite… we must accept a never-ending drift or sliding of meaning… Every revelation yields to yet another secret, ad infinitum." Scholarly commentary (Pavel Nosachev, HSE Working Paper WP20/2014/04, "Umberto Eco and Studies in Western Esotericism") notes Eco's conclusion that "due to infinity of interpretation hermetic mystery turns out to be empty."

### 3A.2 — Eco's constraints on drift (the brakes)
- **Augustinian internal-coherence criterion.** Scholarship summarizing Eco (the *Limits* argument) traces the brake to Augustine's *De Doctrina Christiana*: any interpretation of one part of a text "can be accepted if it is confirmed and must be rejected if it is challenged by another part of the same text. In this sense the internal textual coherence controls the otherwise uncontrollable drives of the reader."
- **The three intentions (Eco 1990: 50–51).** *intentio auctoris* = what the empirical author intended to say; *intentio operis* = what the text wants to say "with reference to its underlying signification system and by virtue of its textual coherence"; *intentio lectoris* = what readers make the text say "with reference to their own system of expectations, their wishes, drives, beliefs." Eco holds interpretation is "always the result of a synthetic oscillation between intentio operis and intentio lectoris," keeping the "idiosyncratic and potentially indomitable intention of the reader" in check (per De Gruyter's *Semiotica* article "From the model reader to the limits of interpretation"). Intentions are "not unlimited but are constrained by the overall meaning of the text or work."
- **"Mark of sanity."** Dutton's summary (verbatim): "it is a mark of sanity to be able to distinguish relevant significant similarities from illusory ones. Obsessive, paranoiac interpretations founder with this very distinction — they overestimate clues and coincidences."
- **Negative parameters.** Per the openedition.org study ("Power, Dangers and Resources of Forgery"), Eco "provides negative parameters to guide interpretation (we can say with certainty what cannot be good, but the list of what can be good is infinite)." Eco elsewhere calls the target of his critique "the cancer of uncontrolled interpretation" (Indiana University Press description of *The Limits of Interpretation*).

### 3A.3 — Good/authentic vs. aberrant/paranoiac over-interpretation
- **The distinction.** Per the *naqd.guilan.ac.ir* study "Umberto Eco and the Semiotic Foundation of Interpretation": in contrast to "aberrant interpretation," which requires a "subject-oriented reading," "authentic interpretation" results from "the reader's restricted freedom."
- **Hermetic semiosis as systematic falsehood.** The openedition.org essay quotes/paraphrases Eco: hermetic semiosis is "an example of the construction of falsehoods that requires a lot of semiotic work and a systematic construction of correlations," resting on "a distorted transitivity, which sees causes and effects where, at most, there are partial similarities." Per Nosachev, Eco holds it is effectively impossible to argue with someone "who accepted Hermetic semiosis as the only system of interpretation."
- **The paranoiac reader.** Eco's novel *Foucault's Pendulum* (1988) dramatizes the failure mode (the "Diabolicals," "the Plan"). Secondary scholarship: Douglas Stephens IV, "Auctor in Fabula: Umberto Eco and the Intentio of Foucault's Pendulum" (Liberty University Senior Honors Thesis, 2015), which links the novel's "interpretive drift" to Eco's nonfiction.

## TARGET 3B — Gematria, notarikon, temurah (scholarly / structural)

### 3B.1 — The mechanism (structure only)
Scholars describe three classic Kabbalistic hermeneutical-combinatorial techniques: **gematria** (numerical-value equivalence of words), **notarikon** (acronym/acrostic — words from initial or final letters), and **temurah** (letter substitution/permutation).

**Foundational source — Gershom Scholem.** Scholem, *Kabbalah* (Jerusalem: Keter Publishing House, 1974), treats gematria at **pp. 337–343** (Part Two, topic 10; U.S. reprints via Quadrangle/NYT Book Co. and Meridian/NAL). This reproduces Scholem's *Encyclopaedia Judaica* entry "Gematria" (Vol. 7, cols. 369–374). **Scholem's verbatim definition:** "GEMATRIA (from Gr. γεωμετρία), one of the aggadic hermeneutical rules for interpreting the Torah (Baraita of 32 Rules, no. 29). It consists of explaining a word or group of words according to the numerical value of the letters, or of substituting other letters of the alphabet for them in accordance with a set system."

**Earliest attestation (Scholem, EJ, verbatim):** "The first use of gematria occurs in an inscription of Sargon II (727–707 B.C.E.) which states that the king built the wall of Khorsabad 16,283 cubits long to correspond with the numerical value of his name."

**Calculation methods Scholem enumerates:** *Mispar Hekhrechi* (absolute/normative), *Mispar Sidduri* (ordinal), *Mispar Katan* (reduced), *Mispar Katan Mispari* (integral reduced), plus the Kabbalistic *milui* ("filling") and *atbash* substitution. (The multiplicity of methods is itself the factual premise of the 3B.2 critique.)

**Notarikon.** A Talmudic method of interpreting biblical words as acronyms (term borrowed from Greek *notarikon*); also a Kabbalistic method of forming divine names from a verse's initial letters. Classic example: the consonants of *nimreṣet* (1 Kgs 2:8) producing *noʾef* ("adulterer"), *moʾābi* ("Moabite"), *roṣeaḥ* ("murderer"), *ṣorer* ("threatener"), *tôʿbāh* ("horror") (per the standard reference treatments).

**Temurah.** Letter transposition/replacement ("exchange," literally "permutation"); simple forms include *atbash* (first letter ↔ last, etc.), *avgad*, and *albam*.

**Mnemonic.** Scholem/EJ and the encyclopedia literature record that the letters *gimmel-nun-tav* of Joseph Gikatilla's *Ginnat Egoz* (Hanau, 1615) are the initials of **g**ematria, **n**otarikon, and **t**emurah; Abulafia's works are described by Scholem as "based on the extensive and extreme use of gematria."

**On centrality.** Scholem/EJ and *My Jewish Learning* (drawing on him) note gematria "was not central to rabbinic literature" — used occasionally to support exegesis (it is the 29th of the 32 aggadic hermeneutical rules attributed to R. Eliezer ben Yose ha-Gelili) — but became "essential to Kabbalah."

**Recent rigorous scholarship (2024).** Elaine Goodfriend, "Gematria in the Hebrew Bible," *Jewish Bible Quarterly* 52/4 (2024) (peer-reviewed; Goodfriend, Cal State Northridge); and Zachary Harris, "Forward and Reverse Gematria are Very Different Beasts," *Open Theology* 10/1 (2024), DOI 10.1515/opth-2022-0245 (De Gruyter). Goodfriend distinguishes "overt" from "secret" gematria in the Hebrew Bible. (Standard secondary references also cite Shmuel Sambursky, "On the Origin and Significance of the Term Gematria," *JJS* 29 (1978), and Stephen J. Lieberman, "A Mesopotamian Background for the So-Called Aggadic 'Measures' of Biblical Hermeneutics?," *HUCA* 58 (1987).)

### 3B.2 — The combinatorial-explosion / no-constraint problem
**Gold-standard rigorous refutation (targets ELS/"Bible Code," a cousin of gematria — flag the scope difference).** Brendan McKay, Dror Bar-Natan, Maya Bar-Hillel & Gil Kalai, "Solving the Bible Code Puzzle," *Statistical Science* 14/2 (1999): 150–173 (DOI 10.1214/ss/1009212243). The authors are a mathematician/computer scientist (McKay, ANU), two mathematicians (Bar-Natan, Kalai), and a decision psychologist (Bar-Hillel, Hebrew University). It rebuts Doron Witztum, Eliyahu Rips & Yoav Rosenberg, "Equidistant Letter Sequences in the Book of Genesis," *Statistical Science* 9 (1994): 429–438. **Verbatim:** the WRR "case is fatally defective, indeed that their result merely reflects on the choices made in designing their experiment and collecting the data for it." Their explanatory keywords are **"data selection"** and **"data tuning"**; control experiments found equivalent "codes" in a Hebrew translation of Tolstoy's *War and Peace*. **Editor's verdict (verbatim):** Robert E. Kass, "Introduction to 'Solving the Bible Code Puzzle'," *Statistical Science* 14/2 (1999): 149 — "considering the work of McKay, Bar-Natan, Kalai and Bar-Hillel as a whole it indeed appears, as they conclude, that the puzzle has been solved." (Kass had originally framed the 1994 WRR paper to readers as "a challenging puzzle.")

**Critique aimed at classical gematria proper (peer-reviewed).** Goodfriend (2024), verbatim: "biblical numerology… is wildly speculative; adherents throughout history have willy-nilly applied numerical values to every element of a text. Controls are necessary to distinguish between plausible examples from those which are arbitrary and unconvincing." She characterizes a "secret"/logoprosodic counting scheme as "arbitrary, in the sense that the desired result determines which words are counted" — i.e., the post-hoc, result-driven objection.

**Non-scholarly framing (explicitly flagged).** The "unfalsifiable pseudo-science… based on nothing more than coincidental occurrences" formulation appears in skeptic/wiki sources (e.g., Religions Wiki on "theomatics"). These are **not** rigorous scholarship and are reported only to mark that the strong "combinatorial explosion → unfalsifiable" slogan circulates mainly in non-academic venues; the rigorous version is McKay et al. (statistical, on ELS) and Goodfriend (philological, on gematria).

### 3B.3 — Abulafia's tzeruf as Idel describes it
**Idel's principal works:** *The Mystical Experience in Abraham Abulafia* (Albany: SUNY Press, 1988); *Language, Torah, and Hermeneutics in Abraham Abulafia* (SUNY, 1989); *Kabbalah: New Perspectives* (New Haven: Yale University Press, 1988).

**Idel's account (structural — a technique for experience, not decoding).** Abulafia's "science of letter-combination" (*hokhmat ha-tzeruf*), also called the "path of names" (*derekh ha-shemot*), is an **ecstatic technique**. Per the literature transmitting Idel (e.g., *My Jewish Learning*, "Abraham Abulafia & Ecstatic Kabbalah," and Elliot Wolfson's account): "The main techniques consisted of letter-combination (in three stages: written, oral, and mental) and recitation of the divine names, which involved as well special breathing exercises and bodily postures." The aim is an altered/unitive/prophetic state; the very letters of the Divine Name are described as appearing before the mystic's inner eye. Abulafia names his system "Prophetic Kabbalah" / "Kabbalah of Names." Idel locates the recitation-of-names technique within a history running from late-antique *Merkavah* texts through the German Pietists, noting parallels with Eleazar of Worms (c.1176–1238). Idel emphasizes (per the academia.edu summary of *The Mystical Experience*) that "as understood by the thirteenth-century mystic Abraham Abulafia, Kabbalah is not primarily a form of gnosis or theosophy" — i.e., it is technique-centered, prioritizing experience over doctrine.

**Lineage and recent work.** Scholem (1941, *Major Trends in Jewish Mysticism*) first treated Abulafia's "technique of meditation"; Idel "critically expanded" Scholem. Recent: Shahar Arzy & Moshe Idel, *Kabbalah: A Neurocognitive Approach to Mystical Experiences* (Yale University Press, 2015). (Note: practitioner/how-to titles on Abulafian tzeruf — e.g., Amazon's *The Wisdom of Abraham Abulafia* — are **excluded** as non-scholarly marketing.)

## TARGET 3C — The simulacrum / why borrowed systems go dead

### 3C.1 — Routinization of charisma (Weber)
**Source.** Max Weber, "Die Veralltäglichung des Charismas" ("the routinization of charisma"), in *Wirtschaft und Gesellschaft* / *Economy and Society*, and in *The Theory of Social and Economic Organization* (trans. A. M. Henderson & Talcott Parsons, 1947).

**Concept.** Charisma is one of three pure types of legitimate authority (with traditional and rational-legal). Weber holds charisma is inherently unstable and exists "only in the process of origination"; with the leader's death there is "a process of transformation by which the followers return to a more everyday existence — charisma becomes routinized (*Veralltäglichung des Charisma*) and traditional and/or rational-legal types of legitimation become important." One modern form of routinization is **depersonalization** (*Versachlichung*), producing the **"charisma of office,"** independent of the office-holder's personal qualities. Secondary literature (the PDX essay "Toward a Theory of the Routinization of Charisma," summarizing Weber) states charismatic authority "must of necessity move in one of three directions: toward dissolution, toward traditional authority, or toward rational-legal authority… to survive it must change, but in changing it must give up its definitive, essentially charismatic qualities."

**Weber's ambivalence / decline language.** Per the Springer *Topoi* article "Charisma and Democracy" (2021), Weber wrote that bureaucratic administration may accomplish not only "the routinization of charisma, but also its 'castration'" (*Economy and Society*, 1978: 1137), heralding "mechanized petrification."

**Application to religious movements.** Scholarly/educated applications are common — e.g., the *By Common Consent* essay applying Weber's routinization to the institutionalization of Mormonism; Encyclopedia.com's "Charisma" entry on lineage charisma (succession of Dalai Lamas) and sacral kingship.

### 3C.2 — Living symbol vs. dead/conventional sign
*(Distinguishing carefully which scholar says what.)*

- **C. S. Peirce — the symbol as a living, growing sign.** In "What Is a Sign?" Peirce writes (verbatim): "A symbol, once in being, spreads among the peoples. In use and in experience, its meaning grows. Such words as force, law, wealth, marriage, bear for us very different meanings than those they bore to our barbarous ancestors." And: "If a man makes a new symbol, it is by thoughts involving concepts. So it is only out of symbols that a new symbol can grow. *Omne symbolum de symbolo*." Peirce uses the dead-tissue analogy (nails, teeth, hair, bone that "have ceased to undergo the metabolic processes which constitute life") and notes "a metaphor is not always to be despised." The growth of symbols is tied to his "Law of Mind" (1892), in which ideas spread and "lose intensity" as they generalize.

- **Paul Ricoeur — "the symbol gives rise to thought"; symbol vs. allegory.** The aphorism closes *The Symbolism of Evil* (1960). Ricoeur distinguishes symbol from allegory (verbatim, as transmitted in commentary): "Allegory is a rhetorical procedure that can be eliminated once it has done its job. Having ascended the ladder, we can then descend it. Allegory is a didactic procedure." By contrast, for the participant in symbolic signification "there are really not two significations, one literal and the other symbolic, but rather a single movement"; "the symbol assimilates rather than apprehends a resemblance," and "there is more in a symbol than in any of its conceptual equivalents." In *The Rule of Metaphor* (1975) Ricoeur develops the "living metaphor," contrasted with **dead metaphors** that "fade into cliché" (e.g., "the leg of a chair").

- **Victor Turner — the life of ritual symbols.** *The Forest of Symbols: Aspects of Ndembu Ritual* (Ithaca: Cornell University Press, 1967). Turner (1967: 28–29; 50–55) identifies three empirical properties of **dominant symbols**: (1) **condensation/polysemy/multivocality** (one symbol represents many things); (2) **unification of disparate significata** (meanings linked by analogy or association); (3) **polarization of meaning / bipolarity** — an **ideological (normative) pole** (moral/social order) and a **sensory (orectic) pole** (physiological phenomena that arouse desire/feeling). The *mudyi* (milk tree) is his paradigm dominant symbol (motherhood, matriliny, tribal unity). Turner also distinguishes three orders of meaning: exegetical, operational, positional.

- **The semiotic "demotivation"/motivation cluster.** Saussure: the sign is "arbitrary and conventional," with "relative motivation" possible within the system. Standard semiotics (Chandler, *Semiotics for Beginners*): "The more a signifier is constrained by the signified, the more 'motivated' the sign is: iconic signs are highly motivated; symbolic signs are unmotivated. The less motivated the sign, the more learning of an agreed convention is required." Eco (*A Theory of Semiotics*, 1976) criticized the conflation of "arbitrary," "conventional," and "digital," observing a photograph "may be both 'motivated' and 'digital'," and that conventionality is not equivalent to arbitrariness. **Gap flag:** I did not find a single canonical scholarly locus that uses the precise term "demotivation of signs" as a settled technical term; the rigorous, well-attested material is the Saussure/Peirce/Eco motivation-vs-convention discussion above. Treat "demotivation" as a descriptive coinage rather than an established citable term.

### 3C.3 — Couliano's morphodynamics (*The Tree of Gnosis*)
**Source.** Ioan P. Couliano (Culianu), *The Tree of Gnosis: Gnostic Mythology from Early Christianity to Modern Nihilism* (San Francisco: HarperSanFrancisco, 1992; orig. French/Italian 1990).

**Thesis.** Gnostic systems are not stable doctrines transmitted intact but are **generated by logical operations on a small set of binary choices/premises** — a recombinant "mind game." Western dualism is read (per the Goodreads scholarly review and secondary summaries) as "a computational program operating on and through the minds of diverse and varied groups everywhere, resulting in a wide permutation of solutions and outcomes based upon the rather simple premises from which it starts," which "negat[es] the need for origin stories."

**Method — morphodynamics.** Couliano defines morphodynamics as "the theory of form, formation, and transformation of organic natures" and posits **"ideal objects"** (logical constructs operating on minds across time; his Goethe/*Urpflanze* analogy: "Goethe did not know that what he was looking for was not a natural object but its ideal program"). He explicitly **rejects pure historical-transmission explanation**: tracing precise diffusion pathways of ideas was, for him, "not only a fruitless attempt, but essentially an inadequate method for the actual problem at hand," to be replaced by morphodynamics.

**The game metaphor (verbatim, p. 247, confirmed):** "the morphodynamics of dualistic (binary) systems can be compared with a board game and could, as a matter of fact, be made into a board game of transformations." Couliano frames modern nihilism (the "death of God") as potentially "one further move that can be made amongst the formulation rules of dualism."

**Related rigorous scholarship.** Moshe Idel, "Ioan P. Coulianu, the American Phase, and 'Ars combinatoria'," in D. Dumbravă & B. Tătaru-Cazaban (eds.), *In-cognita: Ioan Petru Culianu's Approaches to Religion* (2021), pp. 21–57; Giovanni Casadio, "Ermeneutica del dualismo e logica binaria: Ioan Petru Culianu…" (2021).

## Caveats and source-quality flags
- **Unverifiable/likely-fabricated citation.** The task's named "Gematria: A Comprehensive Survey" (allegedly ed. Gabriele Boccaccini, 2024) **could not be verified and appears not to exist.** Boccaccini is a real University of Michigan scholar of Second Temple Judaism/Enoch literature, but his verified bibliography (Eerdmans, Brill, SBL Press author pages; the 4enoch.org bibliography he maintains) contains **no gematria volume**. Treat the citation as a probable confabulation that attaches a real scholar's name to a non-existent title. Genuine 2024 peer-reviewed substitutes: Goodfriend, "Gematria in the Hebrew Bible," *JBQ* 52/4 (2024); Harris, *Open Theology* 10/1 (2024).
- **Scope mismatch in the strongest refutation.** McKay et al. (1999) is the rigorous, peer-reviewed, statistically decisive source — but it refutes **equidistant letter sequences (the "Bible Code")**, not classical gematria/notarikon/temurah. The "anything can be linked to anything" logic transfers, but the formal statistical demolition is specifically about ELS. The cleanest peer-reviewed critique of gematria *proper* is the philological one (Goodfriend 2024), which uses the language of arbitrariness/post-hoc rather than "combinatorial explosion."
- **Eco quotations partly via reviews/secondary work.** Several Eco formulations (the "anything bears relationships… to everything else," the "mark of sanity") are quoted here via Williams (NYRB 1995), Dutton, and peer-reviewed secondary literature; the primary loci are *The Limits of Interpretation* (1990) and *Interpretation and Overinterpretation* ch. 2 (1992).
- **Gematria/temurah/notarikon definitions on the open web** are dominated by practitioner, occult, and "decoder" sites (Gematrix, occult-world, thalira, northernway, etc.), which were **excluded**; the structural definitions above rest on Scholem/*Encyclopaedia Judaica*, the peer-reviewed 2024 articles, and standard reference treatments.
- **"Demotivation" terminology gap** (see 3C.2): not located as a settled technical term in rigorous semiotics; the motivation-vs-convention literature (Saussure, Peirce, Eco 1976) is the citable substitute.
- **No conflicts among the rigorous sources** were detected on the substantive points compiled here; the principal uncertainties are the two unverifiable/scope items flagged above.
