# GROUND (Dive 1): Feasibility Checks and Experimental-Design Methods for a Structured-vs-Flat Data Experiment — A Methods-Deep Source Compilation

**TL;DR**
- **1A (Placement reality):** Text-separability is assessed with (a) embedding models (SBERT, SimCSE, E5, BGE, GTE, OpenAI text-embedding-3) benchmarked on MTEB; (b) internal cluster-validity indices (silhouette, Davies–Bouldin, Calinski–Harabasz, Dunn) and external label-agreement indices (adjusted Rand index, NMI, V-measure, purity), each with defined ranges and formulas; (c) dimensionality reduction/visualization (PCA, t-SNE, UMAP) whose known distortions and the curse of dimensionality/anisotropy bound any "separation" claim.
- **1B (Memorization-proof reasoning test):** Named contamination-detection methods (n-gram overlap, Min-K% Prob membership inference, Oren et al. exchangeability test, Golchin & Surdeanu "guided instruction," rephrased-samples detection), post-cutoff/temporal-holdout benchmarks (LiveBench, LiveCodeBench), and reasoning-vs-memorization probes (counterfactual tasks, GSM-Symbolic, grokking, Carlini extractability). The reasoning-vs-memorization distinction is itself contested.
- **1C (Fair flat baseline):** RAG (Lewis 2020) + DPR (Karpukhin 2020) define the knobs (chunking, embedding model, top-k, reranking) that must be held equal; structured-vs-flat studies (GraphRAG, RAPTOR) and controlled-comparison standards (ablation, matched conditions, multiple seeds, CIs, Dodge et al. reproducibility checklist) define what "fair" means.

## TARGET 1A — Placement reality: can text be placed/separated in representation space?

### 1A.1 Embedding methods and how separability is produced/measured

- **Sentence-BERT (SBERT) — Reimers & Gurevych, EMNLP-IJCNLP 2019** (arXiv 1908.10084; pp. 3982–3992). Modifies pretrained BERT/RoBERTa with **siamese and triplet network structures** plus a **pooling operation** (CLS, mean, or max pooling) to yield fixed-size sentence embeddings comparable by **cosine similarity**. Motivation: vanilla BERT requires both sentences fed jointly — finding the most similar pair among 10,000 sentences needs ~50M inferences (~65 hours); SBERT makes it tractable. Fine-tuned on NLI data; evaluated on STS (Spearman correlation). This is the canonical "embeddings that place semantically similar text near each other" reference.
- **SimCSE — Gao, Yao & Chen, EMNLP 2021** (arXiv 2104.08821). Contrastive framework. **Unsupervised SimCSE**: predict the input sentence itself using only **standard dropout as minimal data augmentation** (removing dropout causes "representation collapse"). **Supervised SimCSE**: uses NLI **entailment pairs as positives and contradiction pairs as hard negatives**. BERT-base results: 76.3% (unsup) and 81.6% (sup) average Spearman on STS; adding hard negatives improved STS-B dev 84.9%→86.2%. Crucially for separability: the paper shows **theoretically and empirically that the contrastive objective regularizes the anisotropic embedding space toward uniformity/isotropy** (flattens the singular spectrum of the embedding Gram matrix), connecting to the alignment/uniformity framing of Wang & Isola (2020).
- **E5 — Wang et al., 2022** (arXiv 2212.03533). "Text Embeddings by Weakly-Supervised Contrastive Pre-training." Trained contrastively with the **InfoNCE loss** on a curated large-scale text-pair dataset **CCPairs**; biencoder Transformer with **average pooling**; inputs prefixed "query:" / "passage:". First model to beat BM25 on BEIR zero-shot without labeled data; fine-tuned E5 topped MTEB. Released sizes E5-small (384-dim, 12 layers), E5-base (768-dim), E5-large (1024-dim); later E5-mistral-7b-instruct (4096-dim). A two-stage recipe: contrastive pretraining on CCPairs then fine-tuning on labeled data with a KL-distillation loss from a cross-encoder teacher.
- **BGE — Xiao, Liu, Zhang & Muennighoff, 2023** ("C-Pack: Packed Resources For General Chinese Embeddings," arXiv 2309.07597). BGE = BAAI General Embedding; the C-Pack release notes state the bge-large-* models "rank 1st on MTEB and C-MTEB benchmark" (08/02/2023). So BGE *does* have a primary paper, not merely leaderboard standings.
- **GTE — Li et al., 2023** ("Towards General Text Embeddings with Multi-stage Contrastive Learning," arXiv 2308.03281). GTE = General Text Embeddings; trained with multi-stage contrastive learning (cited as "GTE (Li et al., 2023b)" in the C-Pack paper).
- **OpenAI text-embedding-3** (text-embedding-3-small / -large): commercial models best characterized through their **MTEB leaderboard standings** rather than a primary methods paper; treat their reported numbers as benchmark-derived.
- **MTEB — Muennighoff, Tazi, Magne & Reimers, 2022** (arXiv 2210.07316; EACL 2023). The Massive Text Embedding Benchmark spans **8 task types, 58 datasets, 112 languages**: classification, clustering, pair classification, reranking, retrieval, STS, summarization, bitext mining. Datasets partitioned by input structure (S2S, P2P, S2P). Headline finding from benchmarking 33 models: **no single embedding method dominates all tasks** — the field has not converged on a universal embedding. The **clustering task** in MTEB directly measures separability of embedded text by known labels (uses v-measure). Expanded later as MMTEB (Enevoldsen et al., 2025). A 2025 maintenance paper (arXiv 2506.21182) introduces a **zero-shot score quantifying distributional overlap** between a model's training data and benchmark tasks to address contamination/leakage concerns.

### 1A.2 Cluster-validity and separation metrics (definitions, ranges, decision logic)

**Internal indices (no ground-truth labels):**
- **Silhouette coefficient — Rousseeuw (1987).** For point *x* in cluster *i*: a(x) = mean intra-cluster distance to other points in *i*; b(x) = minimum over other clusters *j* of the mean distance from *x* to points in *j*. Silhouette s(x) = (b(x) − a(x)) / max[a(x), b(x)]. **Range −1 to +1**; higher is better; values near 0 indicate overlapping clusters; negative indicates likely misassignment. Overall score = mean over all points. (Implemented as `sklearn.metrics.silhouette_score`.)
- **Davies–Bouldin index — Davies & Bouldin (1979).** Average over clusters of the worst-case (maximum) ratio of within-cluster scatter (compactness) to between-cluster separation (centroid distance). **Lower is better**; ≥0; a value below ~1 is commonly read as well-separated, compact clusters. (`davies_bouldin_score`.) Soft/fuzzy versions exist (implemented in scikit-learn, R clusterSim, ELKI).
- **Calinski–Harabasz index (Variance Ratio Criterion) — Caliński & Harabasz (1974).** CH = [B/(k−1)] / [W/(n−k)], where B = between-cluster sum of squares, W = within-cluster sum of squares, n = number of points, k = number of clusters. **Higher is better**; unbounded above; used to pick the number of clusters. (`calinski_harabasz_score`.)
- **Dunn index.** Ratio of the minimum inter-cluster distance to the maximum intra-cluster diameter: D = min_i { min_j [ (min distance between clusters i,j) / (max diameter over clusters k) ] }. **Higher is better**; sensitive to noise/outliers.
- **Caveat from the literature:** A 2025 PeerJ CS comparative study (cs-3309) found the **silhouette coefficient and Davies–Bouldin index more informative than Dunn, Calinski–Harabasz, Shannon entropy, and the gap statistic** for internal evaluation of two convex clusters; all these convex-oriented indices "can be misleading" for concave/nested/density-based clusters, where DBCV, DCSI, CDbw, and VIASCKDE are preferred.

**External indices (labeled / known-group separation):**
- **Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) — Hubert & Arabie (1985), "Comparing partitions," J. Classification 2(1):193–218.** Chance-corrected version of the Rand index. Built on a contingency table of pair agreements; corrected for chance assuming a **generalized hypergeometric distribution** (fixed cluster sizes). **Expected value 0 under random labeling; max value 1**; can be negative. Described in the cluster-validation literature (Milligan & Cooper 1986) as the "measure of choice." A 2020 critique (arXiv 2011.08708) argues the hypergeometric fixed-margins assumption is violated by real clustering (no algorithm fixes cluster sizes) and proposes a multinomial-model variant (MARI).
- **Normalized Mutual Information (NMI).** Mutual information between predicted and true labels normalized (by arithmetic/geometric mean of entropies) to **[0,1]**; 1 = perfect agreement.
- **V-measure / homogeneity / completeness — Rosenberg & Hirschberg (2007).** **Homogeneity**: each cluster contains only members of a single class. **Completeness**: all members of a class are in the same cluster. **V-measure** = their harmonic mean. Range **[0,1]**. (This is the metric MTEB uses for its clustering tasks.)
- **Purity.** Fraction of items correctly assigned when each cluster is labeled by its majority class. Range (0,1]; biased upward by many small clusters (so not chance-corrected — flag as a weak standalone measure).

### 1A.3 Dimensionality reduction / visualization and its pitfalls

- **PCA (Pearson 1901).** Linear projection onto directions of maximal variance; **preserves global/metric distances** along principal axes; recommended for preprocessing and when exact distances matter.
- **t-SNE — van der Maaten & Hinton, JMLR 2008** (9:2579–2605). Minimizes KL divergence between high-dimensional and low-dimensional pairwise-similarity distributions; uses a **Student's t-distribution** in low-D to model similarities. **Pitfalls (well-documented):** (1) **inter-cluster distances are meaningless** — two clusters appearing close on screen are not necessarily close in original space; (2) results vary run-to-run (must fix random_state); (3) **no out-of-sample extension** (cannot embed new points); (4) hyperparameters (perplexity) strongly affect output (Wattenberg et al. 2016).
- **UMAP — McInnes, Healy & Melville (2018)** (arXiv 1802.03426; Nat Rev Methods Primers 4:82, 2024). Grounded in Riemannian geometry / fuzzy simplicial sets (algebraic topology). Runs ~O(n log n); has a `transform()` method (out-of-sample embedding); tends to **prefer larger inter-cluster distances** than t-SNE. **Pitfall:** absolute distances are still distorted by the non-linear projection — "you cannot measure pixels on a UMAP plot and draw metric conclusions."
- **Cross-cutting caution (2024–2026):** A 2025 arXiv analysis ("Stop Misusing t-SNE and UMAP for Visual Analytics," 2506.08725) reports that **t-SNE and UMAP are widely shown to exaggerate class separation** relative to other techniques, and that researchers frequently cite the original papers' global-structure-preservation claims incorrectly. Practical guidance from practitioner sources: cluster in original or PCA-reduced space, not in the 2D projection; don't run K-Means on a 2D t-SNE layout; use cosine distance on normalized embeddings.
- **Cluster tendency (Hopkins statistic):** measures whether data has cluster structure vs. being uniformly random — values near 0.5 indicate random/no clustering tendency, values near 1 indicate highly clusterable data (compile note: convention varies; some formulations invert this). Useful as a pre-check before claiming text "separates."
- **Anisotropy / curse of dimensionality:** raw Transformer embeddings occupy a **narrow anisotropic cone** (the anisotropy problem; Gao et al. 2019; Li et al. 2020; Ethayarajh 2019), which inflates cosine similarities and harms separability. Remedies discussed in the literature: removing dominant principal components (Mu & Viswanath 2018), whitening / mapping to an isotropic distribution (BERT-flow, Li et al. 2020; whitening, Su et al. 2021), or contrastive training (SimCSE). In high dimensions, **distance concentration** means nearest- and farthest-neighbor distances converge, weakening any distance-based separation claim — a standard curse-of-dimensionality caveat to flag.

## TARGET 1B — A reasoning test that memorization cannot game

### 1B.1 Contamination: detection and prevention

- **Definitions.** Train-test contamination/leakage = benchmark test data (or near-duplicates) appears in the model's training/pretraining corpus, inflating scores. Distinguished from MTEB's subtler concern of **training on distributionally similar data** (Maintaining MTEB, 2025).
- **N-gram overlap.** Classic detection: flag test items whose n-grams overlap with training data (used in GPT-3, PaLM contamination studies). Limitation: misses paraphrase/rephrasing.
- **Min-K% Prob — Shi et al., 2023** (arXiv 2310.16789; ICLR 2024). A **reference-free membership-inference** method for pretraining-data detection. For an input, take the **k% of tokens with the lowest predicted probabilities**, average their log-probabilities; **threshold** this score to predict membership (seen vs. unseen). Benchmarked on **WikiMIA** (constructed using temporal cutoffs — events after pretraining are guaranteed non-members). Reported **7.4% AUC improvement** over prior methods on WikiMIA. Applied to copyrighted-book detection, contaminated-downstream-example detection, and machine-unlearning auditing. **Min-K%++ (Shi et al. 2024 / ICLR 2025, arXiv 2404.02936)** improves it by normalizing the token log-prob by its mean/variance over the vocabulary; SOTA among reference-free methods.
- **Proving Test Set Contamination — Oren et al., 2023** (arXiv 2310.17623; ICLR 2024; authors Oren, Meister, Chatterji, Ladhak, Hashimoto). **Black-box, provable** contamination test exploiting **exchangeability**: absent contamination, all orderings of an exchangeable benchmark are equally likely, so the model should assign equal likelihood to the canonical ordering and shuffled orderings. If the model assigns **significantly higher log-likelihood to the canonical ordering** than to shuffled permutations, it has memorized example order → contamination. Uses a **sharded likelihood comparison test** (e.g., 50 shards, 250 permutations per shard; truncate to first 5,000 examples). Sensitive enough to detect contamination in models as small as 1.4B params, test sets of 1,000 examples, and datasets appearing only a few times. Auditing five public models found **little evidence of pervasive contamination**; negative control = BioMedLM (PubMed-only). Caveat: benchmarks with non-exchangeable elements (sequential indices, duplicates) break the false-positive guarantee.
- **"Time Travel in LLMs" — Golchin & Surdeanu, 2023** (arXiv 2308.08493; ICLR 2024; University of Arizona). Method = **"guided instruction"**: a prompt containing the **dataset name, partition/split type, and a random-length initial segment** of a reference instance, asking the LLM to complete it (label embedded for labeled datasets). Compared against a **"general instruction"** that omits dataset/partition identity (control). **Instance-level rule:** flagged contaminated if the completion **exactly or nearly matches** the held-out continuation, measured by **ROUGE-L and BLEURT** (guided > general) or by a **GPT-4 few-shot ICL judge** (1 exact + 3 near-exact labeled exemplars; temperature 0). **Partition-level Algorithm 1:** non-parametric bootstrap significance test (10,000 resamples, p ≤ 0.05) on 10 instances, guided vs. general overlap. **Partition-level Algorithm 2:** GPT-4 ICL flags the partition if **≥1 exact or ≥2 near-exact matches** in a sample of 10. Best method (Algorithm 2) achieved **92–100% accuracy** (GPT-4: 14/14 = 100%; GPT-3.5: 13/14 = 92.86%) vs. human labels; found GPT-4 contaminated with AG News, WNLI, and XSum.
- **Rephrased samples — Yang, Chiang, Zheng, Gonzalez & Stoica, 2023** ("Rethinking benchmark and contamination for language models with rephrased samples"). Shows that **paraphrased or translated test items evade n-gram detection** while still inflating scores, demonstrating that simple overlap filters are insufficient. (Compile note: the task brief's "Skywork — Wei/Zhou et al. 2023" refers to the Skywork LLM technical report's contamination analysis; I could not independently verify the exact author attribution from primary sources in this dive — flag as unverified.)
- **Canary strings.** Deliberately inserted unique strings (e.g., in BIG-bench) so that, if a model can reproduce the canary, its training data included that dataset; a prevention/auditing convention rather than a statistical test.
- **LiveBench and live benchmarks:** see 1B.2.

### 1B.2 Post-cutoff / temporal-holdout evaluation as a contamination control

- **LiveBench — White et al., 2024** (arXiv 2406.19314; titled "A Challenging, Contamination-Limited LLM Benchmark"; lead authors Colin White, Samuel Dooley of Abacus.AI; co-authors include Micah Goldblum, Ravid Shwartz-Ziv, Yann LeCun). Three design desiderata: (1) **frequently-updated questions from recent sources** (recently-released math competitions, arXiv papers, news articles, IMDb synopses); (2) **automatic scoring against objective ground truth** (no LLM judge, avoiding judge bias); (3) **diverse hard tasks** (6 categories: math, coding, reasoning, language, instruction-following, data analysis; 18 tasks initially). **Logic by which post-cutoff data rules out memorization:** if a question is authored from a source released *after* a model's training cutoff, it cannot have been in training data, so above-chance performance cannot be memorization. New questions added monthly; rank correlation between successive updates >0.997; mean scores dropped ~1.2% as the benchmark got harder. Top models scored below 70%.
- **LiveCodeBench — Jain et al., 2024.** Continuously collects competition-programming problems (LeetCode, AtCoder, Codeforces) **time-stamped by release date**, enabling evaluation only on problems released after a model's cutoff. Direct evidence of contamination cited: a **drop in performance on problems released after the cutoff date**, and correlation between performance and a problem's pre-cutoff GitHub frequency. LiveBench draws its coding tasks from LiveCodeBench (problems from Nov 2023 onward).
- **Temporal holdout (general principle):** WikiMIA (Shi et al. 2023) and these benchmarks all rely on the property that **events/texts dated after pretraining are guaranteed non-members** — the cleanest available contamination control.

### 1B.3 Operationalizing "reasoning, not memorization" (and the dispute)

- **Counterfactual tasks — Wu et al., 2023/2024** ("Reasoning or Reciting?", arXiv 2307.02477; NAACL 2024; Wu, Qiu, Ross, Akyürek, Chen, Wang, Kim, Andreas, Kim). Constructs **"counterfactual" variants** of 11 tasks that deviate from default assumptions (e.g., arithmetic in non-base-10; **1-based "ThonPy" instead of 0-based Python indexing**; permuted keyboard layouts; altered spatial frames). Logic: if a model truly reasons, it should transfer to the counterfactual condition; if it relies on memorized default-condition procedures, it degrades. Finding: GPT-4/GPT-3.5/Claude/PaLM-2 show **above-chance but consistently and substantially lower** counterfactual performance → ability is "supported at least in part by non-transferable, default-condition-specific behaviors."
- **GSM-Symbolic — Mirzadeh et al., 2024** (arXiv 2410.05229; ICLR 2025; Apple — Mirzadeh, Alizadeh, Shahrokhi, Tuzel, Bengio, Farajtabar). Generates math problems from **symbolic templates** parameterizing names and numerical values of GSM8K problems, allowing controlled instantiations. Key findings: (1) **performance varies noticeably across instantiations of the same template**; (2) accuracy **drops when only numerical values change** (>10 pp swings); (3) **GSM-NoOp**: adding a single irrelevant-but-plausible clause causes catastrophic drops "up to 65%" (e.g., Phi-3-mini 88.0%→22.4%, ~65.6 pp; GPT-4o 95.2%→63.1%, ~32.1 pp); (4) accuracy degrades ~10 pp per added clause. Interpretation: current LLM math ability is "in-distribution pattern matching, not genuine symbolic reasoning." **Contested:** Ivanova (desirivanova.com) and others note that **variance is statistically expected and quantifiable via confidence intervals**, and some drops are within predictable bounds — so the "no reasoning" conclusion is partly a measurement-interpretation question. (Related: "Functional Benchmarks," Srivastava et al. 2024, arXiv 2402.19450.)
- **Quantifying memorization — Carlini, Ippolito, Jagielski, Lee, Tramèr & Zhang, 2022** (arXiv 2202.07646; ICLR 2023; authors listed alphabetically). Defines **(k-)extractable memorization** (Def 3.1, verbatim): "A string s is extractable with k tokens of context from a model f if there exists a (length-k) string p, such that the concatenation [p ‖ s] is contained in the training data for f, and f produces s when prompted with p using greedy decoding." Three **log-linear** findings — memorization grows with (1) **model capacity** (larger models in a family memorize 2–5× more; R²=99.8%; a tenfold size increase → +19 pp), (2) **data duplication** (more-repeated examples are more extractable), and (3) **context length** (the "discoverability phenomenon": 33% of sequences extractable from a 6B model at 50 tokens of context vs. 65% at 450 tokens). Models: GPT-Neo (125M–6B) on The Pile (825GB); 50-token suffix match under greedy decoding; GPT-2 baseline shows no scale growth, confirming genuine memorization (6B GPT-J memorizes ≥1% of The Pile). This grounds the *mechanism* the contamination tests above try to defeat. Caveat the authors flag: the duplication trend generalizes less cleanly across model families (T5/C4 non-monotonic; OPT effect orders of magnitude smaller).
- **Grokking — Power, Burda, Edwards, Babuschkin & Misra, 2022** (arXiv 2201.02177; **ICLR 2022 MATH-AI workshop paper — NOT a main-track proceedings paper**; flag for correct citation). On small **binary operation tables** (modular arithmetic a∘b=c mod 97; permutations of S₅) a 2-layer decoder Transformer first **overfits (training accuracy ~perfect at <10³ optimization steps)** then, after ~10⁵–10⁶ steps, **validation accuracy suddenly jumps from chance to near-perfect** — "long after severely overfitting, validation accuracy sometimes suddenly begins to increase… toward perfect generalization." **Weight decay** is "particularly effective," "more than halving the amount of samples needed"; weight decay toward the origin beats toward initialization; optimization noise (minibatch/gradient/weight noise) also helps (flatter minima; sharpness–validation Spearman −0.795). Relevance: demonstrates that **memorization and generalization are temporally separable phases**, complicating any single-snapshot reasoning-vs-memorization claim.
- **The distinction is contested (report explicitly):** Whether these probes truly isolate "reasoning" from "retrieval" is disputed. Pattern-matching accounts (Razeghi et al. 2022 on train-frequency/test-performance correlation; Dziri et al. 2023 on computation-graph subgraph frequency; Jiang et al. 2024 on token bias) argue apparent reasoning is sophisticated retrieval; others argue degradation under perturbation only bounds *robustness*, not the presence of reasoning. There is no community-agreed operational separation — only converging-evidence arguments.

## TARGET 1C — The flat control for a structured-vs-flat comparison

### 1C.1 RAG and its evaluation: the knobs that must be held equal

- **RAG — Lewis et al., 2020** (arXiv 2005.11401; NeurIPS 2020). Combines **parametric memory** (a pretrained seq2seq generator, BART) with **non-parametric memory** (a dense vector index of Wikipedia) retrieved via **DPR**. Two variants: **RAG-Sequence** (one retrieved doc conditions the whole output) and **RAG-Token** (different docs per token); both **marginalize over the top-K retrieved documents** (default K=5). Retriever and generator trained jointly end-to-end.
- **Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) — Karpukhin et al., 2020** (arXiv 2004.04906; EMNLP 2020). **Bi-encoder**: query and passage encoded separately into dense vectors; relevance = dot product / inner product; trained with a contrastive objective using **in-batch negatives** plus hard (BM25) negatives; enables efficient **maximum-inner-product / nearest-neighbor search**. Outperforms BM25 on open-domain QA. Passages are fixed-length chunks (typically 100 words in the original).
- **The knobs that must be specified and held equal across conditions** (synthesized from the RAG/DPR pipeline literature): **(1) chunking** — chunk size and boundary strategy (balance context completeness vs. specificity); **(2) embedding/retriever model**; **(3) top-k** retrieved; **(4) reranking** (presence/type of a cross-encoder reranker); plus index type and similarity metric. These are the controllable degrees of freedom that confound a structured-vs-flat comparison if not matched.
- **Position effect — "Lost in the Middle," Liu et al., 2023** (arXiv 2307.03172; TACL 2024; Liu, Lin, Hewitt, Paranjape, Bevilacqua, Petroni, Liang). On multi-document QA and key-value retrieval, performance follows a **U-shaped curve**: highest when relevant info is at the **beginning or end** of the context, significantly degraded in the **middle** — even for explicitly long-context models. Implication for fair comparison: **the order/position in which retrieved chunks are presented is itself a confound** and must be controlled. (Caveat 2024–2026: a 2025 arXiv note reports Gemini 2.5 Flash shows no LITM effect on simple factoid needle-in-haystack QA, attributed to positional-encoding/training improvements — the effect is model-dependent and may be attenuating.)
- **RAGAS — Es, James, Espinosa-Anke & Schockaert, 2023** (arXiv 2309.15217; EACL 2024). Reference-free RAG evaluation across **faithfulness, answer relevancy, context precision** (and context recall) — no ground-truth human annotations needed.
- **ARES — Saad-Falcon et al., 2023** (arXiv 2311.09476). Automated RAG evaluation generating **fine-tuned LLM judges** for **context relevance, answer faithfulness, answer relevance**, trained on synthetic data, and using **prediction-powered inference (PPI; Angelopoulos et al. 2023)** with ~150+ human-annotated points to produce **statistical confidence intervals**. Reported to beat RAGAS by 59.3 and 14.4 points on KILT/SuperGLUE settings.

### 1C.2 Structured-context vs. flat-context comparisons

- **GraphRAG — Edge et al., 2024** (arXiv 2404.16130; Microsoft Research; "From Local to Global: A Graph RAG Approach to Query-Focused Summarization"). Pipeline: LLM **extracts an entity knowledge graph** from source docs → **community detection** partitions the graph → **pre-generated community summaries** → at query time, each community summary yields a partial answer, then partial answers are **map-reduce summarized** into a final answer. **Controlled comparison setup:** tested on **~1M-token corpora** (podcast transcripts and news articles) against a **conventional ("naive") vector-RAG baseline**, using an **LLM-as-judge** to score **comprehensiveness and diversity** on **global "sensemaking" questions** (no ground-truth answer). Result (per the GraphRAG paper/Microsoft Research): GraphRAG "using community summaries at any level of the community hierarchy, outperforms naive RAG on comprehensiveness and diversity (~70–80% win rate)," with the **8k context window giving the best comprehensiveness win rate (58.1%)**. Two query modes: **Local** (precise facts) and **Global** (whole-corpus themes).
- **RAPTOR — Sarthi et al., 2024** (arXiv 2401.18059; ICLR 2024; Sarthi, Abdullah, Tuli, Khanna, Goldie, Manning, Stanford). **Recursively embeds, clusters, and summarizes** text chunks to build a **tree of summaries from the bottom up**, retaining both summaries and original chunks (multi-level abstraction). Two retrieval modes: **tree traversal** (layer-by-layer) vs. **collapsed tree** (all nodes considered simultaneously under a token budget) — collapsed tree works better. **Controlled-comparison design:** holds the **retriever constant across conditions** and tests RAPTOR **with and without** each of **SBERT, BM25, and DPR** (Table 1, NarrativeQA, UnifiedQA-3B) — so the only varied factor is RAPTOR's hierarchical organization vs. flat chunk retrieval; this isolates structure as the treatment. With a constant retriever, **RAPTOR alone reaches 62.4% accuracy, a 2% / 5.1% improvement over DPR / BM25**; RAPTOR+SBERT reaches ROUGE-L 30.87% vs. 29.26% for SBERT alone. Coupling **RAPTOR + GPT-4 set a new state-of-the-art on QuALITY at 82.6%, surpassing the previous best of 62.3%** (~20 absolute points).
- **What these studies hold constant (compile note):** GraphRAG matches the underlying corpus and uses the same judge protocol across conditions; RAPTOR explicitly **varies only the indexing/retrieval structure while holding the embedding/retriever model fixed** — the template for an honest structured-vs-flat ablation.

### 1C.3 Controlled-experiment methodology for ML comparisons

- **Ablation / matched conditions:** the structure-isolating principle exemplified by RAPTOR — hold corpus, embedding model, top-k, generator, and prompt constant; vary **only** the structure (flat chunks vs. graph/tree). Any uncontrolled knob (chunk size, retrieval count, context ordering per Lost-in-the-Middle) is a confound.
- **"Show Your Work" — Dodge, Gururangan, Card, Schwartz & Smith, 2019** (arXiv 1909.03004; EMNLP-IJCNLP 2019). Demonstrates **test-set scores alone are insufficient** to conclude which model is best; introduces **expected validation performance as a function of compute budget** (number of hyperparameter trials / training time). Shows several published comparisons would flip conclusions under different compute budgets. Provides a **reporting checklist** (the basis of the NeurIPS/ACL Responsible NLP Research checklists): report hyperparameter search bounds, number of trials, validation performance, compute, and variance.
- **Statistical-comparison practice (synthesized standard):** run **multiple random seeds/runs**, report **means with confidence intervals or standard deviations**, conduct **significance testing** for paired comparisons, and report compute budget. ARES's use of **PPI confidence intervals** and GSM-Symbolic's emphasis on **CIs over single-point accuracy** instantiate this in the RAG/reasoning context.
- **Documentation standards:** **Datasheets for Datasets** (Gebru et al. 2018/2021) and **Model Cards** (Mitchell et al. 2019) document data provenance, assumptions, and intended use — the metadata that makes a structured-vs-flat comparison reproducible and interpretable. Broader reproducibility framing: Pineau's ML reproducibility checklist (NeurIPS 2019); Gundersen et al. on sources of irreproducibility (arXiv 2204.07610).

## Recommendations (methodological, for a researcher implementing from this compilation)

These are staged implementation notes derived strictly from what the sources prescribe; they are not cross-target synthesis.

1. **For 1A separation claims:** Compute separability metrics **in the original (or PCA-reduced) embedding space**, never in a 2D t-SNE/UMAP layout (van der Maaten & Hinton 2008; McInnes et al. 2018; "Stop Misusing t-SNE/UMAP" 2025). Report **both an internal index (silhouette and/or Davies–Bouldin)** and, when labels exist, **an external chance-corrected index (ARI and/or V-measure)** — the PeerJ 2025 study supports silhouette + DBI as most informative for convex clusters; switch to DBCV-class indices for density/concave structure. Pre-check cluster tendency (Hopkins) and address anisotropy (whitening or contrastive embeddings) before interpreting cosine distances. **Threshold guidance the sources actually give:** silhouette near 0 = overlapping; DBI < ~1 = well-separated; ARI/V-measure = 0 under chance, 1 = perfect.
2. **For 1B contamination control:** Prefer a **temporal holdout** (LiveBench/LiveCodeBench logic) as the primary guarantee, because post-cutoff authorship makes memorization impossible by construction. Layer **statistical detection** (Oren et al. exchangeability test for black-box order memorization; Min-K%++ for membership) and **guided-instruction probing** (Golchin & Surdeanu) as audits. To argue "reasoning not memorization," combine a **counterfactual/perturbation probe** (Wu et al.; GSM-Symbolic templates with value/clause perturbations) with **confidence intervals over multiple instantiations** — and explicitly acknowledge the contested status of the distinction.
3. **For 1C fair baseline:** Hold **corpus, embedding/retriever model, chunking, top-k, reranker, generator, and chunk ordering** identical across structured and flat conditions; vary only structure (the RAPTOR ablation template). Report **multiple seeds, means ± CIs, and significance tests** (Dodge et al. 2019), and document with datasheets/model cards. Use RAGAS/ARES component metrics (faithfulness, context/answer relevance) with PPI confidence intervals where automatic scoring is needed.
4. **Benchmarks that would change the approach:** if cluster tendency (Hopkins) indicates no structure, separability metrics are uninterpretable; if an exchangeability/Min-K% audit flags contamination, fall back to a strict temporal holdout; if "Lost in the Middle" sensitivity is detected for the chosen model, randomize or counterbalance chunk position across conditions.
