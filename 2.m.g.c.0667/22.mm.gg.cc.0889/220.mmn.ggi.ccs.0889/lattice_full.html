seed · rotate · translate

A seed point, cycled through [rotate, rotate, translate]: two rotations in scheduled planes (XY/YZ/XZ…), then one self-scaling displacement d = k·(k+1)C−1·D. The form grows ×(k+1) per cycle; the central hole stays a constant proportion. Then normalized: divide by the measured outer radius rmax, recenter on the center of mass, mapping the form to [−1,1] with rmax = 1 unit.

four things not to drift from:

  1. rotation is radius-preserving reorientation — shape by turning, never resizing.
  2. the displacement self-scales — it holds the hole at a constant proportion as the form grows, re-instancing the structure at a new scale. not raw outward reach.
  3. only ratios and counts survive — never absolutes. fully relational.
  4. normalization (divide by own rmax, recenter COM) keeps the form bounded and divides out absolute scale — which is why only ratios survive, and how the unit stays one: the form measured against itself (rmax/rmax = 1). this is III: unit holds one.
