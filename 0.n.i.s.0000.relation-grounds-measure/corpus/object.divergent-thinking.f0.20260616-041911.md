**statement (plain)** — Divergent thinking is the cognitive process of producing multiple, varied ideas from a single starting point (e.g. "list uses for a brick"), as opposed to *convergent* thinking, which narrows to one correct answer. Performance is scored along dimensions including **fluency** (number of responses), **flexibility** (number of distinct categories), **originality** (statistical rarity), and **elaboration** (detail). It is the most widely used proxy for creative *potential*.

**basis** — Introduced by Guilford as a family of measurable intellectual abilities within his account of creativity [[source.guilford-1950]] [confirmed]. Runco & Acar's review establishes that divergent-thinking tests are reliable and, relative to defined criteria, reasonably valid predictors of creative performance [[source.runco-acar-2012]] [confirmed].

**limits** — Divergent-thinking scores indicate potential, not realized creative achievement; validity is a matter of degree and depends on the criterion and scoring method (e.g. originality scores are sensitive to fluency and to sample composition) [[source.runco-acar-2012]] [confirmed].
