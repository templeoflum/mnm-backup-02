Rainer Schandry, "Heart Beat Perception and Emotional Experience," *Psychophysiology* **18**(4): 483–488 (1981). DOI 10.1111/j.1469-8986.1981.tb02486.x. PMID 7267933.

Contains — the **mental tracking (heartbeat-counting) task**: participants silently count their felt heartbeats over signalled intervals without taking a pulse; the reported count is compared with the actual count from ECG to yield a heartbeat perception score. Schandry reported that better heartbeat perceivers showed higher experienced anxiety and trait emotional lability, linking interoceptive perception to emotional experience.

Role — the foundational psychophysical method for measuring interoceptive (cardiac) accuracy; the Schandry task remains the most widely used interoception measure, though its validity is debated (see Garfinkel 2015 and later critiques of the counting paradigm).
