Contemplative science is the empirical, interdisciplinary study of meditation and other contemplative practices — bridging cognitive science, clinical psychology, and neuroscience with first-person methods drawn from contemplative traditions (chiefly Buddhist). It treats meditation as a trainable family of attentional and emotional-regulatory regimes and studies their phenomenology, neural correlates, and health effects.

**origin** — Varela, Thompson & Rosch, *The Embodied Mind* (MIT Press, 1991) opened a sustained dialogue between cognitive science, phenomenology, and Buddhist meditative psychology, proposing enactive cognition and grounding the program that became contemplative science [[source.varela-thompson-rosch-1991]] [confirmed]. The methodological keystone — *neurophenomenology*, pairing rigorous first-person reports with neural measures — was set out by Varela [[source.varela-1996]] [confirmed]. In parallel, the clinical arm grew from Kabat-Zinn's secular mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR) program, codified in *Full Catastrophe Living* (Delacorte, 1990) [[source.kabat-zinn-1990]] [confirmed].

**codification** — the neuroscientific research program was consolidated by Lutz, Dunne & Davidson's introduction to meditation research in *The Cambridge Handbook of Consciousness* (2007), which framed meditation as a scientific explanandum [[source.lutz-dunne-davidson-2007]] [confirmed], and by the influential focused-attention / open-monitoring taxonomy of Lutz, Slagter, Dunne & Davidson (2008) [[source.lutz-2008]] [confirmed]. The construct of mindfulness was given a working operational definition by Bishop et al. (2004) [[source.bishop-2004]] [confirmed].

**door** — Tang, Hölzel & Posner, "The neuroscience of mindfulness meditation," *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* (2015) is a standard review of mechanisms and evidence [[source.tang-2015]] [confirmed]; Goyal et al.'s JAMA Internal Medicine meta-analysis (2014) is the standard reference for the (small-to-moderate, bracketed) clinical evidence base [[source.goyal-2014]] [confirmed].

**lineage** — the field has two intertwined founding strands: a contemplative-cognitive-science strand (Varela / Mind & Life) and a clinical-mindfulness strand (Kabat-Zinn / MBSR). They are complementary origins rather than competing claims; "contemplative science" as a named program crystallized in the 2000s as both strands converged on shared neuroscientific methods.

*grade [s] — origin (Varela et al. 1991 / Varela 1996 / Kabat-Zinn 1990), codification (Lutz Dunne Davidson 2007, Lutz et al. 2008, Bishop et al. 2004), and door (Tang et al. 2015, Goyal et al. 2014) all present.*
