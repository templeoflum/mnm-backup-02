Verbatim citation: Clark, Andy, and David J. Chalmers. "The Extended Mind." *Analysis* 58, no. 1 (1998): 7–19.

Contains: the argument for active externalism — that parts of the external world, when suitably coupled to the agent, are constituents of cognitive processes and (in the case of standing beliefs) of the mind itself. States the parity principle: if a process in the world would count as cognitive were it done in the head, then it is part of the cognitive process. The Otto-and-Inga thought experiment (Otto's notebook playing the functional role of biological memory) is the central illustration of an extended belief. **[confirmed]**
