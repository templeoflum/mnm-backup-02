Full citation — G. G. Stokes, "On the Theories of the Internal Friction of Fluids in Motion, and of the Equilibrium and Motion of Elastic Solids," *Transactions of the Cambridge Philosophical Society*, vol. 8 (1845), pp. 287–319. Reprinted in *Mathematical and Physical Papers*, vol. I, Cambridge Univ. Press (1880), pp. 75–129.

Contains — an independent, continuum-based derivation of the equations of viscous fluid motion using a stress–strain-rate constitutive relation, giving the modern form of what is now called the Navier–Stokes equations. Stokes noted that the boundary condition at a solid surface was then unknown; the no-slip condition was accepted only much later. The Navier (1822) / Stokes (1845) pairing is the canonical attribution.

Access — historical; full text on the Internet Archive and in Stokes's collected *Mathematical and Physical Papers*. Citation detail (vol. 8, pp. 287–319) confirmed against multiple bibliographic records. **[confirmed as attribution]**
