Full citation — A. Ya. Khinchin (Khintchine), "Sur la loi des grands nombres," *Comptes Rendus de l'Académie des Sciences*, Paris, **188** (1929), 477–479.

Role — the modern **weak law of large numbers for independent, identically distributed random variables**: existence of a finite mean E[X] = μ alone suffices for the sample mean to converge in probability to μ; finite variance is not required. This strictly generalizes Bernoulli's and Chebyshev's earlier (finite-variance) forms. Proved via characteristic functions / the method of moments.

Access — historical; Comptes Rendus digitized at gallica.bnf.fr. Attribution and the finite-mean-suffices statement corroborated by Wolfram MathWorld ("Weak Law of Large Numbers"), Encyclopedia of Mathematics ("Law of large numbers"), and standard texts (Feller). **[confirmed]**

Note — Khinchin also (1924) proved the law of the iterated logarithm and (1928) introduced the term "strong law of large numbers"; those are distinct works.
