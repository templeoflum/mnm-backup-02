Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics*, Book VI (Bekker pages 1138b–1145a; conventional Greek text). Standard English translations: W. D. Ross (Oxford, 1925), rev. J. O. Urmson in *The Complete Works of Aristotle* (Princeton/Bollingen, 1984); Terence Irwin (Hackett, 2nd ed. 1999); Roger Crisp (Cambridge, 2000).

Contains — the enumeration of the five intellectual virtues (states by which the soul grasps truth): *techne* (craft/art), *episteme* (scientific knowledge), *phronesis* (practical wisdom), *sophia* (theoretical wisdom), *nous* (intuitive intellect). *Episteme* concerns what cannot be otherwise (the eternal/necessary); *techne* is "a state involving true reason concerned with production" (poiesis); *phronesis* is "a true and reasoned state of capacity to act with regard to the things that are good or bad for man." The poiesis/praxis distinction — production has an end beyond itself (the artifact), action (praxis) has its end in the doing itself — sits at NE VI.4–5 (Bekker ~1140a–1140b).
