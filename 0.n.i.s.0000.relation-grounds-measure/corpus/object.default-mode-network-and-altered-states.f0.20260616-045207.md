**statement (plain)** — The default mode network (DMN) is a set of brain regions — including medial prefrontal cortex and posterior cingulate cortex — that is active at rest and attenuated during goal-directed tasks. A central finding of altered-states neuroscience is that altered states involve altered DMN function: in the psychedelic state induced by psilocybin, DMN activity and within-network connectivity are *reduced*, and the magnitude of this reduction tracks the subjective intensity of the experience. Changes in DMN activity are likewise reported for meditation and other altered states, making DMN modulation a leading candidate correlate of disruptions to the ordinary sense of self.

**basis** — Raichle et al. establish the default-mode baseline empirically (uniform resting oxygen extraction, consistent task-induced deactivations), giving the network its definition [[source.raichle-2001]] [confirmed]. Carhart-Harris et al. show with ASL and BOLD fMRI that psilocybin *decreases* cerebral blood flow and BOLD signal in DMN hubs and reduces DMN connectivity, with effect size correlating with reported intensity [[source.carhart-harris-2012]] [confirmed].

**limits** — The DMN itself is defined by resting-state correlations, and its precise boundaries and functional role are debated; the link to altered states is strongest and best replicated for classic psychedelics, thinner and more mixed for other states (some meditation and pathological states show different or opposite changes). Correlation with subjective intensity is not yet a mechanistic account. Recorded as an empirical correlate, not a settled cause. [candidate, partially confirmed]
