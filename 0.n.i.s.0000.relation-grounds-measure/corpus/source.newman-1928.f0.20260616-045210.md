M. H. A. Newman, "Mr. Russell's 'Causal Theory of Perception'," *Mind* 37, no. 146 (1928): 137–148. DOI: 10.1093/mind/XXXVII.146.137.

Contains: the original "Newman objection," raised against Bertrand Russell's structuralism in *The Analysis of Matter* (1927). The point: a claim that the world has a certain abstract structure, with the nature of the relata left open, is trivially true of any domain of the right *cardinality* — so "only cardinality questions are open to discovery." Knowing structure-and-nothing-but-structure therefore conveys almost no information. Revived against modern structural realism (notably by Demopoulos & Friedman 1985) as the central threat to ESR and to Ramsey-sentence structuralism.

Access: paywalled at Oxford Academic (Mind); widely reprinted and discussed.
