Full citation — L. Euler, "Methodus generalis summandi progressiones" (E25), *Commentarii academiae scientiarum Petropolitanae* 6 (1732/33), pp. 68–97; presented 1732, printed 1738. (Euler developed the method further in E47, "Inventio summae cuiusque seriei ex dato termino generali," Comm. acad. sci. Petrop. 8, 1741.)

Contains Euler's general summation method relating a finite/infinite sum to an integral plus correction terms — the Euler side of what is now called the Euler–Maclaurin summation formula. Euler used it to evaluate slowly converging series (including ζ(2) to ~20 decimals) and to estimate the constant γ. Per MacTutor, Euler described the method in a 1736 letter to James Stirling and credited Maclaurin's independent discovery.

Role — origin (Euler side): the summation formula. Colin Maclaurin published an equivalent result in his 1742 *Treatise of Fluxions* [[source.maclaurin-1742]].
