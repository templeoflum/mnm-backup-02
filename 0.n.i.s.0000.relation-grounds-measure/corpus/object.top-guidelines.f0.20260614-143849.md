**statement (plain)** — the TOP guidelines are a menu journals use to make research more transparent. There are eight things a journal can ask for (like sharing data, sharing code, or preregistering the study), and for each one the journal picks how strict to be — three levels, from "just say whether you did it" up to "a third party verifies you did it." Journals adopt the standards and levels that fit their field.

**statement** — the Transparency and Openness Promotion (TOP) guidelines are a modular policy framework of **eight standards** — citation, data transparency, analytic-code/materials transparency (materials and code), design and analysis transparency, study preregistration, analysis-plan preregistration, and replication — each implementable at **three levels of increasing stringency**: Level 1 (disclosure of whether practice was followed), Level 2 (require the practice where permissible), Level 3 (require third-party verification). Journals adopt standards and levels independently, embedding [[object.preregistration]] and [[object.registered-report]] into editorial policy. **[confirmed]**

**basis/origin** — Nosek et al., "Promoting an Open Research Culture" (the TOP Guidelines), *Science* 348 (2015):1422–1425, from the Center for Open Science. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**lineage** — single clear authorship (Nosek et al. 2015 / COS). It is a configuration/governance layer over the underlying practices rather than a new diagnosis; it operationalizes the responses to [[object.file-drawer-problem]], [[object.researcher-degrees-of-freedom]], and the [[object.replication-crisis]]. **[confirmed]**
