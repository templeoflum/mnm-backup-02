**statement (plain)** — Mednick's associative theory holds that creative thinking consists of forming associative elements into new combinations that meet some requirement of usefulness. Individuals differ in their **associative hierarchies**: those with "flatter" hierarchies have many associations of roughly equal strength to a stimulus, making remote (less obvious) connections more accessible — and thus more likely to be creative. The **Remote Associates Test (RAT)** operationalizes this: given three cue words (e.g. *cottage / Swiss / cake*), the solver finds one word linking all three (*cheese*).

**basis** — Theory and test introduced by Mednick [[source.mednick-1962]] [confirmed]; treated as a foundational associative account and standard convergent-creativity measure in Sawyer's survey [[source.sawyer-2012]] [confirmed].

**limits** — The RAT taps convergent (single-solution) creative problem solving and verbal/associative ability, not the full breadth of creativity; performance is influenced by vocabulary and language background. The flat-hierarchy mechanism is a model of associative differences rather than a settled causal account [[source.sawyer-2012]] [candidate, partially confirmed].
