**citation** — Isaac Newton, *De analysi per aequationes numero terminorum infinitas* (written 1669, published 1711); method also in *De metodis fluxionum et serierum infinitarum* (written 1671, published 1736). Newton applied it only to polynomials, extracting a sequence of error corrections from an initial estimate.

**role** — origin of the Newton (Newton–Raphson) iteration for root-finding, which becomes Newton's method in optimization when applied to the gradient (set ∇f = 0). Lineage: Joseph Raphson (1690) gave a reusable iterative expression; Thomas Simpson (1740) framed it for general nonlinear equations via calculus, extended it to systems of two equations, and noted its use for optimization by setting the derivative to zero.

**access** — primary works are historical; standard secondary treatment in any numerical-analysis text and on MacTutor / Wikipedia "Newton's method."

**[confirmed as attribution]** — name "Newton's method" is conventional; the modern optimization form (gradient/Hessian) is a later synthesis, lineage carried.
