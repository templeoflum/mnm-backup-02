**origin** — Noam Chomsky, *Syntactic Structures* (Mouton, 1957) — a finite generative grammar specifies the infinite set of grammatical sentences; the inadequacy of finite-state models motivates rules that reference their own categories. [[source.chomsky-1957]] **[confirmed]**

**codification** — Hauser, Chomsky & Fitch, "The Faculty of Language…" (*Science* 298, 2002) — recursion named as the candidate lone uniquely-human, uniquely-linguistic component (FLN), giving the field its modern center of gravity. [[source.hauser-chomsky-fitch-2002]] **[confirmed]**

**door** — Andrew Carnie, *Syntax: A Generative Introduction* (4th ed., Wiley-Blackwell, 2021) — the standard teaching entry into the generative machinery in which recursion lives. [[source.carnie-2021]] **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — the *competence/performance* split and the special unparsability of multiple center-embedding trace to Miller & Chomsky (1963), distinguishing what the grammar generates from what users can process. [[source.miller-chomsky-1963]] The claim that recursion is universal is *contested*: Pinker & Jackendoff (2005) argue recursion is neither the only special thing nor uniquely linguistic [[source.pinker-jackendoff-2005]], and Everett (2005) reports Pirahã as lacking syntactic recursion [[source.everett-2005]]. *(carried as live dispute, not flattened.)*

*grade [s] — origin (Chomsky 1957) + codification (HCF 2002) + door (Carnie 2021); contested universality held as lineage.*
