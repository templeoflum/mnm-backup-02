Verbatim citation: T. M. Luhrmann, *When God Talks Back: Understanding the American Evangelical Relationship with God*, New York: Alfred A. Knopf, 2012.

Contains: an ethnography of the Vineyard movement (charismatic evangelical Christianity) based on fieldwork in Chicago and Northern California, examining how ordinary believers come to experience an interactive, present God. Develops the **absorption hypothesis**: those with a proclivity for absorption (total, immersive attention) who train it through disciplined prayer practice come to report sharper inner sensory imagery and occasional sensory overrides (hearing God, seeing visions). Includes an experimental component in which participants trained with daily audio practice for a month, with absorption and reported spiritual experience increasing with practice — positioning learned, cultivated experience rather than pathology as the mechanism of charismatic religious perception.

Access: paperback Vintage 2012 (ISBN 9780307277275); reviewed in *Pneuma*, *HAU: Journal of Ethnographic Theory* (with author response), and *New Directions in the Anthropology of Christianity*. NYT Notable Book 2012. The absorption-and-training mechanism is also set out in Luhrmann, Nusbaum & Thisted, "The Absorption Hypothesis" (*American Anthropologist*, 2010).
