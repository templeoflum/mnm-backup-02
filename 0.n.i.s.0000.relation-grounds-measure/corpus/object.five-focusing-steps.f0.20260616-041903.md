statement (plain): a repeating cycle, also called the Process of Ongoing Improvement (POOGI):
1. Identify the system's constraint.
2. Exploit the constraint — get the most from it without major investment.
3. Subordinate everything else to the decision made in step 2.
4. Elevate the constraint — invest to increase its capacity if it still limits the system.
5. If in a previous step the constraint has been broken, go back to step 1 — and do not let inertia become the system's constraint.

basis: set out as the five focusing steps in Goldratt's 1990 monograph, where they structure the process of ongoing improvement [[source.goldratt-1990]] [confirmed]; restated and applied throughout the 2010 Handbook as the canonical improvement cycle of TOC [[source.cox-schleier-2010]] [confirmed].

limits: the steps are a heuristic management procedure, established through practice rather than proved. Step 5's warning against inertia is essential — the cycle assumes the operator re-identifies the constraint after each elevation, since solving one constraint typically moves the binding constraint elsewhere.
