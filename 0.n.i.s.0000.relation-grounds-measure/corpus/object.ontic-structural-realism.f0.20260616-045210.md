**statement (plain)** — ontic structural realism (OSR) goes past the *epistemic* claim "we can know only structure" to the *metaphysical* claim "structure is all there is." The reason we can know only the relations is that there is nothing further — no self-subsistent individual objects behind the structure. Objects, if they figure at all, are nodes in or abstractions from the relational structure, not the basic furniture. Motivation comes from modern physics: quantum statistics and the underdetermination of particle individuality, and the treatment of spacetime points, make a metaphysics of self-standing individuals look optional, whereas the *structure* is robust.

**basis** — Ladyman (1998) introduces the epistemic/ontic distinction and argues for the ontic reading [[source.ladyman-1998]] **[confirmed]**. French & Ladyman (2003) develop and defend the metaphysical form via quantum physics [[source.french-ladyman-2003]] **[confirmed]**. Ladyman & Ross (2007) give the book-length statement, pairing OSR for fundamental physics with "rainforest realism" (real patterns) for the special sciences [[source.ladyman-ross-2007]] **[confirmed]**.

**limits / contested** — recorded as a recognized position, not asserted true. Variants run from *moderate* (objects and structure on a par) to *eliminativist* (no objects at all). The eliminativist reading is challenged by the demand that relations require relata (see object.relations-without-relata). Carried as lineage, debate ongoing. **[confirmed]**
