Richard H. Thaler and Cass R. Sunstein, "Libertarian Paternalism," *American Economic Review* 93(2):175–179 (May 2003) — Papers and Proceedings.

Contains — the first published statement of **libertarian paternalism**: the claim that it is both possible and legitimate for institutions to steer people's choices in welfare-improving directions while preserving freedom of choice. Argues that because there is often no neutral way to present options, some "paternalism" (in the sense of influence) is unavoidable; the design question is which influence, not whether. The conceptual seed later expanded into *Nudge* (2008).
