**statement (plain).** A "thin slice" is a brief excerpt of an expressive behavior stream — under five minutes, often under thirty seconds. Judgments based on thin slices predict objective outcomes (e.g., teacher ratings, clinical and social outcomes) at well above chance; lengthening the observation window does not reliably improve accuracy.

**basis.** Meta-analysis of 38 results gave an overall predictive effect size r ≈ .39; slices under ~30 s performed comparably to 4–5 min observations [[source.ambady-rosenthal-1992]] **[confirmed]**. Willis & Todorov extended the finding to static faces, showing trait impressions (trustworthiness, competence) stabilize by ~100 ms exposure and barely improve with more time [[source.willis-todorov-2006]] **[confirmed]**. Limits: predictive accuracy is not the same as correctness — thin-slice judgments can be biased and can encode stereotype rather than valid signal; effect sizes vary widely by outcome domain and by what is being judged. The 1992 estimate is a meta-analytic average over heterogeneous studies, not a fixed constant.
