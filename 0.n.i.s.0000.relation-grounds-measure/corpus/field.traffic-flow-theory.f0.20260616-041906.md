# traffic-flow-theory

Macroscopic traffic flow theory treats vehicular traffic as a compressible continuum, relating the state variables density (k), flow (q), and mean speed (v) through the identity q = k·v and a conservation law for vehicle number.

**origin** — The continuum (kinematic-wave) theory of traffic flow was founded independently by M. J. Lighthill & G. B. Whitham (1955), who applied kinematic-wave theory to estimate how a region of increased concentration moves along a crowded road, and by P. I. Richards (1956), who introduced shock waves into the same continuum picture; together these are the LWR model [[source.lighthill-whitham-1955]] [[source.richards-1956]] [confirmed].

**codification** — The empirical flow-density-speed relation (the fundamental diagram) was first measured and formalized by B. D. Greenshields (1935), who observed a linear speed-density relation yielding a parabolic flow-density curve [[source.greenshields-1935]] [confirmed].

**door** — The modern comprehensive textbook treatment, covering macroscopic and microscopic models, data, and simulation, is Treiber & Kesting, *Traffic Flow Dynamics* (Springer, 2013) [[source.treiber-kesting-2013]] [confirmed].

**lineage** — The LWR founding is dual: Lighthill & Whitham (1955) supplied the kinematic-wave continuum; Richards (1956) supplied the shock-wave completion. The two are conventionally credited jointly rather than to a single originator.

*grade [s] — origin + codification + door all present.*
