**statement (plain)** — an automatism is a coordinated, often skilled or meaningful act — writing, speaking, drawing, tilting a table — carried out without the person consciously intending or owning it. The behavior is purposive and complex, yet the agent experiences it as happening *to* them or as issuing from elsewhere (a spirit, another self). Automatic writing, planchette / Ouija messages, trance speech, and crystal-vision are the classic forms.

**statement (theoretical)** — Janet: automatisms are the visible activity of dissociated systems — *total* (somnambulism, catalepsy) or *partial* (subconscious acts, automatic writing) — running below the personal synthesis [[source.janet-1889]]. Myers classifies **motor automatisms** (automatic writing, speaking, table-tilting) and **sensory automatisms** (crystal-gazing, hallucinated voices) as messages from the **subliminal self** crossing the threshold into supraliminal awareness [[source.myers-1903]].

**basis (empirical / method)** — *support*: automatic writing and ideomotor table movement are reliably producible under divided attention and suggestion; the dissociation of authorship is demonstrable. *limits*: the *content* of automatisms (apparent communications, novel "facts") is explained within the psychology by cryptomnesia, suggestion, and subliminal elaboration — its evidential value for any external (spirit) source is not established and is bracketed. Myers's reading of automatism as evidence of survival is recorded **[confirmed as attribution]** (the text advances it), not as a confirmed finding.

**lineage** — depends on dissociation as its substrate [[object.dissociation]]; the *mechanism* of the motor act is the ideomotor principle; the two registers (psychological vs. spiritualist interpretation of what speaks through the automatism) run through every case from Stainton Moses to Hélène Smith.
