**statement (plain)** — a fonds is everything one creator (an organization, family, or person) produced and gathered while doing its work, taken as a single whole. It is the basic unit the archive keeps together. A record on its own means little; it means most as part of the fonds it belongs to.

**statement** — the fonds is "the entire body of records of an organization, family, or individual that have been created and accumulated as the result of an organic process reflecting the functions of the creator" ([[source.saa-dictionary]], citing Jenkinson 1937, p. 101). It is the chief archival unit of the Continental system and the object that [[object.provenance]] (respect des fonds) keeps intact. Modern theory treats it as **an intellectual construct** — the conceptual sum of descriptions at series level and below capturing the creator's administrative, historical, and functional character (Cook 1993; Haworth 1993) — not merely a physical pile. **[confirmed]**

**basis** — records are organic: they accrue as a by-product of activity, not as deliberate collection. Because meaning is contextual, the fonds (the organic whole) is the natural unit of arrangement, description, and preservation, and provenance forbids intermingling one fonds with another. **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — rooted in respect des fonds (de Wailly, 1841) and the organic conception in the Dutch Manual ([[source.muller-feith-fruin-1898]]); the much-cited definition is Jenkinson's 1937 *Manual* ([[source.jenkinson-1922]], 2nd ed.). Canadian archivists deliberately adopted the French term "fonds" to escape the competing North American labels record group, manuscript group, and collection (Franco 2015, p. 135). Reconceived as an intellectual construct by Terry Cook and Peter Haworth (1993). **[confirmed]**

**sources** — [[source.jenkinson-1922]]; [[source.muller-feith-fruin-1898]]; [[source.schellenberg-1956]]; [[source.saa-dictionary]].
