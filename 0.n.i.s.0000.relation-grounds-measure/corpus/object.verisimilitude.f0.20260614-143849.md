**statement (plain)** — Popper wanted a way to say that a refuted theory can still be *closer to the truth* than the one before it — so that science makes progress even when its theories are false (Newton over Kepler, say). Verisimilitude, or "truthlikeness," is that notion: a measure of how near a theory is to the whole truth, combining how much truth it captures against how much falsehood it carries.

**statement** — Popper defined verisimilitude by partitioning a theory's logical consequences into its *truth-content* (its true consequences) and its *falsity-content* (its false consequences). Theory t₂ has greater verisimilitude than t₁ when t₂'s truth-content includes t₁'s and t₂'s falsity-content does not exceed t₁'s (with at least one strict inclusion), or vice versa. This let Popper define scientific progress as increasing truthlikeness, using Tarski's correspondence theory of truth. **[confirmed]**

**status** — established as a goal/concept of Popper's epistemology, but his *formal definition* is known to fail (see lineage). The intuitive notion survives; the formal one does not.

**basis** — verisimilitude completes the conjecture-and-refutation picture: [[object.conjecture-and-refutation]] explains how theories are eliminated, and verisimilitude is meant to explain why successive surviving conjectures constitute *progress toward truth* rather than mere change. [[field.popperian-falsificationism]]

**lineage (refutation)** — in 1974, David Miller and Pavel Tichý, working independently, proved Popper's qualitative *and* quantitative definitions formally defective: for two **false** theories where one has excess content over the other, both its truth-content and its falsity-content exceed the rival's, so Popper's comparison conditions can be met only when the theory being judged is *true*. Hence on Popper's definition no false theory is closer to the truth than any other — the "relative worthlessness of all falsehoods" — which is exactly the case the concept was meant to cover. Popper conceded the formal failure but maintained the concept's heuristic value; later workers rebuilt truthlikeness on similarity between states of affairs. **[confirmed as attribution]**
