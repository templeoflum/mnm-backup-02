**origin** — Empedocles of Acragas (5th c. BCE) named the four "roots" (*rhizōmata*) — fire, air, water, earth — combined and separated by Love (*Philotēs*) and Strife (*Neikos*). [[source.kirk-raven-schofield-1983]] **[confirmed as attribution]**

**codification** — Aristotle systematized the four as combinations of the elementary qualities (hot/cold × wet/dry) and added a fifth, *aether* (quintessence), the incorruptible substance of the celestial spheres. *On the Heavens* / *On Generation and Corruption*. [[source.aristotle-de-caelo]] **[confirmed as attribution]**

**door** — Kirk, Raven & Schofield, *The Presocratic Philosophers* (1983), for Empedocles; Aristotle, *On the Heavens*.

**lineage** — the four-element scheme passed through Aristotle, Galenic humoral medicine, alchemy, and Western astrology — carried as lineage. Held as the tradition's natural philosophy (members `status: doctrinal`).
