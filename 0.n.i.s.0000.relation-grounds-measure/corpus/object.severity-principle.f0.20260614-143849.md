**statement (plain)** — you have evidence for a claim only to the degree that it has been put through a test that probably would have exposed it as false if it really were false — and it passed anyway. Passing an easy test that could not have caught the error counts for nothing.

**statement** — a claim or hypothesis H passes a *severe test* T with data x to the extent that (i) x agrees with or "fits" H, and (ii) with very high probability the test would have produced a result fitting H less well than x does, *were H false or incorrect*. Severity is a property of the H–x–T triple, not of H alone. Mayo separates two requirements:
- **weak severity** — there is *no* evidence for H if the test was practically incapable of finding flaws in H even when present (an "easy" test warrants nothing);
- **strong severity** — there *is* evidence for H to the extent H survives a test highly capable of detecting flaws or discrepancies, and few or none are found. **[confirmed]**

**basis** — severity is cashed out in [[object.error-probabilities-as-inference|error probabilities]]: condition (ii) is the probability, under the relevant alternative(s), of a result fitting H less well than the observed x. It fuses the [[field.popperian-falsificationism|Popperian]] demand for stringent tests with the Neyman–Pearson machinery of significance level and power, but reads them *epistemically* — as a post-data measure of how well-probed H is in the case at hand, not merely a long-run rejection rule. The quantity answers "how well probed" H is, expressly *not* "how probable" H is.

lineage — introduced in [[source.mayo-1996]] (*Error and the Growth of Experimental Knowledge*); the weak/strong formulation and the "Neyman–Pearson philosophy of induction" framing developed with Aris Spanos [[source.mayo-spanos-2006]]; restated as the organizing principle of [[source.mayo-2018]] (*Statistical Inference as Severe Testing*). The slogan form — "a claim is severely tested to the extent it has been subjected to and passes a test that probably would have found flaws were they present" — is Mayo's. **[confirmed]**
