**statement (plain)** — a registered report is a kind of journal article where the editors review and accept your study *before* you run it, based on the question and method alone. If they grant "in-principle acceptance," they commit to publishing the finished paper no matter how the results turn out — as long as you follow the plan you registered. That removes the incentive to chase significant results.

**statement** — a registered report is a two-stage peer-reviewed publication format. At **Stage 1**, authors submit the introduction, hypotheses, methods, and analysis plan (often with pilot data); reviewers evaluate the question's importance and the design's soundness, and the journal may grant **in-principle acceptance (IPA)**. After IPA, data are collected. At **Stage 2**, the completed manuscript is reviewed for adherence to the registered protocol and is published regardless of whether results are statistically significant. It is a journal-integrated, gated form of [[object.preregistration]] aimed directly at [[object.file-drawer-problem]] and [[object.researcher-degrees-of-freedom]]. **[confirmed]**

**basis/origin** — launched by Chris Chambers as a new publishing initiative at the journal *Cortex* in 2013. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**lineage** — clear single root (Chambers / Cortex 2013); since adopted by 300+ journals. It is a special case of [[object.preregistration]] (which can be done independently of any journal), distinguished by the binding pre-results editorial commitment. **[confirmed]**

**sources** — Chambers, "Registered Reports: a new publishing initiative at Cortex," *Cortex* 49(3) (2013):609–610 [[source.chambers-2013]]; Center for Open Science registered-reports initiative [[source.cos-io]].
