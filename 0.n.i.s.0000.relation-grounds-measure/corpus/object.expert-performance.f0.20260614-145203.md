**statement (plain)** — a research stance for studying what makes experts expert. Instead of asking people about their skill or relying on reputation, you find tasks that *capture* an expert's superior performance under controlled, repeatable conditions in the lab, confirm the performance is genuinely reproducibly better, analyze the mechanisms behind it, and then ask how those mechanisms were acquired through training. Expertise, on this view, is mostly built, not innate.

**statement** — the expert-performance approach (Ericsson and colleagues, framed in the 1993 paper and elaborated since) defines expert performance as *consistently superior performance on representative tasks* that capture the essence of a domain, and insists it be measured under standardized conditions rather than inferred from experience or social status. Once captured, the superior performance is analyzed for its underlying mechanisms (e.g., domain-specific memory and representations) and those mechanisms are traced to extended, structured training — centrally, [[object.deliberate-practice]]. **[confirmed]**

**basis & limits** — a methodological program with strong empirical traction (chess, music, sports, medicine), distinguished from older expertise research by demanding *reproducible* lab performance rather than self-report or tenure. Its strong nurture-leaning interpretation — that deliberate practice largely accounts for individual differences — is contested: [[source.macnamara-2014]] shows practice explains a minority of performance variance, leaving room for genetics, starting age, working memory, and other factors. The *approach* (capture, then explain, then trace) is durable; the *magnitude* of practice's role is the disputed part.

**lineage** — articulated in Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Römer, *Psychological Review* 100(3) (1993) [[source.ericsson-1993]]; codified in the *Cambridge Handbook of Expertise and Expert Performance* (Ericsson et al., eds., 2006/2018); popular synthesis Ericsson & Pool, *Peak* (2016). Empirical bounding: [[source.macnamara-2014]].
