Sarah N. Garfinkel, Anil K. Seth, Adam B. Barrett, Keisuke Suzuki, Hugo D. Critchley, "Knowing your own heart: Distinguishing interoceptive accuracy from interoceptive awareness," *Biological Psychology* **104**: 65–74 (2015). DOI 10.1016/j.biopsycho.2014.11.004. PMID 25451381.

Contains — the operational framework that organizes the current field. Three dissociable dimensions are defined and shown to be separable in data: **interoceptive accuracy** (objective performance on an interoceptive task, e.g. heartbeat detection), **interoceptive sensibility** (self-reported subjective disposition/belief about one's interoceptive ability, via questionnaire), and **interoceptive awareness** (metacognitive correspondence between confidence and accuracy — typically operationalized as the trial-by-trial confidence–accuracy relationship). The paper shows accuracy, sensibility and metacognitive awareness are distinct and only weakly mutually predictive.

Role — door / standard methodological reference; the taxonomy nearly every contemporary interoception paper cites.
