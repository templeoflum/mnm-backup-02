# recognition-primed-decision

**statement (plain):** The recognition-primed decision (RPD) model holds that experienced decision makers facing a familiar situation under pressure do not generate and compare multiple options. Instead they recognize the situation as an instance of a type, which simultaneously primes plausible goals, relevant cues, expectancies, and a typical course of action. They then evaluate that single candidate action by mentally simulating how it would play out; if the simulation reveals problems they modify the action or move to the next plausible one, but they rarely deliberate over a formal option set. The model thus integrates intuition (pattern recognition) with analysis (mental simulation).

**basis (method / support):** RPD was abstracted from critical-decision-method interviews with urban fireground commanders and other experts, who consistently reported acting on the first workable option rather than comparing alternatives [[source.klein-calderwood-macgregor-1989]]. The model's full exposition — its variants (simple match, diagnosing the situation, evaluating a course of action) and the role of mental simulation — appears in *Sources of Power* [[source.klein-1998]]. Klein's retrospective summarizes RPD as a central NDM contribution and reports its replication across domains [[source.klein-2008]]. [confirmed]

**limits:** Evidence rests substantially on retrospective interviews and proficient performers in high-validity domains; RPD describes skilled performance and does not guarantee correctness where the environment is low-validity or the decision maker's experience is miscalibrated (see [[object.expert-intuition]]).
