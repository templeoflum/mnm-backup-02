Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky, "Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk," *Econometrica* 47(2):263–291 (March 1979).

Contains — the foundational descriptive model of choice under risk, advanced as an alternative to expected-utility theory. Introduces the *value function* defined over gains and losses relative to a reference point (concave for gains, convex for losses, steeper for losses → **loss aversion**); the *probability weighting function* (overweighting of small probabilities, the certainty effect); and a two-phase account (editing then evaluation) that explains the isolation effect and systematic violations of expected utility (Allais-type paradoxes). The work was cited in the 2002 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economics awarded to Kahneman.
