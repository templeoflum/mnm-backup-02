Full citation — A. de Moivre, *Miscellanea Analytica de Seriebus et Quadraturis*, London, 1730. (De Moivre had given an earlier form of the approximation in 1721 work toward the normal approximation to the binomial.)

Contains de Moivre's derivation, from a probability/binomial-coefficient problem, of the asymptotic n! ∼ c·n^(n+1/2)e^(−n). He determined the constant c only as an approximate rational expression for its logarithm, leaving its exact value open; James Stirling then showed c = √(2π) [[source.stirling-1730]].

Role — precursor/origin (lower-order part) of Stirling's approximation. The "√(2π)" that completes the formula is Stirling's contribution.

Access — historical scans; described in MacTutor and standard histories.
