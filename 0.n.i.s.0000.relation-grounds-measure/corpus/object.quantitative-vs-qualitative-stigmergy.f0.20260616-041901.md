**statement (plain).** Stigmergic traces are classified along two contrasting types. In **quantitative stigmergy**, traces differ only in intensity or amount; a stronger trace elicits a stronger (more frequent, more intense) version of the *same* response — a graded, positive-feedback dynamic (e.g., ant pheromone trails, where more pheromone draws more ants). In **qualitative stigmergy**, traces differ in *kind*: distinct stimulus configurations release distinct actions, so a different local structure triggers a categorically different behavior (e.g., the shape of a partially built wasp nest determining what type of cell is added next). Quantitative stigmergy ranks existing options; qualitative stigmergy opens new ones by changing the kind of stimulus present. [confirmed]

**basis.** The distinction is drawn and named in Theraulaz & Bonabeau's review of stigmergy, motivated by comparing trail-laying (graded) against nest-construction sequences (discrete) in social insects [[source.theraulaz-bonabeau-1999]]. Heylighen restates and generalizes it, treating the quantitative/qualitative axis as one of several orthogonal dimensions (alongside sematectonic vs. sign-based) needed to characterize any stigmergic system [[source.heylighen-2016]]. [confirmed]

**limits.** The two types are endpoints of a spectrum rather than a strict dichotomy: real systems combine graded and discrete cues, and the same trace can act quantitatively at one stage and qualitatively at another. The classification is descriptive, not predictive — it labels how a trace acts but does not by itself determine the resulting collective structure [[source.heylighen-2016]]. [confirmed]
