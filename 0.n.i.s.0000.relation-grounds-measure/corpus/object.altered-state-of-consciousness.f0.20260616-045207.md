**statement (plain)** — An altered state of consciousness (ASC) is a temporary, reversible change in the overall pattern of subjective experience such that the person recognizes (or an observer would judge) a marked qualitative difference from their normal waking state. ASC arise spontaneously, through physical/physiological stimulation, by psychological means (e.g. meditation, hypnosis), and from disease or drugs. Vaitl et al. organize the phenomenology along four dimensions: activation, awareness span, self-awareness, and sensory dynamics.

**basis** — The review by Vaitl et al. catalogues ASC across induction routes and derives the four-dimension scheme, treating ASC as an empirically tractable category rather than a single state [[source.vaitl-2005]] [confirmed]. Dietrich treats ASC as a natural kind unified at the level of brain function, listing dreaming, the runner's high, meditation, daydreaming, hypnosis, and drug states as members and seeking a shared neural account [[source.dietrich-2003]] [confirmed].

**limits** — "Altered" is defined relative to a baseline waking state whose own boundaries are not sharp; the category is a heterogeneous family unified phenomenologically, not by a single mechanism. The four-dimension scheme is a descriptive framework, and whether the members share a common neural cause remains an open, contested question (status established as a working concept, not a proven kind).
