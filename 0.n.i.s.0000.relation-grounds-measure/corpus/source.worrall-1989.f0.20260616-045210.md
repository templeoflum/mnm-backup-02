John Worrall, "Structural Realism: The Best of Both Worlds?" *Dialectica* 43, no. 1–2 (1989): 99–124. DOI: 10.1111/j.1746-8361.1989.tb00933.x. (Note the question mark in the published title.)

Contains: the modern revival of structural realism. Worrall argues that what is retained across radical theory change is mathematical *structure* (form), not the *content* / nature of the entities — so realism about structure secures the no-miracles intuition while conceding the pessimistic meta-induction's point about content. Credits Poincaré as the historical source of the structuralist move; worked example is the Fresnel→Maxwell transition in optics, where the equations carry over though the ontology (the ether) is discarded.
