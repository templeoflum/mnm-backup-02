# The Grammar of Inward Channeling: A Scholarly Survey of the Self-Silencing, Generative States Across Testimony and Science

## TL;DR
- Across spirit mediumship, glossolalia, automatic writing, oracular speech, and the artist's "dictation," both first-person testimony and the empirical literature converge on a single recurring shape: the practitioner **quiets the executive, self-authoring layer of mind** (the "silence the self" move), after which structured, often sophisticated communication arises that is experienced as **not self-generated**, accompanied by an **altered or absent sense of agency**.
- The phenomenon is **real as a recurrent human experience** — institutionalized in 437 of 488 sampled societies (90%, per Bourguignon) and neurologically distinct (Newberg's glossolalia and Peres/Newberg's mediumship SPECT studies both show *decreased* frontal/executive activity during the state) — even though its metaphysical claims remain bracketed.
- The channel's **instability is itself an invariant, not a defect**: it must be "tuned into," cultivated, and licensed by a cultural framework; it cannot be forced; it depends on trait/state absorption and the strategic relinquishment of metacognitive control. Attempts to measure it as "fake" largely miss this — the thing is, by its nature, intermittent and framework-dependent.

## Key Findings

**1. The state has a measurable neural signature, and it is one of *subtraction*.** The most striking convergence between testimony and science is that the felt experience of "something moving through me / I am not doing this" corresponds to *reduced* activity in the brain's executive and self-authoring systems. Andrew Newberg's SPECT study of glossolalia (2006) found decreased frontal-lobe activity during speaking in tongues relative to gospel singing — exactly the region associated with willful self-control. Julio Peres and Newberg's study of Brazilian mediums (PLoS ONE, 2012) found that experienced psychographers showed *lower* activity in frontal and other regions during trance writing — while producing text of *higher* complexity than their normal writing. Arne Dietrich's "transient hypofrontality" hypothesis generalizes this: altered states are unified by a downregulation of prefrontal executive function.

**2. The felt loss of authorship is a genuine cognitive mechanism, not merely a belief or a lie.** William Carpenter's ideomotor principle (1852) established that muscular action can occur without conscious volition — the real motor basis of automatic writing, the Ouija planchette, table-tilting, and dowsing. Daniel Wegner's "apparent mental causation" and Zoltan Dienes's "cold control" theory of hypnosis show that the *sense of agency* is an inferential, fallible add-on that can detach from action: one can genuinely intend and execute while lacking the higher-order thought "I am intending this." This is the cognitive engine of involuntariness.

**3. The phenomenon is cross-culturally near-universal and structurally patterned.** Erika Bourguignon's survey of 488 societies found 437 (90%) had institutionalized, culturally patterned altered states. I. M. Lewis's *Ecstatic Religion* maps the recurring social structure of possession. Felicitas Goodman's cross-cultural phonetic analysis of glossolalia found the same prosodic contour across unrelated languages — evidence that the underlying state, not the linguistic content, shapes the output.

**4. The subconscious is genuinely *productive*.** From Frederic Myers's "subliminal self" to Théodore Flournoy's analysis of Hélène Smith (cryptomnesia and "subliminal creativity") to Jung's "active imagination," the serious tradition treats the channel as tapping a real, coherent, sometimes superior generative capacity — without needing external spirits to explain it.

**5. The channel must be cultivated and licensed.** Tanya Luhrmann's "absorption" and "porosity" work shows that hearing a voice-not-one's-own is a *trainable skill* predicted by a measurable trait and shaped by a community's interpretive framework. You channel within a tradition's vocabulary.

## Details

### Cognitive science / neuroscience: the mechanics of the state

**Transient hypofrontality (Arne Dietrich).** Dietrich's 2003 paper "Functional neuroanatomy of altered states of consciousness: The transient hypofrontality hypothesis" (and Dietrich & Al-Shawaf, 2018) proposes that altered states share a common mechanism: a transient downregulation of the prefrontal cortex, with each state's phenomenological uniqueness arising from *which* frontal circuits go offline. The model uses an "onion-peeling" metaphor: alteration proceeds by "phenomenological subtraction," removing the most sophisticated layers of conscious content. This provides the general frame within which the more specific channeling studies sit, and it directly supports the user's intuition that the state is defined by what is *removed* (executive self-control) rather than added.

**Glossolalia (Newberg et al., 2006).** "The measurement of regional cerebral blood flow during glossolalia: A preliminary SPECT study" (Psychiatry Research: Neuroimaging) scanned five practitioners during glossolalia versus gospel singing. The frontal lobes, parietal lobes, and left caudate were most affected; frontal activity *decreased* during tongues. Newberg's framing maps precisely onto the testimony: "these subjects truly believe that the spirit of God is moving through them... our brain imaging research shows" decreased activity in the frontal lobes, "which is what normally makes us feel as if we're in control." Note: the parietal pattern *differed* from meditation (where Newberg found frontal *increase* and parietal decrease) — i.e., channeling-type states are neurologically distinct from concentrative meditation.

**Mediumship / automatic writing (Peres, Moreira-Almeida, Caixeta, Leão, Newberg, 2012).** "Neuroimaging during Trance State: A Contribution to the Study of Dissociation" (PLoS ONE) examined ten Brazilian Spiritist psychographers (five experienced, with 15–47 years of automatic writing; five less expert) using SPECT during trance and non-trance writing. Experienced mediums showed *lower* activity in the left culmen, left hippocampus, left inferior occipital gyrus, left anterior cingulate, right superior temporal gyrus, and right precentral gyrus during psychography. Crucially, the psychographed content scored *higher* in complexity than control writing. The authors' own interpretation is the key datapoint for the user: "the fact that subjects produced complex content in a trance dissociative state suggests they were not merely relaxed, and relaxation seems an unlikely explanation for the underactivation of brain areas specifically related to the cognitive processing being carried out." This is the empirical core of the "productive subconscious / superior output" invariant.

**The ideomotor principle (William Carpenter, 1852).** Carpenter coined "ideo-motor" to describe muscular motions arising "merely from ideas associated with motion existing in the mind, without any conscious effort of volition." Faraday's table-turning investigation and Jastrow's lab work demonstrated it. Carpenter's own conclusion — "Putting intentional deceit out of the question for the moment... the supposed communications are nothing else than products of their own automatic mental operations, guided by the principle of suggestion" — is the canonical statement that the motor phenomenon is *real and non-fraudulent* while not requiring spirits. This is the precise mechanism the user wants framed as a genuine mechanism rather than proof of fraud.

**The sense of agency and its detachment (Wegner; Dienes).** Daniel Wegner's *The Illusion of Conscious Will* (2002) and the Wegner & Wheatley "apparent mental causation" model (1999) argue that the experience of conscious will is constructed by an inferential system using cues of priority, consistency, and exclusivity; it can be present without true causation and absent when present. Wegner explicitly applied this to "Ouija board spelling, automatic writing... spirit possession, dissociative identity disorder, and trance channeling." Zoltan Dienes and Josef Perner's "cold control" theory of hypnosis (2007) locates involuntariness in metacognition: a hypnotic response is "intending to perform some motor or cognitive action, while remaining unaware of the intention." The subject genuinely executes, but lacks the higher-order thought of intending — so the act feels as if it happens of its own accord. This is arguably the single best mechanistic account of the "silence the self / it moves through me" report, and it explicitly does *not* require fakery.

**Default mode network, ego dissolution, predictive processing.** Contemporary work links the felt continuity of the autobiographical self to the default mode network (DMN); its downregulation correlates with "ego dissolution" (Ego-Dissolution Inventory; psychedelic studies). The REBUS / predictive-processing frame (Carhart-Harris & Friston) treats the self as a high-level model that, when its precision is relaxed, allows normally pruned content to surface — a mechanistic gloss on why "silencing the self" releases coherent but non-self-attributed material. (These DMN/psychedelic findings are adjacent rather than direct studies of channeling, and should be treated as suggestive analogues.)

### Psychology / history of psychical research (the serious, non-debunking strand)

**F. W. H. Myers, *Human Personality and Its Survival of Bodily Death* (1903).** Myers's "subliminal self" is the user's "subconscious channel" almost exactly: a wider reservoir of consciousness below the "limen" (threshold) of ordinary awareness, from which both pathology and "supernormal" faculty, genius, and inspiration emerge. He treated mediumistic trance (e.g., Leonora Piper, William Stainton Moses) as "automatisms" ranging "from the rudest motor automatisms to the most startling sensory apparition," and possession as "an extreme case of automatism affecting the whole psychical area," where "a secondary consciousness not only crops up here and there through the primary, but for a time displaces it." William Braud later remarked that much of psychical research and depth psychology since is "a series of footnotes to Myers." The threshold/liminality metaphor the user flagged as an invariant originates substantially here.

**William James.** In *The Varieties of Religious Experience* (1902) and his psychical writings, James championed "transmarginal" / subliminal consciousness as "the most important step forward" in the psychology of his era, treating religious "sensory and motor automatisms" and sudden conversion as eruptions from an "active subconscious self." He called the medium Leonora Piper his "white crow" (the one exception that disproves the rule "all crows are black") and warned that science is distorted "as much by fixed beliefs about impossibility as by fraud." James is the bridge figure linking the psychical-research catalogue to mainstream psychology.

**Théodore Flournoy, *From India to the Planet Mars* (1900).** Flournoy's five-year study of the medium Hélène Smith (Catherine-Élise Müller) — who produced "Martian" language and past-life romances — explained the productions not as fraud and not as spirits but as **cryptomnesia** (a term he popularized), suggestion, and **subliminal creativity**: "romances of the subliminal imagination, derived largely from forgotten sources." Ferdinand de Saussure analyzed the "Martian" and concluded it was "Sanskritoid," a cryptomnesic reconstruction. Henri Ellenberger paired this book with Freud's *Interpretation of Dreams* as the two 1900 classics of dynamic psychiatry. This is the landmark case for the "channeled content is creatively generated by the subconscious" invariant.

**Carl Jung.** Jung's medical dissertation (1902) analyzed the occult phenomena of his medium cousin Hélène Preiswerk. He later developed "active imagination" — deliberately "dropping" into a fantasizing state and dialoguing with autonomous inner figures — recorded in *The Red Book* (Liber Novus, composed 1913–c.1930, published 2009). His channeled figure Philemon taught him, in his words, "that there are things in the psyche which I do not produce, but which produce themselves and have their own life." Jung's case is pivotal because it is *deliberate, cultivated* inward channeling by a major psychologist who framed the autonomy of the material as a psychological fact, bracketing metaphysics.

**Pierre Janet** supplied the foundational concept of dissociation and the "secondary self," which he (unlike Myers) regarded as pathological — an early instance of the testimony/science divergence over whether the channel is illness or faculty.

### Anthropology / religious studies (the cross-cultural invariants)

**Erika Bourguignon's cross-cultural survey.** Between 1963 and 1968, Bourguignon and colleagues analyzed ethnographic data on 488 societies; 437 (90%) had one or more institutionalized, culturally patterned forms of altered states of consciousness. As she reported in *Religion, Altered States of Consciousness, and Social Change* (1973), regional incidence ranged "from a high of 97% of the societies of aboriginal North America to a low of 80% in the Circum-Mediterranean region." She distinguished "trance" (soul journey/absence) from "possession trance" (displacement by an external entity). In her later monograph *Possession* (1976), she found that 77% of surveyed societies held possession beliefs, and that "possession trance is significantly linked to female participation and occurs more in societies with complex social roles and hierarchies." Her conclusion — that this is "a matter of major importance, not merely a bit of anthropological esoterica" — anchors the claim that the phenomenon is a near-universal human capacity.

**I. M. Lewis, *Ecstatic Religion* (1971).** Lewis's enduring distinction is between **central possession cults** (male-dominated, reinforcing established morality and power, spirits seen as moral) and **peripheral possession** (marginal, often women and the oppressed, a vehicle of protest). He treated possession as a culturally interpreted dissociative experience ("executive possession," where the spirit takes over speech and action), bridging psychology and sociology. The key invariant here: the *same* underlying experiential capacity is channeled into different social grammars.

**Tanya Luhrmann, *When God Talks Back* (2012).** Luhrmann's work on charismatic evangelicals is the best modern account of *cultivation*. Hearing God's voice is a trainable skill predicted by **absorption** (measured by the Tellegen Absorption Scale) and shaped by **porosity** (a cultural model permitting mind–world boundary crossing). Her American Anthropologist study (March 2010) found a strong correlation between absorption levels and reported sensory phenomena attributed to divine intervention; one volunteer, after affirming nearly all 34 absorption items, told her, "The man who created this scale lived inside my head." In her experimental work — reported in Luhrmann & Morgain, "Prayer as Inner Sense Cultivation: An Attentional Learning Theory of Spiritual Experience" (Ethos 40(4), 2012, pp. 359–389) — Christians were randomly assigned to kataphatic (imaginative) prayer or Bible study; after one month, the kataphatic group reported more unusual sensory experience, more vivid mental imagery, and more "sensory overrides" (hearing/seeing what is not materially present). Her formulation — these practices "change mental experience, and those changes help people to experience God as more real... To say this is not to say that God is an illusion" — is the methodological model for the user's whole stance: take the experience as real, study its cultivation, bracket the metaphysics. This is the central source for both the "trainable/tunable" invariant and the "cultural framework licenses content" invariant.

**Felicitas Goodman's glossolalia and trance work.** Goodman (1914–2005), a linguist-anthropologist trained under Bourguignon, conducted the landmark cross-cultural study *Speaking in Tongues: A Cross-Cultural Study of Glossolalia* (University of Chicago Press, 1972), with fieldwork in Mexico City, the Maya Yucatán, and Hammond, Indiana. Her central finding, first stated in "Phonetic Analysis of Glossolalia in Four Cultural Settings" (*Journal for the Scientific Study of Religion*, 1969), is that glossolalic utterances across unrelated languages share a common **prosodic contour**: "the individual utterance [has] a threshold of onset, a brief rising gradient of intensity, a peak, and a final, often precipitous decay." She argued "this agreement, despite cultural diversity and difference in language, exists because glossolalia is an artifact of a dissociative state termed trance." In the 1972 book she defined glossolalia as "a vocalization pattern, a speech automatism that is produced on the substratum of hyperarousal dissociation, reflecting directly... neurophysiologic processes present in this mental state," and stressed it is "lexically noncommunicative" — what it communicates is "commitment to the group" and "sharing of its ritual behavior." She identified induction factors of cultural expectation, learning/imitation (in her Mexican film, parishioners imitated the pastor's key phrases and gesticulations), and eventual conscious inducibility. Her later work at the **Cuyamungue Institute** (founded 1978) on "ritual body postures" claimed that specific body postures combined with rhythmic sonic driving reliably produce *characteristically different* trance experiences — and reported, with Prof. Johann Kugler at Munich (1983), trance correlates including simultaneous Beta and Theta EEG, dropped blood pressure with raised pulse, decreased cortisol, and a beta-endorphin surge. (The posture/biometric work has limited independent replication and should be treated as her reported findings.)

**The critical counterpoint within the field (testimony/science divergence).** The linguist **William Samarin** (*Tongues of Men and Angels*, 1972) agreed glossolalia is not a real language — calling it "a façade language," "meaningless but phonologically structured human utterance" — but *rejected* Goodman's trance-artifact thesis: "glossolalia is not simply a product of dissociative states, like trance," it is "not aberrant behavior, only anomalous." This was later reinforced experimentally by Spanos, Cross, Lepage & Coristine, "Glossolalia as Learned Behavior: An Experimental Demonstration" (Journal of Abnormal Psychology 95(1), 1986, pp. 21–23), who reported that "about 20% of subjects exhibited fluent glossolalia on the baseline trial... Seventy percent of trained subjects spoke fluent glossolalia on the posttest. Our findings are more consistent with social learning than with altered state conceptions of glossolalia." This is a genuine and important place where the empirical record *diverges internally*: is the channel an altered neurophysiological state (Goodman; Newberg's frontal findings) or an ordinary, learnable verbal skill (Samarin; Spanos)? The honest answer, consistent with the user's hypothesis, is that both capture part of it — the behavior is learnable *and* the experienced/expert form correlates with measurable state changes.

**Oracular / mantic speech: the Pythia at Delphi.** The priestess of Apollo delivered prophecy in an altered, "possessed" state, her ecstatic raw utterances refined into hexameter by male priests (Plutarch, himself a Delphic priest, describes both the vapor and the editing). The 2001 de Boer/Hale geological work identified ethylene-bearing fault emissions as a plausible partial trigger (later contested as insufficient at current emission levels). The Delphi case adds two invariants: **induction via environment/ritual**, and the **interpretive framing of raw output** by a tradition (the priests' versification mirrors Yeats arranging his wife's script, or the medium translating "Martian").

### The creative / literary record (the "muse" as inward channeling)

**Rilke and the Duino Elegies.** The paradigmatic dictation account: at Duino Castle in January 1912, absorbed in answering a business letter while walking the cliffs in a storm, Rilke heard in the wind a voice speaking the first line — "Who, if I cried out, would hear me among the angels' hierarchies?" — and wrote it down "as if he were taking dictation." In February 1922 at Muzot he "received" the bulk of the Elegies and the Sonnets to Orpheus in a matter of days, describing "a boundless storm, a hurricane of the spirit." Rilke himself likened it to "taking dictation." The case shows the invariants of involuntariness, sudden onset, productivity (major work in days), and — crucially — *cultivation*: the 1922 "reception" was preceded by a decade of "patience, practice, discipline, and attunement."

**W. B. Yeats and *A Vision*.** Four days after his 1917 marriage, George Yeats began automatic writing. Per George Mills Harper, *The Making of Yeats's "A Vision"* (1987): "Over a period of approximately 30 months they collaborated in 450 sittings, he asking questions, she responding to fill a total of more than 3,600 pages." When Yeats offered to spend his life interpreting it, the "communicators" replied, "No... we have come to give you metaphors for poetry." This is the clearest literary instance of the channel as a deliberately worked, two-person practice — and of the interpretive labor (Yeats systematizing the script into a philosophy) that the raw channeled material requires.

**Coleridge, Blake, Milton.** Coleridge claimed "Kubla Khan" came whole in an opium reverie (composing 200–300 lines "without any sensation or consciousness of effort"), interrupted by the "person from Porlock." Blake said *Milton* and *Jerusalem* were dictated to him "without Premeditation and even against my Will." Milton described his "Celestial Patroness" who "dictates to me slumbering" the unpremeditated verse of *Paradise Lost*. The topos of the dictating Muse is continuous from Homer's "Sing, goddess" to the present.

**Surrealist automatism (André Breton).** Breton defined Surrealism in the 1924 *Manifesto* as "pure psychic automatism... the dictation of thought in the absence of all control exercised by reason and outside all moral or aesthetic concerns." His method (in *Les Champs magnétiques*, 1919, with Soupault): enter "the most passive, or receptive state of mind possible," write as fast as possible without conscious guidance, trust "the inexhaustible nature of the murmur." Scholars (e.g., Tessel Bauduin) note the surrealists took these techniques *directly from Spiritualism and psychical research* and then "divested automatism of all things spiritual" — a clean illustration of the same mechanism re-licensed under a different framework (art rather than spirits).

**The daimon / genius and the flow adjacency.** Socrates' *daimonion* (an inner voice that checked his actions), the Roman *genius*/Greek *daimon* as an attendant external-feeling creative spirit, and Elizabeth Gilbert's *Big Magic* (which revives the externalized-creativity framing — treating ideas as quasi-autonomous entities that "move through" the artist) all map the testimony of non-self-authorship. Mihály Csíkszentmihályi's **flow** is the adjacent absorptive state — loss of self-consciousness and the merging of action and awareness — though flow typically retains a sense of skilled agency, distinguishing it from full channeling's *loss* of authorship. Flow is best understood as the mild end of a continuum whose far end is trance possession.

## The Grammar: Invariants Where Testimony and Science Converge

Synthesizing both records, the recurring structural features — the "language, not a truth" the user seeks — are:

1. **Self-quieting / executive suspension as the precondition.** The "silence the self" move. *Testimony:* Breton's "most passive, receptive state"; Rilke's listening stillness; the medium's emptying. *Science:* transient hypofrontality (Dietrich); decreased frontal activity in glossolalia (Newberg) and psychography (Peres); DMN downregulation. **Strong convergence.**

2. **Altered or absent sense of agency / felt involuntariness.** *Testimony:* "we have come to give you metaphors"; Blake "against my Will"; "the god had spoken." *Science:* Wegner's apparent mental causation; Dienes's cold control (intending without the higher-order thought of intending); the ideomotor principle. **Strong convergence** — and notably, the science explains the involuntariness *without* invoking fraud.

3. **The productive, structured subconscious.** Channeled output is often coherent and sometimes superior. *Testimony:* Yeats's 3,600 pages; Rilke's Elegies in days; Hélène Smith's "Martian." *Science:* Peres's finding of *higher* complexity in trance writing; Flournoy's "subliminal creativity"; Myers's subliminal genius. **Convergence**, with the science reframing "spirit" as subliminal/cryptomnesic generativity.

4. **Instability / un-forceability — the channel must be "tuned into."** This is the user's central hypothesis, and it is borne out as an invariant. *Testimony:* Rilke's decade of waiting between the 1912 onset and 1922 completion; the channel "falling silent"; Yeats's wife sitting hours for "a scarce sentence." *Science:* absorption is both a *trait* (Tellegen scale) and a *trainable state* (Luhrmann) — i.e., variable across persons and time; Goodman's documented *attenuation* of glossolalia over time; the dependence on expectancy (Kirsch) and metacognitive relinquishment (Dienes), which cannot be willed directly. The instability is structural: the very act of executive control that "forcing" requires is what suppresses the state. **This is why measurement is hard** — and it vindicates the user's framing that null/"fake" results are often measuring the wrong thing or a non-stationary target.

5. **Cultivation and a licensing cultural framework.** You channel within a tradition's vocabulary. *Testimony:* Yeats channels an esoteric "system"; the Vineyard Christian hears "God"; Hélène Smith channels "Mars"; the surrealist channels "the unconscious." *Science:* Luhrmann's training studies and "porosity"; Goodman's induction-by-imitation; Bourguignon's "institutionalized, culturally patterned" states. The content is shaped by the available framework even as the underlying state-mechanics are constant. **Strong convergence.**

6. **Threshold / liminality.** The recurring spatial metaphor of a boundary crossed. *Testimony:* the "between" state, the doorway. *Science:* Myers's *limen*/subliminal; James's "transmarginal"; the onset/peak/decay contour of glossolalia (Goodman) as a literal threshold-crossing in the vocalization itself.

7. **Induction by rhythm, breath, repetition, sensory restriction, and ritual expectancy.** *Testimony:* the storm/wind (Rilke), the séance, the drumming. *Science:* sonic driving (Goodman's postures), the ethylene vapor and ritual of Delphi, hyperarousal vs. the equally valid hypoarousal route (meditation), expectancy effects (Kirsch).

### Where the records DIVERGE
- **State vs. learned skill.** Goodman/Newberg (distinct neurophysiological state) vs. Samarin/Spanos (ordinary learnable verbal behavior). Best resolution: a learnable behavior whose expert form recruits measurable state changes.
- **Faculty vs. pathology.** Myers/James (the subliminal as a wider faculty) vs. Janet (dissociation as illness). Modern dissociation research splits the difference: the same mechanisms underlie both adaptive and clinical dissociation.
- **The metaphysical surplus.** Testimony attributes the source to an external other (spirit, god, muse, idea-as-entity); the science attributes it to subliminal/ideomotor/metacognitive processes. The user's brief correctly brackets this: the *grammar* is identical regardless of which attribution is true.

## Recommendations

**For mapping the grammar (immediate next steps):**
1. **Treat the seven invariants above as the backbone.** They are the "language." Organize any further reading around them rather than around traditions, since the traditions are dialects of the same grammar.
2. **Read the four keystone primary sources in this order:** Myers (1903, for the subliminal-channel architecture and the threshold metaphor) → Flournoy (1900, for the productive-subconscious case study) → Luhrmann (2012, for cultivation/absorption/porosity, the modern methodological model) → Goodman (1972, for the cross-cultural structural invariant). These four alone give you the spine.
3. **Pair each with its scientific mechanism:** Carpenter (ideomotor) + Wegner + Dienes for involuntariness; Dietrich + Newberg + Peres for the subtractive neural signature.

**For the instability problem specifically (the user's core hypothesis):**
4. **Reframe instability as a measurable variable, not noise.** The productive research question is not "is it real?" but "what predicts the channel opening and closing?" The existing levers are: trait absorption (Tellegen), state cultivation (Luhrmann's prayer-training paradigm), expectancy (Kirsch), and metacognitive relinquishment (Dienes). A study design that *manipulates these and measures frontal/DMN downregulation as the dependent variable* is the live frontier.
5. **Benchmarks that would change the picture:** (a) If a replication of Peres/Newberg with larger samples and pre-registration *fails* to find the complexity-with-hypofrontality dissociation, the "productive subconscious in a distinct state" invariant weakens toward the Samarin/Spanos "ordinary learned skill" pole. (b) If absorption-training reliably produces the neural signature on demand, the "un-forceability" invariant is revealed as a *training-threshold* phenomenon rather than an intrinsic instability. (c) Conversely, if high-absorption trained subjects *still* show high trial-to-trial variability in entering the state, the user's "inherently unstable channel" hypothesis is strongly confirmed.

**For framing/writing:**
6. **Use Luhrmann's stance as the model voice:** "To say this is not to say that God is an illusion." Take the experience as real-as-experience, describe its mechanics and cultivation, and bracket the metaphysics explicitly and repeatedly. This is both intellectually honest and exactly the user's requested posture.

## Caveats
- **Small samples.** Both keystone neuroimaging studies are explicitly "preliminary": Newberg's glossolalia study scanned five subjects; Peres/Newberg scanned ten mediums. The findings are suggestive and directionally consistent with theory, but not definitively established; treat effect sizes and exact regions as provisional.
- **The state/skill debate is unresolved.** The Goodman (trance) vs. Samarin/Spanos (learned behavior) divergence is real and live; I have presented a synthesis, but the field has not settled it.
- **Adjacent ≠ identical.** DMN/ego-dissolution and psychedelic findings, and Csíkszentmihályi's flow, are *analogues* that illuminate the mechanism; they are not direct studies of channeling/mediumship and should not be over-read as such.
- **Source asymmetry.** The historical psychical-research and literary records are first-person and rich but uncontrolled; the modern empirical record is controlled but thin. The "convergence" claimed here is therefore between a deep-but-soft record and a rigorous-but-shallow one — itself a reason the phenomenon resists tidy measurement.
- **Cuyamungue/posture and Delphi-vapor claims** carry weaker evidential support (limited replication; contested geology, respectively) and are flagged as such in-text.
- **Bracketing is a methodological choice, not a metaphysical verdict.** Declining to adjudicate whether spirits/futures/entities are real is, per the brief, deliberate; nothing here should be read as either endorsing or denying those claims.
