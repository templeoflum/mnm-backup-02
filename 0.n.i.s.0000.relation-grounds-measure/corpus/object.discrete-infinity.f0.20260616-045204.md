**statement (plain)** — discrete infinity is the property that a language has infinitely many distinct expressions, each built from a finite stock of discrete elements (words, morphemes) with no "in-between" forms and no longest sentence. Recursion is the mechanism that delivers it: because a category can re-embed itself, there is always a next, longer, well-formed expression. It is the formal sense in which language makes "infinite use of finite means."

**basis** — Chomsky (1957) makes finite-means / infinite-output the defining feat of a generative grammar. [[source.chomsky-1957]] **[confirmed]** Hauser, Chomsky & Fitch (2002) name discrete infinity as the signature output of the recursive core (FLN) and the property to look for comparatively across species and domains. [[source.hauser-chomsky-fitch-2002]] **[confirmed]** *Limits:* discrete infinity is an idealization over competence — actual utterances are bounded by memory, attention, and lifespan (the competence/performance distinction); it is a structural claim, not a count of produced sentences.
