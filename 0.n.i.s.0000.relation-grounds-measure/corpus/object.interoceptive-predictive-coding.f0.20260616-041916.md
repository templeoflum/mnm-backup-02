**statement (plain)** — Interoceptive predictive coding casts the brain as continuously generating predictions about the state of the internal body and comparing them to incoming visceral afferent signals; perception, emotion and bodily self-experience arise from this active-inference process rather than from passive read-out of afferents. Emotions are inferred causes of interoceptive prediction error; allostatic regulation is the action side of the same inference (the body acts to minimize predicted error).

**basis** — Seth (2013) introduced **interoceptive inference**: subjective feeling states are actively-inferred generative models of the causes of interoceptive signals, generalizing appraisal theories of emotion and tying interoceptive prediction error to body ownership and conscious selfhood [[source.seth-2013]] **[confirmed]**. Barrett & Simmons (2015) advanced the **Embodied Predictive Interoception Coding (EPIC)** model, mapping prediction onto agranular visceromotor cortices (anterior insula, anterior cingulate) and prediction error onto granular regions, unifying interoception and allostasis in a corticocortical architecture [[source.barrett-2015]] **[confirmed]**.

*Limits* — interoceptive predictive coding is a theoretical/computational framework, well-motivated and influential but not decisively confirmed; specific anatomical claims (EPIC's laminar prediction/error assignment) and the universality of active-inference accounts of emotion remain debated. It reframes — rather than replaces — Craig's homeostatic-afferent model and is supported more by integrative argument and convergent neuroimaging than by a single decisive experiment.
