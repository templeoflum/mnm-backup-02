**statement (plain)** — in generative work the real artwork is the procedure, not any one picture it makes. The artist's act is to specify a rule or program; running it is "perfunctory," and it may yield many outputs, all equally legitimate. Value and authorship sit in the system, not in the surface it renders.

**statement** — Sol LeWitt, "Paragraphs on Conceptual Art" (1967): "the idea becomes a machine that makes the art," and "all of the planning and decisions are made beforehand and the execution is a perfunctory affair." [[source.lewitt-1967]] This is the conceptual-art root of treating the generating process — rather than the rendered object — as the locus of the work. **[confirmed]**

**basis** — the concept does the work of separating *specification* from *execution*. Once the rule is authored, output is a consequence, so the same process can ground an edition, an animation, or a live performance with no privileged "original." This is what makes generative art (and its descendants — [[object.live-coding]], rule-based systems like [[object.cellular-automaton-emergence]]) cohere as one field: each treats an authored process as the artwork. The link to [[object.generative-art]] is direct — Galanter's "system... set into motion" is the same separation, generalised beyond conceptual art. **[confirmed as attribution]** for the LeWitt-to-generative lineage (the connection is drawn by secondary commentary; LeWitt himself wrote within conceptual art, not "generative art").

**lineage** — the phrase "the idea becomes a machine that makes the art" is usually cited from the 1967 Artforum essay; it is sometimes dated to 1965 (and quoted by Joseph Kosuth, "Art after Philosophy," 1969). Carried as lineage, not conflict. Process art (Morris, the late 1960s) is a sibling current but emphasises material process over rule-specification — related, not identical.

**sources** — [[source.lewitt-1967]] ("Paragraphs on Conceptual Art," Artforum 1967); [[source.galanter-2003]] (system/autonomy definition). Corroboration: Wikiquote/Sol LeWitt, TheArtStory, multiple conceptual-art readers.
