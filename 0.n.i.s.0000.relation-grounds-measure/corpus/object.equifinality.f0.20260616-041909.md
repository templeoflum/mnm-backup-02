**statement (plain)** — *Equifinality* is the property whereby an open system can arrive at the same final (steady) state starting from different initial conditions and travelling by different routes. It contrasts with closed deterministic systems, where the final state is fixed unequivocally by the initial conditions (as planetary positions at time *t* are fixed by their positions at *t₀*). The developing embryo is the canonical illustration: perturbed or partial starting configurations still converge on the same whole organism. [[source.von-bertalanffy-1950]] **[confirmed]**

**statement (formal)** — For an open system governed by a system of flow equations, equifinality holds when the steady-state solution is a stable attractor independent of initial conditions: trajectories from a range of initial states converge to the same time-independent solution determined by the system parameters and boundary throughput, not by where they began. Bertalanffy presented this as a consequence of open-system kinetics rather than of vitalistic agency. [[source.von-bertalanffy-1968]] **[confirmed]**

**basis** — Introduced and named in Bertalanffy's open-systems work and stated for a wide audience in the 1950 *Science* paper [[source.von-bertalanffy-1950]]; formalized in the 1968 book as a derivable feature of open-system dynamics, advanced specifically to give a mechanistic (non-vitalist) account of biological self-regulation and regeneration [[source.von-bertalanffy-1968]]. **Support**: the concept transferred widely into the social and behavioral sciences (e.g. organization theory, family-systems and developmental psychology) and, with the term used more loosely, into geomorphology and archaeology to denote many-causes-one-outcome. **Limits**: outside the formal open-system setting "equifinality" is often used as a qualitative label (multiple causes converging on one outcome), which weakens its content; rigorous equifinality requires the stability/attractor conditions to actually hold and is not guaranteed for every open system. **[confirmed]**
