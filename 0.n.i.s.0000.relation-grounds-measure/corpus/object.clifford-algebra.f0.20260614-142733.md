**statement (plain)** — a Clifford algebra is the algebra you get by taking a vector space with a notion of length (a quadratic form) and declaring that every vector, multiplied by itself, equals its squared length — a scalar. It is the smallest associative algebra in which this holds. A real Clifford algebra equipped with the geometric reading of its products is what is called a geometric algebra.

**statement (formal)** — let V be a vector space over a field K (char ≠ 2) with quadratic form Q. The [[object.clifford-algebra]] Cl(V, Q) is the unital associative K-algebra generated by V subject to the fundamental relation
v² = Q(v)·1   for all v ∈ V,
equivalently (by polarization, with ⟨·,·⟩ the symmetric bilinear form of Q)
uv + vu = 2⟨u, v⟩·1   for all u, v ∈ V.
Concretely it is the quotient of the tensor algebra by the two-sided ideal enforcing this relation:
Cl(V, Q) = T(V) / I_Q,   I_Q = ⟨ v ⊗ v − Q(v)·1 : v ∈ V ⟩.
It carries a natural ℤ/2-grading Cl = Cl⁰ ⊕ Cl¹ (even/odd), and as a vector space dim Cl(V,Q) = 2ⁿ for dim V = n. **[confirmed]**

**proof (definitional)** — definition, not theorem. Existence is by the explicit quotient construction T(V)/I_Q; the algebra so defined satisfies the relation by design, and its universal property (see [[object.clifford-algebra-universal-property]]) makes it unique up to unique isomorphism. The ℤ/2-grading descends from the even/odd grading of T(V) because the relation v⊗v − Q(v)·1 is even. **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — Clifford (1878) introduced these algebras (which he called "geometric algebras") generalizing Grassmann and Hamilton; he did not give the modern tensor-algebra-quotient / universal-property formulation, which is a 20th-century abstraction (Chevalley, Bourbaki). Carried as lineage rather than asserting a single inventor of the abstract definition. **[confirmed as attribution]**
