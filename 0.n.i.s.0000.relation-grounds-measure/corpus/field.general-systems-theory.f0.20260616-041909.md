**origin** — Ludwig von Bertalanffy, "The Theory of Open Systems in Physics and Biology," *Science* 111 (1950): 23–29, with the companion "An Outline of General System Theory" (same year) — the open-system / steady-state / equifinality program stated for a general audience, growing out of his 1930s–40s work on organismic biology. [[source.von-bertalanffy-1950]] **[confirmed]**

**codification** — Ludwig von Bertalanffy, *General System Theory: Foundations, Development, Applications* (George Braziller, 1968) — the synthesizing book; the field was institutionalized in 1954 with the Society for General Systems Research (Bertalanffy, Boulding, Rapoport, Gerard) and its yearbook *General Systems* (from 1956). [[source.von-bertalanffy-1968]] **[confirmed]**

**door** — Lars Skyttner, *General Systems Theory: Problems, Perspectives, Practice*, 2nd ed. (World Scientific, 2005) — a single-volume survey of the field's theories and applications; Boulding's "Skeleton of Science" (1956) remains the standard short map of the system hierarchy. [[source.skyttner-2005]] [[source.boulding-1956]] **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — closely entwined with cybernetics (Wiener 1948, Ashby 1956); the two grew up together at the Macy Conferences and the SGSR. General systems theory is the broader open-systems / isomorphism program; cybernetics is the narrower science of feedback and control. See [[field.cybernetics-control-theory]].

*grade s — origin, codification, and door all present.*
