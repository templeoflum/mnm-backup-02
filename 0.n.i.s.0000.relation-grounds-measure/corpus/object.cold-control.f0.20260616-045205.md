**statement (plain)** — when a hypnotized person's arm "rises by itself," they really do form the intention to raise it — but they hold a mistaken second-order belief that they are *not* intending it. The first-order intention works normally; only the metacognitive awareness of intending is suppressed. That mismatch is what makes the action feel involuntary, and is what makes a response count as "hypnotic."

**statement (theoretical)** — on Rosenthal's higher-order-thought (HOT) theory, a mental state is conscious when a HOT represents one as being in it. **Cold control**: hypnotic responding is the strategic relinquishment of accurate HOTs about one's own intentions — executive control exercised *without* the conscious awareness of exercising it. No special "trance state" or extra dissociative machinery is needed beyond intact first-order intention plus an inaccurate (or absent) HOT of intending [[source.dienes-perner-2007]]. It sits alongside Wegner's apparent-mental-causation account, which likewise locates the felt involuntariness in the will-*experience* rather than in the action's control [[source.wegner-2002]].

**basis (empirical / method)** — *support*: cold control predicts that hypnotic suggestibility tracks metacognitive capacity and can be modulated by manipulating metacognition — supported by studies linking suggestibility to HOT/metacognitive measures and by rTMS to left DLPFC increasing suggestibility (Dienes & Hutton, 2013). It is one of the most actively researched contemporary theories of hypnosis. *limits*: it is one theory among rivals (dissociated-control, response-set, social-cognitive); direct measurement of the relevant HOTs is indirect, and the framework's reliance on a specific (HOT) theory of consciousness is itself contested.

**lineage** — extends Rosenthal's HOT theory of consciousness to the executive/intentional domain; contrasts with Hilgard-style **neodissociation** (which posits a structural amnesic barrier) and with Bowers's dissociated-control theory; complements Wegner's apparent mental causation. A live, debated position, not a settled result.
