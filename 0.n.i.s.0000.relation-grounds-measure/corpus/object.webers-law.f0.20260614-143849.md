**statement (plain)** — the smallest change in a stimulus you can just notice is not a fixed amount but a fixed *proportion* of the stimulus already present. To tell a 10-pound weight from a heavier one you might need +1 lb; to tell a 100-pound weight apart you need about +10 lb. The ratio stays roughly constant across the middle range of intensity.

**statement (formula)** — for a baseline stimulus of intensity I, the just-noticeable difference (JND) ΔI satisfies

ΔI / I = k,

where k is the **Weber fraction** (Weber's constant), characteristic of the sensory dimension. **[confirmed]**

**basis / empirical support & limits** — an empirical regularity, first observed by Weber in weight-lifting (kinesthetic) discrimination and extended to line length, loudness, brightness, etc. It holds approximately across a broad middle range of intensities but **fails near the absolute threshold (low intensities)** and often **at very high intensities**; a common correction is the modified form ΔI/(I + a) = k. The Weber fraction differs by modality. **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — Ernst Heinrich Weber, physiologist, *De Tactu* (1834; full title *De pulsu, resorptione, auditu et tactu. Annotationes anatomicae et physiologicae*) [[source.weber-1834]]. Weber stated the proportionality empirically; the name "Weber's law" and its integration into a science were supplied by his student G. T. Fechner [[source.fechner-1860]]. The combined logarithmic statement is the [[object.fechners-law]] (sometimes "Weber–Fechner law"). **[confirmed]** *(caveat: some low-quality sources misattribute Fechner's 1860 Elemente to Weber — incorrect.)*

**door** — [[source.gescheider-1997]] (Gescheider, *Psychophysics: The Fundamentals*); Britannica "Weber's law."
