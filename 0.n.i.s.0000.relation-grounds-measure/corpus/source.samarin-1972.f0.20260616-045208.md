**citation (verbatim)** — Samarin, William J. *Tongues of Men and Angels: The Religious Language of Pentecostalism*. New York: Macmillan, 1972. Pp. xv + 277.

**what it contains** — Samarin, a professor of anthropology and linguistics at the University of Toronto, analyzes a large sample of glossolalia recorded over five years in private and public Pentecostal meetings across Europe, the Caribbean, and North America, supplemented by interviews and questionnaires. His stated aim is sociolinguistic: to describe what glossolalia is and how it functions in the Pentecostal movement. He concludes that glossolalia is "only a facade of language" — the syllable strings form no words, the speech stream is not internally organized, and there is no systematic relation between speech units and concepts. His definition: "meaningless but phonologically structured human utterance, believed by the speaker to be a real language but bearing no systematic resemblance to any natural language, living or dead." He also argues glossolalia is not simply a product of dissociative states like trance.
