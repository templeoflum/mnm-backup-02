**statement (plain)** — every situation in which people choose has a structure — the order and number of options, defaults, the framing of outcomes, the salience and timing of information. A *choice architect* is anyone (often unavoidably) who arranges that structure. The central claim is that there is no neutral design: any presentation of options exerts some influence on what is chosen, so the architect's only real choice is which influence, not whether to influence.

**basis** — methodological/empirical. Support: the concept is the organizing frame of [[source.thaler-sunstein-2008]] *Nudge*, motivated by the body of decision research showing choices shift with presentation (framing, defaults, anchoring, ordering). The "no neutral option" point is argued in [[source.thaler-sunstein-2003]] "Libertarian Paternalism." Limits: "choice architecture" is a design vocabulary, not a predictive theory — it names where influence lives but does not by itself say how large an effect a given design will have; that comes from the underlying empirical results (e.g. the default effect). **[confirmed]**
