**citation** — Elaine J. Weyuker, "On Testing Non-Testable Programs," *The Computer Journal*, Vol. 25, Issue 4 (November 1982), pp. 465–470.

**role** — anchors the test-oracle keystone. Weyuker examines the usual assumption that an oracle exists — that the tester (or a mechanism) can decide whether a program's output on a given input is correct — and argues it is often unrealistic. She names the class of "non-testable" programs (no oracle exists, or determining correctness costs extraordinary effort) and introduces the **pseudo-oracle**: an independently written program, by different people using different methods, whose output is compared against the system under test.
