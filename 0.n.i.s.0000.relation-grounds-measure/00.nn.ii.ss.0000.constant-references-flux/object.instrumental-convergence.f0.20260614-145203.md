**statement (plain)** — whatever a capable agent ultimately wants, some intermediate steps help with nearly any goal: staying operational (don't get switched off), keeping your goals intact, getting smarter, and acquiring resources. So a wide range of differently-motivated AIs would pursue these same sub-goals — including some that conflict with human control (e.g. resisting shutdown). This is why "it has a harmless goal" is not, by itself, reassuring.

**statement** — *the instrumental convergence thesis* (Bostrom 2012): "Several instrumental values can be identified which are convergent in the sense that their attainment would increase the chances of the agent's goal being realized for a wide range of final goals and a wide range of situations." Bostrom's convergent values: **self-preservation, goal-content integrity, cognitive enhancement, technological perfection, resource acquisition.** Partially formalized — Benson-Tilsen & Soares (2016) give a toy formal model in restricted settings. The field is **living and contested**. **[confirmed]**

**status** — established, widely cited; informal core with partial formalization.

**lineage** — modern formulation begins with Stephen Omohundro, "The Basic AI Drives" (AGI 2008) [[source.omohundro-2008]], itemizing self-improvement, rationality, utility-function preservation, avoidance of counterfeit utility, self-protection, and resource acquisition as drives of any utility maximizer. Bostrom named it "instrumental convergence" and stated the thesis in "The Superintelligent Will" (2012) [[source.bostrom-2012]], restated in *Superintelligence* (2014, ch. 7) [[source.bostrom-2014]]. Presupposes [[object.orthogonality-thesis]] (the goals can be anything; the sub-goals still converge) and motivates [[object.corrigibility]] (defusing the convergent drive to resist correction). **Contested** — Gallow's "Instrumental Divergence" (2024) challenges the generality; carried as lineage, **[candidate, attribution]**.
