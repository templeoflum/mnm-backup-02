**statement (plain)** — PMEST is Ranganathan's claim that every facet of every subject can be sorted under one of exactly five fundamental categories: Personality (the core thing or theme), Matter (its substance or material), Energy (its action or process), Space (its place), and Time (its period). Run from most concrete/specific to most abstract, these five give a default citation order — the order in which facets are written down when you build a class number.

**statement** — the five fundamental categories, in citation order, are **P**ersonality (the distinguishing/focal characteristic of the subject), **M**atter (the physical material or property), **E**nergy (an action, process, or operation), **S**pace (geographic location), and **T**ime (temporal period). Ranganathan postulated these as "five and only five" categories, necessary and sufficient to characterize any document; the order P→M→E→S→T reflects decreasing concreteness and fixes the facet (citation) sequence. **[confirmed]**

**basis** — PMEST is a postulate, not a theorem: it is asserted as the closed set of fundamental categories and is justified by its working generality across subjects ("a masterstroke in generalizing myriads of facets"), not derived. It is explicitly described as Ranganathan's "famous, although debatable" theory; its exhaustiveness and the assignment of particular facets to particular categories are contested in the literature. It supplies the ordering principle for [[object.facet-analysis]] and the spine of the [[object.colon-classification]]. **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — the categories are discussed in *Prolegomena to Library Classification* (1937) [[source.ranganathan-1937]]; the explicit "five and only five fundamental categories" postulate (PMEST) was introduced with the 4th edition of *Colon Classification* (1952). Reported origin of the underlying facet assimilation: c. 1928–1952 [[source.ranganathan-1933]]. **[candidate, dating]** — secondary sources agree the PMEST postulate crystallized in 1952 but differ on whether 1937 *Prolegomena* already states the five-category closure; carried as lineage pending the original text.
