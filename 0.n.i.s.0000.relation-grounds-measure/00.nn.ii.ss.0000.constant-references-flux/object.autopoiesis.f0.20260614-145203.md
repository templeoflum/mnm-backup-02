**statement (plain)** — a living thing makes itself. It is a network of processes that keeps producing the very parts that, in turn, keep the network running — and that closure is what makes it a distinct unity in space. A cell is the basic example: its chemistry continuously builds the membrane and machinery that continuously run that chemistry. Autopoiesis ("self-production") is offered as the mark of the living.

**statement** — an autopoietic system is a network of processes of production (transformation and destruction) of components which (i) through their interactions and transformations continuously regenerate and realize the network of processes that produced them, and (ii) constitute it (the network) as a concrete unity in the space in which the components exist, by specifying the topological domain of its realization as such a network. Living systems are autopoietic systems in physical space.

**basis** — Maturana and Varela distinguished **organization** (the relations that define a unity as a member of a class — what must be conserved for it to remain that kind of thing) from **structure** (the actual components and relations that realize a particular unity at a moment). Autopoiesis is an *organization*: it is conserved while structure changes. The system is **operationally closed** in its organization yet **structurally coupled** to its environment through ongoing perturbation. The concept reframed life, cognition, and autonomy without appeal to goals or external purpose, and became a core text of [[field.second-order-cybernetics]] through its self-reference and circular organization.

**lineage** — coined by Humberto R. Maturana and Francisco J. Varela in the 1972 Spanish work *De máquinas y seres vivos* (Editorial Universitaria, Santiago de Chile). The canonical English statement is *Autopoiesis and Cognition: The Realization of the Living* (D. Reidel, 1980; Boston Studies in the Philosophy of Science, vol. 42), which pairs the autopoiesis essay with Maturana's "Biology of Cognition" (1970) and carries a preface by Stafford Beer [[source.maturana-varela-1980]]. The "1972" date is for the coinage; "1980" for the standard cited collection.
