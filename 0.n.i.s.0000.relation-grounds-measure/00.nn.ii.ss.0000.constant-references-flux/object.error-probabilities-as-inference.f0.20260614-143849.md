**statement (plain)** — inference that goes beyond the data must control and report how often the method would get things wrong. The error rates of a testing procedure — how often it would mistakenly pass a false claim or reject a true one — are what license an inductive conclusion, and they carry an *evidential* meaning, not just a long-run accounting one.

**statement** — error probabilities (Type I and Type II error rates, significance levels, power, confidence-coverage probabilities, the distribution of test statistics / p-values under hypotheses) are the basis of statistical inference in the error-statistical account. Mayo and Spanos give them an **epistemic reading**: classical test and estimation procedures are *inferential* — they license inductive (data-transcending) inferences — and their error probabilities "indicate the reliability of the inferences licensed by the procedures." This stance is **probativism** (probability measures the well-testedness / probing capacity of a procedure) as opposed to **probabilism** (probability measures degree of belief or support in a hypothesis). **[confirmed]**

**basis** — the requirement is that "inductive inference — i.e., data-transcending inference — calls for methods of controlling and evaluating error probabilities." Pre-data error probabilities (Type I, Type II, coverage) govern a method's capacity; post-data evaluations (p-value, severity) assess what is warranted in the case at hand. The concept supplies the raw material the [[object.severity-principle|severity principle]] computes on: a severe test is precisely one whose error probabilities make a flaw-revealing result highly probable were the claim false.

lineage — descends from the Neyman–Pearson theory of tests (error rates, power) and Fisherian significance testing (p-values), but reinterprets both. Mayo's epistemic, case-specific reading departs from Neyman's avowedly *behavioral* construal (error rates as long-run action-guidance). Argued in [[source.mayo-1996]] and systematized with Aris Spanos [[source.mayo-spanos-2011]]; central to [[source.mayo-2018]]. Links to [[field.mathematical-statistics]] for the underlying sampling-distribution apparatus. **[confirmed]**
