# Chunk 0 — "The Measuring Canon": An Archaeological Research Map

## TL;DR

- **Rung Zero passes cleanly.** The user's three-tier sampling-theory hypothesis is correct and survives independent cross-checking: **Nyquist (1928)** is the origin/precursor, **Shannon's 1949** *Communication in the Presence of Noise* is where the sampling theorem is formally stated and proved, and **Shannon's 1948** *A Mathematical Theory of Communication* founds a *different* field (information theory) — a distinction that loose sources routinely blur, and the exact error the calibration was built to catch.

This lesson propagates into the cards: present lineages, don't flatten to one founder.

Cards I could not re-verify by two independent sources this session are explicitly tagged **[candidate, partially confirmed]** or **[candidate, unconfirmed]**.

## Key Findings

1. **The 1948/1949 Shannon distinction is real and well-documented.** The sampling theorem ("Theorem 1") with explicit statement and proof lives in the 1949 IRE paper, not the 1948 BSTJ paper. Use this as your template for separating an *origin* from a *formal codification*.
2. **Attribution honesty beats completeness (per your instruction).** Where founding is contested (probability, groups, sampling, open science), the cards present the competing claimants rather than a single name.
3. **Free, canonical "doors" exist for nearly every stone/tool field** — DLMF, MacKay, Blitzstein–Hwang, Judson, Strang/Axler, *Topology Without Tears*, the SI Brochure, cos.io — these should form the spine of self-study.

## Rung Zero Calibration — Sampling Theory Attributions

**Verdict: All three attributions CONFIRMED, with the precise origin/codification distinction the user flagged.**

**1. Nyquist 1928 — CONFIRMED as candidate ORIGIN.** H. Nyquist, "Certain Topics in Telegraph Transmission Theory," *Transactions of the AIEE*, vol. 47, pp. 617–644, Feb. 1928 (presented at the AIEE Winter Convention, New York, Feb. 13–17, 1928; reprinted *Proc. IEEE* vol. 90 no. 2, Feb. 2002). Per Wikipedia's Nyquist–Shannon article: "The sampling theorem was implied by the work of Harry Nyquist in 1928… in which he showed that up to 2B independent pulse samples could be sent through a system of bandwidth B; but he did not explicitly consider the problem of sampling and reconstruction of continuous signals." So Nyquist is correctly an *origin/precursor*, not the formal statement. Confidence: high.

**2. Shannon 1949 — CONFIRMED as CODIFICATION (explicit statement + proof).** C. E. Shannon, "Communication in the Presence of Noise," *Proceedings of the IRE*, vol. 37, no. 1, pp. 10–21, Jan. 1949. The sampling theorem appears as "Theorem 1" with explicit statement ("If a function f(t) contains no frequencies higher than W cps, it is completely determined by giving its ordinates at a series of points spaced 1/2W seconds apart") and proof. Shannon credits Nyquist (the "Nyquist interval" 1/2W). Confidence: high.

**3. Shannon 1948 — CONFIRMED as origin of INFORMATION THEORY, NOT the sampling-theorem source.** C. E. Shannon, "A Mathematical Theory of Communication," *Bell System Technical Journal*, vol. 27, pp. 379–423 and 623–656, July & Oct. 1948. This founded information theory (entropy, channel capacity, the "bit"). The sampling theorem's formal statement/proof is in the *1949* paper. Some secondary/popular sources sloppily attribute the sampling theorem to the 1948 paper — this is the exact attribution error the calibration was designed to catch. Confidence: high.

**Earlier/parallel claimants (WKS attribution history) — CONFIRMED as a genuine multi-claimant lineage:**
- **E. T. Whittaker (1915)**, "On the Functions Which Are Represented by the Expansions of the Interpolation Theory," *Proceedings of the Royal Society of Edinburgh, Section A*, vol. 35, pp. 181–194 (DOI 10.1017/S0370164600017806) — the cardinal series; mathematical priority.
- **V. A. Kotelnikov (1933)**, "On the transmission capacity of the 'ether' and of cables in electrical communications" — independent, with explicit communications application; called "Kotelnikov's theorem" in Russian literature, "named after Vladimir Kotelnikov, who independently discovered it in 1933."
- **J. M. Whittaker (1935)**, *Interpolatory Function Theory* (Cambridge Tracts no. 33) — refinement.
- **Karl Küpfmüller (1928)**, "Über die Dynamik der selbsttätigen Verstärkungsregler," *Elektrische Nachrichtentechnik*, vol. 5, no. 11, pp. 459–467; per Wikipedia he "showed a similar result, and discussed the sinc-function impulse response of a band-limiting filter… sometimes referred to as a Küpfmüller filter."
- **Herbert Raabe (1939)**, dissertation "Untersuchungen an der wechselzeitigen Mehrfachübertragung (Multiplexübertragung)," TH Berlin (submitted 24 April 1939), pub. *Elektrische Nachrichtentechnik* vol. 16, pp. 213–228; per Lüke (cited in Wikipedia) Raabe "proved the theorem in his 1939 Ph.D. dissertation" (the "Raabe condition").
- **I. Someya (1949)** in the Japanese literature; **Gabor (1946)**.
- Scholarly consensus name: the **Whittaker–Kotelnikov–Shannon (WKS) sampling theorem**. Per Meijering, "A Chronology of Interpolation" (*Proc. IEEE* vol. 90 no. 3, March 2002), as summarized by Wikipedia: "Exactly how, when, or why Harry Nyquist had his name attached to the sampling theorem remains obscure." The "Nyquist–Shannon" pairing is the most common engineering name.

**Calibration takeaway:** The user's three-tier hypothesis holds. The lesson propagated into the field cards: distinguish the *implying* precursor from the *formal statement*, and present multi-claimant lineages rather than a single founder.

## SLICE 00 — "The Patient's Mathematics" (13 fields)

**analytic number theory [s]**
- ORIGIN: P. G. L. Dirichlet (1837), Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions, introducing Dirichlet characters and L-functions. Dirichlet is universally "the father of analytic number theory." Confidence: high (verified).
- CODIFICATION: B. Riemann (1859), "Über die Anzahl der Primzahlen unter einer gegebenen Größe" (*Monatsberichte der Berliner Akademie*) — complex-analytic study of ζ(s); the discipline's defining synthesis. Confidence: high.
- DOOR: T. M. Apostol, *Introduction to Analytic Number Theory* (Springer, 1976); Davenport's lecture notes circulate freely as PDFs.

**multiplicative number theory [s]**
- ORIGIN: Euler product (L. Euler, 1737, "Variae observationes circa series infinitas") linking ∏(1−p⁻ˢ)⁻¹ to ζ(s); extended by Dirichlet (1837). Lineage Euler→Dirichlet. Confidence: high for lineage.
- CODIFICATION: H. Davenport, *Multiplicative Number Theory* (Markham, 1967; Springer GTM 74). Confidence: high.
- DOOR: Davenport; Apostol.

**diophantine approximation [s]**
- ORIGIN: Dirichlet's approximation theorem (1842, pigeonhole); J. Liouville (1844) constructed the first transcendental numbers via approximation bounds. Present Dirichlet (approximation) + Liouville (transcendence). [candidate, partially confirmed]
- CODIFICATION: J. W. S. Cassels, *An Introduction to Diophantine Approximation* (Cambridge Tracts, 1957). Confidence: medium.
- DOOR: Cassels; A. Ya. Khinchin, *Continued Fractions* (Dover) for the CF route.

**continued fractions [s]**
- ORIGIN: L. Euler (1737/1744), "De fractionibus continuis dissertatio" — first systematic theory; precursors Bombelli, Wallis, Brouncker (17th c.). [candidate, partially confirmed]
- CODIFICATION: O. Perron, *Die Lehre von den Kettenbrüchen* (Teubner, 1913); A. Ya. Khinchin, *Continued Fractions* (1935 Russian; 1964 English). Confidence: medium-high.
- DOOR: Khinchin, *Continued Fractions* (Dover) — short, classic.

**sieve methods [t]**
- ORIGIN: Sieve of Eratosthenes (ancient, c. 3rd c. BCE) as the namesake; the modern combinatorial sieve was founded by **Viggo Brun (1915), "Über das Goldbachsche Gesetz und die Anzahl der Primzahlpaare," *Archiv for Mathematik og Naturvidenskab*, B34(8)** — per Wikipedia's Brun sieve entry, "It was developed by Viggo Brun in 1915 and later generalized to the fundamental lemma of sieve theory." Confidence: high for Brun's role.
- DOOR: A. C. Cojocaru & M. R. Murty, *An Introduction to Sieve Methods and Their Applications* (Cambridge, 2005); heavyweight reference Halberstam & Richert, *Sieve Methods* (1974).

**prime-gap statistics [s]**
- ORIGIN: Bertrand's postulate (1845; proved Chebyshev 1852) and large-gap results (Westzynthius 1931) are precursors; the probabilistic/statistical framing is H. Cramér (1936), "On the order of magnitude of the difference between consecutive prime numbers" (*Acta Arithmetica*), introducing the Cramér random model. Diffuse origin. [candidate, unconfirmed] for any single founder.
- CODIFICATION: No single canonical treatise; lives inside ANT monographs (e.g., Montgomery & Vaughan, *Multiplicative Number Theory I*, 2007). [candidate, unconfirmed]
- DOOR: Survey literature (Soundararajan on small gaps; the Polymath8 / Zhang–Maynard–Tao bounded-gaps work, free on arXiv).

**elementary number theory [s]**
- ORIGIN: Euclid, *Elements*, Books VII–IX (c. 300 BCE) — infinitude of primes, Euclidean algorithm. Confidence: high (canon).
- CODIFICATION: C. F. Gauss (1801), *Disquisitiones Arithmeticae* — consolidated congruences, quadratic reciprocity; the founding modern treatise. Confidence: high.
- DOOR: G. H. Hardy & E. M. Wright, *An Introduction to the Theory of Numbers* (1938); gentler: D. Burton, *Elementary Number Theory*.

**complex analysis [s]**
- ORIGIN: A.-L. Cauchy (1814–1825), Cauchy integral theorem/formula; parallel foundations by Riemann (1851 dissertation) and Weierstrass. Cauchy = origin, Riemann/Weierstrass co-founders. Confidence: high.
- CODIFICATION: E. T. Whittaker & G. N. Watson, *A Course of Modern Analysis* (1902); modern standard L. Ahlfors, *Complex Analysis* (1953). Confidence: high.
- DOOR: Ahlfors (canonical); free MIT/Wisconsin OCW notes; Needham, *Visual Complex Analysis* for intuition.

**special functions & series acceleration [t]**
- ORIGIN: L. Euler (gamma function 1729–30; Euler–Maclaurin summation; Euler transform). Tradition broadly 18th–19th c. (Bessel, Legendre, Gauss hypergeometric). Confidence: high for Euler's foundational role; "field origin" diffuse. [candidate, partially confirmed]
- DOOR: M. Abramowitz & I. Stegun, *Handbook of Mathematical Functions* (NBS, 1964, free public-domain PDF); modern open-access successor the **NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions (DLMF, dlmf.nist.gov)**.

**asymptotic analysis [s]**
- ORIGIN: H. Poincaré (1886), "Sur les intégrales irrégulières des équations linéaires" (*Acta Mathematica*) — rigorous asymptotic series; precursors Stirling (1730), Laplace. [candidate, partially confirmed]
- CODIFICATION: N. G. de Bruijn, *Asymptotic Methods in Analysis* (1958). Confidence: medium-high.
- DOOR: de Bruijn (Dover); Bender & Orszag, *Advanced Mathematical Methods for Scientists and Engineers*.

**harmonic/Fourier analysis [s]**
- ORIGIN: J. Fourier (1822), *Théorie analytique de la chaleur* (precursor memoir 1807/1811) — trigonometric series for heat conduction. Confidence: high (canon).
- CODIFICATION: A. Zygmund, *Trigonometric Series* (1935); for modern Euclidean harmonic analysis, Stein & Weiss, *Introduction to Fourier Analysis on Euclidean Spaces* (1971). Confidence: high.
- DOOR: Stein & Shakarchi, *Fourier Analysis: An Introduction* (Princeton, 2003); gentler: T. W. Körner, *Fourier Analysis*.

**real analysis & limits [s]**
- ORIGIN: A.-L. Cauchy (1821), *Cours d'analyse* (rigorous limits); B. Bolzano (1817) and K. Weierstrass (ε–δ, 1860s) complete the arithmetization. Cauchy/Bolzano/Weierstrass lineage. Confidence: high.
- CODIFICATION: W. Rudin, *Principles of Mathematical Analysis* (1953) is the modern codifying textbook; historically the rigor program culminated with Weierstrass/Dedekind. Confidence: high.
- DOOR: Free: T. Tao, *Analysis I*; S. Abbott, *Understanding Analysis*; canonical: "Baby Rudin."

**approximation theory [t]**
- ORIGIN: P. L. Chebyshev (1850s, best uniform approximation / Chebyshev polynomials) and the Weierstrass approximation theorem (K. Weierstrass, 1885). Chebyshev (origin) + Weierstrass (existence theorem). Confidence: high for both results.
- DOOR: L. N. Trefethen, *Approximation Theory and Approximation Practice* (SIAM) — draft chapters freely available; classic: E. W. Cheney, *Introduction to Approximation Theory*.

## SLICE 01 — "Structure & Motion" (10 fields)

**group theory [s]**
- ORIGIN (contested — present lineage): É. Galois (1830s, solvability of equations; term "groupe"); A.-L. Cauchy (1845–46, permutation groups). Lagrange (1770s) and Ruffini are precursors; Galois is the usual origin marker. Confidence: high.
- CODIFICATION: C. Jordan (1870), *Traité des substitutions et des équations algébriques* — first comprehensive treatise; the abstract axiomatic definition is A. Cayley (1854), "On the theory of groups, as depending on the symbolic equation θⁿ=1." Present both. Confidence: high.
- DOOR: Free: T. Judson, *Abstract Algebra: Theory and Applications* (open-access); canonical: Dummit & Foote; gentle: M. A. Armstrong, *Groups and Symmetry*.

**Lie theory [s]**
- ORIGIN: S. Lie (winter 1873–74, by his own dating) — theory of continuous transformation groups; motivated by Galois theory for differential equations. Confidence: high (verified).
- CODIFICATION: S. Lie & F. Engel, *Theorie der Transformationsgruppen*, 3 vols. (1888, 1890, 1893); semisimple classification completed by Killing/Cartan (Cartan 1894). Confidence: high.
- DOOR: Free: A. Kirillov Jr., *An Introduction to Lie Groups and Lie Algebras* (draft PDF); canonical: B. C. Hall, *Lie Groups, Lie Algebras, and Representations*.

**geometric algebra [t]**
- ORIGIN: H. Grassmann (1844), *Die lineale Ausdehnungslehre*; W. K. Clifford (1878), "Applications of Grassmann's Extensive Algebra" (*American J. Math.*) — Clifford algebras. Modern revival by David Hestenes (*Space-Time Algebra*, 1966). [candidate, partially confirmed]
- DOOR: D. Hestenes, *New Foundations for Classical Mechanics*; C. Doran & A. Lasenby, *Geometric Algebra for Physicists* (2003); Alan Macdonald, *Linear and Geometric Algebra* (accessible).

**linear algebra [s]**
- ORIGIN: A. Cayley (1858), "A Memoir on the Theory of Matrices" (*Phil. Trans. Royal Soc.*) — matrix algebra; determinants/systems precursors (Leibniz, Cramer 1750, Gauss). Confidence: high for Cayley's memoir.
- CODIFICATION: P. Halmos, *Finite-Dimensional Vector Spaces* (1942) gave the modern abstract treatment; van der Waerden's *Moderne Algebra* (1930) embedded it in algebra. [candidate, partially confirmed] on codification choice.
- DOOR: Free: G. Strang, *Introduction to Linear Algebra* + MIT OCW 18.06; S. Axler, *Linear Algebra Done Right* (open-access 4th ed.).

**projective geometry [s]**
- ORIGIN: G. Desargues (1639), *Brouillon project…*; revived by J.-V. Poncelet (1822), *Traité des propriétés projectives des figures*. Desargues (origin) + Poncelet (modern founder). Confidence: high.
- CODIFICATION: K. G. C. von Staudt (1847), *Geometrie der Lage* (coordinate-free); Veblen & Young, *Projective Geometry* (1910–18) axiomatized it. [candidate, partially confirmed] on codification.
- DOOR: H. S. M. Coxeter, *Projective Geometry* (2nd ed.); free lecture notes (N. J. Wildberger).

**differential geometry [s]**
- ORIGIN: C. F. Gauss (1827), *Disquisitiones generales circa superficies curvas* (Theorema Egregium); generalized by B. Riemann (1854 habilitation, "Über die Hypothesen, welche der Geometrie zu Grunde liegen," pub. 1868). Confidence: high.
- CODIFICATION: É. Cartan (moving frames, 1920s) and the global theory; textbook codification often credited to do Carmo. Confidence: high for Gauss–Riemann origin.
- DOOR: M. do Carmo, *Differential Geometry of Curves and Surfaces* (1976); gentle: A. Pressley, *Elementary Differential Geometry*.

**point-set topology [s]**
- ORIGIN: M. Fréchet (1906), "Sur quelques points du calcul fonctionnel" (*Rendiconti Circolo Mat. Palermo*) — metric spaces; F. Hausdorff (1914), *Grundzüge der Mengenlehre* — neighborhood axioms, Hausdorff spaces. Present both. Confidence: high (verified).
- CODIFICATION: J. L. Kelley, *General Topology* (1955); also Bourbaki, *Topologie Générale*. Confidence: high.
- DOOR: Free: S. Morris, *Topology Without Tears* (open PDF); canonical: J. Munkres, *Topology*.

**order & lattice theory [s]**
- ORIGIN: R. Dedekind (1897, 1900, "Über die von drei Moduln erzeugte Dualgruppe") — first lattice ("Dualgruppe") concepts; Boole's algebra of logic (1854) is a precursor. [candidate, partially confirmed]
- CODIFICATION: G. Birkhoff, *Lattice Theory* (AMS, 1940) — the field-defining treatise. Confidence: high.
- DOOR: B. A. Davey & H. A. Priestley, *Introduction to Lattices and Order* (Cambridge, 2002).

**dynamical systems [s]**
- ORIGIN: H. Poincaré (1890, "Sur le problème des trois corps et les équations de la dynamique," *Acta Math.*; 1892–99, *Les méthodes nouvelles de la mécanique céleste*, 3 vols.) — qualitative theory, phase space, chaos precursors. Confidence: high (canon).
- CODIFICATION: G. D. Birkhoff, *Dynamical Systems* (AMS Colloquium, 1927). Confidence: high.
- DOOR: S. Strogatz, *Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos* (accessible); Hirsch, Smale & Devaney, *Differential Equations, Dynamical Systems, and an Introduction to Chaos*.

**ergodic theory & equidistribution [s]**
- ORIGIN: L. Boltzmann (ergodic hypothesis, 1870s) as physical motivation; the mathematical birth is G. D. Birkhoff (1931, "Proof of the Ergodic Theorem," *PNAS* 17:656–660, pointwise) and J. von Neumann (1932, "Proof of the Quasi-Ergodic Hypothesis," *PNAS* 18:70–82, mean ergodic theorem). Equidistribution: H. Weyl (1916), "Über die Gleichverteilung von Zahlen mod. Eins." Confidence: high (verified for ergodic theorems).
- CODIFICATION: P. Walters, *An Introduction to Ergodic Theory* (Springer GTM 79, 1982); earlier P. Halmos, *Lectures on Ergodic Theory* (1956). Confidence: high.
- DOOR: Walters (canonical); free: M. Einsiedler & T. Ward, *Ergodic Theory with a View Towards Number Theory* (draft circulates).

## SLICE 02 — "Chance & Method" (16 fields)

**random matrix theory [s]**
- ORIGIN: J. Wishart (1928, sample covariance matrices, *Biometrika*) in statistics; E. Wigner (1955, "Characteristic Vectors of Bordered Matrices…," *Annals of Math.* 62:548–564; 1958, semicircle law, *Annals* 67:325–328) in physics. (Hurwitz 1897 is a deeper mathematical precursor per Diaconis–Forrester.) Multi-rooted origin. Confidence: high (verified).
- CODIFICATION: M. L. Mehta, *Random Matrices* (1967). Confidence: high.
- DOOR: Free: T. Tao, *Topics in Random Matrix Theory* (draft PDF / AMS GSM 132); Anderson, Guionnet & Zeitouni, *An Introduction to Random Matrices*.

**point-process statistics [s]**
- ORIGIN: Poisson process (S. D. Poisson, 1837, as distribution); modern theory C. Palm (1943) and A. Khinchin (1955, queueing/point processes). Contested/diffuse. [candidate, partially confirmed]
- CODIFICATION: D. J. Daley & D. Vere-Jones, *An Introduction to the Theory of Point Processes* (Springer, 1988; 2nd ed. 2003/2008). Confidence: high (standard).
- DOOR: Daley & Vere-Jones; gentler: Baddeley et al. for spatial point processes (free R/spatstat materials).

**probability theory [s]**
- ORIGIN (genuinely contested — present the lineage, do not flatten): G. Cardano, *Liber de Ludo Aleae* (written c. 1564, pub. 1663) — earliest systematic treatment of games of chance; the conventional "birth" is the Pascal–Fermat correspondence of 1654 (problem of points); C. Huygens (1657), *De ratiociniis in ludo aleae*, first published book; P.-S. Laplace's classical definition (1812). Confidence: high that all are real claimants.
- CODIFICATION: A. N. Kolmogorov (1933), *Grundbegriffe der Wahrscheinlichkeitsrechnung* — measure-theoretic axiomatization; "the mostly undisputed axiomatic basis." Confidence: high (verified).
- DOOR: Free: J. Blitzstein & J. Hwang, *Introduction to Probability* (open PDF + Harvard Stat110); canonical: Grimmett & Stirzaker, *Probability and Random Processes*.

**mathematical statistics [s]**
- ORIGIN: R. A. Fisher (1922), "On the Mathematical Foundations of Theoretical Statistics" (*Phil. Trans. Royal Soc. A* 222:309–368) — introduced consistency, efficiency, sufficiency, likelihood, "parameter." Confidence: high (verified). (Precursors: K. Pearson; Gauss/Laplace least squares.)
- CODIFICATION: H. Cramér (1946), *Mathematical Methods of Statistics* — the measure-theoretic consolidation. Confidence: high.
- DOOR: Free: L. Wasserman, *All of Statistics*; canonical: Casella & Berger, *Statistical Inference*.

**extreme value theory [t]**
- ORIGIN: M. Fréchet (1927) and R. A. Fisher & L. H. C. Tippett (1928), "Limiting forms of the frequency distribution of the largest or smallest member of a sample" (*Proc. Cambridge Phil. Soc.* 24:180–190); rigorous proof B. Gnedenko (1943, *Annals of Math.* 44:423–453). The Fisher–Tippett–Gnedenko theorem. Confidence: high (verified).
- DOOR: S. Coles, *An Introduction to Statistical Modeling of Extreme Values* (Springer, 2001); historical reference E. J. Gumbel, *Statistics of Extremes* (1958).

**Monte Carlo methods [t]**
- ORIGIN: N. Metropolis & S. Ulam (1949), "The Monte Carlo Method" (*J. American Statistical Assoc.* 44, no. 247:335–341) — the conventional birth; developed with von Neumann at Los Alamos (1946–47). MCMC follows in Metropolis et al. (1953). Confidence: high (verified).
- DOOR: Free: D. MacKay, *Information Theory, Inference, and Learning Algorithms* (free PDF, MCMC chapters); canonical: Robert & Casella, *Monte Carlo Statistical Methods*.

**information theory [s]**
- ORIGIN: C. E. Shannon (1948), "A Mathematical Theory of Communication" (*Bell System Technical Journal* 27:379–423, 623–656). Precursors Nyquist (1924) and Hartley (1928). Confidence: high (verified).
- CODIFICATION: C. E. Shannon & W. Weaver (1949), *The Mathematical Theory of Communication* (book); modern textbook codification T. Cover & J. Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory* (1991). Confidence: high.
- DOOR: Free: D. MacKay, *Information Theory, Inference, and Learning Algorithms*; canonical: Cover & Thomas.

**metrology [s]**
- ORIGIN: The Metre Convention ("Treaty of the Metre," 20 May 1875, Paris, 17 states), creating the BIPM/CGPM/CIPM. No single founding paper; institutional origin. Confidence: high (verified).
- CODIFICATION: The SI was defined by the 11th CGPM (1960, Resolution 12); the consolidating treatise is the BIPM *SI Brochure* (9th ed., 2019), with the 2019 redefinition fixing the Planck constant h = 6.62607015×10⁻³⁴ J·s and other constants (26th CGPM 2018, effective 20 May 2019). Confidence: high.
- DOOR: Free: *SI Brochure*, 9th ed. (bipm.org PDF) and NIST Special Publication 330 (2019).

**representational measurement theory [s]**
- ORIGIN: S. S. Stevens (1946), "On the Theory of Scales of Measurement" (*Science* 103, no. 2684:677–680) — nominal/ordinal/interval/ratio scales; deeper axiomatic root O. Hölder (1901). Confidence: high.
- CODIFICATION: D. Krantz, R. D. Luce, P. Suppes & A. Tversky, *Foundations of Measurement*, 3 vols. (Academic Press, 1971, 1989, 1990) — the authoritative RTM treatise. Confidence: high (verified).
- DOOR: *Foundations of Measurement* (Dover reprints, 2007); orientation via SEP entries on measurement.

**experimental design [s]**
- ORIGIN: R. A. Fisher (1935), *The Design of Experiments* (Oliver & Boyd) — randomization, replication, blocking; precursor *Statistical Methods for Research Workers* (1925) and the 1926 Rothamsted work. Confidence: high (verified).
- CODIFICATION: W. G. Cochran & G. M. Cox, *Experimental Designs* (1950); Fisher's 1935 book is both origin and a codifying text. Confidence: high.
- DOOR: Box, Hunter & Hunter, *Statistics for Experimenters*; Montgomery, *Design and Analysis of Experiments*.

**preregistration & open science [s]**
- ORIGIN (contested/multi-rooted — present the lineage): R. Rosenthal (1979), "The file drawer problem and tolerance for null results" (*Psychological Bulletin* 86(3):638–641) diagnosed publication bias (precursor); medical trial registries predate psychology. Modern movement: C. Chambers (2013), "Registered Reports: A new publishing initiative at Cortex" (*Cortex* 49(3):609–610); the Center for Open Science / OSF founded 2013 by B. Nosek & J. Spies; Open Science Collaboration (2015), "Estimating the reproducibility of psychological science" (*Science* 349(6251):aac4716). Confidence: high for key events (verified); origin genuinely distributed.
- CODIFICATION: Nosek et al. (2015), "Promoting an Open Research Culture" (TOP Guidelines, *Science* 348:1422–1425). Confidence: high.
- DOOR: Free: cos.io and osf.io (preregistration templates, tutorials).

**error-statistical philosophy [f] — orientation note only**
- Founded by Deborah G. Mayo: *Error and the Growth of Experimental Knowledge* (Univ. of Chicago Press, 1996; 1998 Lakatos Prize) and *Statistical Inference as Severe Testing: How to Get Beyond the Statistics Wars* (Cambridge UP, 2018). The program grounds inference in "severe testing," fusing Popperian falsification with Neyman–Pearson statistics. Accessible door: Mayo's blog errorstatistics.com and the open multi-reviewer discussion (arXiv:1905.08876). Confidence: high (verified).

**Popperian falsificationism [s]**
- ORIGIN: K. Popper (1934), *Logik der Forschung* (Vienna: Julius Springer; imprint dated 1935) — demarcation by falsifiability, the verification/falsification asymmetry. Confidence: high (verified).
- CODIFICATION: K. Popper (1959), *The Logic of Scientific Discovery* (English, substantially expanded) — the mature statement. Confidence: high.
- DOOR: K. Popper, *Conjectures and Refutations* (1963) — most accessible entry; or *The Logic of Scientific Discovery*.

**psychophysics [t]**
- ORIGIN: G. T. Fechner (1860), *Elemente der Psychophysik* (Leipzig: Breitkopf und Härtel) — coined "psychophysics," classic methods; building on E. H. Weber's *De Tactu* (1834, Weber's law). **exploratory data analysis [t]**

## Recommendations

1. **Anchor on the verified, short, public-domain origin papers first** — Shannon 1948 & 1949, Fisher 1922, Nyquist 1928, Birkhoff 1931 / von Neumann 1932 are all freely available and short enough to read directly. They calibrate your eye for "what an origin actually contains" versus what gets attributed to it later (the central Rung Zero lesson).
2. **Use the open-access doors as your spine** for the [s]/[t] fields: DLMF (special functions), MacKay (information theory + Monte Carlo), Blitzstein–Hwang (probability), Judson (groups), Strang/Axler (linear algebra), Morris's *Topology Without Tears*, the SI Brochure, cos.io. These are simultaneously free and canonical — the rare combination your framework prizes.
3. **Transcribe the contested foundings as lineages, not single names** (probability: Cardano → Pascal–Fermat → Huygens → Laplace → Kolmogorov; group theory: Galois/Cauchy → Cayley → Jordan; sampling: Whittaker/Kotelnikov/Shannon; open science: Rosenthal precursor → Chambers/Nosek). Treat the contestation as content to master.
4. **Threshold for promotion to "settled":** any card currently tagged **[candidate, unconfirmed]** (prime-gap statistics origin and codification) or **[candidate, partially confirmed]** (diophantine approximation, continued fractions, special functions, asymptotic analysis, geometric algebra, linear-algebra codification, projective-geometry codification, order & lattice origin, point-process statistics) should get a dedicated two-independent-source check before you ink it as fact. That is the single highest-value next action.

## Caveats

- **Verification depth varies by field.** Independent two-source web verification was completed this session for: sampling theory (Rung Zero), analytic/multiplicative number theory, group theory, Lie theory, information theory, ergodic theory, random matrix theory, mathematical statistics, falsificationism, experimental design, EDA, extreme value theory, Monte Carlo, representational measurement theory, point-set topology — plus dedicated verification for metrology, open science, error-statistics, psychophysics, and auditing. Fields drawn from standard canon but **not independently re-verified by two sources this session** carry explicit [candidate…] tags.
- **Origin vs. codification is frequently a judgment call.** For real analysis, complex analysis, and differential geometry, the "origin" is a cluster of near-simultaneous figures rather than one paper; lineages are presented.
- **"Door" recommendations** favor free/open resources where a genuinely canonical one exists; otherwise the standard paid textbook is named. The availability of specific PDFs changes over time.
- **The [f] field** (error-statistical philosophy) receives only an orientation note per the sizing rules; the two [t] number-theory/analysis tool fields and others receive origin + door without a codification tier, as specified.
- **Popper dating nuance:** *Logik der Forschung* carries a 1935 imprint but is conventionally dated 1934 (the year of release); both dates appear in reputable sources.
