**statement (plain)** — a microworld is a deliberately small, simplified world inside the computer — like the Turtle's drawing world — that a child can play in and master completely. Because the world is bounded and consistent, the learner can experiment freely, make and fix mistakes, and in doing so discover a big idea (a piece of geometry, physics, or math) by living inside a place where that idea is natural. Papert's image is "mathland": a country where you learn math the easy way you'd learn French by living in France.

**statement** — Papert gives a microworld as "a subset of reality or a constructed reality whose structure matches that of a given cognitive mechanism so as to provide an environment where the latter can operate effectively" (*Mindstorms*, 1980). In practice it is a constrained, internally consistent computational environment that embeds an important idea in a form learners can readily explore, with an easy-to-understand set of operations and room to grow. The best microworlds let a beginner act meaningfully at once while still rewarding sophisticated work — a [[object.hard-fun-low-floor-high-ceiling]] property. **[confirmed]**

**basis** — the microworld is the concrete setting in which [[object.constructionism]] is enacted: it gives the learner a manipulable, shareable space to build in. Its power comes from being a *world*, not a lesson — bounded enough to be fully explorable, rich enough to host a genuine idea. "Mathland" is Papert's name for the principle that an idea is learned best by inhabiting an environment where that idea is the native way of acting. The concept is established design vocabulary in educational technology, not a formalized result. **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — the term was coined by Papert in *Mindstorms* (1980) [[source.papert-1980]] out of the MIT Logo work; turtle graphics is its canonical instance. The notion was later generalized and elaborated by the broader Logo / constructionism community (e.g. simulation microworlds, the "computational document" framing). **[confirmed as attribution]**
