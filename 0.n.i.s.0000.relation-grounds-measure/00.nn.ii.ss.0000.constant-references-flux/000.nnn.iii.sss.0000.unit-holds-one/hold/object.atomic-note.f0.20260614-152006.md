**statement (plain)** — an atomic note holds exactly one idea — one concept, argument, model, observation, or counter-argument — written in your own words and complete enough to stand on its own when read out of context. Keeping notes small and single-purpose is what lets you link them precisely: you connect one idea to another, not one bloated page to another.

**statement** — the Principle of Atomicity, as stated by zettelkasten.de, is "a processing direction in note-taking, aiming for one knowledge building block per note"; "purely atomic notes are notes that contain exactly one knowledge building block." Its justification is structural: "The very idea of links implies that there are components that you link" — atomicity supplies the discrete components. It is described as "not a rigid law, but a guiding compass." **[confirmed]**

**basis** — a practitioner heuristic, not a theorem. Its warrant is functional: granular notes give links precise endpoints, which is what lets new structure emerge in the [[object.zettelkasten-slip-box]]. Luhmann's archive evidences the practice (each card carries a bounded idea in his own words), per Schmidt [[source.schmidt-2018]]; the explicit *principle* and the word "atomic" are codification by Ahrens [[source.ahrens-2017]] and the zettelkasten.de community.

**lineage** — Luhmann practiced one-idea-per-card but did not use the term "atomic note." The terminology and its formalization are an Ahrens-era / community framing built to make the method teachable. **[candidate, terminology is later interpretation]** — carry the attribution as: practice = Luhmann (confirmed via Schmidt's archive); name and stated principle = Ahrens 2017 + zettelkasten.de (confirmed as attribution, not as Luhmann's own).

**door** — zettelkasten.de, "The Complete Guide to Atomic Note-Taking" (atomicity/guide); Ahrens, *How to Take Smart Notes* (2017), on the types and sizing of notes.
