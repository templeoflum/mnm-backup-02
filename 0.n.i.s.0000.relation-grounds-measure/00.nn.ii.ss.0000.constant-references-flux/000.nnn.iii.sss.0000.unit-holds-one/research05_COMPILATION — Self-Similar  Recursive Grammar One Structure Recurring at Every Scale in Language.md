## TARGET 2A — The fractal structure of language (Alabdulmohsin et al. 2024)

**Canonical source:** Ibrahim Alabdulmohsin, Vinh Q. Tran, Mostafa Dehghani (all Google DeepMind), "Fractal Patterns May Illuminate the Success of Next-Token Prediction," arXiv:2402.01825. v1 (Feb 2, 2024) was titled "Fractal Patterns May Unravel the Intelligence in Next-Token Prediction"; v2 (May 22, 2024) retitled. Published at NeurIPS 2024.

### 2A.1 — The claim that language is self-similar across scales

- The paper's central established claim (verbatim abstract): "We establish that language is: (1) self-similar, exhibiting complexities at all levels of granularity, with no particular characteristic context length, and (2) long-range dependent (LRD), with a Hurst parameter of approximately H=0.70±0.09."
- The self-similarity claim is operationalized over the *information-theoretic complexity* of language — the number of bits (surprise) assigned to each token. They convert text into a sequence of bits using an LLM: z_t = −log p(w_t | w_[t−1]), computed for prefixes up to 2048 tokens (~8 pages). PaLM2-L (Unicorn) is used to approximate the joint distribution.
- "an object is called 'self-similar' if it is invariant across scales, meaning its statistical or geometric properties stay consistent irrespective of the magnification applied to it."
- Word→sentence→paragraph→text mirroring (verbatim): "self-similarity implies that the patterns at the level of a paragraph are reflective of the patterns seen at the level of a whole text, which is reminiscent of the recursive structure of language." And: "surprising paragraphs will follow predictable paragraphs, in a manner that is statistically similar to how surprising sentences follow predictable sentences."
- They argue short-term patterns "mirror the patterns/dependencies over larger scopes, like entire documents," which "may shed some light on how next-token prediction can capture the structure of text across multiple levels of granularity, from words and clauses to broader contexts and intents."
- They credit prior arguments for linguistic self-similarity to Willinger et al. and Altmann et al. (2012), attributing self-similarity to language's "recursive structure," but note prior work used "rudimentary approximations" (substituting a word with its frequency/length, per Ausloos 2012; or recurrence of a single predetermined word, per Najafi & Darooneh 2015 / Altmann et al. 2012).

### 2A.2 — Quantification: Hölder exponent and Hurst exponent

- **Self-similarity (Hölder) exponent**, denoted S: reported value **S = 0.59 ± 0.08** ("In language, we find it to be S=0.59±0.08, confirming statistical self-similarity"). It is estimated via a power law p_ε(τ) ~ τ^(−S), where peak probability is plotted against granularity level τ. The text states the median exponent is S = 0.59 ± 0.08.
- **Hurst parameter**, denoted H: reported value **H = 0.70 ± 0.09** (also stated as ≈0.7). Estimated via rescaled-range analysis: R(n)/S(n) ~ n^H. Range/interpretation given verbatim: "H only takes values in [0,1]. A value H>0.5 implies predictability in the data, while H=0.5 indicates random increments." H=1 = predictability; H=0.5 = noise.
- They note their estimate "lies nearly midway between predictability (H=1) and noise (H=0.5)" and that "a Hurst parameter of about 0.75 turns out to occur commonly in nature, including in river discharges, Ethernet traffic, temperatures, precipitation, and tree rings."
- They also estimate the **fractal dimension** and the related **Joseph exponent** (the paper lists three parameters: self-similarity/Hölder exponent, Hurst parameter, fractal dimension, plus the Joseph exponent).
- Robustness: fractal parameters are "robust" across architectures and scales (models from 1B to over 500B parameters; PaLM, PaLM2 XXS–L, decoder-only T5.1.1) and across domains (ArXiv, GitHub).
- Practical result: the "median" Hurst exponent improves on perplexity-based bits-per-byte (BPB) in predicting downstream performance. Per the NeurIPS 2024 proceedings, combining BPB and Hurst into a new metric "HB = 1/BPB + H" can "increase the adjusted R2 from approximately 0.65 when using solely BPB, to over 0.86 with the new metric."
- **Note on conventions (cross-source):** The follow-up paper "A Tale of Two Structures" (Alabdulmohsin & Steiner, ICML 2025) states the convention that "smaller values of S indicating a more significant self-similar structure (heavier tail)" and "larger values of the Hurst exponent H≫0.5 indicate more dependence across time." So for strong self-similarity, S is the heavy-tail/decay parameter while H>0.5 indicates strong long-range dependence.

### 2A.3 — The control finding: language is fractal but vision is not

- The 2024 fractal paper itself contrasts a self-similar integral process (from Wikipedia text) against "a process that is not self-similar, looking smoother at larger time scales" (Figure 1).
- The cleanest control statement appears in the follow-up RINS paper (Alabdulmohsin & Zhai 2025), which interprets the original finding: "RINS does not help in the pure vision domain, but it can still improve performance in multimodal systems that also process language, such as contrastive models." This is offered as evidence that the recursive/scale-invariant structure is specific to the sequential/linguistic domain.
- **Flag:** In the 2402.01825 paper itself, the sharp "vision (ImageNet/ViT) is NOT fractal" contrast as the user phrased it is most explicitly articulated through the RINS follow-up's domain-specificity argument (recursion helps language, not pure vision). The original paper establishes self-similarity *in language* and shows robustness across language domains (ArXiv/GitHub); the explicit vision-as-non-fractal control is most directly stated in the RINS paper's framing rather than as a headline experiment in 2402.01825. Researchers should consult the original §domain analysis for exact scope.

## TARGET 2B — Fractal Linguistics & recursion as a cognitive default

### 2B.1 — "Fractal Linguistics" by Karine Megerdoomian — verification

- **VERIFIED, with an important caveat about genre.** The titled work "Fractal Linguistics: Recursion, Scaling, and the Hidden Geometry of Language" exists as a **blog post** by Karine Megerdoomian, dated November 5, 2025, on her personal site (karinemegerdoomian.org). It is NOT a peer-reviewed journal article. It explicitly describes "an idea I call Fractal Linguistics" that she has been "playing around with."
- **Author identity (verified):** Karine Megerdoomian, PhD in Linguistics from the University of Southern California (1995–2002); ~18 years at MITRE Corporation (most recently Principal AI Engineer); founder of the Zoorna Institute for Language, AI and Society. A recognized computational linguist specializing in Persian and Armenian; her formal linguistic work centers on Persian complex predicates and the syntax-semantics interface.
- **The underlying peer-ish/conference work it rests on (verified):** Megerdoomian's NACIL2 (Second North American Conference in Iranian Linguistics, University of Arizona, April 19–21, 2019) keynote, "Recursion and Scaling in Complex Predicates: Light Verbs as Underspecified Regular Verbs" (also on YouTube). Her foundational formal work is her USC PhD dissertation "Beyond Words and Phrases: A Unified Theory of Predicate Composition" (2000/2002; published Saarbrücken: VDM Verlag 2009) and "Event structure and complex predicates in Persian," Canadian Journal of Linguistics 46 (2001).
- A forthcoming paper is mentioned in the blog: "Fractional Dimensions in Language" (status: forthcoming, unverifiable).

- **The self-similar-syntax claim (verbatim from blog):** "The same compositional logic that determines aspect and argument structure at the sentence level seems to reappear inside the predicate itself. It was as if each complex predicate was a miniature version of the verb phrase: a self-similar structure, recursively repeating the same syntactic and semantic relations that govern the larger clause."
- Construction evidence cited: Persian complex predicates such as *ranj keshidan* ("to drag pain" = to suffer), *rang zadan* ("to hit color" = to paint), *rowqan zadan* ("to hit oil" = to oil/apply oil). Claim (verbatim): "a complex predicate is a small-scale recursion of the verb phrase," projecting arguments, defining event boundaries, and determining aspect. "When you zoom in on a Persian complex predicate, you find the same geometry of relations that defines the entire clause: argument structure, aspect, telicity, and event delimitation."
- The "linguistic Mandelbrot set" framing: "The syntax-semantics interface, in this sense, behaves fractal-like: scaling up or down doesn't change its fundamental pattern."

### 2B.2 — Light verbs as "underspecified" event templates

- **Megerdoomian's account (verbatim, blog + NACIL2 abstract):** Persian light verbs *zadan* ("hit"), *gereftan* ("take"), *kardan* ("do") "are often described as semantically 'light,' but that term hides a deeper truth. They aren't empty but rather underspecified, containing just enough structure to project an event template without fixing its content."
- NACIL2 abstract (verbatim): "I propose that the light verb is an underspecified full verb, and the relationships the light verb forms with its complements in the CP parallel those between the full verb and its complements. The lexical polysemy arises from the combination of (i) the amount of underspecification in the structure of the light verb and (ii) the properties of the nonverbal element with which it builds a CP… the light verb predicate structure is a smaller scale representation of the verb phrase construction. As a result… there is no longer a need for the light verb category since it is the level of structural specification that determines where the verb's meaning and properties fall in the continuum." Megerdoomian rejects predefined semantic classes for light verbs (contra Samvelian 2012; contra Butt & Geuder 2013's semantic-continuum-as-gradual-extension).
- **Megerdoomian (2001, 2012) structural position:** the non-verbal (NV) element and light verb (LV) form a constituent (one syntactic unit), which then takes the internal argument as complement; the LV can undergo movement motivated by aspectual readings.
- **Folli, Harley & Karimi (2005), "Determinants of event type in Persian complex predicates," Lingua 115(10):1365–1401:** analyze the interdependence of NV elements and the LV in determining syntactic properties, event structure, and alternation possibilities. They argue for a constructionalist approach (à la Hale & Keyser 1993, 2002) over a projectionist one. Contra Megerdoomian, they hold the two elements are separate heads each projecting its own phrase, with the NV taking the internal argument as complement. Key finding: the LV determines agentivity (*kardan*/*zadan* = agentive vs. *xordan* "collide" = non-agentive) and eventiveness, but "fails to completely determine its event structure and telicity. Thus, depending on the NV element, the same LV may occur in different types of event structure." Light verbs contribute aspectual character (e.g., *zadan* "hit" contributes punctuality; *keshidan* "pull" contributes duration — Megerdoomian 2002a).
- **Family (2014):** identifies fourteen most-frequently-used Persian light verbs.
- **General "underspecified light verb" account (cross-linguistic):** Eva Wittenberg, Martin Paczynski, Heike Wiese, Ray Jackendoff & Gina R. Kuperberg (2014), "The difference between 'giving a rose' and 'giving a kiss': Sustained neural activity to the light verb construction," *Journal of Memory and Language* 73:31–42, characterize LVCs as expressions that "consist of a semantically underspecified light verb ('give') and an event nominal that contributes most of the meaning and also activates an argument structure of its own ('kiss')." Their ERP data support models where both syntactic and semantic compositionality contribute (Butt 2010; Culicover & Jackendoff 2005; Jackendoff 2002), against treating LVCs as syntactically simpler.

### 2B.3 — "Self-similarity and recursion as default modes in human cognition" (Fischmeister, Martins, Beisteiner & Fitch, Cortex 2017)

- **Verified citation:** Florian P. Fischmeister, Mauricio J. D. Martins, Roland Beisteiner, W. Tecumseh Fitch (2017), *Cortex* 97:183–201; DOI 10.1016/j.cortex.2016.08.016 (Epub Sep 23, 2016). Note: the user named three authors; the paper has **four** (Beisteiner is the third author). Affiliations: Medical University of Vienna; University of Vienna (Dept. Cognitive Biology); Berlin School of Mind and Brain / Max Planck Institute Leipzig.
- **Method (verified from full text):** Task-free resting-state fMRI plus task fMRI in **35 healthy participants** (19 M, 16 F, ages 20–32, right-handed native German speakers). Stimuli were visual fractals: the **Visual Recursion Task (VRT)** vs. the **Embedded Iteration Task (EIT)**. The *same final fourth-iteration images* were used in both; only the generating rule differed — recursive (VRT) vs. non-recursive/iterative (EIT). A positional-similarity control task (PSVT) was included. 3T Siemens, seed-to-voxel functional connectivity (CONN/SPM8) using DMN and Fronto-Parietal Control Network (FPCN) seeds from the Power et al. (2011) parcellation.
- **Core findings (verbatim abstract):** "We observed increased connectivity within Default Mode Network (DMN) related brain areas during the representation of recursion, while non-recursive representations yielded increased connectivity within the Fronto-Parietal Control-Network."
- A clean double dissociation: recursion > non-recursion increased connectivity in DMN core areas (medial prefrontal cortex, inferior parietal lobule, lateral temporal cortex, right precuneus, and the angular gyrus, plus some occipital areas), with no significant reverse effect in DMN; non-recursion > recursion increased connectivity within FPCN core areas (lateral frontal, temporal, parietal cortices). Behaviorally all conditions were performed well (VRT 95.5% correct, EIT 91.4%, PSVT 96.1%); VRT had the fastest reaction time (2.34s).
- **Central conclusion (verbatim):** "human hierarchical information processing using recursion is supported by the DMN. In particular, the representation of recursion seems to constitute an internally-biased mode of information-processing that is mediated by both the core and dorsal-medial subsystems of the DMN… Compressed internal rule representations mediated by the DMN may help humans to represent and process hierarchical structures in complex environments by considerably reducing information processing load." Recursion is "closer to the default mode of human cognition."
- **Important caveat / honest flag re domain-generality:** The study's own empirical contribution is restricted to the **visual** domain. Domain-generality beyond vision is offered as a *hypothesis*, NOT demonstrated (verbatim): "Our conclusion… is currently only valid for the cognitive domain applied in this study, i.e., the visual domain. However, we suspect that the decisive difference… might extend to other domains for which humans are able to build recursive representations, e.g., music and spoken language… Future research will be necessary to test the generality of this hypothesis." They also bracket the supramodal question explicitly. Background supporting the non-language-specific view: prior work showed visual recursion is "independent of verbal resources (Martins et al., 2015) and does not recruit classical perisylvian language areas (Martins, Fischmeister, et al., 2014)."

## TARGET 2C — Compositional / categorical structure across scales

### 2C.1 — Formal compositionality (Frege's principle)

- **Standard statement (Barbara Partee's widely-quoted formulation):** "The meaning of a compound expression is a function of the meanings of its parts and of the way they are syntactically combined."
- **SEP / Pelletier formulation (The Principle of Semantic Compositionality):** "the meaning of a (syntactically complex) whole is a function only of the meanings of its (syntactic) parts together with the manner in which these parts were combined."
- Two necessary components: (i) meanings of the parts (words/phrases); (ii) rules of combination (grammatical structure). The "position-carries-role" idea is captured in the requirement that the *manner of combination* (syntactic structure), not just the parts, determines the whole's meaning.
- **Attribution:** Called "Frege's Principle," credited to Gottlob Frege (associated with his 1892 work). Multiple sources note Frege never explicitly stated it; it may have been assumed by Boole earlier; it gained formal prominence via Richard Montague's model-theoretic semantics (homomorphism between syntax and semantics) and Barbara Partee. Note Frege's *context principle* (Foundations of Arithmetic, 1884: never ask for the meaning of a word in isolation, only in the context of a proposition) is in tension with strong forms of compositionality.
- Distinctions: "strong compositionality" (meaning determined by immediate parts + top-level syntactic function) vs. "weak compositionality" (determined by parts + complete syntactic combination). SEP also distinguishes (C) from a stronger "building principle."
- **Known limits/objections (per sources):** idioms ("kick the bucket," "spill the beans," "break a leg" — meaning not a function of parts); context-dependence; pragmatic factors (irony); belief reports; quotation; ambiguity. Wikipedia: "The principle of compositionality usually holds when only syntactic factors play in the increased complexity… while it becomes more problematic… when the complexity increase is due to sentence or discourse context, semantic memory, or sensory cues."

### 2C.2 — Recursive Inference Scaling / RINS (Alabdulmohsin & Zhai 2025)

- **Verified citation:** Ibrahim Alabdulmohsin & Xiaohua Zhai (Google DeepMind), "Recursive Inference Scaling: A Winning Path to Scalable Inference in Language and Multimodal Systems," arXiv:2502.07503 (v1 Feb 11, 2025; v4 May 8, 2025). v1 title: "Harnessing Language's Fractal Geometry with Recursive Inference Scaling." Accepted to NeurIPS 2025.
- **Motivation (verbatim):** "Inspired by recent findings on the fractal geometry of language, we introduce Recursive INference Scaling (RINS)… RINS is a particular form of recursive depth." It treats recursive depth as "a form of scale-invariant decoding" that exploits language's self-similarity.
- **What RINS does (verbatim definition):** "RINS corresponds to architectures with degree 1 and signature A^rB, for some integer r>1. In other words, RINS partitions a model depthwise into two equally-sized blocks A and B… The first block A is recursively applied on its own output r times before applying B." (E.g., in a 12-layer net, A = first 6 layers, B = second 6; A is looped r times.) Recursion is a form of parameter sharing (cf. Universal Transformers, ALBERT).
- **What it shows:**
  - Outperforms +55 other parameter-sharing/recursion variants, including "repeat-all-over" (RAO, signature ABAB; Mobile LLM, Liu et al. 2024) and latent recurrent thinking (Geiping et al. 2025), on a **compute-matched** regime (controlling training FLOPs).
  - For fixed model size and training compute, RINS "substantially improves language modeling performance."
  - Generalizes to multimodal systems: "+2% improvement in 0-shot ImageNet accuracy for SigLIP-B/16."
  - Data scaling laws (verbatim): "by deriving data scaling laws, we show that RINS improves both the asymptotic performance limits and the scaling exponents." Optimal recursion count r increases monotonically with training compute; smaller models benefit more.
  - "No-regret" strategy (verbatim): "with light-weight (linear) adapters (comprising <1% of model parameters) and stochastic dropout, RINS offers a no-regret strategy, meaning that RINS-enabled pretraining improves performance in language modeling even when recursive depth is not applied at inference time."
  - **Domain-specificity evidence (verbatim):** "RINS does not help in the pure vision domain, but it can still improve performance in multimodal systems that also process language, such as contrastive models." Offered as evidence the gains stem from exploiting language's self-similarity.

### 2C.3 — Rigorous work formalizing a single recurring relational schema across hierarchical levels

**Established / peer-reviewed:**
- **X-bar theory (Chomsky 1970, "Remarks on Nominalization"; Jackendoff 1977):** the strongest classical candidate for "one schema at every level." Posits a uniform template — head (X⁰) → intermediate projection (X′) → maximal projection (XP) — applying to ALL phrasal categories (NP, VP, AP, PP). Core tenets: endocentricity (every phrase has a head of its own category) and recursive specifier-head-complement configurations. Wikipedia: "X" is a variable substitutable by N, V, A, P; "all phrasal categories have the [same] structure." Later: Kayne's antisymmetry (universal specifier-head-complement order) and Chomsky's Minimalist "bare phrase structure" / Merge (free recursive phrase formation) descend from it.
- **Faculty of language / recursion (Hauser, Chomsky & Fitch 2002, *Science* 298:1569–1579):** the canonical statement that recursion is the core computational mechanism. They distinguish FLB (broad: sensory-motor + conceptual-intentional + recursion) from FLN (narrow). Verbatim: "We hypothesize that FLN only includes recursion and is the only uniquely human component of the faculty of language," providing "the capacity to generate an infinite range of expressions from a finite set of elements" and yielding "discrete infinity." They explicitly suggest recursion may operate outside language (number, navigation, social relations) — i.e., a single recurring computational schema across domains. (Contested: e.g., Pinker & Jackendoff 2005; Everaert et al.; debates over Pirahã.)
- **Categorial / type-logical grammar (Ajdukiewicz 1935; Bar-Hillel 1953; Lambek 1958; Montague 1970s; Steedman's CCG):** treats syntactic categories as semantic types; constituents combine as functions and arguments via a small set of type-combination rules applied recursively at every level. Montague's program (per SEP/ScienceDirect): "the syntax is an algebra, the semantics is an algebra, and there is a homomorphism" between them — formalizing compositionality as a structure-preserving map. Lambek's typelogical grammar realizes "Montague's compositionality program in an uncompromising way," with the Curry-Howard proofs-as-programs interpretation linking syntactic proofs to semantic (lambda-calculus) terms. This is a rigorous "single recurring relational schema" (function application / type combination) across levels.
- **Quantitative fractal-structure work (physics/quantitative linguistics):** Menzerath-Altmann law (as the construct grows, constituents shrink — "a certain kind of self-similarity… at every level"); Ausloos (2012, multifractal Hurst/length-frequency time series); Najafi & Darooneh (2015, PLOS ONE, fractal dimension per word for keyword extraction); Andres et al. (2012, *Journal of Quantitative Linguistics*, fractal analysis of texts); Hřebíček (1994); "On the fractal patterns of language structures" (Ribeiro, Bernardes & Mello, PLOS ONE 2023); Vasilii A. Gromov, Nikita S. Borodin & Asel S. Yerbolova, "A Language and Its Dimensions: Intrinsic Dimensions of Language Fractal Structures" (arXiv:2311.10217; published in *Complexity*, Wiley, 2024), which reports that "the intrinsic dimensions appear to be non-integer values (typical for fractal sets), close to 9 for both of the Russian and English language." These establish statistical/geometric self-similarity but generally do NOT formalize a single relational *schema*; they measure scalar fractal parameters.
- **Frontier ML (2024–2026):** Alabdulmohsin et al. 2024 (2A); RINS (2C.2); "A Tale of Two Structures: Do LLMs Capture the Fractal Complexity of Language?" (Alabdulmohsin & Steiner, ICML 2025, arXiv:2502.14924) — finds natural-language fractal parameters lie in a narrow range while LLM outputs vary widely (usable for synthetic-text detection); Hurst is a strong quality predictor; releases the GAGLE benchmark dataset, described verbatim as "a benchmark dataset called GAGLE, comprising over 240,000 articles generated by various LLMs," with findings robust across "Gemini 1.0 Pro, Mistral-7B and Gemma-2B."

**Named active researchers across this target:** Ibrahim Alabdulmohsin, Mostafa Dehghani, Vinh Q. Tran, Xiaohua Zhai, Andreas Steiner (Google DeepMind); W. Tecumseh Fitch, Mauricio Martins, Florian Fischmeister (cognitive biology/neuroscience of recursion); Noam Chomsky, Ray Jackendoff (X-bar/recursion); Barbara Partee, Richard Montague (compositionality); Mark Steedman, Joachim Lambek, Michael Moortgat (categorial/type-logical grammar); Karine Megerdoomian, Heidi Harley, Simin Karimi, Raffaella Folli, Pollet Samvelian (Persian complex predicates/light verbs).
