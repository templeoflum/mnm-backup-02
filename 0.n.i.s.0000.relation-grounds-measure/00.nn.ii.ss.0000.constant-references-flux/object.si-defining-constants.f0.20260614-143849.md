**statement (plain)** — in 2019 the SI was rebuilt around seven fundamental constants, each assigned an exact, unchangeable number. Because the constants are fixed, every unit is defined indirectly through them — no physical artifact (like the old kilogram cylinder) is needed any longer. The metre, kilogram, second and the rest are now consequences of nature's constants rather than separate definitions.

**statement (precise)** — the 2019 SI fixes the exact numerical values of seven defining constants:
- caesium-133 hyperfine transition frequency ΔνCs = **9 192 631 770 Hz**
- speed of light in vacuum c = **299 792 458 m·s⁻¹**
- Planck constant h = **6.626 070 15 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s**
- elementary charge e = **1.602 176 634 × 10⁻¹⁹ C**
- Boltzmann constant k = **1.380 649 × 10⁻²³ J·K⁻¹**
- Avogadro constant NA = **6.022 140 76 × 10²³ mol⁻¹**
- luminous efficacy of 540 THz radiation Kcd = **683 lm·W⁻¹**

Fixing these seven values defines the entire SI; the seven base units are derived from them. **[confirmed]**

**status** — in force since 20 May 2019.

**lineage** — adopted unanimously by the 26th [[source.si-brochure-2019]] / CGPM (Resolution 1, November 2018) and made effective on 20 May 2019, the 144th anniversary of the [[source.metre-convention-1875]]. The redefinition re-expressed four base units — the [[object.kilogram-planck-definition|kilogram]], ampere, kelvin and mole — in terms of fixed constants (h, e, k, NA respectively); the second, metre and candela had already been tied to ΔνCs, c and Kcd. The defining text is the *SI Brochure*, 9th ed. [[source.si-brochure-2019]], US edition [[source.nist-sp330-2019]]. **[confirmed]**
