**statement (plain)** — science doesn't pile up confirmations until a law is proven. It starts from a problem, throws out a bold guess (a conjecture), then attacks that guess with the hardest tests it can devise. A guess that survives is not "proven true" — it is *corroborated*, held provisionally until something refutes it or a better guess replaces it. Progress is trial and the elimination of error, not the accumulation of proof.

**statement** — the hypothetico-deductive cycle: problem → tentative theory (conjecture) → deduction of testable consequences → severe attempted refutation (experiment/observation aimed at falsifying) → error elimination → new problem. Theories that survive severe testing are *corroborated*; corroboration measures how well a theory has stood up to refutation and is explicitly distinguished from inductive confirmation and from probability — a highly corroborated theory is not thereby probable, and corroboration is not a verification. Science is anti-inductivist: observations function as tests of conjectures, never as their inductive source. **[confirmed]**

**status** — established as a methodological position; informal (a normative account of method, not a formal theorem).

**basis** — the method rests on the [[object.verification-falsification-asymmetry]]: since universal laws can be refuted but not verified, the rational strategy is to conjecture boldly and test severely, keeping only what has not yet been refuted. Corroboration replaces verification as the measure of a theory's standing. [[field.popperian-falsificationism]]

**lineage** — *Conjectures and Refutations* (1963) is the most accessible and named statement of the method, but the conjecture-and-test, anti-inductivist core is already in *Logik der Forschung* (1934/1935). The account was later contested by Lakatos (research programmes) and Kuhn (paradigms) as a description of how science actually proceeds. **[confirmed as attribution]**
