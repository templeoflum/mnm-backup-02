Full citation — J.-L. Lagrange, "Réflexions sur la résolution algébrique des équations," *Nouveaux Mémoires de l'Académie royale des sciences et belles-lettres de Berlin* (read 1770–1771, published 1772; reprinted in *Œuvres de Lagrange*, vol. 3). Distinct work from [[source.lagrange-1770]] (the Berlin continued-fractions memoirs) — same author, different paper.

Role — origin / pre-history of [[object.lagrange-theorem]]: Lagrange studied how a rational function of the n roots of a polynomial changes under the n! permutations of those roots, and observed that the number of distinct values it takes always divides n!. This is the special case of the modern theorem (subgroup order divides group order) for the symmetric group Sₙ acting by permutation — stated long before abstract groups existed. Lagrange did not prove or state the general theorem.

Access — *Œuvres de Lagrange* (ed. Serret, Gauthier-Villars) digitized at BnF/archive.org.
