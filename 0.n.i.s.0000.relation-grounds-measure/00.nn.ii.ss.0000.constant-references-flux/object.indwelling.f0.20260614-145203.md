**statement (plain)** — we come to know something from the inside by making its parts part of ourselves. A blind person's stick stops being a thing they hold and becomes a thing they feel the world *through*; a tool, a notation, or a theory, once mastered, is no longer looked at but lived in and looked through. Polanyi calls this *indwelling* (interiorization): we assimilate the particulars into our body so we can attend from them to the meaning they disclose. Skill and understanding are acquired this way — by dwelling in a practice under guidance, as in apprenticeship — not by being told a set of rules.

**statement** — *indwelling* is the mode by which the subsidiary (proximal) term of tacit knowing is established: we know a comprehensive entity "by interiorizing its parts" or "by making ourselves dwell in them," extending our bodily presence to incorporate tools, frameworks, and the conduct of others. It is the integrative act underlying the from-to structure; interpretative frameworks and skills are indwelt rather than observed.

**basis** — grounded in Polanyi's cases of bodily extension: the probe/stick whose tip becomes a sentient boundary, the spectacles we see through but not at, the way we read another's mind by dwelling in their physiognomy and behaviour and lose it the moment we focus on the bodily workings themselves. Acquisition by apprenticeship and practice (rather than explicit instruction) is offered as corroborating evidence. **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — introduced by Polanyi in *Personal Knowledge* (1958) [[source.polanyi-1958]] and elaborated in *The Tacit Dimension* (1966) [[source.polanyi-1966]]; it generalizes the older notion that comprehension requires the knower's participation. Polanyi ties indwelling explicitly to his rejection of wholly objective, impersonal knowledge. **[confirmed]**

**sources** — Polanyi, *Personal Knowledge* (1958) [[source.polanyi-1958]]; *The Tacit Dimension* (1966) [[source.polanyi-1966]]; Polanyi Society, "The Structure of Consciousness"; New World Encyclopedia, "Michael Polanyi."
