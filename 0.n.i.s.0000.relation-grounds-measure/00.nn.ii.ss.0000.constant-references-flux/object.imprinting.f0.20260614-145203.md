**statement (plain)** — in certain animals — especially ground-running birds like geese and ducks — a newly hatched young one will, within a narrow window of hours after hatching, latch onto the first moving thing it sees and treat it as its parent, following it thereafter. The learning is fast, happens only during that early "critical period," and is hard to reverse. Lorenz famously had goslings imprint on him and follow him about.

**statement** — **filial imprinting**: a form of phase-sensitive learning in which a precocial (nidifugous) young bird rapidly acquires a social/following attachment to the first salient moving object encountered after hatching, during a restricted **critical (sensitive) period**, with the attachment being durable and resistant to reversal. Lorenz's German term is **Prägung** ("stamping in"). In greylag geese the period is roughly the first ~13–16 hours after hatching, declining sharply thereafter. Lorenz embedded it in the broader **Kumpan** ("companion") concept — distinct imprinting-like processes building functionally separate social companions. **[confirmed]**

**basis** — an empirical concept of classical ethology, grounded in observation and rearing experiments with greylag geese, ducks, and chickens. The classic demonstration is goslings imprinting on Lorenz himself rather than the mother. The "irreversible / fixed critical period" framing has since been qualified by later research (sensitive rather than absolutely closed period; partial reversibility), but imprinting and the critical-period idea remain foundational. **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — first reported by **Douglas Spalding** in 1873 (chicks following the first moving object — "stamping in"); the German term **Prägung** introduced by **Oskar Heinroth** (1911), Lorenz's mentor; **Konrad Lorenz**, Heinroth's student, named the phenomenon *imprinting* and made it foundational through [[source.lorenz-1935]] (the Kumpan monograph) and its English condensation [[source.lorenz-1937]] ("The Companion in the Bird's World," *The Auk*). Lorenz/Tinbergen/von Frisch shared the 1973 Nobel. Spalding-(1873) and Heinroth-(1911) priority carried as lineage, not competing attribution. **[confirmed]**
