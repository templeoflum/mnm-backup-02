Reading this diagram

Two halves per layer. Every layer in the framework splits into Identity (the part that holds constant) and Trajectory (the part that varies per movement). Identity is authored — it lives in the realization spec. Trajectory is computed from spline signals — math drives it.

The split answers a recurring question. When working with the framework you constantly ask: where does property X live? Is it Identity or Trajectory? This diagram is the answer table. The Figure's hair color is Identity (Character row, left column). The Figure walking-in-M1-versus-standing-in-M2 is Trajectory (Character row, right column). The brass latch's verdigris is Identity (Object row, left). The Window being-distant-then-at-hand-then-open is Trajectory (Object row, right).

Dependency order matters. Layers are stacked vertically in dependency order: math drives content (because math primitives populate the Character / Object / Motif content), content drives surface (because the Figure and Window appear visually and sonically), surface drives voice (because voice describes what's seen and heard). When composing a realization, work top-down: pick math identity first, then content, then surface, then voice.

Workflow connection. Identity content enters the workflow at Pass 5 (form, with generic placeholders) and Pass 6 (substitution, where work-specific identities get committed). Trajectory enters at Pass 7 (outline, where per-movement beats and arcs are applied) and shapes everything downstream. The four-block prompt template (Identity-block / Scene / Length / Avoid) at Pass 5 is structured around this split.
