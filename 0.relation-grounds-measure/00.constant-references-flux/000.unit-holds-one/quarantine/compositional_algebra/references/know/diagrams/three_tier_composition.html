structural notes

Reading this diagram

Three tiers, one composition. A realization spec (Tier 3) is the complete cross-layer bundle that defines how a work renders. Each of its six layers can be specified at Tier 2 (a named preset) or at Tier 1 (atomic sub-function choices). The choice of tier per layer is independent: a realization can use a preset for voice, atomic for medium, preset for visual world, and so on.

Preset-based path (Realization A in the diagram). Each layer names a Tier 2 preset. The preset is shorthand for a bundle of Tier 1 choices. Fastest composition path because it leverages reusable bundles; smallest realization spec. Use when an existing preset serves the realization's intent without modification.

Atomic path (Realization B). Tier 3 spec names Tier 1 atomic choices directly for some or all layers. More verbose but more expressive — every sub-function choice is explicit, no preset is assumed. Use when no preset fits cleanly, or when you want every choice visible in the spec.

Locked versus overrideable. Tier 2 presets mark some of their constituent Tier 1 choices as locked — they're constitutive of the preset's identity, and overriding them means the realization has left the preset and become a sibling rather than the preset proper. Other choices are overrideable — a realization can deviate without losing the preset's identity. The Voice preset's Positioning (Intimate) and Configuration (Solo) are locked; Register, Stance, and Affect are overrideable.

Mix tiers per layer freely. Realizations don't have to commit to one tier across the board. A realization that uses the Watercolor medium preset but needs a specific atomic combination for voice can do so. The preset-vs-atomic decision is per-layer, not per-realization.
