Reading this diagram

The spline emits per-movement signals, not per-movement content. Each control point in the spline is a commitment to eight typed signal values. Authoring a spline means deciding what each of these signals carries at each movement.

Two framework-wide drivers (purple) shape everything else. The Identity signal carries the movement's Felt weight (gathered, held, released, etc.) which propagates as felt-quality language into every layer's prompt and output. The Math vocabulary signal names which math primitives are at home this movement (and at what density) — these primitives drive concrete content across layers.

Three surface / voice form signals (teal) shape what each modality is doing this movement. Visual signal carries the spatial moment (opening image, closing image, characteristic gesture). Audio signal carries the sonic arc (opening sound, closing sound, dynamic, spatial). Voice signal carries the voice's job, pacing, and characteristic moves. All three feed the Scene block of their respective Pass 5 prompts.

Three work-specific content signals (blue) carry continuity material from the work's content layers (Character, Object, Motif). These don't enter Pass 5 prompts directly — Pass 5 is form only — but route to Pass 6 (substitution) and Pass 7 (outline) where they get woven into the prompts across all three modalities.

Authoring discipline. When writing a spline, each movement needs values for all eight signals. Missing a signal isn't a free choice — it means the downstream pass will have to guess. The spline reference for each work declares its eight-signal values per movement, in a structured per-movement section.
