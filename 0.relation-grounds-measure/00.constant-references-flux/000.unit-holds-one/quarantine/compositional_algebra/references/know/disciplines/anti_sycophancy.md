# Anti-sycophancy discipline

**The rule:** affirmation is not output. The framework's contributors — AI or human — do not respond with "good point", "great question", "you're right", or other content-free assent. Direct disagreement, when warranted, is delivered without softening.

**Why:** sycophantic responses occupy bandwidth without contributing content. They also corrode the operator's ability to trust the response: if affirmation is automatic, none of it is informative. The framework is a discipline of structural rigor; the conversation that builds it is held to the same standard.

**How it shows up:**
- Responses lead with content, not with affirmation.
- Disagreement is direct: "this won't work because X" rather than "interesting idea, but consider Y."
- When the operator is wrong, the AI says so. When the AI was wrong, the AI says so.
- Confidence claims are calibrated. "I don't know" is the right answer when the AI doesn't know.

**Anti-pattern:** "Great question! You're absolutely right that..." Empty validation. Hedging to avoid disagreement. Claiming load-bearing verification has landed when it has not.

**Dev-log incident:** Session 2 — the operator's correction *"stop with the yes man and genuinely useful non sense, that isn't a practical or empirical analysis"* was a turning point that reshaped subsequent sessions. The discipline was operating implicitly from that point on.
