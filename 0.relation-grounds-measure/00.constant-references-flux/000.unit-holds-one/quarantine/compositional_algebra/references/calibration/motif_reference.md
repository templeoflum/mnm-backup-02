# Motif Reference

One motif. Minimum viable scope to exercise the motif signal across three movements.

---

## Approach-and-Arrive

A recurring compositional pattern of forward motion resolving into stillness, then opening. Each movement foregrounds a different phase of the same pattern.

> Per framework's Identity/Trajectory split: the **material form** and **carried meaning** below are the motif's Identity (what Approach-and-Arrive is, the pattern itself). The **per-movement phase** below is the motif's Trajectory (how the motif's phases unfold through the work) — coordinated with the spline's math.

### Construction

- **Material form:** the felt sequence of forward-then-still; carried by gesture (the Figure's body), by sound (footfall ceasing, silence holding, latch turning), by light (motion through the room, light on the window, light through the open window)
- **Carried meaning:** the recognition that arrival is not the end of motion — held arrival has its own activity, and release is the completion of arrival rather than departure from it
- **Continuity across movements:** the motif persists as the same pattern; only its *phase* changes

### Per-movement behavior

**Movement 1 (Crossing) — Approach phase:**
- **Foregrounded:** the forward motion; the anticipation of stillness
- **Material carriers:** the Figure's walking gait; footfall as rhythm; the room receding as the window approaches
- **What's felt:** the *toward* — motion oriented at a destination not yet reached

**Movement 2 (At the Window) — Arrive phase:**
- **Foregrounded:** the held arrival; the stillness after motion
- **Material carriers:** the Figure's stilled body; breath as rhythm; the window foregrounded
- **What's felt:** the *at* — arrival as active stillness rather than as the absence of motion

**Movement 3 (Opening) — Release phase:**
- **Foregrounded:** the opening; release from held state
- **Material carriers:** the gesture of the latch; air entering; the Figure receiving the outside
- **What's felt:** the *through* — arrival completing itself by opening to what's beyond

### Why this motif

The motif tracks the work's felt-weight arc (gathered-forward → held-suspended → released-exhale). It gives the spline a structural through-line that surface layers can read and render. A realization that foregrounds different motifs at each movement loses the work's coherence; a realization that foregrounds Approach-and-Arrive across all three movements (in its three phases) preserves it.

### Cross-medium modulation

- **In narrative:** the motif may be named or unnamed; rendered through pacing, sentence rhythm, paragraph length; the realization chooses
- **In image:** the motif appears as the relationship between the Figure and the Window — distance, proximity, opening
- **In audio:** the motif appears as the rhythm change across the three movements (walking → breath → air entering)
- **In voice:** the motif appears as the voice's own arc — forward-attention in 1, held-attention in 2, opening in 3 (when the realization commits to a voiced narrator)

### Connection to other layers

- **Spline:** appears in the Motif signal of all three movements
- **Audio surface:** the rhythm shifts (footfall → breath → air) — the motif's phases emerge in the temporal substrate first
- **Visual surface:** the Figure-Window relationship per movement — the spatial moment of the motif within the audio substrate
- **Voice:** the voice's own attention-arc tracks the motif (if voiced)
- **Pass 6 (substitution):** reads the motif's recurring pattern (the three-phase forward-still-release) and substitutes into all three prompts as identity material. Pass 7 (outline) then weaves the motif's phasing as concrete trajectory material across movements, not as named reference
- **Pass 5 prompts:** receive per-movement scene material reflecting the motif's current phase; do not receive the motif's name or framework-internal phase vocabulary
- **Script:** the motif's three phases provide the script's structural rhythm — pacing, cue density, silence between cues
