**statement (plain)** — if all you claim about the world is that it has a certain abstract *structure* — some system of relations holding over a domain, with the nature of the relata left open — then that claim is **trivially true** of *any* collection of objects of the right size. Given a domain of suitable cardinality, set theory guarantees relations exist that realize the named structure. So a purely structural description fixes nothing beyond the *cardinality* of the domain: "only cardinality questions are open to discovery." The threat to structural realism: structure-and-nothing-but-structure conveys almost no contentful, world-directed information, so SR risks collapsing into the claim that only the observable (plus a cardinality count) is knowable.

**statement (formal)** — for a consistent theory whose observational consequences are true, the Ramsey sentence is satisfiable as a theorem of (second-order) logic / set theory provided the domain has the required cardinality; hence trivial realization. This is the Ramsey-sentence form of the objection developed by Demopoulos & Friedman.

**basis** — originates in Newman's 1928 review of Russell's *Analysis of Matter* [[source.newman-1928]] **[confirmed]**. Stated and assessed as the central objection to ESR / Ramsey-sentence structuralism in the SEP survey [[source.sep-structural-realism]] **[confirmed]**, and pressed within Psillos's broader critique that the structure/nature distinction cannot be sustained [[source.psillos-2001]] **[confirmed]**.

**limits / contested** — the objection is itself disputed: replies appeal to *natural* (non-arbitrary) relations, causal/modal constraints, or restricting the quantifiers, to block trivial realization. Carried as a live objection, not a verdict. **[confirmed]**
