# naturalistic-decision-making

Naturalistic decision making (NDM) studies how proficient people actually make decisions in field settings — characterized by time pressure, high stakes, inadequate information, ill-defined goals, dynamic conditions, and team coordination — rather than how they ought to decide under classical decision-analytic norms.

**origin** — the field crystallized around fieldwork on fireground commanders, the data showing experts rarely compared options but recognized situations and acted; the method that produced it was the critical decision method [[source.klein-calderwood-macgregor-1989]]. [confirmed]

**codification** — NDM was named and consolidated as a research program at the 1989 Dayton conference and the edited volumes that followed, with the 1997 volume mapping the young field's models and applications [[source.zsambok-klein-1997]]. [confirmed]

**door** — a compact retrospective survey of the field's origins, scope, and contributions, suitable as an entry point [[source.klein-2008]]. [confirmed]

**lineage** — NDM stands in deliberate contrast to the heuristics-and-biases tradition; the 2009 adversarial collaboration between Kahneman (biases) and Klein (NDM) mapped the boundary conditions under which expert intuition is and is not trustworthy, marking the field's reconciliation with classical decision research [[source.kahneman-klein-2009]]. [confirmed]

*grade [t] — origin and codification present (door is a survey by a field founder rather than an independent standard textbook).*
