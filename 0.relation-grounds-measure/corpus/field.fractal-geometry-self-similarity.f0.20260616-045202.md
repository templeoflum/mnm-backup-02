**origin** — F. Hausdorff, "Dimension und äußeres Maß," *Math. Ann.* 79 (1918), 157–179 — the dimension-dependent outer measure and a dimension taking arbitrary non-negative real values (today the Hausdorff dimension), the measure-theoretic root of fractal dimension. [[source.hausdorff-1918]] **[confirmed]**

**codification** — B. Mandelbrot, *The Fractal Geometry of Nature* (W. H. Freeman, 1982) — synthesizes the scattered "monster" sets (Cantor, Koch, Sierpiński, Weierstrass) into one geometry of nature, coins and defines "fractal," and makes the subject a field. Preceded by his *Les objets fractals* (Flammarion, 1975), where the word first appears. [[source.mandelbrot-1982]] [[source.mandelbrot-1975]] **[confirmed]**

**door** — K. Falconer, *Fractal Geometry: Mathematical Foundations and Applications* (Wiley, 1st ed. 1990) — the standard rigorous textbook (Hausdorff measure/dimension, IFS, dimension techniques, applications). [[source.falconer-1990]] **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — two strands converge: a *measure-theoretic* one (Hausdorff 1918 → Besicovitch's geometric measure theory 1920s–30s → Moran 1946 similarity dimension), and a *constructive* one (Hutchinson 1981 iterated function systems giving self-similar sets as unique attractors, popularized by Barnsley). Mandelbrot named and unified them; the field sits across measure theory, dynamical systems, and geometry.

*grade s — origin (Hausdorff 1918) + codification (Mandelbrot 1982/1975) + door (Falconer 1990), all web-confirmed.*
