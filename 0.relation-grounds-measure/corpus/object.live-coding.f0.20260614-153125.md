**statement (plain)** — live coding is a performance practice where the artist writes and rewrites a running program in front of an audience, and the program generates the music and visuals on the fly. The screen is projected so the audience sees the code change. The algorithm is not a hidden tool — it is the performance.

**statement** — the practice is codified by TOPLAP (founded February 2004 in Hamburg) and its "ManifestoDraft." The manifesto's central demands: "Obscurantism is dangerous. Show us your screens," and "Live coding is not about tools. Algorithms are thoughts. Chainsaws are tools." Code is to be "seen as well as heard, underlying algorithms viewed as well as their visual outcome." [[source.toplap-2004]] **[confirmed]**

**basis** — live coding is generative art [[object.generative-art]] made *in real time and in public*: the authored process [[object.process-as-artwork]] is edited while it runs, collapsing specification and execution into one continuous act. Two commitments are load-bearing: (1) **transparency** — performers' screens are projected, not hidden; (2) **improvisation** — the program is changed during the performance rather than pre-rendered. The practice anchors the "live" half of the field and gives rise to the Algorave (events where music/visuals are live-coded from algorithms). **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — young and practitioner-origin. TOPLAP traces to a meeting "in a smoky bar in Hamburg in 2004," and its manifesto is explicitly a *draft*, acknowledged as inspired by an earlier "Generative Manifesto" (2000) by Alex McLean and Alex Ward. Founding month (February 2004) and city (Hamburg) are confirmed by the field card and corroborating accounts; the Wikipedia entry confirms February 2004 but is less specific on city. Carried as settled-with-minor-source-variance.
