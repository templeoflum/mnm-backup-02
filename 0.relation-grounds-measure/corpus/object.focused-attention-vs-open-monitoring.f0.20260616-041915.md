**statement (plain)** — Meditation practices studied in contemplative science are commonly divided into two styles. *Focused attention* (FA) meditation involves voluntarily directing and sustaining attention on a chosen object (e.g. the breath), noticing distraction and returning attention to the object. *Open monitoring* (OM) meditation involves non-reactive, moment-to-moment monitoring of the contents of experience without selecting a single object. The two are endpoints/components rather than mutually exclusive categories; many practices combine them.

**basis** — Introduced by Lutz, Slagter, Dunne & Davidson as a framework for treating meditation as a family of attentional/emotional regulatory regimes, mapping each style to candidate cognitive and neural processes [[source.lutz-2008]] [confirmed]. The distinction is adopted as an organizing scheme in subsequent neuroscience reviews, where FA and OM are associated with partly distinct attention-network and monitoring-related activity [[source.tang-2015]] [confirmed].

**limits** — FA/OM is an analytic taxonomy, not a sharp partition: real practices blend styles and vary across traditions and expertise; reported neural correlates are findings-as-studied, with mechanisms still under investigation and methodological cautions noted [[source.tang-2015]] [confirmed].
