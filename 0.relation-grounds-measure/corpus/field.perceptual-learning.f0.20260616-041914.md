**origin** — J. J. Gibson & E. J. Gibson, "Perceptual learning: Differentiation or enrichment?" *Psychological Review* 62(1), 32–41 (1955) — frames the field's defining question and argues for differentiation: experience sharpens discrimination of information already in stimulation, rather than enriching percepts with added associations. [[source.gibson-gibson-1955]] **[confirmed]**

**codification** — E. J. Gibson, *Principles of Perceptual Learning and Development* (Appleton-Century-Crofts, 1969) — the systematic monograph that established perceptual learning as a developmental and theoretical program; differentiation theory worked out in full. [[source.gibson-1969]] **[confirmed]**

**door** — R. L. Goldstone, "Perceptual Learning," *Annual Review of Psychology* 49, 585–612 (1998) — standard review organizing the field around four mechanisms (attention weighting, imprinting, differentiation, unitization); Kellman & Garrigan (2009) and Kellman, Massey & Son (2010) carry the modern expertise/instruction line. [[source.goldstone-1998]] [[source.kellman-garrigan-2009]] **[confirmed]**

lineage — the 1955 Gibson & Gibson paper sets the differentiation-vs-enrichment frame and is co-authored; E. J. Gibson's 1969 monograph is the field's codifying text and the source most cited as the foundation. The neuroscience strand (specificity of learning, V1 plasticity) opens with Karni & Sagi (1991) [[source.karni-sagi-1991]]. **[confirmed]**

*grade [s] — origin + codification + door all present.*
