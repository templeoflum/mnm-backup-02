**statement (plain)** — Interoception is the sense of the body's internal physiological state: the reception and representation of afferent signals originating inside the organism (cardiac, respiratory, gastrointestinal, thermal, nociceptive, metabolic, muscular), and the perceptual and affective processing built on them. It is distinguished from exteroception (the external senses) and proprioception (musculoskeletal position/movement).

**basis** — Sherrington (1906) introduced the term as one of three receptor classes — exteroceptive, proprioceptive, interoceptive — partitioned by the *source* of stimulation; in his usage interoception denoted visceral receptors specifically [[source.sherrington-1906]] **[confirmed]**. Craig (2002) broadened the concept: interoception is a general afferent representation of the body's homeostatic condition, carried by a lamina I spinothalamocortical pathway to the insular cortex, and proposed as the basis of feelings [[source.craig-2002]]; Craig (2009) localized the integrative re-representation in the anterior insula [[source.craig-2009]] **[confirmed]**.

*Limits* — the boundary between "interoception" (Sherrington's narrow visceral sense) and Craig's broad homeostatic-afferent sense is not settled; some authors restrict the term to viscera, others include temperature and pain. Craig's specific lamina-I/insula model is influential but not universally accepted as the sole substrate.
