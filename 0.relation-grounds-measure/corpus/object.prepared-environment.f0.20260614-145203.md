**statement (plain)** — the prepared environment is a deliberately arranged space, scaled and stocked for the child, in which the child is free to move, choose work, and repeat it. The adult's first job is not to teach but to prepare and maintain this environment so that the child can educate themselves through it.

**statement** — an educational environment tailored to basic human characteristics, to the characteristics of children at a given developmental plane, and to each child's individual personality: child-sized furniture, ordered and accessible materials, freedom of movement and choice within limits, and a trained adult whose role is to prepare, observe, and link the child to the environment rather than to instruct directly. It supplies "such material as is necessary and sufficient for auto-education."

**basis** — the concept emerged empirically from Montessori's first [[Casa dei Bambini]] in the San Lorenzo district of Rome (opened 6 January 1907), where she observed that children flourished when free to choose hands-on work in a space designed for them. It is the spatial precondition for [[object.auto-education]], [[object.normalization]], and the exercise of [[object.sensitive-periods]]. **[confirmed]**

lineage — first articulated in *Il Metodo della Pedagogia Scientifica* (1909) [[source.montessori-1909]]; English in *The Montessori Method* (1912) [[source.montessori-1912]]; developed across her later works including *The Absorbent Mind* (1949) [[source.montessori-1949]]. Confirmed by the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy ("an environment which will allow the child to express himself spontaneously") and the Association Montessori Internationale (AMI). **[confirmed]**
