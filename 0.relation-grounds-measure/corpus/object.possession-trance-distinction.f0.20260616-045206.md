**statement (plain)** — Bourguignon separates two things that are often conflated. **Possession** is a *belief* — that a person can be entered or controlled by a spirit or other agent. **Trance** is an *altered state of consciousness* (a psychobiological condition). **Possession trance** is the case where an ASC is *interpreted by the culture as* possession. The three vary independently: a society may hold possession beliefs without trance (illness attributed to invading spirits), or have trance interpreted as a soul-journey rather than possession (non-possession trance).

**statement (distribution)** — in a sample of 488 societies, 437 (≈90%) had one or more institutionalized forms of altered states of consciousness; ≈251 interpreted some of these as spirit possession. Trance type co-varies with social complexity: possession trance is associated with greater agricultural dependence, social stratification, and political integration, while simpler (e.g. hunter-gatherer) societies tend toward non-possession trance / soul-journey forms.

**basis** — a cross-cultural coding study (Human Relations Area Files / ethnographic sample) directed by Bourguignon; the typology and the social-complexity correlation are its central empirical findings [[source.bourguignon-1973]]. The conceptual separation of possession-as-belief from trance-as-state is also used and elaborated in Lewis's comparative treatment of ecstatic religion [[source.lewis-1971]]. **[confirmed]**

limits — figures derive from coded secondary ethnographies of varying quality, and the "altered states of consciousness" construct has itself been critiqued as importing a Western psychobiological category onto culturally diverse practices; the distinction is widely adopted as an organizing scheme rather than a settled causal theory.
