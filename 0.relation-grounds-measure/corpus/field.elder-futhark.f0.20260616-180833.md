**origin** — the Elder Futhark, the earliest runic script (~2nd–8th c. CE) used across the Germanic world; 24 runes grouped in three *ættir* (families) of eight. [[source.rune-poems]] **[confirmed as attribution]**

**codification** — rune *meanings* derive from the three medieval rune poems (Old English ~9th–10th c.; Old Norwegian; Old Icelandic), which gloss the rune-names. [[source.rune-poems]] **[confirmed as attribution]**

**door** — the rune poems (Old English / Old Norwegian / Old Icelandic).

**lineage** — **the Elder Futhark rune-NAMES are Proto-Germanic reconstructions** (no contemporary name-list survives; reconstructed from the later Anglo-Saxon and Norse poems) — carried as a reconstruction note. Divinatory use is attested in tradition and revived in the modern era (Blum, 1982). Held as the tradition's system (members `status: doctrinal`).
