**citation (verbatim)** — Samuel Eilenberg and Saunders MacLane, "General Theory of Natural Equivalences," *Transactions of the American Mathematical Society*, Vol. 58, No. 2 (Sep. 1945), pp. 231–294. (Presented to the Society September 8, 1942; received by the editors May 15, 1945.) DOI 10.1090/S0002-9947-1945-0013131-6.

**what it contains** — the paper that founded category theory. It introduces, in order, the concepts of *natural transformation* (and "natural equivalence"), and to make those precise it defines *functor* and *category*. The motivating problem is to give a rigorous meaning to the informal "natural" isomorphisms occurring in algebra and topology (e.g. a finite-dimensional vector space and its double dual). Categories, functors, and natural transformations are the three primitive notions that all of the field is built on; this is their origin document.
