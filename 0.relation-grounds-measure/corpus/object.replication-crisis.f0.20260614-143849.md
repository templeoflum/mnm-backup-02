**statement (plain)** — the replication crisis is the realization, building through the 2010s, that a large share of published scientific results do not hold up when other researchers repeat the studies. Because being able to reproduce a result is central to science, these failures shook confidence in whole bodies of research — most visibly in psychology, but also medicine and other fields.

**statement** — the replication crisis is the methodological crisis arising from widespread failures to reproduce published scientific results, undermining the credibility of affected literatures. Its diagnosed causes — [[object.file-drawer-problem]] (publication bias), [[object.researcher-degrees-of-freedom]] (p-hacking), HARKing, small samples — are the same problems that [[object.preregistration]] and the [[object.registered-report]] were designed to counter. **[confirmed]**

**basis/origin** — the phrase was coined in the early 2010s amid growing awareness (precursors: failed social-priming and Bem ESP results; Amgen/Bayer biomedical non-replications; p-hacking demonstrations). The landmark empirical anchor is the Open Science Collaboration's Reproducibility Project: Psychology — of 100 studies (97 with originally significant effects), only 36% of replications yielded significant findings, with effect sizes roughly half the originals. **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — diffuse coinage (no single author; "early 2010s"), so carried as **[candidate, term origin]** for the name itself, while the key evidential event ([[source.osc-2015]], *Science* 349(6251):aac4716) is firmly attributed. [[source.ioannidis-2005]] is the influential precursor argument ("Why Most Published Research Findings Are False"). **[confirmed for key events]**
