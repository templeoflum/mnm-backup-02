**statement (plain)** — to find what something essentially is, imagine it changed in every way you can, and watch which features you can drop without the thing ceasing to be that kind of thing. The features that survive all variation are its essence (*eidos*); the rest are accidental. A triangle stays a triangle if you stretch a side, but not if you add a fourth — so "three sides" is essential, exact lengths are not.

**statement** — the *eidetic reduction* moves from the consciousness of individual, concrete instances to the intuition of their *eidos* (Greek: form, essence). Its technique is *free imaginative variation* (*freie Variation*): one runs through arbitrary variants of an object in imagination and reads off the invariant that holds across all of them as the necessary, essential structure — that without which an object of that kind is inconceivable. Husserl's essences are *immanent* in the phenomena, not a separate Platonic realm. **[confirmed]**

**statement (note)** — distinct from the phenomenological/transcendental reduction: the eidetic reduction targets *essences* (universal structures), while the epoché-led reduction brackets *existence* to reach constituting consciousness. The two are complementary methods, often run together.

**basis** — a methodological procedure for essence-intuition (*Wesensschau*), not a theorem; warranted by the invariance it discloses under variation rather than by deductive proof.

**lineage** — rooted in the doctrine of essences in *Logical Investigations* (1900–01) [[source.husserl-1900]]; developed as method in *Ideen I* (*Ideas I*, 1913) [[source.husserl-1913]] and elaborated in *Erfahrung und Urteil* (*Experience and Judgment*, posthumous, 1939). Builds on the bracketing move of [[object.epoche-phenomenological-reduction]]. The term "eidetic" derives from Greek *eidos* (form/shape/essence). **[confirmed]**
