Full citation — Jacob Bernoulli, *Ars Conjectandi* (Basel: Thurneysen Brothers, 1713), Part IV. Published posthumously, eight years after the author's death, edited by his nephew Nicolaus Bernoulli.

Role — two distinct contributions in one book. **Part II** introduces the [[object.bernoulli-numbers]] as the universal constants giving a uniform formula for sums of powers (Faulhaber's formula). **Part IV** gives the first statement and proof of a law of large numbers (the **weak law** for Bernoulli trials). Bernoulli's "golden theorem" (*theorema aureum*): the observed relative frequency of successes in n independent trials converges in probability to the true probability p as n grows, and he gave explicit bounds on how large n must be for a desired confidence ("moral certainty"). The name "law of large numbers" was coined later by Poisson (1837).

Access — historical; Latin original digitized; English translation by Edith Dudley Sylla, *The Art of Conjecturing* (Johns Hopkins, 2006). Attribution corroborated by MacTutor, Wolfram MathWorld, and Seneta's "A Tricentenary history of the Law of Large Numbers" (*Bernoulli* 19(4), 2013). **[confirmed]**
