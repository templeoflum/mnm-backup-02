**statement (plain)** — when people (a) believe God can speak through their senses, (b) have a proclivity for **absorption** — the capacity for total, immersive attention that heightens the felt reality of the attended object — and (c) train that capacity through disciplined prayer practice, they come to report sharper inner sensory imagery and occasional **sensory overrides** (hearing God's voice, seeing visions). Ecstatic experience is thus modeled as *learned, cultivated perception*, not pathology or mere belief.

**basis** — ethnography of the Vineyard charismatic-evangelical movement plus an experimental study: participants trained with daily audio prayer practice for a month, and absorption scores and reported spiritual experiences rose with practice [[source.luhrmann-2012]]. Absorption is operationalized via the Tellegen Absorption Scale. This learned-state, attention-driven mechanism parallels the wider field's treatment of trance phenomena as cultivated/entrained altered states — e.g. glossolalia analyzed as an artifact of an entered trance state [[source.goodman-1972]]. **[confirmed]**

limits — the experimental sample is small and Western/Christian-specific; absorption proclivity is partly a stable trait, so the causal weight of training versus selection is debated; generalization from a charismatic-Christian setting to possession trance broadly is by analogy, not direct demonstration. The insider claim that God is the source of the experience is bracketed — recorded as what practitioners report, not as established.

**sources** — [[source.luhrmann-2012]]; [[source.goodman-1972]]; Luhrmann, Nusbaum & Thisted, "The Absorption Hypothesis: Learning to Hear God in Evangelical Christianity," *American Anthropologist* 112(1), 2010. **[confirmed]**
