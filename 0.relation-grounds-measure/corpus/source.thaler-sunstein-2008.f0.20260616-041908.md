Richard H. Thaler and Cass R. Sunstein, *Nudge: Improving Decisions About Health, Wealth, and Happiness* (Yale University Press, New Haven, 2008).

Contains — the codifying text of applied behavioral economics. Defines a **choice architect** as anyone who organizes the context in which people make decisions, and a **nudge** as any aspect of the choice architecture that alters behavior predictably without forbidding options or significantly changing economic incentives. Argues for **libertarian paternalism** — institutions may steer choices toward welfare-improving outcomes while preserving freedom to opt out. Worked domains include retirement saving (automatic enrollment, Save More Tomorrow), organ donation defaults, health-plan and investment choice.
