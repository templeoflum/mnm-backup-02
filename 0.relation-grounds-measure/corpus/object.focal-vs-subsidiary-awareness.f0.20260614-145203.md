**statement (plain)** — when you use a skill you pay attention to two things in two different ways. You are *focally* aware of what you are doing (driving the nail, reading the meaning of a sentence) and *subsidiarily* aware of the particulars you rely on to do it (the feel of the hammer, the individual letters). If you switch your focus onto the particulars themselves — stare at your fingers while typing, or at single words while reading — the skilled performance falls apart. The clues work only while you attend from them, not to them.

**statement** — tacit knowing has a two-term *from-to* structure. The *proximal* (subsidiary) term comprises the clues, tools, or particulars we are aware of only as they bear on something else; the *distal* (focal) term is the comprehensive entity we attend to. We attend *from* the proximal *to* the distal. The relation is asymmetric and functional: shifting focal attention onto the subsidiary particulars dissolves the integration that gives them meaning.

**basis** — supported by Polanyi's worked cases: the stereoscope (we are focally aware of the single stereo-image, subsidiarily of the two flat pictures), the probe or stick whose tip we feel "out there" while the grip in the hand recedes to subsidiary awareness, the pianist who falters when attending to finger movements rather than the music. Each shows the destructive effect of focalizing the subsidiary, which is the distinction's central evidence. **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — developed by Polanyi in *Personal Knowledge* (1958) [[source.polanyi-1958]] and restated in *The Tacit Dimension* (1966) with the "proximal/distal" and "from-to" vocabulary [[source.polanyi-1966]]. The terms *focal* and *subsidiary awareness* are Polanyi's own. **[confirmed]**
