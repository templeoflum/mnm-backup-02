**citation (verbatim)** — Osser, Harry A., Peter F. Ostwald, Brian MacWhinney, and Robert L. Casey. "Glossolalic speech from a psycholinguistic perspective." *Journal of Psycholinguistic Research* 2, no. 1 (1973): 9–19. doi:10.1007/BF01067109.

**what it contains** — A psycholinguistic study of glossolalia produced by four speakers in an experimental setting. The authors compared acoustical patterns (signal waveform, fundamental frequency, amplitude changes) and computed vowel and consonant frequencies, comparing the glossolalic samples to General American English. The analysis identified two distinct forms: a "formulaic" form tending toward stereotypy and repetition, and an "innovative" form with more novelty and unpredictability in the chaining of speech-like elements.
