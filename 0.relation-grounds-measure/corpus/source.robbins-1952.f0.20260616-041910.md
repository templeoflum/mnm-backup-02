Herbert Robbins, "Some aspects of the sequential design of experiments," *Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society*, vol. 58, no. 5 (1952), pp. 527–535.

**citation** — poses the sequential design problem in which the size and composition of samples are functions of the observations themselves rather than fixed in advance. Frames the two-armed case (choosing repeatedly between two experiments of unknown success probabilities) that becomes the canonical multi-armed bandit problem, and introduces sampling rules with asymptotically optimal behavior.

**role** — origin / attribution for [[object.explore-exploit-tradeoff]] and [[object.multi-armed-bandit]].
