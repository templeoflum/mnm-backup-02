**statement (plain)** — a schedule of reinforcement is the rule that decides which responses get reinforced. Reinforcement can depend on the *number* of responses (ratio) or on the *passage of time* (interval), and each rule can be *fixed* (constant requirement) or *variable* (changing around an average). The four basic schedules — fixed-ratio, variable-ratio, fixed-interval, variable-interval — each produce a characteristic, reproducible pattern of responding (e.g., variable-ratio yields high steady rates resistant to extinction; fixed-interval yields a post-reinforcement pause then acceleration).

**statement** — given an operant response, the reinforcement contingency is defined by (a) whether delivery is contingent on a response count (ratio) or on the first response after an elapsed time (interval), and (b) whether the requirement is constant (fixed) or distributed about a mean (variable). The 2×2 {ratio, interval} × {fixed, variable}, plus continuous reinforcement (CRF, every response) and compound/concurrent schedules, partitions the basic schedule space. The schedule is itself an independent variable controlling response rate and temporal pattern. **[confirmed]**

**basis** — empirical: established across roughly 70,000 hours of cumulative-recorder data, primarily with pigeons, in Ferster & Skinner (1957); the schedule-specific response patterns are among the most replicated findings in behavioral science. **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — the ratio/interval distinction and the role of intermittent reinforcement were already present in [[source.skinner-1938]] *The Behavior of Organisms* (1938); the comprehensive, systematic treatment is [[source.ferster-skinner-1957]] *Schedules of Reinforcement* (1957). Builds on [[object.reinforcement-and-punishment]] and presupposes the [[object.operant-paradigm]] (free-operant chamber + cumulative recorder). **[confirmed]**
