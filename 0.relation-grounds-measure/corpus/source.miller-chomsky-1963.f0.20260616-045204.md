**verbatim citation** — Miller, George A., and Noam Chomsky. "Finitary Models of Language Users." In *Handbook of Mathematical Psychology*, vol. II, edited by R. Duncan Luce, Robert R. Bush, and Eugene Galanter, 419–491. New York and London: John Wiley & Sons, 1963.

**what it contains** — models the *user* of language (talker and listener) rather than the language itself. Argues that competence (the grammar) and performance (real-time parsing in a finite memory) come apart: a recursive grammar generates self-embedded structures of arbitrary depth, but multiply center-embedded sentences (e.g., "the rat the cat the dog chased killed ate the malt") become unacceptable to listeners after one or two embeddings, while equivalent right- or left-branching recursion stays easy. Locates the difficulty in finite processing resources (memory/stack limits), not in the grammar. The competence/performance split and the special difficulty of self-embedding originate here.
