**statement (plain)** — center-embedding is recursion that inserts a constituent into the *middle* of a constituent of the same kind, leaving material on both sides ("the rat the cat chased ran"). A single embedding is fine; two or more stacked center-embeddings ("the rat the cat the dog chased killed ate") stay grammatical by the rules but become nearly impossible to understand. This dissociates *competence* (what the grammar generates) from *performance* (what a user can process in finite memory). It is the classic demonstration that grammaticality and acceptability are not the same.

**statement (formal)** — a self-embedding (center-embedding) rule has the form A ⇒* αAβ with both α and β nonempty; iterating it produces nested dependencies x₁x₂…xₙ … yₙ…y₂y₁ whose matching requires unbounded stack memory, the hallmark of (at least) context-free, non-finite-state structure.

**basis** — Chomsky (1957) uses self-embedding to show natural language exceeds finite-state grammars. [[source.chomsky-1957]] **[confirmed]** Miller & Chomsky (1963) give the processing account: multiple center-embeddings are generated by the grammar yet defeat the finite-memory parser, while right/left-branching recursion does not. [[source.miller-chomsky-1963]] **[confirmed]** *Limits:* the exact depth limit (~1–2) and whether the difficulty is memory, interference, or experience-based is still studied; the qualitative competence/performance split is robust.
