Full citation — P. Lévy, *Calcul des probabilités* (Paris: Gauthier-Villars, 1925).

Role — Lévy's systematic development of the **central limit theorem** via characteristic functions, contemporaneous with and complementary to Lindeberg's 1922 work; the classical i.i.d. result is jointly named the **Lindeberg–Lévy theorem**. Lévy's continuity theorem (convergence of characteristic functions ⇔ convergence in distribution) is the analytic engine. He returned to limit theory in *Théorie de l'addition des variables aléatoires* (1937).

Access — historical; widely held. Attribution corroborated by Wikipedia ("Central limit theorem"), MacTutor ("Paul Lévy"), and standard texts. **[confirmed]**

Note — distinct from [[source.levy-1929]] (the Bulletin SMF paper on continued-fraction quotients / Gauss–Kuzmin); the CLT work is the 1925 treatise (and the 1937 *Addition*).
