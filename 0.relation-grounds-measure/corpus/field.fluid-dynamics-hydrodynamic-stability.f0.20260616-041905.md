**origin** — the governing dynamics: Navier (1822) derived equations of viscous fluid motion [[source.navier-1822]], given their definitive continuum form by Stokes (1845) — the Navier–Stokes equations [[source.stokes-1845]]. The stability question opens with Reynolds (1883), whose pipe-flow experiments established the dimensionless transition criterion [[source.reynolds-1883]], and Rayleigh (1880), whose inviscid theory gave the inflection-point criterion [[source.rayleigh-1880]]. **[confirmed]**

**codification** — the viscous linear theory: the Orr–Sommerfeld eigenvalue equation, derived independently by Orr (1907) [[source.orr-1907]] and Sommerfeld (1908) [[source.sommerfeld-1908]], framing laminar–turbulent transition as a hydrodynamic-stability problem. **[confirmed]**

**door** — Drazin & Reid, *Hydrodynamic Stability* (Cambridge, 1981) — the standard monograph [[source.drazin-reid-1981]]; Chandrasekhar (1961) and Schmid & Henningson (2001) alongside. **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — the founding is layered, not single: the equations of motion (Navier/Stokes, 1822–1845) precede and are distinct from the stability theory (Rayleigh 1880, Reynolds 1883, Orr–Sommerfeld 1907–1908); the Kelvin–Helmholtz shear-layer instability (Helmholtz 1868, Kelvin 1871) is a parallel inviscid root.

*grade [s] — origin, codification, and door all present.*
