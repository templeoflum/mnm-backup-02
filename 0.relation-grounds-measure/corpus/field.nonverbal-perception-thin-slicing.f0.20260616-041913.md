The field studies how observers form rapid, often accurate judgments of persons and situations from minimal nonverbal information, and how nonverbal cues can be objectively measured.

**origin.** The measurement program begins with the Facial Action Coding System, which gave nonverbal behavior an objective, anatomically grounded coding vocabulary [[source.ekman-friesen-1978]] **[confirmed as attribution]**, and with the Profile of Nonverbal Sensitivity (PONS), which established nonverbal-decoding accuracy as a measurable individual-difference variable [[source.rosenthal-1979-b]] **[confirmed as attribution]**.

**codification.** Ambady & Rosenthal's meta-analysis named and quantified "thin slices," showing that brief (often sub-30-second) samples of expressive behavior predict interpersonal outcomes at substantial effect sizes and that longer observation does not reliably help [[source.ambady-rosenthal-1992]] **[confirmed]**; Willis & Todorov pushed the result to the sub-second regime of static-face trait inference [[source.willis-todorov-2006]] **[confirmed]**.

**door.** No single standard textbook is identified here; the Ambady & Rosenthal meta-analysis and the PONS monograph serve as the field's canonical entry points pending a survey/textbook door.
