A. D. Craig, "How do you feel? Interoception: the sense of the physiological condition of the body," *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* **3**(8): 655–666 (2002). DOI 10.1038/nrn894. PMID 12154366.

Contains — the modern reframing of interoception. Craig argues that primates (notably humans) possess a distinct afferent system that represents *all* aspects of the physiological condition of the body — not only visceral but thermal, nociceptive, metabolic, muscular — carried by lamina I neurons of the spinal/trigeminal dorsal horn, relayed through a dedicated thalamocortical pathway to the dorsal posterior insula, and re-represented in the anterior insula. He proposes this homeostatic-afferent representation as the neural foundation for subjective feelings, emotion and a sense of the physical self.

Role — codification; the most-cited single statement of the contemporary concept of interoception.
