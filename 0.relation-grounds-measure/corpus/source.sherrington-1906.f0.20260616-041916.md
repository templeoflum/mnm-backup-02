Charles Scott Sherrington, *The Integrative Action of the Nervous System* (Yale University Press / Scribner's, 1906; from the Silliman Lectures delivered 1904). The foundational text of modern neurophysiology — Sherrington here also coined "synapse."

Contains — the receptor taxonomy that originates the term: sense organs partitioned into **exteroceptive** (responding to the external world: light, sound, touch), **proprioceptive** (deep receptors in muscles and joints), and **interoceptive** (receptors in the viscera / interior surfaces of the body). The distinction rests on the source of stimulation rather than the modality; only exteroception carries "projicience" (referral to external space).

Role — origin / coinage of "interoception"; standing reference for the receptive-field partition that the field still uses.

Access — in print (reissued editions, incl. Yale 1947 with new foreword); widely excerpted; the receptor-class passage is the canonical citation for the term's origin. **[confirmed]**
