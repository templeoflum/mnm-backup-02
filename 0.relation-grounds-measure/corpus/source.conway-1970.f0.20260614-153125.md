**citation** — John Horton Conway, the "Game of Life" cellular automaton, devised 1970. Conway did not publish it himself in a journal of his own; it first reached the public through Martin Gardner's "Mathematical Games" column, *Scientific American* 223(4), October 1970 [[source.gardner-1970]].

**role** — original work / origin of the keystone object [[object.cellular-automaton-emergence]]. Game of Life simplifies the self-replicating cellular automata of von Neumann and Ulam (1940s) into a two-state, eight-neighbour rule on a square grid, and is Turing-complete.

**access** — rules and history widely documented (LifeWiki, Wikipedia, Scholarpedia); the primary publication is Gardner's column.
