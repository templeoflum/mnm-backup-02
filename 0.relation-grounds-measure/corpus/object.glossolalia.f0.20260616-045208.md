**statement (plain)** — Glossolalia ("speaking in tongues") is the production of fluent, phonologically structured strings of syllables that sound like speech and are experienced by the speaker as language, but that do not encode words, grammar, or systematic meaning. As a scientific object it names the observable vocal behavior, independent of the religious significance practitioners ascribe to it.

**basis** — Samarin, analyzing a five-year recorded corpus of Pentecostal glossolalia, defines it as "meaningless but phonologically structured human utterance, believed by the speaker to be a real language but bearing no systematic resemblance to any natural language, living or dead" [[source.samarin-1972]] [confirmed]. Goodman's cross-cultural fieldwork independently characterizes the same behavior phonologically and finds it recurs with structural regularity across unrelated cultures and traditions [[source.goodman-1972]] [confirmed].

**limits** — The term is defined operationally by sound structure and the absence of decodable content; it brackets, rather than resolves, what the behavior means to its practitioners. The religious framing held within Pentecostal and other traditions is recorded as held, not asserted. Boundary cases (e.g. xenoglossy claims, scripted vs. spontaneous utterance) sit at the edge of the operational definition.
