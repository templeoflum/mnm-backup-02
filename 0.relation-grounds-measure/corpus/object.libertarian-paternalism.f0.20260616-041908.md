**statement (plain)** — it is both possible and legitimate for private and public institutions to influence people's choices in directions that improve their welfare (the *paternalist* element) while keeping the choices free and low-cost to reverse — no option forbidden, opting out always easy (the *libertarian* element). The argument rests on the no-neutral-presentation point: since some choice architecture is unavoidable, designing it toward the chooser's own judged interest is preferable to designing it arbitrarily.

**basis** — methodological/normative. Support: stated and named in [[source.thaler-sunstein-2003]] "Libertarian Paternalism" (*AER*) and elaborated as the governing principle of [[source.thaler-sunstein-2008]] *Nudge*. Limits: contested in the literature — critics question who decides what counts as "welfare-improving," whether nudges respect autonomy or manipulate it, the risk of paternalist overreach, and whether "freedom to opt out" is real when inertia is precisely what the design exploits. The principle is a normative stance with empirical backing, not a proven result. **[confirmed]**
