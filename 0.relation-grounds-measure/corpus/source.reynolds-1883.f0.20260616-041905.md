Full citation — O. Reynolds, "An experimental investigation of the circumstances which determine whether the motion of water shall be direct or sinuous, and of the law of resistance in parallel channels," *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society of London*, vol. 174 (1883), pp. 935–982. DOI: 10.1098/rstl.1883.0029.

Contains — the classic dye-streak experiments on water forced through a straight glass tube, distinguishing "direct" (laminar) from "sinuous" (turbulent) motion, and the demonstration that transition is governed by a single dimensionless group (later named the **Reynolds number**) combining velocity, length scale, density and viscosity. Foundational both for the Reynolds number and for the empirical basis of the laminar–turbulent transition problem.

Access — Royal Society (royalsocietypublishing.org); open scans widely available (UCL, Manchester archives). Citation detail confirmed against the Royal Society record and the *Phil. Trans. A* 125th-anniversary introduction (rsta.2008.0217). **[confirmed]**
