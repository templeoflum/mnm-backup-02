Karlene H. Roberts, "Some Characteristics of One Type of High Reliability Organization," *Organization Science* 1, no. 2 (1990): 160–176 — an early, widely cited statement of the **high reliability organization (HRO)** research program of the UC Berkeley group (Roberts, Todd La Porte, Gene Rochlin), which asked the inverse of Perrow's question: not why hazardous systems fail, but how some hazardous, complex, tightly coupled organizations operate for long periods nearly error-free.

Contains — defines HROs as organizations that could do great harm to themselves and their environment yet sustain excellent operating records; takes the nuclear-powered aircraft carrier as the type case; engages directly with Perrow's (1984) risk components and Shrivastava's antecedents to catastrophe, arguing organizational processes (redundancy, continuous training, decision migration, a culture of reliability) can hold accidents off.

Lineage note — the program's earliest field report is G. I. Rochlin, T. R. La Porte, and K. H. Roberts, "The Self-Designing High-Reliability Organization: Aircraft Carrier Flight Operations at Sea," *Naval War College Review* 40, no. 4 (1987). The three Berkeley study sites were carrier flight operations, FAA air traffic control, and the Pacific Gas & Electric grid.
