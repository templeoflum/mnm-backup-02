**statement (plain)** — Neurophenomenology is a research method in which rigorous, trained first-person descriptions of lived experience are gathered alongside third-person neural/behavioral data, and the two are used as *mutual constraints*: phenomenological categories guide the analysis of neural data, and neural findings refine the first-person inquiry. It is proposed as a working response to the "hard problem" of consciousness — not a metaphysical solution but a disciplined pragmatics for studying experience.

**basis** — Set out by Varela, situating the method in the lineage of Husserlian phenomenology and arguing that first-person experience is an irreducible field requiring explicit method rather than a "theoretical fix" [[source.varela-1996]] [confirmed]. Its conceptual ground — the enactive, embodied dialogue between cognitive science and disciplined experience (including contemplative training) — is developed in *The Embodied Mind* [[source.varela-thompson-rosch-1991]] [confirmed].

**limits** — Neurophenomenology is a methodological program, not a settled result: the quality of first-person data depends on subjects' training, and the mutual-constraint relation is interpretive rather than a formal reduction. It is reported here as a studied method, status established within contemplative science.
