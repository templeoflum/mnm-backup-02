**statement (plain)** — the kilogram is no longer the mass of a metal cylinder kept in Paris. Since 2019 it is defined by fixing the Planck constant to an exact value; combined with the already-fixed second and metre, this pins down what one kilogram is. Any well-equipped lab can now reproduce the kilogram from physics rather than by comparing against a single object.

**statement (precise)** — "The kilogram, symbol kg, is the SI unit of mass. It is defined by taking the fixed numerical value of the Planck constant h to be 6.626 070 15 × 10⁻³⁴ when expressed in the unit J·s, which is equal to kg·m²·s⁻¹, where the metre and the second are defined in terms of c and ΔνCs." **[confirmed as attribution]** (verbatim from the *SI Brochure*, 9th ed.)

**status** — in force since 20 May 2019.

**lineage** — replaces the International Prototype of the Kilogram (IPK), the platinum-iridium artifact that had defined the kilogram since the 1889 CGPM; the IPK was retired because its mass drifted relative to its official copies. The new definition was adopted by the 26th CGPM (Resolution 1, 2018) as part of the [[object.si-defining-constants]] and took effect 20 May 2019. Practical realization (the *mise en pratique*) uses two independent routes: the **Kibble balance** (watt balance), which relates mechanical to electrical power, and the **X-ray crystal density / Avogadro** method using a near-perfect silicon-28 sphere. **[confirmed]**

**sources** — [[source.si-brochure-2019]] (definition, mise en pratique); [[source.nist-sp330-2019]] (US edition, Kibble balance realization).
