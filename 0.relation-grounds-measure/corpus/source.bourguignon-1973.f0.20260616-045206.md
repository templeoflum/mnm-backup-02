Verbatim citation: E. Bourguignon (ed.), *Religion, Altered States of Consciousness, and Social Change*, Columbus, OH: Ohio State University Press, 1973.

Contains: the synthesizing report of the Cross-Cultural Study of Dissociational States, which Bourguignon directed (1963–1968). Draws on a sample of 488 societies coded for institutionalized altered states of consciousness (ASC); finds 437 of 488 (≈90%) have one or more institutionalized forms of ASC, and that these are interpreted as spirit possession in a subset (≈251). Develops the analytic split between **possession** (a belief that a person can be entered/controlled by a spirit) and **possession trance** (an ASC interpreted as possession), and relates the distribution of trance type to social complexity (possession trance correlating with greater agricultural dependence, stratification, and political integration). Five embedded case studies relate possession trance to social change across tribal, peasant, proletarian, and middle-class settings.

Access: Internet Archive (archive.org/details/religionaltereds0000bour); ISBN 9780814201671. Bourguignon's companion overview "Possession and Trance" appears in the *Encyclopedia of Medical Anthropology* (Springer) and her monograph *Possession* (1976).
