**statement (plain)** — when an action in a situation is followed by something satisfying, that action becomes more likely the next time the situation recurs; when it is followed by discomfort, it becomes less likely. Consequences select behavior. This is the root principle from which reinforcement is later derived.

**statement** — Thorndike's 1911 wording: "Of several responses made to the same situation, those which are accompanied or closely followed by satisfaction to the animal will, other things being equal, be more firmly connected with the situation, so that, when it recurs, they will be more likely to recur; those which are accompanied or closely followed by discomfort to the animal will, other things being equal, have their connections with that situation weakened, so that, when it recurs, they will be less likely to occur. The greater the satisfaction or discomfort, the greater the strengthening or weakening of the bond." **[confirmed]**

**basis** — empirical, from puzzle-box experiments with cats, dogs, and chicks: escape latency decreased gradually across trials (trial-and-error learning, not insight), evidence that successful responses were progressively stamped in. **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — first reported in the 1898 puzzle-box monograph ("Animal Intelligence: An Experimental Study of the Associative Processes in Animals," *Psychological Review* Monograph Supplement), which described the method and the connection of "a certain act with a certain situation and resultant pleasure" but did not yet name a law. The principle was named and given its definitive statement as the "Law of Effect" in [[source.thorndike-1898]] Thorndike's 1911 book *Animal Intelligence: Experimental Studies*. Thorndike later (1932) revised it, retaining the strengthening half while weakening his claim about the symmetric punishing half. B. F. Skinner replaced Thorndike's mentalistic terms ("satisfaction," "discomfort") with the observable functional terms reinforcement and punishment, carrying the law forward as the [[field.operant-conditioning]] framework. **[confirmed]**

**sources** — [[source.thorndike-1898]] (1898 monograph; 1911 *Animal Intelligence* for the named law).
