# naturalistic-decision-making

**statement (plain):** NDM is a framework for studying how proficient decision makers actually operate in real-world settings. Its defining commitment is to examine decisions made under field conditions — time pressure, high stakes, inadequate or ambiguous information, ill-structured problems, shifting goals, and dynamic, team-embedded environments — rather than the stylized gambles of laboratory decision research. Its characteristic finding is that experts lean on situation recognition and experience rather than on systematic generation and comparison of options.

**basis (method / support):** The framework was consolidated as a named program through the conference volumes following the 1989 Dayton meeting; the 1997 volume maps its early models and applications across aviation, health care, military command, nuclear power, and manufacturing [[source.zsambok-klein-1997]]. Klein's retrospective characterizes the same defining task features and methods (cognitive task analysis) and traces the emergence in the 1980s [[source.klein-2008]]. The book-length presentation grounds the framework in field studies of firefighters, nurses, pilots, and commanders [[source.klein-1998]]. [confirmed]

**limits:** NDM is descriptive rather than normative; it does not by itself specify when expert judgment should be trusted. The 2009 boundary-conditions work (see [[object.expert-intuition]]) supplies that constraint. Critics note reliance on retrospective self-report and difficulty isolating which cues drive recognition.
