**statement (plain)** — a perceptual learning module (PLM) is a structured instructional technology that applies perceptual learning to teaching. Learners complete many short trials — typically rapid classification or mapping tasks across varied instances and representations, with immediate feedback and adaptive sequencing — so that the perceptual system learns to pick up the relevant patterns and structure automatically. The goal is improved information *extraction* (discovery) and *fluency* (speed, automaticity, low attentional cost), not just declarative knowledge.

**basis** — Kellman & Garrigan (2009) lay out the rationale: expertise rests on learned information extraction, and PLMs are designed to produce both discovery and fluency effects [[source.kellman-garrigan-2009]] **[confirmed]**. Kellman, Massey & Son (2010) describe PLM design and report classroom studies in middle/high-school mathematics, finding gains in pattern recognition, mapping across representations, and fluency [[source.kellman-massey-son-2010]] **[confirmed]**. **Limits:** evidence is strongest in well-structured domains (mathematics, medical-image reading, aviation); transfer beyond trained categories and long-term retention vary, and PLMs supplement rather than replace conceptual instruction.
