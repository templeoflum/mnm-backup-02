**statement (plain)** — The dual-network model holds that creative cognition emerges from the interaction of two large-scale brain systems: the **default mode network (DMN)**, associated with spontaneous, self-generated, associative thought (candidate-idea *generation*), and the **executive control network (ECN)**, associated with goal-directed control (idea *evaluation* and constraint to task goals). Creativity is characterized not by one network but by their coupling and the flexible switching between generation and evaluation.

**basis** — Synthesized by Beaty et al. from resting-state and task fMRI spanning divergent thinking and domain-specific creative production (poetry, musical improvisation, visual art) [[source.beaty-2016]] [confirmed]; presented within the cognitive-neuroscience strand of Sawyer's survey [[source.sawyer-2012]] [confirmed].

**limits** — The account is a correlational, network-level framework built on fMRI connectivity, not a causal mechanism; network labels (DMN/ECN) are coarse, generation-vs-evaluation mapping is a simplification, and effect patterns vary by task and domain [[source.beaty-2016]] [confirmed].
