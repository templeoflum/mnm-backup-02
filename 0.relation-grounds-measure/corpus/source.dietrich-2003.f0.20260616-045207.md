Verbatim citation: A. Dietrich, "Functional neuroanatomy of altered states of consciousness: The transient hypofrontality hypothesis," *Consciousness and Cognition*, vol. 12, no. 2, pp. 231–256, 2003.

Contains: the transient hypofrontality hypothesis — the proposal that altered states of consciousness (dreaming, endurance running / runner's high, meditation, daydreaming, hypnosis, and various drug-induced states) are unified by a transient, reversible deregulation (downregulation) of prefrontal cortex. Argues the phenomenological signature of each state follows from which prefrontal/frontal circuits are differentially attenuated, while higher-order executive functions (working memory, abstract reasoning, sustained attention, self-reflective awareness, temporal integration) localize to the prefrontal cortex and are selectively impaired.
