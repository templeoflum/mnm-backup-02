**statement (plain)** — scaffolding is the support a tutor gives a learner so they can solve a task or reach a goal that would be beyond their own unaided effort. The tutor "controls" the parts of the task the learner can't yet manage, leaving the learner to handle only the parts within reach — and that support is meant to be removed as the learner takes over.

**statement** — Wood, Bruner & Ross (1976) introduced "scaffolding" as "a process that enables a child or novice to solve a task or achieve a goal that would be beyond his unassisted efforts." Their study of an adult tutoring 3–5-year-olds on a block-construction task identified six tutoring functions: (1) recruitment, (2) reduction in degrees of freedom, (3) direction maintenance, (4) marking critical features, (5) frustration control, and (6) demonstration / modelling. **[confirmed]**

**basis** — the metaphor casts adult support as a temporary structure erected around the learner's emerging competence: it does for the learner what the learner cannot yet do, is adjusted to the learner's current grasp, and is dismantled as competence grows. The six functions describe what an effective tutor actually does within that support; they are not strictly sequential and may operate simultaneously.

**lineage** — the *term* "scaffolding" was coined by David Wood, Jerome S. Bruner & Gail Ross in "The Role of Tutoring in Problem Solving," *J. Child Psychology and Psychiatry* 17(2) (1976):89–100 [[source.wood-bruner-ross-1976]]. **Distinction (carried as lineage):** Vygotsky himself never used the term — scaffolding was developed by Bruner, Wood, and Ross and was *later* aligned with the [[object.zone-of-proximal-development]]; the pairing of "scaffolding within the ZPD" is a subsequent synthesis, not Vygotsky's own. **[confirmed]**

**sources** — definition, the six functions, and the coinage confirmed across the 1976 primary paper [[source.wood-bruner-ross-1976]] and independent secondary sources (Wikipedia "Zone of proximal development," which states Vygotsky never used the term; structural-learning.com teacher's guide; Margulieux article summary of Wood et al. 1976).
