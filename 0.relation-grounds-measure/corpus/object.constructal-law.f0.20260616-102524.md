**statement (plain)** — Bejan's constructal law holds that for a flow system to survive (to keep flowing under its constraints), its shape and structure must change over time so that whatever flows through it meets less and less resistance. Applied to the problem of channelling heat out of a volume with a fixed amount of high-conductivity material, optimizing block-by-block from small to large produces a branching, tree-shaped network — and Bejan argues the same tendency explains why river basins, lungs, blood vessels, lightning, and even traffic and social organization take tree-like form.

**statement (formula)** — there is no single equation; the law is a direction of evolution applied through constrained optimization. In the founding heat-conduction problem, each assembly minimizes the global thermal resistance between a volume generating heat and a sink, subject to a fixed high-conductivity material fraction; optimizing the aspect ratio and channel width at each scale and stacking the optimized blocks yields the tree network with every geometric detail fixed theoretically. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — the construction and the tree-network result are given in Bejan's founding paper [[source.bejan-1997]] [confirmed]; the general statement and its wide application are developed in *Design with Constructal Theory* [[source.bejan-lorente-2008]] [confirmed]. **Limits / lineage:** Bejan presents it as a **law of physics** of universal scope, and that universal status is **contested** — critics hold it is a unifying design heuristic or a restatement of optimization principles rather than an independent physical law, and that its explanatory claims across biology and society are post-hoc. Recorded here as an influential, well-attested principle with its "law" status carried as Bejan's claim, not asserted as settled physics. **[confirmed as attribution]**
