**citation (verbatim)** — Goodman, Felicitas D. *Speaking in Tongues: A Cross-Cultural Study of Glossolalia*. Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1972.

**what it contains** — The first cross-cultural analysis of glossolalia. Goodman, trained in linguistics and anthropology, conducted fieldwork with apostolic congregations in Mexico City, with Maya in the Yucatán, and with a congregation in Hammond, Indiana, preserving observations on sound recordings and film, and compared these with recordings from non-Christian ritual settings (e.g., Africa, Borneo, Indonesia, Japan). Her phonological and suprasegmental analysis found surprising cross-cultural agreement in the structure of the utterances. She argues that glossolalists speak as they do because their speech behavior is modified in a particular mental state often termed trance — that the striking accent, intonation, and phonetic features express bodily changes during trance rather than constituting any natural language.
