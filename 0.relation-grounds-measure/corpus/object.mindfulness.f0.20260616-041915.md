**statement (plain)** — As operationalized in contemplative science, mindfulness is a two-component psychological construct: (1) *self-regulation of attention* maintained on immediate experience, and (2) an *orientation* toward that experience characterized by curiosity, openness, and acceptance. It denotes a present-centered, non-judgmental awareness, distinct from (though derived from) the broader Buddhist notion of *sati*.

**basis** — The consensus operational definition is Bishop et al.'s two-component model, which specifies each component in terms of behaviors, experiential manifestations, and psychological processes to support measurement [[source.bishop-2004]] [confirmed]. Mindfulness so defined is the construct targeted by mindfulness-based interventions and reviewed in the neuroscience literature, where its trainable effects are organized under attention control, emotion regulation, and self-awareness [[source.tang-2015]] [confirmed].

**limits** — Definitions of mindfulness vary across researchers and instruments; the construct's boundaries, measurement (largely self-report), and relation to traditional contemplative meanings remain debated. Reported as a studied construct, status established — not a settled or unitary entity [[source.bishop-2004]] [confirmed].
