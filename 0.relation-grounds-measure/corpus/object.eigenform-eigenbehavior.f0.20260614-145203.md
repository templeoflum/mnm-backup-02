**statement (plain)** — what we call an "object" is not a thing sitting out there independent of us — it is the stable pattern that an observer's repeated interaction with the world keeps returning to. Look, act, look again; where that loop settles down to something that no longer changes, that stability *is* the object. Von Foerster's slogan: "objects are tokens for eigenbehaviors."

**statement** — an eigenform is a fixed point of a recursive operation: if F is the transformation an observing system applies, an eigenform J satisfies J = F(J), so that applying the process again returns the same form. Objects are eigenforms — tokens standing for the eigenbehaviors (stable, recurrent behaviors) generated when an observer recursively interacts with its environment. Stability under recursion, not external substance, constitutes objecthood.

**basis** — the concept follows from the [[object.observer-included-in-the-system]] principle: if the observer is inside the loop, then objects must be products of the observer's recursive activity rather than givens. Von Foerster used the image of an infinitely nested structure (a box within a box within a box…) whose form is unchanged by adding one more nesting — a fixed point of the nesting operation. Kauffman formalized this as the mathematics of fixed points (J = F(J)), connecting it to self-reference and, by analogy, to the eigenvector in quantum mechanics (where observation selects stable states). The upshot: "map and territory" become conjoined — the object is the stability of the process, not a thing behind it.

**lineage** — introduced by Heinz von Foerster in "Objects: Tokens for (Eigen-)Behaviors" (1976; French version in the 1977 Piaget festschrift *Hommage à Jean Piaget*; reprinted in *Observing Systems*, 1981, and *Understanding Understanding*, Springer, 2003) [[source.von-foerster-1976]], [[source.von-foerster-2003]]. Developed and mathematically formalized by Louis H. Kauffman in "Eigenforms — Objects as Tokens for Eigenbehaviors" (*Cybernetics and Human Knowing*, 2003) [[source.kauffman-2003]].
