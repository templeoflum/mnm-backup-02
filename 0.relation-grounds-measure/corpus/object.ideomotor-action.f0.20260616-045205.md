**statement (plain)** — merely thinking of or expecting a movement tends to produce that movement, in small involuntary muscle action, without any conscious decision to move. Because the mover does not feel themselves willing it, the motion seems to come from outside — which is why ideomotor action underlies table-turning, the divining rod, the pendulum, and Ouija-board pointing.

**statement (theoretical)** — Carpenter's **ideo-motor principle**: an idea, if not inhibited, passes into motor expression by the same reflex economy as sensation passes into movement — "the influence of suggestion in modifying and directing muscular movement, independently of volition" [[source.carpenter-1852]]. Wegner's reframing: such cases expose that the *experience* of conscious will is an inference (apparent mental causation), not the cause of action; when a movement's true antecedents are unconscious, the will-experience detaches and the act feels automatic or externally authored [[source.wegner-2002]].

**basis (empirical / method)** — *support*: the effect is robustly demonstrable — controlled studies of pendulum and table movement, blinding experiments that abolish "messages" when the operator cannot know the answer, and Wegner & Wheatley's (1999) priority/consistency/exclusivity manipulations of the will-experience. It is the consensus debunking mechanism for table-turning and dowsing. *limits*: ideomotor action explains the *movement and its felt involuntariness*; it does not by itself adjudicate the strong claim that all conscious will is illusory — that further inference (Wegner) is a contested philosophical position.

**lineage** — Carpenter (1852) coins the principle within a naturalizing program against spiritualism; James and Lotze develop ideomotor accounts of voluntary action; Wegner (2002) folds it into a general theory of apparent mental causation — whose "illusion" reading is disputed (objections that it refutes only an inflated notion of will). Supplies the motor mechanism used by automatism [[object.automatism]].
