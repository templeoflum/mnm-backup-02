# critical-decision-method

**statement (plain):** The Critical Decision Method (CDM) is a retrospective interview technique for eliciting expert knowledge. An interviewer selects a single non-routine, challenging incident the expert handled, has them recount it, and then makes several structured sweeps back through the timeline using cognitive probes — for critical cues, the basis of perceptual and conceptual discriminations, typicality judgments, expectancies, goals, options considered, and decision points. The technique is a variant of Flanagan's critical incident technique extended specifically to surface the cognitive basis of skilled performance in naturalistic, time-pressured, information-rich, dynamic settings.

**basis (method / support):** CDM was introduced and specified — including its probes and its applications to fireground commanders, tank platoon leaders, engineers, paramedics, and programmers — in the founding methodological paper [[source.klein-calderwood-macgregor-1989]]. Klein's retrospective identifies cognitive task analysis, of which CDM is the canonical NDM instance, as a core method of the field [[source.klein-2008]]. [confirmed]

**limits:** As a retrospective self-report method, CDM is subject to memory distortion, post-hoc rationalization, and selection of incidents the expert chooses to recount; inter-analyst reliability of the coded knowledge depends on training and structured analysis. It elicits what experts can articulate, which may understate genuinely tacit components of skill.
