Full citation — T. Bayes, "An Essay towards solving a Problem in the Doctrine of Chances," communicated by Richard Price, *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society of London* **53** (1763), 370–418. Read to the Royal Society **23 December 1763**.

Role — first published statement of the inverse-probability result now called Bayes's theorem, with Bayes's solution to the "inverse" problem (inferring an unknown success probability from observed outcomes, using what is now called a uniform prior). Bayes wrote the essay in the late 1740s but never published it; after his death (1761) his papers passed to **Richard Price**, who edited the manuscript, added an introduction and appendix, and communicated it to the Royal Society. Price's editorial contribution is substantial enough that the work is sometimes cited as Bayes–Price.

Access — historical; Philosophical Transactions digitized (Royal Society; JSTOR). Attribution, the 23 December 1763 reading, and Price's role corroborated by Wikipedia ("Bayes' theorem"), MacTutor, and the historyofinformation.com record. **[confirmed]**
