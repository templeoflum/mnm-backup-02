# field.queueing-theory

Queueing theory studies systems in which arriving demand waits for and contends over limited servers, deriving the statistics of queue length, waiting time, and server utilization from the stochastic processes of arrival and service.

**Origin:** Founded by A. K. Erlang, who applied probability to telephone traffic — showing Poisson arrivals (1909) and deriving the loss and delay formulae for exchanges (1917). [[source.erlang-1909]] [[source.erlang-1917]] [confirmed]

**Codification:** Kendall (1953) recast the subject in modern stochastic-process terms, introducing the embedded-Markov-chain method and the A/B/c notation that standardized how models are named; Little (1961) supplied the distribution-free theorem L = λW that ties mean queue length to mean delay across all such models. [[source.kendall-1953]] [[source.little-1961]] [confirmed]

**Door:** Gross, Shortle, Thompson & Harris, *Fundamentals of Queueing Theory* — the standard graduate text and reference. [[source.gross-harris]] [confirmed]

*Grade [s] — origin (Erlang 1909/1917), codification (Kendall 1953; Little 1961), and door (Gross & Harris) all present and cited.*
