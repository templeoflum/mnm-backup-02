**statement (plain)** — how does the ear know a sound's pitch? Two answers. **Place theory**: each frequency vibrates a particular spot on the basilar membrane, so pitch = which spot fires (a frequency-to-position map, like a piano keyboard inside the cochlea). **Temporal (frequency) theory**: nerves fire in step with the sound wave, so pitch = the *rate* of firing. Each fails alone — place struggles at low frequencies and with the missing fundamental; pure timing fails at high frequencies because single nerves cannot fire fast enough. The accepted picture combines them: timing dominates below ~1 kHz, place above ~5 kHz.

**statement** — pitch is encoded by two mechanisms. **Place (tonotopic) coding**: the basilar membrane's mechanical tuning maps frequency to a position of peak excitation; pitch reads from the excited place. **Temporal coding**: auditory-nerve spikes phase-lock to the stimulus waveform, carrying frequency as inter-spike timing; the **volley principle** lets fiber populations represent frequencies above any single fiber's firing rate by alternating. The combined **place–volley** account codes low pitches temporally and high pitches by rate-place. **[confirmed]**

**basis** — place theory is grounded in cochlear tonotopy (Helmholtz's resonance hypothesis, later confirmed by von Békésy's traveling-wave observations). Temporal coding is grounded in phase-locking (reliable below ~1–4 kHz, fading above) and the volley principle. The decisive evidence that place alone is insufficient is the [[object.missing-fundamental]] (pitch with no f0 energy). Frequency limits (~1 kHz, ~5 kHz crossovers) are empirical and approximate; the model is a synthesis, not a closed law (formalized: false). **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — place/resonance theory Hermann von Helmholtz (1860s; *On the Sensations of Tone*) [[source.helmholtz-1863]]; frequency ("telephone") theory Ernest Rutherford (1886); the **volley principle** Ernest Glen Wever & Charles Bray (1937) [[source.wever-bray-1937]], resolving the single-fiber rate ceiling. Békésy's mid-20th-c. work confirmed the traveling wave underlying place. The synthesis is the standard modern view in [[source.fastl-zwicker]]. **[confirmed]**
