**statement (plain)** — "If, as we confront some task, a part of the world functions as a process which, were it done in the head, we would have no hesitation in recognizing as part of the cognitive process, then that part of the world is … part of the cognitive process." A functional criterion: location inside or outside the skull is not what makes a process cognitive. [[source.clark-chalmers-1998]] **[confirmed]**

**basis (argument / support)** — The principle is the load-bearing premise of the extended mind argument; stated in Clark & Chalmers (1998) and summarized as such in the SEP "Embodied Cognition" survey [[source.sep-embodied-cognition]]. **[confirmed]** Limits: critics (Adams & Aizawa) argue parity is too permissive without an independent "mark of the cognitive"; defenders reply that parity is about functional role, not surface similarity. A principle, not a proof.
