**statement (plain)** — Active externalism: when an external resource is suitably coupled to an agent and plays the right functional role, it is a constituent of the cognitive process — and, for standing states like belief, of the mind itself. Otto, who reliably consults his notebook, has dispositional beliefs stored in the notebook just as Inga has them in biological memory. [[source.clark-chalmers-1998]] **[confirmed]**

**basis (argument / support)** — Argued from the parity principle [[object.parity-principle]] plus coupling and functional-role considerations, illustrated by the Otto thought experiment [[source.clark-chalmers-1998]]; developed at book length with responses to objections in Clark (2008) [[source.clark-2008]]. **[confirmed]** Limits: contested. The "cognitive bloat" worry and Adams & Aizawa's "mark of the cognitive" critique argue external states lack intrinsic content / the right kind of process; Rupert defends embedded (not extended) cognition. The thesis is a live philosophical position, not a settled result.
