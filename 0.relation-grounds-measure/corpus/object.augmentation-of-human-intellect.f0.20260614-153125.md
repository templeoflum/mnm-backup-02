**statement (plain)** — augmenting human intellect is the program of increasing a person's capacity to approach a complex problem, understand it to suit their needs, and reach solutions — by treating the human and their tools as a single co-evolving system. Engelbart's claim was that computers should not merely automate or calculate but actively extend human thought, and that the way to get there is to build tools that improve the tool-builders ("bootstrapping"). It is the founding conceptual framework of HCI: the orientation that the goal of interactive computing is human capability, not computation alone.

**statement (formula)** — none; a conceptual framework. Engelbart structured it as the **H-LAM/T system**: a Human using Language, Artifacts, and Methodology, in which the human is Trained — the four means by which capability is augmented, treated as one integrated, co-evolving whole rather than isolated tools. **[confirmed]**

**basis** — a research vision and systems-engineering program, not an empirical law or theorem. Set out in Engelbart's 1962 SRI report and demonstrated in the 1968 "Mother of All Demos" (mouse, hypertext, live collaborative editing, windows). Status established as the field's founding framework; influence is historical and conceptual, so it is not formalized. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**lineage** — direct antecedent is [[Vannevar Bush]]'s 1945 essay "As We May Think" and its imagined memex [[source.bush-1945]], which Engelbart credited as a formative influence. The framework itself is [[Douglas Engelbart]]'s "Augmenting Human Intellect: A Conceptual Framework," SRI summary report AFOSR-3233 (1962) [[source.engelbart-1962]]. Related founding artifacts: Sutherland's Sketchpad (1963) and Licklider's "Man-Computer Symbiosis" (1960). **[confirmed as attribution]**

**sources** — [[source.engelbart-1962]] (origin report, free at dougengelbart.org; Internet Archive); [[source.bush-1945]] (antecedent, *The Atlantic*, 1945). Cross-checked against the Doug Engelbart Institute and the [[Augmenting Human Intellect]] record on Internet Archive.
