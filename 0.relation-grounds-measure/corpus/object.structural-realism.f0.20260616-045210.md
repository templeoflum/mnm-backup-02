**statement (plain)** — scientific theories that get replaced (e.g. Fresnel's optics → Maxwell's) keep their *equations* even when they drop their *ontology* (the ether). Structural realism takes this as the clue: be a realist about the **structure** mature science describes — the pattern of relations / the mathematical form — while staying agnostic (or revisionary) about the intrinsic nature of the things related. It is offered as "the best of both worlds": it honours the **no-miracles argument** (success would be a miracle if theories weren't latching onto something real) while surviving the **pessimistic meta-induction** (past theories were abandoned, so present content is unsafe), because *structure* is what carries across the change.

**basis** — Worrall (1989) revives the position, crediting Poincaré, and motivates it through the Fresnel→Maxwell case and the two competing meta-arguments [[source.worrall-1989]] **[confirmed]**. The SEP survey treats it as a recognized, actively debated realist position in philosophy of science [[source.sep-structural-realism]] **[confirmed]**.

**limits / contested** — a real position in the literature, *not* asserted here as true. It splits into **epistemic** (we can know only structure) and **ontic** (structure is all there is) variants [[source.ladyman-1998]] **[confirmed]**. Standing objection: the **Newman problem** — pure structure plus cardinality is trivially satisfiable, so "structure alone" may say too little [[source.newman-1928]] (see object.newman-objection). Psillos (2001) argues the structure/nature distinction underwriting it cannot be sustained [[source.psillos-2001]]. Carried as lineage, not resolved.
