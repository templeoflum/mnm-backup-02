Sennett, Richard. *The Craftsman*. New Haven & London: Yale University Press, 2008. (x + 326 pp.; ISBN 9780300119091.) First volume of Sennett's *Homo Faber* trilogy.

Contains — the thesis that craftsmanship is "the desire to do a job well for its own sake," and that this impulse extends far beyond manual trades to the programmer, doctor, artist, parent, and citizen. Sennett argues that thinking and feeling are contained *within* the process of making (resisting the head/hand split), and traces the craftsman through case studies — Roman brickmakers, Renaissance goldsmiths, Stradivari's workshop, the Enlightenment encyclopedists, Linux open-source programmers, glassblowers, cooks, musicians. Themes: the workshop and its authority, tacit knowledge and the hand, resistance and ambiguity, the ethics of "the good craftsman."
