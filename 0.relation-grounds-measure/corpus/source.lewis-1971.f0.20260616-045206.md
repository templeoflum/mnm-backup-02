Verbatim citation: I. M. Lewis, *Ecstatic Religion: An Anthropological Study of Spirit Possession and Shamanism*, Harmondsworth: Penguin Books (Pelican), 1971.

Contains: a wide-ranging comparative study of possession and shamanism covering the psychological, medical, aesthetic, religious, and cultural aspects — soul-loss, ecstatic trance, divination, exorcism. Its central contribution is a sociological typology: **central possession cults** (often male-dominated, reinforcing official morality and established power) versus **peripheral / marginal cults** (frequently a form of oblique protest by the socially subordinate, especially women), with the associated **deprivation thesis** that recruitment to peripheral possession compensates for socio-economic deprivation and jural impotence. Draws on Arctic shamanism, women's possession in East Africa and the Maghreb (e.g. *zar*, *bori*), Haitian Vodou, and modern psychotherapy.
