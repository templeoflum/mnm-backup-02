**statement (plain)** — instead of a record passing through separate stages one after another, the continuum model says the same record is being created, captured, organized, and shared all at once and over time — by the creator, the organization, and society together. Managing records and archiving them are not two jobs in sequence; they are one continuous activity.

**statement** — the records continuum model (RCM) is an abstract conceptual model of recordkeeping built on four nested **dimensions** — Create, Capture, Organise, Pluralise — crossed by four **axes** — Identity, Transactionality, Evidentiality, Recordkeeping. Drawn as concentric rings, each intersection names recordkeeping activity occurring across spacetime, from a single document to collective memory. It treats management and archiving as **simultaneous and recursive**, not as the successive phases of the [[object.records-lifecycle]]. **[confirmed]**

**basis** — the model is post-custodial: records are not handed off from one custodian to the next but are continuously formed and re-formed by overlapping processes and actors. This frame handles digital records — reactivated, copied, distributed, with fluid stage boundaries — that strain the linear life-cycle. It is grounded in Anthony Giddens's structuration theory, which treats structure and action as mutually constituting. **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — developed by Frank Upward at Monash University (with Sue McKemmish and Livia Iacovino) and first published as "Structuring the Records Continuum — Part One: Postcustodial Principles and Properties," *Archives & Manuscripts* 24(2), 1996 ([[source.upward-1996]]), with Part Two (Structuration Theory) in 1997. It draws on the Australian recordkeeping tradition — Ian Maclean's "continuum of records administration" (1950s–60s) and Peter Scott's series system — and on Jay Atherton's 1980s revival of the "records continuum" term. **[confirmed]**
