**origin** — S. Eilenberg & S. Mac Lane, "General Theory of Natural Equivalences" (*Trans. Amer. Math. Soc.* 58, 1945) — the paper that defines *category*, *functor*, and *natural transformation*, founding the field. It grew from their 1942 need to make "natural" isomorphisms precise. [[source.eilenberg-maclane-1945]] **[confirmed]**

**codification** — D. M. Kan, "Adjoint Functors" (*Trans. Amer. Math. Soc.* 87, 1958) supplied adjunctions and Kan extensions, the universal-construction machinery that turned the 1945 vocabulary into a working theory; the framework was consolidated through the 1950s–60s (the Yoneda lemma, abelian categories à la Grothendieck, toposes). [[source.kan-1958]] **[confirmed]**

**door** — S. Mac Lane, *Categories for the Working Mathematician* (Springer GTM 5, 1971; 2nd ed. 1998) — the standard graduate text by a co-founder; modern alternative: E. Riehl, *Category Theory in Context* (Dover, 2016). [[source.maclane-1971]] [[source.riehl-2016]]

*grade [s] — origin (Eilenberg & Mac Lane 1945) + codification (Kan 1958 and the 1950s–60s consolidation) + door (Mac Lane 1971 / Riehl 2016) all present and sourced.*
