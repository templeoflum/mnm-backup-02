**statement (plain)** — the more knowledgeable other (MKO) is whoever has a better understanding or higher ability than the learner for the particular task at hand. It is the person whose "adult guidance or collaboration with more capable peers" makes the upper edge of the zone of proximal development reachable. Crucially it need not be a teacher or an adult — it is anyone more skilled in *that specific* thing: a parent, coach, peer, or even a tool or resource.

**statement** — in Vygotsky's sociocultural account, development is mediated socially: a learner advances through the [[object.zone-of-proximal-development]] via interaction with someone more competent. The "more knowledgeable other" names that partner — "someone who has a better understanding or a higher ability level than the learner with respect to a particular task, process, or concept." **[confirmed]**

**basis** — the construct follows directly from the ZPD definition, whose "potential" level is reached only "under adult guidance or in collaboration with more capable peers." The MKO is the supplier of that guidance; the breadth of the category (peer, parent, mentor, expert — not necessarily a formal teacher) follows from defining it by relative competence on the task rather than by role or age.

**lineage** — the underlying idea is Vygotsky's, embedded in his sociocultural theory and the ZPD as transmitted via *Mind in Society* [[source.vygotsky-1978]]. The crisp three-word label "more knowledgeable other" (and the acronym MKO) is a **later didactic crystallization** widely attributed to Vygotsky in the secondary/teaching literature; it is best carried as *associated with* Vygotsky rather than as a verbatim term he authored. **[confirmed as attribution]** *(primary-source verbatim coinage not opened here; the concept's grounding in the ZPD is confirmed.)*
