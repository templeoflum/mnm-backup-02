**statement (plain).** FACS is a comprehensive, anatomically grounded coding scheme that describes visible facial movement as combinations of Action Units (AUs) — each AU corresponding to the contraction/relaxation of one or a few facial muscles. It provides an objective, behavior-level vocabulary for what a face is doing, independent of any interpretation of emotion.

**basis (method).** Codifies facial movement into discrete AUs (originally ~44) that can be scored from video by trained, certified coders; self-instructional design supports inter-coder reliability [[source.ekman-friesen-1978]] **[confirmed as attribution]**. It is the measurement backbone for the face channel in nonverbal-sensitivity research, the kind of cue corpus the PONS and related instruments sample [[source.rosenthal-1979-b]] **[confirmed as attribution]**. Limits: FACS is descriptive, not interpretive — AU patterns do not map one-to-one onto emotions, and manual coding is labor-intensive (motivating later automated AU detection). Citation details web-verified; primary manuals not opened.
