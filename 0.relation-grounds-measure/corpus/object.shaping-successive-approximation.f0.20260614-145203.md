**statement (plain)** — to train a behavior that the organism never spontaneously performs, you reinforce successively closer approximations to the target: first reinforce a rough movement in the right direction, then withhold reinforcement until a closer version appears, and so on, raising the criterion step by step until the full target behavior is in place. This is differential reinforcement of successive approximations.

**statement** — let T be a target operant of low or zero baseline probability. Shaping reinforces a sequence of response classes R₁, R₂, …, Rₙ = T, where each Rᵢ is a successively closer approximation to T; reinforcement is differential (delivered for Rᵢ, withheld for poorer approximations) and the criterion is advanced as each Rᵢ stabilizes. The method exploits response variability plus the selection principle of the [[object.law-of-effect]]. **[confirmed]**

**basis** — empirical: first described as response differentiation by mechanically reinforcing successive approximations of lever-pressing in rats (1938); demonstrated dramatically by free-hand shaping (teaching pigeons to bowl/guide, 1943, Project Pigeon), which made the real-time, hand-shaping technique vivid and led to the coined term. **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — the procedure appears in [[source.skinner-1938]] *The Behavior of Organisms* (1938) as differentiation by reinforcing successive approximations; the free-operant hand-shaping demonstration dates to 1943 (Project Pigeon, with Keller Breland); the term "shaping" entered Skinner's usage around 1951 and the method is treated in [[source.skinner-1953]] *Science and Human Behavior* (1953). Depends on [[object.reinforcement-and-punishment]] and the [[object.operant-paradigm]]. **[confirmed]** *(the 1943 / ~1951 dates for the demonstration and the term are carried as well-documented secondary history, distinct from the 1938 first description.)*
