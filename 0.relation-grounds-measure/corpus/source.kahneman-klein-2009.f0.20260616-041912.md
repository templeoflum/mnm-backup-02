# kahneman-klein-2009

**Citation (verbatim):** Kahneman, D., & Klein, G. (2009). Conditions for intuitive expertise: A failure to disagree. *American Psychologist, 64*(6), 515-526. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016755

**Contains:** A joint article by representatives of two traditions usually seen as opposed — Kahneman's heuristics-and-biases program and Klein's naturalistic decision making. The authors attempt to map the boundary that separates genuine intuitive skill from overconfident, biased impressions. They converge on two necessary conditions for the development of valid intuitive expertise: (1) an environment of sufficiently high validity/regularity (predictable, lawful structure to learn), as opposed to a low-validity, chaotic, or hypercomplex one; and (2) an adequate opportunity to learn those regularities through prolonged practice with accurate and timely feedback. Where these conditions fail, subjectively confident intuitions are unreliable.
