**statement (plain)** — **Hestia** (Greek *Ἑστία*, "hearth") is the goddess of the hearth, the home, and domestic and civic fire. She is the eldest child of **Kronos** and **Rhea** — swallowed first and, by the reversal, disgorged last — sister to Zeus, Poseidon, Hades, Hera, and Demeter (*Theogony* 453–454). A **virgin goddess**, she refuses the suits of Poseidon and Apollo and is granted by Zeus to keep her maidenhood and to sit at the centre of the house, receiving honour in every home and temple (*Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite* 21–32). She keeps to Olympus rather than roaming, and is honoured **first** in offerings — the opening and closing portion of sacrifice. In some later accounts she yields her seat among the twelve Olympians to **Dionysus**.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: the hearth, the household, the civic hearth of the city (the *prytaneion*), and the fire of sacrifice. Roles: eldest of the Kronos-children, virgin goddess, and the one honoured first and last in offerings. Key attribute: the ever-burning hearth-fire itself. Etymology: Greek *Hestíā* is simply "hearth," the common noun and the goddess being one; the Roman *interpretatio* identified her with **Vesta**, whose tended flame and Vestal virgins are the Roman counterpart. The yielding of her seat to Dionysus is a late and non-canonical tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and the Homeric Hymns [[source.homer]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The hearth-domain, virginity, and precedence in offerings and the Vesta *interpretatio* are attested; the yielding of her seat to Dionysus is a late tradition, and the genealogies are attested tradition, variable across sources, to be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
