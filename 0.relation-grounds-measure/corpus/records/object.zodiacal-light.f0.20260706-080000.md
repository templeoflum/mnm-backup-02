**statement (plain)** — Zodiacal light is a faint, diffuse triangular or cone-shaped glow extending along the ecliptic plane in the night sky, most visible near the horizon after evening twilight or before morning twilight, caused by sunlight scattered from interplanetary dust particles concentrated in the plane of the solar system. This phenomenon results from forward scattering of sunlight by micron-sized dust grains distributed throughout the inner solar system, with the dust concentration and thus brightness decreasing with distance from the sun. The zodiacal light appears brightest near the horizon along the ecliptic, tapering to an apex extending 60 to 90 degrees or more from the sun's position below the horizon.
