**statement (plain)** — Binocular rivalry is a fundamental visual phenomenon that occurs when two incompatible images are presented simultaneously, one to each eye. Rather than perceiving a stable blend or superposition of the two images, observers experience spontaneous alternations in which one image dominates conscious perception while the other is suppressed, with dominance switching back and forth every few seconds. First systematically described by Charles Wheatstone in 1838 using his newly invented stereoscope, binocular rivalry has become one of the most extensively studied paradigms in visual neuroscience for investigating the neural correlates of conscious perception.
