**statement (plain)** — A rational donkey stands exactly midway between two identical, equally appealing bales of hay. With no sufficient reason to prefer one, it cannot choose — and starves. If rational choice requires a reason to prefer, perfect symmetry paralyzes reason; something else (will, chance, instinct) must break the tie.

**statement (framework)** — targets the **principle of sufficient reason** in the theory of action: under exact symmetry, reason alone cannot act. Debated by Spinoza, Leibniz, Schopenhauer; reborn as symmetry-breaking in deterministic algorithms and agent choice among equal-valued options in AI. Established; the role of the will stays **open**.

**basis** — the balanced-man argument in Aristotle's *De Caelo* 295b30 [[source.aristotle-de-caelo]]; developed by al-Ghazali and named for Buridan. **[confirmed as attribution]**
