**origin** — Ogham, an alphabet of the early Irish (Primitive Irish) language (~4th–6th c. CE inscriptions in Ireland and Britain); 20 primary letters (*feda*) in four families (*aicmí*) of five. [[source.mcmanus-1991]] **[confirmed as attribution]**

**codification** — the letter-names and their tree/plant associations + kennings (*Bríatharogaim*, word-oghams) are set out in the medieval Ogham tract (*In Lebor Ogaim*) and the *Auraicept na n-Éces*; Damian McManus, *A Guide to Ogam* (1991), is the standard scholarly treatment. [[source.mcmanus-1991]] **[confirmed as attribution]**

**door** — McManus, *A Guide to Ogam* (1991).

**lineage** — the tree associations are partly traditional kennings, not all literal botany — carried as a note. **This is genuine medieval Ogham — distinct from the modern "Celtic tree zodiac/calendar" (Robert Graves, 1948), which is a 20th-c. invention** (Bin 3, not grounded). Held as the tradition's system (members `status: doctrinal`).
