**statement (plain)** — the **Moirai** (Greek *Μοῖραι*, "Apportioners"; the **Fates**) are the three goddesses who apportion mortal destiny: **Clotho** ("Spinner") spins the thread of life, **Lachesis** ("Allotter") measures and allots it, and **Atropos** ("Unturnable") cuts it. Hesiod gives their parentage twice — as daughters of **Nyx** (Night) (*Theogony* 217–219) and as daughters of **Zeus** and **Themis** (*Theogony* 904–906) — reflecting variant tradition rather than a single settled descent.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: fate, destiny, and the apportioned span of mortal life. Roles: the three who spin, measure, and sever the thread of each life. Etymology: Greek *Moîrai* from *moîra*, "portion, share, lot"; their names are **Klōthṓ** ("spinner"), **Láchesis** ("allotment"), and **Átropos** ("unturnable, inflexible"). The Roman *interpretatio* identified them with the **Parcae**. Their emblem is the thread of life, spun, measured, and cut. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The three names, their offices at the thread, and the Parcae *interpretatio* are attested; the two parentages are both attested in Hesiod and are variant tradition, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
