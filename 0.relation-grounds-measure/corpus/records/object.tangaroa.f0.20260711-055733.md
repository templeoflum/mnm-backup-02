**statement (plain)** — **Tangaroa** is the great Polynesian god of the sea and all its creatures. In the Māori cycle he is a son of **Rangi** and **Papa** who, when the parents were parted and **Tāwhirimātea** raised his storms, fled to the ocean and took the fish and sea-things as his own. In **Tahitian** tradition the same figure, as **Ta'aroa**, is far greater: the supreme creator who existed alone within the cosmic egg (shell) before shaping the world and the other gods from himself.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: ocean, sea creatures, the deep, navigation. In Māori tradition son of **Rangi** and **Papa**; in Tahitian tradition the supreme creator. Cognate with the Hawaiian **Kanaloa** and the Tahitian **Ta'aroa**. NOTE cognates: **Tangaroa ≈ Hawaiian Kanaloa ≈ Tahitian Ta'aroa** (Kanaloa recorded separately). Māori / pan-Polynesian. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested in Grey, *Polynesian Mythology* (1855) [[source.grey-1855]] for the Māori sea-god and in Beckwith, *Hawaiian Mythology* (1940) [[source.beckwith-1940]] for the Polynesian background; documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). These myths come through oral tradition collected and recorded in the 19th–20th c. (Grey, Beckwith, and later scholars); the god's rank varies sharply across islands — a fled sea-god in Māori telling, a supreme creator (Ta'aroa) in Tahiti. **[confirmed as attribution]**
