**statement (plain)** — Standardized work fixes the current best-known way to do a task — its content, sequence, timing, and expected outcome — as an explicit baseline that everyone follows. Far from freezing the work, this is the precondition for improving it: only against a stable standard can a deviation show up as a signal, and only a shared baseline lets an improvement be tested and, if it works, become the new standard. "Without a standard there is no logical basis for making a decision or taking action" (Ohno's spirit).

**relevance** — the paradox at the heart of TPS (Spear & Bowen): rigidly specified work is what *enables* continuous improvement, because a standard turns every anomaly into a visible experiment. The stable base that kaizen ([[object.kaizen]]) ratchets forward, and the manufacturing analogue of a fixed close-of-shift / handover procedure that makes drift visible [[source.spear-bowen-1999]] **[confirmed as attribution]**.
