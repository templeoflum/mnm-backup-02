**statement (plain)** — Lenticular printing is a technology that produces images with an illusion of depth or the ability to change appearance when viewed from different angles, achieved by printing interlaced images behind an array of cylindrical lenses (lenticules) that selectively direct different image strips to each eye or to viewers at different positions. The technique can create effects including stereoscopic depth perception, flip images that alternate between two or more distinct pictures, animation sequences that appear to move as viewing angle changes, and zoom effects where portions of the image appear to magnify. Lenticular printing exploits the geometric optics of lens arrays to control which image information reaches the observer as a function of viewing position, effectively multiplexing multiple images into a single physical substrate.
