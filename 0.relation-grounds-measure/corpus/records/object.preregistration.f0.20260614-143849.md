**statement (plain)** — preregistration is writing down what you will study and how you will analyze it, then time-stamping that plan in a public registry, before you collect (or look at) your data. Once the data are in, anyone can check the published study against the original plan — so a planned, confirmatory test cannot be quietly rewritten into an after-the-fact story.

**statement** — preregistration is the practice of creating a time-stamped, read-only record of a study's research questions, hypotheses, design, sampling/stopping rules, and analysis plan in a registry prior to data collection (or, for existing data, prior to analysis). Its function is to make the line between confirmatory (planned, hypothesis-testing) and exploratory (data-driven, hypothesis-generating) work auditable, thereby constraining [[object.researcher-degrees-of-freedom]] (p-hacking) and [[object.file-drawer-problem]] / HARKing. **[confirmed]**

**basis/origin** — there is no single inventor. The modern open-science form took hold around 2012–2013, driven by the [[object.replication-crisis]], with infrastructure from the [[source.cos-io]] (Center for Open Science) and [[source.osf-io]] (Open Science Framework), plus services such as AsPredicted. It builds on a longer lineage of clinical-trial registration: ClinicalTrials.gov (established under the FDA Modernization Act, 1997) and the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors' 2005 trial-registration mandate. **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — carried as multi-rooted: the medical trial-registry tradition (1997–2005) and the psychology/social-science open-science tradition (~2012–2013) are distinct roots that converge on the same practice. The diagnostic precursors that motivated it — [[source.rosenthal-1979]] (file drawer), [[source.ioannidis-2005]] (most findings false), [[source.simmons-nelson-simonsohn-2011]] (researcher degrees of freedom) — are attributions of the problem, not of the practice. **[confirmed]**

**sources** — Nosek et al. 2015, TOP Guidelines [[source.nosek-2015]]; Chambers 2013 [[source.chambers-2013]]; [[source.cos-io]]; [[source.osf-io]].
