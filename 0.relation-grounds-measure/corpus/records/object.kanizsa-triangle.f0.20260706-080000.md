**statement (plain)** — The Kanizsa triangle, introduced by Italian psychologist Gaetano Kanizsa in 1955, is among the most iconic and extensively studied examples of illusory contour formation and modal surface completion. The classic configuration consists of three black circular discs with wedge-shaped notches removed, positioned at the vertices of an imaginary triangle, often accompanied by three angle brackets with their vertices pointing inward. Despite the absence of any physical luminance gradient defining the triangle's edges, observers perceive a vivid white equilateral triangle that appears to float above the inducing elements, with clearly defined illusory contours along its three sides and a subjective impression that the illusory triangle is slightly brighter than the background.
