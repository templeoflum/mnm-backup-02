**statement (plain)** — At any single instant, a flying arrow occupies exactly the space of its own length — so at that instant it is not moving. If time is composed entirely of such instants, and the arrow is at rest at each, then it is at rest throughout: motion never happens, despite appearances.

**statement (framework)** — unlike the Achilles and Dichotomy, the Arrow does not rest on infinite divisibility but attacks **instantaneous motion** itself — how a succession of changeless states can constitute change. Resistant to the convergent-series resolution; pushes on the relation between the static mathematical description of a state (the 'at-at' theory of motion) and dynamic process. Established; **open**.
