**statement (plain)** — **Raijin** is the god of thunder and lightning, a fearsome storm deity of Japanese folk and Buddhist-influenced tradition. He is shown as a muscular, demon-like figure encircled by a ring of drums (taiko), which he beats to make thunder. He is frequently paired with **Fūjin**, the wind god, the two standing together as guardian figures at temple gates.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: thunder, lightning, storms, fear, the protection of temples. Raijin is depicted as a demonic figure surrounded by drums that produce thunder, and is classically paired with Fūjin as guardians at temple and city gates (as at the Rajōmon). **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — Raijin is documented in Ashkenazi (2003) and this is documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). He is a folk and Buddhist-influenced storm deity rather than a chronicled Shinto kami; his demon-like iconography and his guardian pairing with Fūjin at temple gates belong to the living syncretic and folk tradition, not to the 8th-century chronicles. **[confirmed as attribution]**
