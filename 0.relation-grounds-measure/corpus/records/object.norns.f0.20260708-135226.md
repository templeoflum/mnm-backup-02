**statement (plain)** — The **Norns** (Old Norse *nornir*) are the female powers who shape the fates of gods and men. The three chief norns are commonly named **Urðr**, **Verðandi**, and **Skuld** ("what became," "what is becoming," and roughly "what shall be" or "debt/obligation"). *Völuspá* tells that these three maidens came from the well beneath **Yggdrasil** and laid down the destinies of the children of men, and Snorri in *Gylfaginning* places them by the **Well of Urðr** at one of the roots of the World Tree, where each day they take water and clay from the well and sprinkle it over the tree so its limbs do not rot. Beyond the three, the sources speak of many norns — some of the Aesir, some of elves, some of dwarves — who come to each child at birth to shape its life (*Fáfnismál*).

**statement (attestation)** — domains: fate, destiny, the appointed span of a life. Key figures: **Urðr**, **Verðandi**, and **Skuld**, the three chief norns; their dwelling at the **Well of Urðr** by a root of **Yggdrasil**; the many norns who attend each birth. The coming of the three maidens and the shaping of human destinies appear in the Poetic Edda in *Völuspá*, and the plural norns at birth in *Fáfnismál*; Snorri's *Gylfaginning* supplies the trio's names together and the daily watering of Yggdrasil from the Well of Urðr. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Norse mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested across the Poetic Edda [[source.poetic-edda]] and systematized in Snorri's Prose Edda [[source.prose-edda]]; the standard reference treatment is Lindow [[source.lindow-2001]]. The three named together and the watering of Yggdrasil from the Well of Urðr come chiefly from Snorri's *Gylfaginning* and should be read as attested tradition through his 13th-c. Christian framing, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
