**statement (plain)** — **Hathor** is the goddess of love, beauty, music, dance, joy, and motherhood. She is depicted as a cow, or as a woman with cow horns enclosing a **sun-disk**. As the **Eye of Ra** she is the daughter and instrument of the sun god, and she serves as a nurse and protector of the **pharaoh**. Her joyful aspect has a fierce counterpart: in the myth of the destruction of humankind the raging Eye of Ra, associated with **Sekhmet**, is pacified and returns as Hathor. Her chief cult was at **Dendera**.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: love, beauty, music, dance, joy, and motherhood; also the sky and the sun as the Eye of Ra. Chief cult centre: **Dendera**. Iconography: a cow, or a cow-horned woman with a sun-disk. She nurses and protects the pharaoh; her fierce aspect is linked to **Sekhmet** in the tale of the near-destruction of humankind. The Greeks by *interpretatio graeca* equated her with **Aphrodite**. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Egyptian mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested from the Pyramid Texts [[source.pyramid-texts]] onward and present in the Book of the Dead [[source.book-of-the-dead]]; the standard reference treatment is Wilkinson [[source.wilkinson-2003]]. The Dendera cult and the Aphrodite *interpretatio graeca* are established; her domains, her role as Eye of Ra and nurse of the pharaoh, and her fierce Sekhmet-linked aspect are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
