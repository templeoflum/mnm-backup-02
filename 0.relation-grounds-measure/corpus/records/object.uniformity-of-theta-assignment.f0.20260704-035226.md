**statement (plain)** — the grammar–ontology bridge at its strongest: what an argument *means* (its thematic role) fixes *where it sits* in syntactic structure. Every Agent is born in the same structural position, every Theme in its own — surface variety is derived by movement from a uniform base.

**statement (formal)** — verbatim (Baker 1988:46): "Identical thematic relationships between items are represented by identical structural relationships between those items at the level of D-Structure." Baker (1997): a medium-coarse version with three role prototypes — agent, theme, goal/path/location (e.g. Agent → spec of vP; Theme → the lower VP). **[confirmed]**

**basis** — `open`: a working hypothesis, not settled doctrine — the Minimalist Program's dispensing with D-structure is a standing challenge, and argument-structure alternations remain actively debated; Hale–Keyser's l-syntax and the neo-constructionists carry the same "structure determines roles" program forward. Kin: the Universal Alignment Hypothesis (Perlmutter & Postal, Relational Grammar) maps roles to positions via a hierarchy. [[source.baker-1988]] [[source.hale-keyser-1993]]
