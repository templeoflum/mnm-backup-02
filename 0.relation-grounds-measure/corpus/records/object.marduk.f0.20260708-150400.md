**statement (plain)** — **Marduk** is the champion and patron god of Babylon. In the **Enuma Elish** the younger gods, threatened by the primordial **Tiamat** and her host of monsters, make Marduk their champion and grant him supreme kingship; he goes out, slays Tiamat in single combat, and splits her body to form the heavens and the earth, ordering the cosmos and setting the stars and the calendar. For this he is exalted as king of the gods and given his fifty names. In the first millennium BCE he was the head of the Babylonian pantheon.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: kingship of the gods, creation, cosmic order, the city of Babylon. Culture: Babylonian **Marduk** (later syncretized with **Bel**, "lord"). Chief cult centre: the temple **Esagila** and the great ziggurat **Etemenanki** at **Babylon**. In the Enuma Elish he defeats **Tiamat**, creates heaven and earth from her divided body, and is proclaimed king of the gods with his fifty names. The epic was recited at the Babylonian New Year (*akitu*) festival in his honour. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Mesopotamian mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested centrally in the Enuma Elish [[source.enuma-elish]] and treated in the standard reference by Black & Green [[source.black-green-1992]]. His Babylon cult (Esagila, Etemenanki) and headship of the pantheon are established; the combat-and-creation myth is attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
