**statement (plain)** — The Benary Cross Illusion, first described by Wilhelm Benary in 1924, is a striking demonstration that lightness perception cannot be explained solely by local luminance ratios. The illusion consists of a black cross on a white background, with identical gray triangles positioned in two different configurations: one triangle placed on the arm of the cross (where it borders both black and white regions) and another positioned in the concave region between the cross arms (also bordering both black and white). Despite having identical luminance values and similar proportions of black and white in their immediate surrounds, the triangle on the cross arm appears substantially lighter than the triangle in the concavity.
