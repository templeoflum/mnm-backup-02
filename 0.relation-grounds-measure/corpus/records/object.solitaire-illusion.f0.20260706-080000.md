**statement (plain)** — The Solitaire Illusion is a visual phenomenon in which a pattern of dots or discrete elements appears to contain unequal numbers of different-colored items, despite the actual quantities being identical. This illusion demonstrates the fallibility of rapid numerosity judgments and reveals how spatial arrangement, grouping principles, and attentional distribution can bias perceived quantity. The effect typically manifests when dots of different colors are arranged in patterns that promote differential grouping or spatial clustering, causing the visual system to misestimate relative numerosity without explicit counting.
