**statement (plain)** — **Gilgamesh** is the semi-divine king of **Uruk**, two-thirds god and one-third man, the hero of the **Epic of Gilgamesh**. The gods create the wild man **Enkidu** to match him, and after a wrestling match the two become inseparable friends; together they journey to the Cedar Forest and slay its guardian **Humbaba**, and later kill the **Bull of Heaven** sent by **Ishtar** after Gilgamesh spurns her. When Enkidu dies as punishment, Gilgamesh, terrified of death, sets out to find the immortal flood-survivor **Utnapishtim**. He fails the test to stay awake, loses the plant of youth to a serpent, and returns to Uruk having learned that immortality is not for men — his lasting monument is the city's walls.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: kingship, heroism, the fear of death, the quest for immortality. Culture: Sumerian **Bilgames**, Akkadian **Gilgamesh**; remembered as a king of **Uruk** and later a god of the underworld. The standard Babylonian epic recounts his friendship with **Enkidu**, the slaying of **Humbaba** in the Cedar Forest, the killing of the **Bull of Heaven**, Enkidu's death, and Gilgamesh's failed quest for immortality (**Utnapishtim**, the sleep-test, and the plant of youth taken by a snake). **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Mesopotamian mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested centrally in the Epic of Gilgamesh [[source.epic-of-gilgamesh]] and treated in the standard reference by Black & Green [[source.black-green-1992]]. His kingship of Uruk and heroic cycle are established; the epic is attested tradition, variable across its Sumerian, Old Babylonian, and standard versions, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
