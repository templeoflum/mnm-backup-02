**statement (plain)** — **Ra** (also **Re**) is the sun god and creator, king of the gods. He sails across the sky by day in the solar barque and passes through the underworld by night, where he is renewed and reborn each dawn; his daily cycle is the model of order against chaos. He is the head of the great **Ennead** of Heliopolis and the source of creation from which the other gods descend. In later theology he is joined with **Amun** as **Amun-Ra**, the supreme god of the New Kingdom.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: the sun, creation, kingship, and cosmic order. Chief cult centre: **Heliopolis** (Egyptian *Iunu*), where he heads the **Ennead**. Iconography: a falcon-headed man crowned with the solar disk; the solar barque (the day-barque *Mandjet* and night-barque *Mesektet*). His nightly journey through the underworld and his defence against the serpent **Apophis** are central to the funerary texts. Later syncretized as **Amun-Ra**. The Greeks by *interpretatio graeca* equated him with **Helios**, the sun. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Egyptian mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested from the Pyramid Texts [[source.pyramid-texts]] onward and central to the Book of the Dead [[source.book-of-the-dead]]; the standard reference treatment is Wilkinson [[source.wilkinson-2003]]. The Heliopolitan cult and the *interpretatio graeca* with Helios are established; the solar and creation myths are attested tradition, variable across periods and sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
