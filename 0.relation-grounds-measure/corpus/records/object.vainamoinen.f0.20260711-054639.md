**statement (plain)** — **Väinämöinen** is the primordial sage and central hero of the Kalevala. Born old from the primordial waters, he is the greatest of singers and magicians, working his power through song. He makes the first **kantele** (a zither) and leads the quest to the North for the **Sampo**, the mill of endless prosperity.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: wisdom, song-magic, the primordial. He works magic and shapes the world through incantation and runo-song. At the epic's close he departs in a copper boat, leaving the kantele and his songs to his people and promising to return when he is needed again. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — Väinämöinen is attested throughout the Kalevala and treated in modern scholarship ([[source.pentikainen-1999]]); this is documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). The Kalevala is Elias Lönnrot's nineteenth-century compilation of oral folk poetry, not an ancient text, so its connected biography of Väinämöinen is Lönnrot's arrangement of much older, variant oral material. **[confirmed as attribution]**
