**statement (plain)** — The Checker Shadow Illusion, created by Edward Adelson in 1995, is one of the most powerful and counterintuitive demonstrations of lightness constancy mechanisms in human vision. The illusion depicts a checkerboard with a cylinder casting a shadow across its surface. Two squares on the checkerboard, one labeled A (outside the shadow) and one labeled B (inside the shadow), appear dramatically different in lightness: square A appears dark gray or black, while square B appears light gray or white.
