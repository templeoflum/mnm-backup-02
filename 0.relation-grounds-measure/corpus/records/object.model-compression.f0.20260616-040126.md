**statement (plain)** — a large, slow, high-accuracy model (typically an ensemble) is used as an oracle to label a large pool of data; a single small model is then trained to reproduce those labels. The small model recovers most of the ensemble's accuracy at a fraction of the size and inference cost. The function, not the parameters, is what is preserved.

**basis** — Buciluă et al. (2006) showed a single compressed neural net matching an ensemble's accuracy while being roughly "1000 times smaller and 1000 times faster," using the ensemble to label synthetic data generated by the MUNGE procedure. [[source.bucilua-2006]] Surveys treat model compression as the conceptual root of distillation. [[source.gou-2021]] **Limits** — the student is bounded by the teacher's quality and by the coverage of the transfer data; mimicking hard labels alone discards the teacher's confidence structure, which the later soft-target method recovers. **[confirmed]**
