William James, *The Varieties of Religious Experience: A Study in Human Nature* (Longmans, Green & Co., 1902); the Gifford Lectures.

Contains — James's treatment of the "transmarginal" or subliminal field of consciousness as "the most important step forward" in the psychology of his era: religious conversion, inspiration, and "sensory and motor automatisms" as eruptions from an "active subconscious self." Pairs with his psychical writings (the medium Leonora Piper as his "white crow") and his warning that science is distorted "as much by fixed beliefs about impossibility as by fraud." The bridge from the psychical-research catalogue to mainstream psychology.
