**statement (plain)** — **Obatala** (**Oxalá** in Candomblé) is the eldest of the **Orisha**, father of the pantheon and sculptor of humankind. Tasked by **Olodumare** to fashion the land and to mould human bodies from clay, he is the patron of purity, wisdom, and peace. In the Yoruba tradition he holds the disabled sacred, and is associated with white cloth and calm.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: creation, purity, wisdom, peace, human form. Moulder of human bodies from clay; associated with white (cloth, chalk, beads) and coolness of temper; in one tradition he drank palm wine and shaped some bodies imperfectly, and so became patron of the disabled, whom he holds sacred. Diaspora name: **Oxalá** (Candomblé). **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — documented in Idowu (1962) and Karade (1994); this is documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). It is an oral tradition carried in the **Ifá** corpus (the *odu*) and recorded by scholars; the myths of his moulding of bodies and his attributes vary between Yorubaland and the diaspora traditions of Santería and Candomblé. **[confirmed as attribution]**
