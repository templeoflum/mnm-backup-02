**statement (plain)** — In Māori tradition **Hine-nui-te-pō**, "Great Woman of Night," is the goddess of death and the underworld. She was once **Hine-tītama**, the Dawn Maiden, the first woman shaped by **Tāne** — and, in some tellings, taken by Tāne as wife; on learning that Tāne was both her maker and her father she fled in shame to the dark below, becoming ruler of the dead. There the hero **Māui** met his death, crushed while trying to pass through her body to win immortality for humankind.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: death, night, the underworld. Once **Hine-tītama**, the Dawn Maiden, first woman made by **Tāne**; ruler of the dead; slayer of **Māui**. Aliases: **Hine-nui-te-pō** (Māori). Māori tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested in Grey, *Polynesian Mythology* (1855) [[source.grey-1855]]; documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). These myths come through Māori oral tradition collected and recorded in the 19th–20th c. (Grey and later scholars); the account of her origin as Hine-tītama and of Māui's death varies across iwi and island traditions. **[confirmed as attribution]**
