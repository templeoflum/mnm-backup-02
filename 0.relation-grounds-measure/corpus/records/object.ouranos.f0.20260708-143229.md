**statement (plain)** — **Ouranos** (Greek *Οὐρανός*, "Sky") is the primordial Sky and the first divine ruler of the cosmos. In Hesiod he is brought forth by **Gaia** (Earth) to cover her on every side, and then becomes her consort (*Theogony* 126–128). By Gaia he fathers the twelve **Titans**, the **Cyclopes**, and the **Hecatoncheires**, but hating his offspring he hides them away within the earth. Gaia, in pain, fashions a sickle and calls on her children; the youngest, **Kronos**, ambushes and castrates his father. From the blood that falls on the earth spring the **Erinyes**, the **Giants**, and the Meliae; from the severed genitals cast into the sea rises the foam from which **Aphrodite** is born (*Theogony* 176–206). Thus overthrown, Ouranos yields rule to Kronos.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: the sky and heavens, and the first sovereignty of the cosmos in the succession myth. Roles: primordial father, imprisoner of his own children, and the first ruler to be overthrown — the opening term of the succession Ouranos → Kronos → Zeus. Etymology: Greek *Ouranós*, "sky, heaven," of uncertain further derivation; the Roman *interpretatio* identified him with **Caelus**. His castration is the founding act of the divine succession. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and Homer's epics [[source.homer]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The castration myth and the Caelus *interpretatio* are attested; the genealogies and the succession myth are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
