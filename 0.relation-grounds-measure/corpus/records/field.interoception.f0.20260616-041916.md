Interoception is the sense of the physiological condition of the body — the afferent representation of signals arising from inside the organism (cardiac, respiratory, gastrointestinal, thermal, nociceptive, metabolic) and the perceptual, affective and self-related processing built on them. It is studied at three levels: the peripheral and central neuroanatomy of visceral afferents, the psychophysics of how accurately and how vividly individuals perceive those signals, and the theoretical accounts (homeostatic, predictive) of how interoceptive representation grounds emotion and selfhood.

**origin** — Charles Sherrington's *The Integrative Action of the Nervous System* (1906) coined the term, partitioning sensory receptors into exteroceptive, proprioceptive and **interoceptive** (visceral) classes [[source.sherrington-1906]] **[confirmed]**. The physiological substrate — self-regulation of the internal milieu — was named **homeostasis** by Walter Cannon, *The Wisdom of the Body* (1932), extending Claude Bernard's *milieu intérieur* [[source.cannon-1932]] **[confirmed]**.

**codification** — A. D. (Bud) Craig's "How do you feel? Interoception: the sense of the physiological condition of the body" (*Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 2002) recast interoception from the visceral-afferent sense into a general representation of the body's homeostatic condition, mapped onto a lamina I spinothalamocortical pathway re-represented in the insula, and proposed it as the foundation for subjective feelings [[source.craig-2002]]; his 2009 sequel located the integrative substrate in the anterior insular cortex [[source.craig-2009]] **[confirmed]**.

**door** — Garfinkel, Seth, Barrett, Suzuki & Critchley, "Knowing your own heart" (*Biological Psychology*, 2015), provides the standard operational framework that organizes the contemporary field, dissociating interoceptive **accuracy**, **sensibility**, and **awareness** [[source.garfinkel-2015]] **[confirmed]**.

**lineage** — three partly independent strands feed the modern field and contest its founding emphasis: (1) the neurophysiological line from Sherrington through Cannon to Craig's insular model; (2) the experiential/clinical line of Gendlin's *Focusing* (1978), where the bodily "felt sense" is cultivated as a therapeutic faculty [[source.gendlin-1978]]; (3) the computational line of interoceptive predictive coding / active inference (Seth 2013; Barrett & Simmons 2015), which reframes interoception as Bayesian inference on bodily signals underwriting emotion and the embodied self [[source.seth-2013]] [[source.barrett-2015]]. The psychophysical method that grounds measurement throughout is Schandry's heartbeat-counting task (1981) [[source.schandry-1981]]. **[confirmed]**

*grade [t] — origin and codification/door present (the door, Garfinkel 2015, is a framework paper rather than a comprehensive textbook); no single canonical textbook anchors the field.*
