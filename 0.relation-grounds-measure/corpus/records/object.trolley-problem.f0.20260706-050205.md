**statement (plain)** — A runaway trolley will kill five on the track. You can pull a lever diverting it to a side track where it kills one. Most say pull. In the Footbridge variant, the only way to stop it is to push a large stranger onto the track. The arithmetic is identical — one death to save five — yet most recoil from pushing.

**statement (framework)** — the engine that separates **consequentialist** (count the deaths) from **deontological** (killing vs. letting die, using-as-means vs. side-effect, the doctrine of double effect) moral reasoning; a testbed for the empirical/neuro turn in ethics and for autonomous-vehicle and triage algorithms. Established as the central case in modern moral philosophy; the right principle stays **open**.

**basis** — Foot's "Problem of Abortion and the Doctrine of the Double Effect" (1967) [[source.foot-1967]], refined by Thomson's "The Trolley Problem" (1985) [[source.thomson-1985]]. **[confirmed as attribution]**
