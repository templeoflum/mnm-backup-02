**statement (plain)** — The Judd Illusion is a variant of the classic Müller-Lyer illusion, discovered by American psychologist Charles Hubbard Judd in 1899. While the standard Müller-Lyer illusion uses arrowheads or fins pointing in opposite directions at the two ends of a line, the Judd variant features arrowheads pointing in the same direction at both ends. This configuration creates a compelling illusion where the physical midpoint of the line appears displaced or shifted from its actual position.
