**statement (plain)** — Mary, raised in a black-and-white room, learns *every physical fact* about colour vision — wavelengths, retinal and cortical mechanisms, the brain states of seeing red. Released, she sees a red rose for the first time. Does she learn something new? Jackson's answer: yes — *what red is like*. If so, her complete physical knowledge was incomplete.

**statement (framework)** — an argument against **physicalism** via a knowledge/qualia gap; kin to Nagel's bat and the zombie argument. The standard physicalist replies: Mary gains an *ability* or *acquaintance*, not a new *fact* (the ability/old-fact-new-guise hypotheses). Jackson himself later recanted and became a physicalist. Established as canonical; whether it refutes physicalism stays **open**.

**basis** — Jackson's "Epiphenomenal Qualia" (1982) and "What Mary Didn't Know" (1986) [[source.jackson-1982]]. **[confirmed as attribution]**
