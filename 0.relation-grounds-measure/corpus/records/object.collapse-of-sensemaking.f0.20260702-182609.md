**statement (plain)** — "sensemaking" is the ongoing work of turning a confusing situation into an understood one that can be acted on together. It can *collapse*: under sudden stress the shared frame and the role structure that held it fall apart, and people can no longer read the situation or each other — an order that falls outside the crew's frame (Dodge's "escape fire" at Mann Gulch) is simply not understood, and coordinated response dissolves. Weick's counter-question is what makes a group *resilient* to this: improvisation, "virtual role systems," an attitude of wisdom, respectful interaction.

**statement (framework)** — the failure mode against which collective mind [[object.collective-mind]] and HRO mindfulness [[object.high-reliability-organization]] are the protection.

**basis** — Weick's reading of the 1949 Mann Gulch fire [[source.weick-1993-mann-gulch]]. **Contested** — Basbøll and others allege scholarship problems, and a 2023 reanalysis argues the crew did not actually lose its structure; carried as an influential interpretation, not settled fact. *Support* — foundational to organizational sensemaking research. **[confirmed]**
