The *Homeric Hymns*, a collection of thirty-three anonymous hexameter poems addressed to individual gods, composed across the archaic and classical periods (the major hymns c. 7th–6th c. BCE). Conventionally called "Homeric" for their epic metre and diction, they are not the work of Homer. The longer hymns are primary narrative sources for several central myths: the *Hymn to Demeter* (the abduction of Persephone and the founding of the Eleusinian Mysteries), the *Hymn to Apollo* (the god's birth on Delos and the founding of the Delphic oracle), the *Hymn to Hermes* (his precocious first day, the cattle-theft and the invention of the lyre), the *Hymn to Aphrodite* (Aphrodite and Anchises), and the *Hymn to Dionysus*. **[confirmed as attribution]**
