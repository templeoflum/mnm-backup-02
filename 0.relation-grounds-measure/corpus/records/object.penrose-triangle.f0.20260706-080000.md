**statement (plain)** — The Penrose Triangle, also known as the Penrose Tribar or Reutersvärd Triangle, is a classic impossible object independently created by Swedish artist Oscar Reutersvärd in 1934 and later rediscovered and popularized by British psychiatrist Lionel Penrose and his son, mathematician Roger Penrose, in 1958. The figure depicts a triangular structure composed of three rectangular bars joined at right angles, creating a configuration that appears three-dimensional but cannot exist in Euclidean space. Each of the three bars appears to form a normal right-angle corner with its two adjacent bars, but the global configuration violates fundamental geometric constraints because following the structure around its complete circuit reveals contradictory depth relationships.
