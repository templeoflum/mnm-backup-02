**statement (plain)** — In Hawaiian tradition **Pele** (**Pelehonuamea**) is the fiercely passionate and jealous goddess of volcanoes, fire, and lightning. She and her family, the Pele clan, voyaged from **Kahiki**, the ancestral homeland, to the Hawaiian islands seeking a home, pursued across the ocean by her sea-goddess sister **Nāmaka**. She builds new land with her lava and consumes forests and villages in eruption, at last settling in the fire-pit of **Kīlauea**, where she creates and destroys in her passion.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: volcanoes, fire, lightning, dance, passion, creation and destruction. Of the Pele clan out of **Kahiki**; elder sister and rival **Nāmaka** (the sea); youngest sister **Hi'iaka**. Aliases: **Pelehonuamea** (Hawaiian). Hawaiian tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested in Beckwith, *Hawaiian Mythology* (1940) [[source.beckwith-1940]]; documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). These myths come through Hawaiian oral tradition collected and recorded in the 19th–20th c.; the details of Pele's voyage from Kahiki and of her contest with Nāmaka vary across the islands. **[confirmed as attribution]**
