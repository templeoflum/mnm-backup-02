**statement (plain)** — A channel factor is a small feature of the situation that gates whether a behavior happens — a minor facilitator that opens the channel, or a minor barrier that closes it. Because behavior flows through Lewinian channels, a tiny change at the gate can have an effect far larger than its apparent significance. Ross & Nisbett's example: students with strong intentions to get a tetanus shot mostly failed to act until given a campus map with the health center marked and asked to pick a time — trivial additions that opened the channel.

**relevance** — the empirical core of "channeling beats forcing" in human systems, and the concept from which Thaler & Sunstein's choice architecture descends ([[object.nudge]]). Builds on Lewin's force-field/channel theory ([[object.force-field-analysis]]) [[source.ross-nisbett-1991]] **[confirmed]**.
