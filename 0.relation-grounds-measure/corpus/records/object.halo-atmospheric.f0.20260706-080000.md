**statement (plain)** — Atmospheric halos are optical phenomena appearing as rings, arcs, or bright spots around the sun or moon, caused primarily by refraction and reflection of light through hexagonal ice crystals suspended in the upper atmosphere, typically in cirrus or cirrostratus clouds. The most common halo is the 22-degree halo, appearing as a ring with an angular radius of approximately 22 degrees from the sun or moon, resulting from light refraction through ice crystals with a prism angle of 60 degrees. Less common is the 46-degree halo, produced by light passing through crystals oriented differently.
