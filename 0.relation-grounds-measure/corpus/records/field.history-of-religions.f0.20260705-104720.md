**origin** — Mircea Eliade, *The Sacred and the Profane* (1957; Eng. trans. Trask 1959) and *Rites and Symbols of Initiation* (1958): *hierophany* (the manifestation of the sacred that "ontologically founds the world"), *homo religiosus*, initiation as a change of existential status via death-and-rebirth. Method comparative and, by his own admission, not historicist. [[source.eliade-1957]] [[source.eliade-1958]] **[confirmed]**

**codification / fault-line** — the post-1970s critical turn against Eliade's religionism: Jonathan Z. Smith, *Map Is Not Territory* (1978) & *To Take Place* (1987) — constructed/local place over universal cosmology; Russell McCutcheon, *Manufacturing Religion* (1997) — the "sui generis religion" critique; Steven Wasserstrom, *Religion after Religion* (1999) — the Eranos crypto-theology reading. Eliade is the named contested figure. [[source.smith-jz-1987]] [[source.mccutcheon-1997]] [[source.wasserstrom-1999]] **[confirmed]**

**door** — Eliade, *The Sacred and the Profane* (short); McCutcheon (1997) for the critique.

**lineage** — the Eliade-vs-critics debate is carried as the field's methodological fault line, exactly as [[field.western-esotericism]] carries Faivre-vs-Hanegraaff. Distinct from the Müller/Dumézil/oral-tradition lineage of [[field.comparative-mythology]] and from Husserlian [[field.phenomenology]].
