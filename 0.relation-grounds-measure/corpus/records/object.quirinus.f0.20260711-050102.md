**statement (plain)** — **Quirinus** is an ancient Roman god, held to be of **Sabine** origin, and a member of the **Archaic Triad** alongside **Jupiter** and **Mars** — the older grouping that preceded the Capitoline Triad of Jupiter, Juno, and Minerva. By the classical period he was identified with the deified **Romulus**, taken up after his disappearance from earth. He is associated with the *Quirites*, the body of Roman citizens in peace and in civic assembly, and was served by a dedicated priest, the Flamen Quirinalis.

**statement (attestation)** — member of the Archaic Triad with Jupiter and Mars; a god of Sabine origin; by the classical period equated with the deified Romulus, whose apotheosis Ovid narrates (*Metamorphoses* 14, *Fasti* 2). Associated with the *Quirites* — the Roman citizen-body in peace and civic assembly — and with the priesthood of the Flamen Quirinalis. His etymology (from *Quirites*, from the Sabine town Cures, or from *co-viri*, "assembled men") is disputed. Associated figures: Jupiter, Mars, Romulus. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested in Ovid (*Fasti* Book 2, *Metamorphoses* Book 14) and Livy; this is documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted fact. The identification with the deified Romulus and the derivation of his name are held as attested tradition, variously reported and disputed across the sources rather than settled fact. **[confirmed as attribution]**
