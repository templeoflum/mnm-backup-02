**statement (plain)** - skill grows through five stages. The novice follows context-free rules "just like a computer following a program." The advanced beginner picks up situational maxims. The competent performer adopts a plan to organize an overwhelming field, and takes responsibility. The proficient performer *sees* what's relevant intuitively but still deliberates the action. The expert no longer deliberates at all - "when things are proceeding normally, experts don't solve problems and don't make decisions; they do what normally works."

**basis** - Dreyfus & Dreyfus (1986); origin 1980 USAF report; a sixth "mastery" stage appears in later articulations (stage-naming variance carried as lineage). Chess illustration - masters distinguish ~50,000 position-types (1986; ~100,000 in Rousse-Dreyfus 2021; Simon's chunking estimate spans both, so the figure rides as a flagged conflict, not a picked number); the Kaplan experiment. Built as a critique of rule-based AI; applied to nursing by Benner. [[source.dreyfus-1986]] **[confirmed]** Prose kin: [[object.expert-performance]], [[object.tacit-knowing]].
