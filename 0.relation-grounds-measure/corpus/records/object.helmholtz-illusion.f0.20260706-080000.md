**statement (plain)** — The Helmholtz Illusion, also known as the Irradiation Illusion, encompasses two related perceptual phenomena described by German physicist and physiologist Hermann von Helmholtz in 1867. The first variant demonstrates that bright or white regions appear larger than physically equal dark regions, a phenomenon Helmholtz termed "irradiation." This effect occurs because light areas seem to encroach upon adjacent dark areas, causing the bright region to appear expanded. The second variant involves filled geometric shapes: a square filled with horizontal lines appears taller and narrower than an identical square filled with vertical lines, which appears wider and shorter.
