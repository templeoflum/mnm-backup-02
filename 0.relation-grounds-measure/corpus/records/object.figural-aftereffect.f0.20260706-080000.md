**statement (plain)** — The figural aftereffect, systematically characterized by Wolfgang Köhler and Hans Wallach in 1944, refers to a family of phenomena in which prolonged viewing of a figure (the inspection or adaptation figure) causes subsequently viewed test figures to appear shifted, distorted, or displaced away from the adapted position or configuration. These aftereffects can involve changes in perceived size, position, shape, orientation, or other spatial properties of test stimuli. Köhler and Wallach's original demonstrations showed that after fixating on a figure, a subsequently presented test figure appears displaced away from the location of the inspection figure, with the displacement magnitude decreasing with increasing spatial separation between inspection and test figures.
