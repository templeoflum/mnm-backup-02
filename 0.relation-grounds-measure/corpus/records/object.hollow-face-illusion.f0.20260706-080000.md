**statement (plain)** — The Hollow-Face Illusion, also known as the Concave Face Illusion or Rotating Mask Illusion, is a robust depth inversion phenomenon in which the concave interior surface of a face mask is perceived as a normal convex face, despite available depth cues indicating the true concave structure. First systematically studied by Richard Gregory, this illusion demonstrates the extraordinary strength of top-down face-processing expectations in overriding bottom-up sensory evidence. When observers view a rotating hollow mask, it appears to be a normal face rotating in the opposite direction, with the nose appearing to protrude rather than recede.

**statement (framework)** — category: depth 3d stereoscopic; perceptual mechanism(s): depth-cue-conflict, top-down-expectation, face-processing, predictive-coding.
