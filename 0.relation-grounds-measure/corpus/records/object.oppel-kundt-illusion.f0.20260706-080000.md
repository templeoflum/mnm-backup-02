**statement (plain)** — The Oppel-Kundt Illusion, also known as the Filled Space Illusion, was first described by Johann Joseph Oppel in 1855 and later investigated systematically by August Kundt. The illusion demonstrates that a linear spatial extent filled with regularly spaced elements such as dots, lines, or other markers appears substantially longer than an equal empty extent, with magnitude typically ranging from 5-15% overestimation depending on the number and spacing of filling elements. The effect is most pronounced with moderate filling densities and diminishes with very sparse or very dense element arrangements.
