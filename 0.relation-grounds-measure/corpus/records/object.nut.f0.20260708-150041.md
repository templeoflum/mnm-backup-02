**statement (plain)** — **Nut** is the sky goddess, her star-covered body arched over the earth as the vault of the heavens. She is the daughter of **Shu** (air) and **Tefnut** (moisture), and the sister-wife of **Geb**, the earth; from their union come **Osiris**, **Isis**, **Set**, and **Nephthys**. Each evening she swallows the sun (**Ra**), which passes through her body in the night, and each dawn she gives birth to it again. She belongs to the Heliopolitan **Ennead** and stretches protectively over the dead.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: the sky, the heavens, and the daily cycle of the sun. Iconography: a woman with a star-covered body arched over the earth, often supported by Shu and stretched above the reclining Geb. Family: daughter of **Shu** and **Tefnut**; sister-wife of **Geb**; mother of **Osiris**, **Isis**, **Set**, and **Nephthys**. The swallowing of the sun at dusk and its rebirth at dawn is a standard element of the solar and funerary tradition from the Pyramid Texts onward. No standard *interpretatio graeca* is established. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Egyptian mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested from the Pyramid Texts [[source.pyramid-texts]] onward and present in the Book of the Dead [[source.book-of-the-dead]]; the standard reference treatment is Wilkinson [[source.wilkinson-2003]]. Her role as the arched sky and mother of the Osirian gods is established; the swallowing and rebirth of the sun and her genealogy are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
