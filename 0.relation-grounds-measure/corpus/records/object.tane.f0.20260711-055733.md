**statement (plain)** — In Māori tradition **Tāne** (Tāne Mahuta) is the god of forests and birds who at last forced **Rangi** and **Papa** apart, lying on his back and thrusting his father skyward with his legs so that light and living things could fill the world between them. He clothed his mother Papa with trees and peopled the forest with birds and insects. In many tellings he shapes the first woman from the earth and so begins the line of humankind.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: forests, light, life, birds, the making of humans. Son of **Rangi** and **Papa**; brother of **Tū**, **Tangaroa**, **Rongo**, and **Tāwhirimātea**. Aliases: **Tāne Mahuta** (Māori). Cognate with the Hawaiian **Kāne** (recorded separately). Māori tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested in Grey, *Polynesian Mythology* (1855) [[source.grey-1855]]; documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). These myths come through Māori oral tradition collected and recorded in the 19th–20th c. (Grey and later scholars); the account of the separation and of Tāne's making of the first woman varies across iwi. NOTE the cognate: **Tāne ≈ the Hawaiian Kāne** (recorded separately). **[confirmed as attribution]**
