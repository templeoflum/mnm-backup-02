**statement (plain)** — Monocular Rivalry is a perceptual phenomenon in which two or more overlapping patterns presented simultaneously to a single eye produce alternating perception, with one pattern dominating awareness while the other is suppressed, followed by spontaneous reversals. Unlike binocular rivalry, which involves competition between different images presented to different eyes, monocular rivalry demonstrates that perceptual competition and alternation can occur even when both competing stimuli fall on the same retina. This finding has important theoretical implications, indicating that rivalry is not simply a matter of interocular competition but can arise from more central mechanisms involving competition between different stimulus interpretations or feature representations.
