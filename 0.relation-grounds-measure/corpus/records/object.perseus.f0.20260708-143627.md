**statement (plain)** — **Perseus** (Greek *Περσεύς*) is an Argive hero: son of **Zeus** and **Danaë**, daughter of Acrisius of Argos. Warned that his grandson would kill him, Acrisius shuts Danaë away, but Zeus reaches her as a **shower of gold**, and Perseus is born; grandfather and mother are cast adrift in a chest and wash ashore on Seriphos. Grown, Perseus is sent by King **Polydectes** to fetch the head of the **Gorgon Medusa**, whose gaze turns beholders to stone. Aided by **Athena** and **Hermes** — a mirror-bright shield, winged sandals, the **cap of invisibility** (Hades' *kynē*), and a special pouch (*kibisis*) — he beheads Medusa while looking only at her reflection. Flying home with the head, he finds **Andromeda** chained to a rock as sacrifice to a sea-monster, slays the monster, and takes her to wife. He later uses the petrifying head against his enemies and, in fulfilment of the prophecy, accidentally kills Acrisius with a discus.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: heroic quest, monster-slaying, divine aid. Parentage: Zeus and Danaë of Argos. Key attributes and gifts: **Medusa's head** (the *gorgoneion*, which petrifies its beholders and is later fixed on Athena's aegis), the mirror-shield, the winged sandals, the **cap of invisibility**, and the *harpē* (curved sword). Companions of the deed: the aid of **Athena** and **Hermes**, and the **Graeae**, whom he compels to reveal the way. The winged horse **Pegasus** springs from Medusa's severed neck. The narrative is attested across Hesiod and Pindar and given fullest connected form in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca; details vary across sources. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in the Hesiodic corpus [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and later poets, and systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The sequence of gifts, aids, and episodes is attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
