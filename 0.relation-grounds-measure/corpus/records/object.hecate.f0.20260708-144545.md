**statement (plain)** — **Hecate** (Greek *Ἑκάτη*) is the goddess of witchcraft, crossroads, the night, ghosts, and magic. Hesiod singles her out for a long passage of praise (*Theogony* 411–452), honoring her across sky, earth, and sea and calling her privileged above others by Zeus. In later tradition she is triple-formed, bearing torches and keys and attended by dogs, and she is associated with the moon and closely linked to **Artemis**.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: witchcraft, crossroads, the night, ghosts, and magic. Roles: a widely-honored goddess granted portions in sky, earth, and sea (Hesiod); later chthonic and liminal power of crossroads and the restless dead. Etymology: Greek *Hekátē*, of disputed derivation; she has no fixed Roman *interpretatio*, being Latinized as **Hecate** and at times conflated with **Trivia** ("of the three ways"). Her later attributes are torches, keys, and dogs, and her triple form. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The Theogony 411–452 passage of honor is attested; the triple form, torches, keys, dogs, and the associations with the moon and Artemis are attested tradition, variable across sources and largely later, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
