**statement (plain)** — **Oduduwa** (Odùduwà) is the **Orisha** who completed the making of the earth in Yoruba tradition. When **Obatala** fell asleep drunk on the task, Oduduwa descended with soil in a snail shell, a five-toed hen to scatter it, and a palm nut to plant, and so finished the dry land. He founded **Ile-Ife**, the sacred cradle-city of the Yoruba, and is remembered both as an Orisha and as the ancestral first king from whom the Yoruba royal lines descend.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: earth, foundation, the Yoruba nation, kingship, completion. Completer of the earth after **Obatala** faltered; founder of **Ile-Ife**; progenitor of the Yoruba kings; carried both as a deity and as a historicized founding ancestor. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — documented in Idowu (1962) and Karade (1994); this is documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). It is an oral tradition carried in the **Ifá** corpus (the *odu*) and recorded by scholars; Oduduwa is carried both as a deity and as a historicized founding ancestor, and the account varies between Yorubaland and the diaspora traditions. **[confirmed as attribution]**
