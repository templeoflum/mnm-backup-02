James Ladyman, "Mathematical Structuralism and the Identity of Indiscernibles," *Analysis* 65, no. 3 (2005): 218–221.

Contains — the influential reply to the Keränen/Burgess identity objection: symmetric positions like i and −i are **weakly discernible** — they stand in an irreflexive relation to each other (each is the additive inverse of the *other*, not of itself) — so they are two, not one, without needing to be absolutely discernible by a distinguishing property. Imports Quine/Saunders weak discernibility into the structuralism debate. Contested by Ketland (2006); primitive-identity reply in Leitgeb & Ladyman (2008).
