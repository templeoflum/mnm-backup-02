**statement (plain)** — knowledge comes in two kinds that behave very differently. *Explicit* knowledge can be written down, stored, and handed over without the knower present — a recipe, a manual, a theorem. *Tacit* knowledge is personal and contextual; it lives in a skilled person and can usually only be picked up by doing, watching, and practising — knowing how to swim, weld, or diagnose by feel. The classic ancestor of this split is Ryle's distinction between *knowing how* (an ability) and *knowing that* (a fact). Polanyi's point is sharper: the explicit always rests on a tacit base, so the two are not symmetric opposites.

**statement** — the distinction separates *explicit/codified* knowledge — articulable in propositions, transferable without the knower, centrally storable — from *tacit* knowledge — personal, context-bound, acquired through practical experience, distributed across knowing subjects and not easily aggregated. Its philosophical root is Ryle's *knowing-how* vs *knowing-that*: knowing-how is an intelligent capacity that need not reduce to consulting explicit rules. Polanyi radicalizes it by holding that explicit knowledge is itself rooted in tacit knowing.

**basis** — Ryle's side rests on the *regress* argument against the "intellectualist legend": if every intelligent act required first contemplating an explicit proposition, that contemplation would itself need to be done intelligently, and so on without end — so knowing-how cannot reduce to knowing-that. Polanyi's side rests on the irreducibility cases of tacit knowing (skills and recognition that resist full articulation) and on the claim that rules must be tacitly applied. The management framing (Nonaka–Takeuchi) is supported empirically by studies of knowledge conversion in firms rather than by argument. **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — *knowing-how / knowing-that* drawn by Gilbert Ryle, "Knowing How and Knowing That" (1945–46) and *The Concept of Mind* (1949, ch. 2) [[source.ryle-1949]]. *Tacit / explicit* developed by Polanyi, *Personal Knowledge* (1958) [[source.polanyi-1958]] and *The Tacit Dimension* (1966) [[source.polanyi-1966]]. Carried into organization theory by Ikujiro Nonaka and Hirotaka Takeuchi via the SECI model (from 1990; *The Knowledge-Creating Company*, 1995). **[confirmed as attribution]** for the Nonaka–Takeuchi framing (their works not opened here). **[confirmed]** for the Ryle and Polanyi lineage.
