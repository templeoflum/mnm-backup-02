**statement (plain)** — **Hades** (Greek *Ἅιδης*) is the lord of the underworld and ruler of the dead — his name given also to the realm itself. He is a son of the Titans **Kronos** and **Rhea** and thus a brother of **Zeus**, **Poseidon**, **Demeter**, **Hera**, and **Hestia**. After the Olympians overthrow the Titans, the three brothers cast lots for the cosmos: Zeus takes the sky, Poseidon the sea, and Hades the **underworld** (*Iliad* 15.187–193). He abducts **Persephone**, daughter of Demeter, to be his queen — the myth that grounds the cycle of the seasons. He possesses the **cap (or helm) of invisibility**, which renders its wearer unseen. Unlike the other great gods he does not dwell on Olympus but reigns apart in his own realm among the dead.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: the underworld, the dead, and (through his realm's associations) hidden wealth beneath the earth. Key attributes: the **helm of invisibility** (given him by the Cyclopes), the throne of the underworld, the guard-dog **Cerberus**, and the *bident* (in later iconography); the epithet *Plouton* ("the wealthy"), a euphemistic name used to avoid the ill-omened "Hades." Parentage: son of Kronos and Rhea, brother of Zeus; husband of Persephone. He is not counted among the Olympian dwellers on the mountain. The Roman *interpretatio* identified him with **Pluto** (also Dis Pater). **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and Homer's epics and the Homeric Hymns [[source.homer]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The division-of-the-cosmos and the Pluto *interpretatio* are well established; the etymology of the name is disputed, and the *Plouton* euphemism and the details of the underworld tradition should be read as attested tradition, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
