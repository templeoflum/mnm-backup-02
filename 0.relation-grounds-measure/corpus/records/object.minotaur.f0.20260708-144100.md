**statement (plain)** — the **Minotaur** (Greek *Μινώταυρος*, "bull of Minos") is a monster with the body of a man and the head of a **bull**, born to **Pasiphae**, wife of King **Minos** of Crete, after Poseidon caused her to conceive an unnatural passion for the **Cretan Bull**. To hide this shame, Minos has the craftsman **Daedalus** build the **Labyrinth**, an inextricable maze, and confines the Minotaur within it. The creature is fed on the **youths and maidens** sent as tribute from **Athens**. At last **Theseus** comes among the tribute, and with the help of Minos' daughter **Ariadne** — who gives him a thread to retrace his path — he enters the Labyrinth, kills the Minotaur, and escapes.

**statement (attestation)** — domain: the monstrous hybrid, the maze and its confinement, and the tribute exacted in death. Genealogy: son of Pasiphae by the Cretan Bull; his given name in some sources is **Asterius** (or Asterion). Etymology: Greek *Minōtauros*, a compound of **Minos** and *tauros*, "bull." He has no distinct Roman name, being carried over as *Minotaurus*. The story belongs to the **Theseus** cycle and to the Cretan legends of Minos and Daedalus. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]] and the wider Theseus tradition; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The birth, the Labyrinth, and the slaying by Theseus are attested; the details of the tribute and Ariadne's aid are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
