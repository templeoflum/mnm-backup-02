**statement (plain)** — humans, too, coordinate through the traces work leaves in a shared medium: a sequence of trades builds a partition wall, each "paying heed to the material evidence of work previously accomplished" without talking to the next; open-source developers coordinate largely through the codebase, issue tracker, and build state rather than conversation; Wikipedia's imperfect article is itself the stimulus to the next editor. Christensen frames this as a "heed concept" (Ryle), deliberately shorn of the insect stimulus-response model.

**statement (framework)** — the load-bearing finding is that human stigmergy is **real but partial**: it always *supplements* explicit coordination, never replaces it. Christensen names a "trinity" — stigmergy · articulation work [[object.articulation-work]] · awareness — none interchangeable; FLOSS studies find coordination *blends* implicit (stigmergic) and explicit mechanisms. Humans also differ from insects in *intent*: people deliberately shape traces for others (a conspicuously-hung cable, a clear commit message), adding a semantic layer insects lack — the sharpest debate over whether "human stigmergy" stretches the term.

**basis** — Christensen's construction ethnography [[source.christensen-2013]]; FLOSS coordination [[source.bolici-howison-crowston-2016]]; Parunak's survey [[source.parunak-2006]]. *Limits* — Parunak's near-tautology ("hard to find a human institution that is *not* stigmergic") is itself the warning: a concept explaining everything explains nothing; rigor is in specifying medium/trace/decay/rule per case. **[confirmed]**
