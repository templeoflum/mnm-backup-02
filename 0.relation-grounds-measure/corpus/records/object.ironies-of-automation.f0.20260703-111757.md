**statement (plain)** — Automation is supposed to remove the human's burden, but the "classic" approach produces the opposite in three ways. (1) The designer automates everything routine and leaves the operator only the tasks too hard to automate — the residue is the *most* demanding part. (2) Manual and cognitive skills decay when unused, so the operator is least practiced exactly when a takeover is needed. (3) The operator is asked to monitor a highly reliable automated process for rare failures — a task humans are notoriously bad at — and then to diagnose and act under time pressure at the moment things have already gone wrong.

**relevance** — the founding caution for any supervisory-control or dispatch design: more automation reshapes the human's job rather than shrinking it, and the residual role must be designed for, not assumed away. Motivates situation-awareness support ([[object.situation-awareness]]), good interfaces ([[object.ecological-interface-design]]), and keeping the operator meaningfully in the loop [[source.bainbridge-1983]] **[confirmed]**.
