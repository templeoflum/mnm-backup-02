**statement (plain)** — **Krishna** (Sanskrit *Kṛṣṇa*, "the dark one") is the eighth **avatar** of **Vishnu**, and in the devotional traditions is worshipped as the supreme god in his own right. He appears in two great aspects: the **divine cowherd** — the mischievous, flute-playing child of Vrindavana who steals butter and dances with the cowherd-maidens (**gopis**), the playful **lover** of Radha and the gopis — and the **prince and warrior** of Dvaraka, kinsman and counsellor of the **Pandavas**. On the field of Kurukshetra he serves as **Arjuna's charioteer** and delivers the **Bhagavad Gita**, the discourse on duty, devotion, and the self.

**statement (attestation)** — role: eighth avatar of Vishnu; cowherd-god, prince, charioteer, and teacher. Key attributes: dark/blue complexion; the **flute** (bansuri); the peacock feather; the cowherds and the **gopis**; the chariot of Arjuna; the discus **Sudarshana**. Texts: the prince and teacher of the **Mahabharata** — where the **Bhagavad Gita** is his discourse to **Arjuna** — while the childhood and cowherd myths are told in the **Harivamsha** (an appendix to the Mahabharata) and, most fully, in the **Bhagavata Purana**. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Hindu mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested chiefly in the **Mahabharata** [[source.mahabharata]] — as the Pandavas' ally, Arjuna's charioteer, and the speaker of the Bhagavad Gita — with the cowherd-and-gopi cycle elaborated in the Harivamsha and the Bhagavata Purana. His identity as the eighth avatar of Vishnu and the fusion of the prince, cowherd, and supreme-god aspects are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
