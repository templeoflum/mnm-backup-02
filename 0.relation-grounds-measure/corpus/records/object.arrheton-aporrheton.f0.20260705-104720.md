**statement (plain)** — two Greek adjectives name the mystery secret: *arrheton* (ἄρρητον, "unspeakable") and *aporrheton* (ἀπόρρητον, "forbidden"). Burkert treats them as near-interchangeable and names the paradox: a mystery "must not be betrayed, but it cannot really be betrayed because told in public it would appear insignificant"; violations did no harm to the institutions, while protection of the secrecy greatly added to their prestige.

**basis** — Bielawski (2016) catalogues the evidence; Burkert, *Ancient Mystery Cults* (1987, p. 9). In *Greek Religion* (1985, p. 207) Burkert leaves open whether the sacred was forbidden (*aporrheton*) or unspeakable (*arrheton*) in an absolute sense — legal prohibition vs. deeper ineffability.
