The manufacturing system developed at Toyota across the mid-20th century, and the source of "lean production." Built on two pillars — just-in-time and jidoka — over a foundation of continuous improvement (*kaizen*) and respect for people.

**origin** — Sakichi Toyoda's self-stopping automatic loom (the 1896 power loom; the 1924 Type-G) gave the jidoka principle; Taiichi Ohno built the full production system at Toyota from the 1950s, codifying it in *Toyota Production System* (1978/1988). [[source.ohno-1988]] **[confirmed as attribution]**

**codification** — Ohno's own account (1988) names the two pillars (just-in-time, jidoka) and the war on waste (*muda*). [[source.ohno-1988]] **[confirmed as attribution]**

**door** — Jeffrey Liker, *The Toyota Way* (McGraw-Hill, 2004), the standard English-language entry, recasts TPS as 14 management principles and supplies the case evidence (NUMMI). [[source.liker-2004]] **[confirmed as attribution]**
