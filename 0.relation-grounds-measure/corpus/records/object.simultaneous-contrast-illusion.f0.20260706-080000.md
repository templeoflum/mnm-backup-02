**statement (plain)** — The Simultaneous Contrast Illusion is the foundational phenomenon in lightness perception whereby an identical gray patch appears lighter when surrounded by a dark background and darker when surrounded by a light background. First systematically described by French chemist Michel Eugène Chevreul in 1839, this illusion demonstrates that perceived lightness is not determined solely by the absolute luminance of a surface, but by its relationship to surrounding luminance values. The effect is "simultaneous" because the contrast occurs when both the target and surround are viewed at the same time, distinguishing it from successive contrast effects that occur after temporal adaptation.
