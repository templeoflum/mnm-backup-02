**statement (plain)** — **Fenrir** (Old Norse *Fenrir*, also *Fenrisúlfr*, "Fenris-wolf") is a monstrous wolf, the son of **Loki** and the giantess **Angrboða** and brother of the serpent **Jörmungandr** and of **Hel**. Snorri tells in *Gylfaginning* that the gods, foreseeing danger, reared him among themselves, but as he grew they resolved to bind him; he burst the fetters Leyding and Dromi, so the dwarves made the slender, unbreakable fetter **Gleipnir**. The wolf would let himself be bound only if one of the gods laid a hand in his jaws as a pledge, and **Tyr** alone did so — losing his hand when the wolf found himself held fast. At **Ragnarök** Fenrir breaks loose and swallows **Odin**, and is then killed by Odin's son **Vidar**, who avenges his father.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: bound doom, the devouring wolf of the end-times. Key relations: son of **Loki** and **Angrboða**; sibling of **Jörmungandr** and **Hel**; bound by **Gleipnir** at the cost of **Tyr**'s hand; slayer of **Odin** and slain in turn by **Vidar**. The wolf's doom and the swallowing of Odin appear in the Poetic Edda in *Völuspá* and *Vafþrúðnismál*, and his parentage is named in *Lokasenna* and *Hyndluljóð*; Snorri's *Gylfaginning* supplies the extended narrative of the three fetters, Gleipnir, and Tyr's pledged hand. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Norse mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested across the Poetic Edda [[source.poetic-edda]] and systematized in Snorri's Prose Edda [[source.prose-edda]]; the standard reference treatment is Lindow [[source.lindow-2001]]. The binding by Gleipnir and Tyr's pledged hand come chiefly from Snorri's *Gylfaginning* and should be read as attested tradition through his 13th-c. Christian framing, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
