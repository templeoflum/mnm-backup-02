**statement (plain)** — The Ternus Effect, discovered by Josef Ternus in 1926, is a classic apparent motion phenomenon that demonstrates two qualitatively distinct types of motion perception from sequential displays of dot arrays. The standard Ternus display consists of three aligned dots presented in two alternating frames: the first frame shows dots at positions 1, 2, and 3, while the second frame shows dots at positions 2, 3, and 4. Depending on the interstimulus interval between frames, observers perceive either element motion, in which the outer dots appear to move individually while the central dots remain stationary, or group motion, in which all three dots appear to move together as a unified group.
