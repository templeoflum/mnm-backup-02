**statement (plain)** — In Māori tradition **Tāwhirimātea** is the god of storms and winds and the one son who refused to part **Rangi** and **Papa**. Enraged that his brothers had separated their parents, he followed his father Rangi into the sky, fathered the winds and storm-clouds, and descended in fury to make war upon those below. He drove **Tāne**'s forests down, chased **Tangaroa** into the sea and **Rongo** into the earth; only **Tū** stood unbroken against him.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: storms, wind, weather, revenge. Son of **Rangi** and **Papa**; brother of **Tāne**, **Tū**, **Tangaroa**, and **Rongo**; father of the winds and storm-clouds. Aliases: **Tāwhirimātea** (Māori). Māori tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested in Grey, *Polynesian Mythology* (1855) [[source.grey-1855]]; documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). These myths come through Māori oral tradition collected and recorded in the 19th–20th c. (Grey and later scholars); the roster of the storm-god's children and the sequence of his attacks vary across iwi. **[confirmed as attribution]**
