**statement (plain)** — **Fionn mac Cumhaill** (anglicized **Finn**) is the hero-leader of the **Fianna**, the roving warrior-band of the **Fenian Cycle** (the *Fiannaíocht*). As a boy he burns his thumb cooking the **Salmon of Knowledge** for the poet Finn Éces and, sucking it, receives all wisdom — thereafter gaining foreknowledge by putting his thumb to his mouth (the *imbas forosnai*). He leads the Fianna in the reign of **Cormac mac Airt**, and is the father of the poet-warrior **Oisín** and grandfather of **Oscar**. The cycle includes the tale of **Diarmuid and Gráinne**, in which Fionn's promised bride elopes with his follower Diarmuid.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: heroic leadership, hunting and the wild, poetic wisdom and foreknowledge. Key attributes: the wisdom-thumb (from the **Salmon of Knowledge**); the **Fianna** warrior-band; the hounds **Bran** and **Sceólang**. Associations: father of **Oisín**; leader under **Cormac mac Airt**; rival/lord in the Diarmuid and Gráinne tale. Text: the **Fenian (Fionn) Cycle** — chiefly **Acallam na Senórach** and related tales. Etymology: *Fionn*, "fair, white, bright." Irish tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]** *[the Fenian Cycle is a distinct fourth cycle of Irish tradition; the misattribution to the Ulster Cycle was corrected at settle — `source.fenian-cycle` created + re-pointed here, 20260711]*

**basis** — a figure of Irish mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in the Fenian Cycle — chiefly Acallam na Senórach; the standard reference treatment is MacKillop, *Dictionary of Celtic Mythology*. The Salmon of Knowledge, the leadership of the Fianna, and the paternity of Oisín are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
