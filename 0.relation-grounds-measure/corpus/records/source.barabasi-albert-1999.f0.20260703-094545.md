Albert-László Barabási & Réka Albert, "Emergence of scaling in random networks," *Science* 286, no. 5439 (15 October 1999): 509–512.

Contains — the observation that the vertex connectivities of many large real networks follow a scale-free power law P(k) ~ k^−γ (with γ typically between 2 and 3), unlike the exponentially bounded degree distribution of a random (Erdős–Rényi) graph. Attributes this to two mechanisms absent from classical random-graph models: networks **grow** by continual addition of nodes, and new nodes attach **preferentially** to already well-connected nodes. Shows a simple model with both mechanisms yields a stationary power law with γ = 3.

Access — DOI 10.1126/science.286.5439.509. One of the most-cited papers in network science (~38,000 citations). **[confirmed]**
