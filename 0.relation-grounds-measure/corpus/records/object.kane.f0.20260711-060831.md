**statement (plain)** — In Hawaiian tradition **Kāne** is the god of life, fresh water, and sunlight, the leading one of the four great gods (with **Kū**, **Lono**, and **Kanaloa**). He is the generative life-force and the creator of the first man, the giver of life; he is the fresh water that sustains all living things. With his companion **Kanaloa** he opened springs across the land.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: life, fresh water, sunlight, forests, procreation. Highest of the four great gods (**Kū**, **Lono**, **Kanaloa**); companion **Kanaloa** in opening water-springs. Cognate with the Māori **Tāne** (recorded separately). Hawaiian tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested in Beckwith, *Hawaiian Mythology* (1940) [[source.beckwith-1940]]; documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). These myths come through Hawaiian oral tradition collected and recorded in the 19th–20th c.; the accounts of Kāne's creation of man and his many "Kāne forms" vary across the islands. NOTE the cognate: **Kāne ≈ the Māori Tāne** (recorded separately). **[confirmed as attribution]**
