**statement (plain)** — The Fick Illusion is a geometric size illusion in which a vertical line appears longer than a horizontal line of equal physical length when presented in an inverted-T configuration. First described by German physiologist Adolf Eugen Fick in 1851, this illusion represents one of the earliest documented anisotropies in spatial perception. The effect is robust across viewing conditions and demonstrates a systematic bias in the perception of vertical versus horizontal extent.

**statement (framework)** — category: geometric size length; perceptual mechanism(s): orientation-selectivity, spatial-filtering.
