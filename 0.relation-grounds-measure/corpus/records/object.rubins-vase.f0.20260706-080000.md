**statement (plain)** — Rubin's Vase, introduced by Danish psychologist Edgar Rubin in 1915, is the paradigmatic demonstration of figure-ground organization and perceptual bistability. The image consists of a black-and-white design that can be perceived either as a white vase against a black background or as two black face profiles facing each other against a white background. Critically, observers cannot perceive both interpretations simultaneously—the visual system must designate one region as "figure" and the other as "ground," with the shared contour belonging exclusively to the figural region.
