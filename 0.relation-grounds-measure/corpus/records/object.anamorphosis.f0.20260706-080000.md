**statement (plain)** — Anamorphosis refers to a class of perspective techniques that produce images appearing severely distorted when viewed frontally but resolving into recognizable scenes when observed from a specific oblique angle or reflected in a curved mirror. Dating back to the Renaissance period, anamorphic art exploits the geometric principles of linear perspective in reverse, deliberately creating extreme foreshortening or curvilinear distortion that becomes perceptually corrected only from the intended viewpoint. The technique exists in two primary forms: perspective anamorphosis, requiring viewing from an acute angle to the picture plane, and mirror anamorphosis, requiring reflection in a cylindrical or conical mirror positioned at a specific location.
