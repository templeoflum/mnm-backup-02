**origin** — Plato, *Theaetetus* (c. 369 BCE) — the first sustained inquiry into "what is knowledge?", canvassing and rejecting knowledge as perception, as true belief, and as true belief with an account (*logos*) — the seed of the justified-true-belief analysis. [[source.plato-theaetetus]] **[confirmed as attribution]**

**codification** — René Descartes, *Meditations* (1641) — the method of doubt and the foundationalist search for indubitable grounds set the modern agenda; Edmund Gettier's "Is Justified True Belief Knowledge?" (1963) then broke the classical JTB analysis, launching contemporary analytic epistemology. [[source.descartes-1641]] [[source.gettier-1963]] **[confirmed as attribution]**

**door** — Robert Audi, *Epistemology: A Contemporary Introduction* (Routledge) — the standard survey: sources of knowledge, the structure of justification (foundationalism vs. coherentism), internalism/externalism, skepticism. [[source.audi-2011]]
