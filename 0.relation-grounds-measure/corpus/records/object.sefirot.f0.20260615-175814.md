**statement (plain)** — the *sefirot* are ten emanations or "vessels" through which *Ein-Sof* (the infinite, unknowable divine) unfolds into creation. In *Sefer Yetzirah* they are primarily "numbers"; in later Kabbalah they become dynamic, mutually-defined attributes ("their end is in their beginning"), each known only by its function and position within the system.

**basis** — *Sefer Yetzirah* (the ten *sefirot belimah*); developed in the *Sefer ha-Bahir* and the *Zohar*; read by Scholem as a closed emanative structure. [[source.sefer-yetzirah]] [[source.scholem-kabbalah-1974]] **[confirmed]** *(status `doctrinal`: recorded as the tradition's tenet, not asserted.)*

**lineage (research07 re-mine, 20260704)** — Busi reads *sefirah* via the divine-action verbs as "act of writing," not merely "number" — the sefirot as acts of divine cosmogonic writing, paralleling Philo ([[source.busi-2007]], **[confirmed as attribution]**). Scholem's synonym-catalog (*ma'amarot, dibburim, shemot, orot, kohot, ketarim...*) underscores that each sefirah is defined by function and position within the system — relational constitution; he insisted the term "is not connected with the Greek 'sphere'" and, in SY, marks "modifications of the will of God," not ordinary emanations.
