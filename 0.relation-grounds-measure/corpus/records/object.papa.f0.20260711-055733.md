**statement (plain)** — In Māori tradition **Papa** (Papatūānuku, the Earth Mother) lay in primordial embrace with **Rangi**, the Sky Father, until their children forced them apart to let in the light. Papa remains below as the earth itself, her body the land on which all life is nurtured. She grieves the parting; in some tellings her warmth is the longing that rises from the earth toward Rangi above, answered by his falling tears.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: earth, fertility, motherhood, foundation. Consort of **Rangi**; mother of **Tāne**, **Tū**, **Tangaroa**, **Rongo**, **Tāwhirimātea**, and the other atua. Aliases: **Papatūānuku** (Māori). Māori tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested in Grey, *Polynesian Mythology* (1855) [[source.grey-1855]]; documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). These myths come through Māori oral tradition collected and recorded in the 19th–20th c. (Grey and later scholars); details of the earth-mother's role vary across iwi and island traditions. **[confirmed as attribution]**
