**statement (plain)** — Pinna's Overlapping Illusion is one of several geometric and chromatic illusions discovered by Italian perceptual psychologist Baingio Pinna, who has contributed extensively to the catalog of visual phenomena involving color spreading, shape perception, and spatial interactions. The overlapping illusion specifically involves configurations where multiple shapes or regions overlap in particular ways, producing systematic distortions in perceived size, position, or spatial relationships. When shapes partially occlude one another with specific edge configurations and contrast relationships, observers may misperceive the relative sizes of the overlapping regions, or may experience illusory shifts in the perceived positions of boundaries.
