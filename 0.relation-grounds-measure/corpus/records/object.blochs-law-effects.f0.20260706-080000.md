**statement (plain)** — Bloch's Law describes a fundamental principle of temporal integration in visual perception: within a critical duration, the visual system integrates luminance over time such that perceived brightness depends on the total energy (luminance multiplied by duration) rather than either parameter alone. Formally, I × t = k, where I is stimulus intensity, t is duration, and k is a constant threshold. This means that a brief, intense flash can appear equally bright as a longer, dimmer presentation if their products are equal.
