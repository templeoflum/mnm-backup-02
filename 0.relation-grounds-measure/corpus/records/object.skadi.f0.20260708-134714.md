**statement (plain)** — **Skaði** (Old Norse *Skaði*) is a giantess associated with skiing, snowshoes, winter, mountains, and hunting, the daughter of the giant **Þjazi**. After the gods kill her father, Snorri tells that she comes armed to Asgard to seek redress; in settlement she is allowed to choose a husband from among the gods by their feet alone, and, aiming for **Baldr**, chooses **Njörðr** — an ill-matched pairing, for she loves her father's mountains at **Þrymheimr** and he his shore at **Nóatún**. In the account of **Loki**'s binding, it is Skaði who sets the venomous serpent above the bound Loki so that its poison drips upon him.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: winter, mountains, skiing and snowshoes, and hunting. Key relations: daughter of **Þjazi**; wife of **Njörðr** in the mismatched marriage; the one who fastens the serpent over the bound **Loki**. Snorri preserves the armed coming for redress, the choice of husband by the feet, and the serpent; her name appears in the Poetic Edda in *Grímnismál* and *Lokasenna*. Her name has been connected to the land-name **Scandinavia** / *Skaðin-*, an established but not certain philological proposal. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Norse mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested across the Poetic Edda [[source.poetic-edda]] and systematized in Snorri's Prose Edda [[source.prose-edda]]; the standard reference treatment is Lindow [[source.lindow-2001]]. The redress, the marriage-choice, and the serpent over Loki come chiefly from Snorri and should be read as attested tradition through his 13th-c.
