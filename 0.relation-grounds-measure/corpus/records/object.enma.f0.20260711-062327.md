**statement (plain)** — **Enma-Ō** (Enma Daiō) is the king of the underworld and judge of the dead. He is the Japanese Buddhist form of **Yama**, the Hindu and Buddhist king of the dead. He weighs each soul's deeds and determines its fate among the six realms of rebirth, presiding over the Buddhist hells (Jigoku).

**statement (attestation)** — domains: the underworld, judgment, death, karma, justice. Enma-Ō is the Japanese Buddhist form of Yama; he judges the deceased and determines their rebirth among the six realms, presiding over the Buddhist hells (Jigoku). **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — Enma is documented in Ashkenazi (2003) and this is documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). He is a Buddhist figure of non-Japanese origin — the Japanese form of the Hindu and Buddhist Yama — not a native Shinto kami; his role as judge-king of the dead and warden of the Buddhist hells belongs to the living Buddhist tradition, not the 8th-century Shinto chronicles. **[confirmed as attribution]**
