**statement (plain)** — The tilt aftereffect, first systematically investigated by James J. Gibson and Mowbray Radner in 1937, is a fundamental orientation adaptation phenomenon in which prolonged viewing of lines or gratings tilted in one direction causes subsequently viewed vertical or near-vertical lines to appear tilted in the opposite direction. For example, after adapting to lines tilted 15 degrees clockwise from vertical, a truly vertical test line appears tilted counterclockwise.

**statement (framework)** — category: aftereffect adaptation; perceptual mechanism(s): neural-adaptation, orientation-selectivity.
