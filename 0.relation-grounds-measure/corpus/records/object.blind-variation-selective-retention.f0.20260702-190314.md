**statement (plain)** — creativity (and knowledge-growth generally) works like natural selection: ideas are generated as *blind* variations — produced without foresight of which will succeed — and the useful ones are *selectively retained*, the rest discarded. "Blind" does not mean random; it means the generator cannot know in advance which variant will work. A formal "generate broadly, then select" account.

**statement (framework)** — Campbell's BVSR (1960), elaborated across exceptional creativity by Simonton. Resonant with generate-then-select structures elsewhere (divergent-then-convergent thinking [[object.divergent-thinking]]).

**basis** — Campbell 1960 [[source.campbell-1960]]. **Contested** — Gabora and Weisberg argue creative variation is *not* truly blind (it is guided by expertise and partial insight) and that the Darwinian framing is inappropriate; carried as an influential but disputed model, not settled fact. **[confirmed]**

**sources** — [[source.campbell-1960]] (the BVSR model; contestation noted). **[confirmed]**
