**statement (plain)** — **Set** (also **Seth**) is the god of storm, disorder, the desert, and violence. He murders his brother **Osiris** and contests the throne with his nephew **Horus**, and is the great figure of chaos and hostility opposed to order. Yet he is ambivalent, not purely evil: he also stands in the prow of **Ra's** solar barque and defends it each night against the serpent **Apophis**, so that the sun may rise. He embodies the strength and violence that both threaten and protect the cosmic order.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: storms, the desert, foreign lands, disorder, and violence. Iconography: the composite **Set-animal** — an unidentified beast with a curved snout, tall square-topped ears, and a forked tail — or a man with its head. His killing of Osiris and his contest with Horus mark him as the agent of chaos; his defence of Ra's barque against Apophis marks his protective, ambivalent aspect. The Greeks by *interpretatio graeca* equated him with **Typhon**, the monstrous adversary of the gods. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Egyptian mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested from the Pyramid Texts [[source.pyramid-texts]] onward and in the funerary and mythological texts [[source.book-of-the-dead]]; the standard reference treatment is Wilkinson [[source.wilkinson-2003]]. The *interpretatio* with Typhon and the ambivalence (adversary of Osiris / defender of Ra) are established; the myths and his shifting reputation across periods are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
