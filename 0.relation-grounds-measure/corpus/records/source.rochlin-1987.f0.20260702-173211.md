Gene I. Rochlin, Todd R. La Porte, and Karlene H. Roberts, "The Self-Designing High-Reliability Organization: Aircraft Carrier Flight Operations at Sea," *Naval War College Review* 40, no. 4 (Autumn 1987): 76–90.

Contains — the earliest field report of the UC Berkeley **high reliability organization** program, from fieldwork aboard U.S. Navy nuclear aircraft carriers. Its "Authority Overlays" section is the locus classicus for distributed stop-authority: "Even the lowest rating on the deck has not only the authority but the obligation to suspend flight operations immediately, under the proper circumstances, without first clearing it with superiors… he will not be penalized for being wrong and will often be publicly congratulated if he is right." Also argues authority migrates to the person on the spot, while noting formal rank remains "the lubricant that makes the informal processes work."

Lineage — the program's later, more-cited statements are Roberts, *Organization Science* (1990) [[source.roberts-1990]] and the codification in Weick & Sutcliffe (2001) [[source.weick-sutcliffe-2001]]. This 1987 report is the primary carrier fieldwork both build on; previously named only inside [[source.roberts-1990]]'s lineage note, promoted to its own record 20260702. **[confirmed]**
