Paul Benacerraf, "What Numbers Could Not Be," *The Philosophical Review* 74, no. 1 (1965): 47–73.

Contains — the argument that since there are infinitely many equally-good ways to identify the natural numbers with pure sets (von Neumann, Zermelo, …), no particular identification can be *the* truth about what numbers are; therefore numbers are not objects at all but **positions in a structure**, and "all that matters" about them are their structural relations. The closing line: "there are no such things as numbers; which is not to say that there are not at least two prime numbers between 15 and 20." The founding motivation for structuralism about mathematics.
