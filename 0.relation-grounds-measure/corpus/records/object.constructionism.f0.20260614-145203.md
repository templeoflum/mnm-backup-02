**statement (plain)** — people learn best not by being told things but by making things — and especially by making things they can show, share, and talk about with others. Building a sand castle, a computer program, or a theory is itself how the understanding gets built. The made object and the conversation around it carry the learning as much as the making does.

**statement** — constructionism holds that the construction of knowledge in the head ([[constructivism]], from Piaget) happens "especially felicitously in a context where the learner is consciously engaged in constructing a public entity, whether it's a sand castle on the beach or a theory of the universe" (Papert & Harel, 1991). Papert & Harel explicitly warn that the catchy gloss "learning-by-making" is too thin — constructionism is meant as "much richer and more multifaceted, and very much deeper in its implications than could be conveyed by any such formula." **[confirmed]**

**basis** — the principle adds an "N" to constructivism: where Piaget's [[constructivism]] says knowledge is reconstructed by the learner rather than transmitted, Papert's constructionism says this reconstruction is most powerful when externalized into a shareable, manipulable artifact. The artifact makes the developing idea public, criticizable, and revisable; the discussion and critique around the product are part of the learning, not a sequel to it. The principle is established practice and design philosophy, not a formalized or proved result. **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — the underlying idea (knowledge built, not poured) descends from Jean Piaget, with whom Papert worked in Geneva (1958–1963). Papert introduced the constructionist program in *Mindstorms* (1980) [[source.papert-1980]]; the term "constructionism" is given its sharpest definition and explicitly situated against constructivism in Papert & Harel's "Situating Constructionism" (1991) [[source.papert-harel-1991]]. **[confirmed as attribution]** *(the 1991 piece is the standard citation for the named principle; the underlying idea is already operative in the 1980 book.)*
