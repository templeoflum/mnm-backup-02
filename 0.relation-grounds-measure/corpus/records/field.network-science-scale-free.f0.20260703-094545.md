**origin** — Albert-László Barabási & Réka Albert, "Emergence of scaling in random networks," *Science* 286(5439):509–512 (1999) — the observation that many real networks (the WWW, citation graphs, metabolic networks) have power-law degree distributions rather than the Poisson distribution of Erdős–Rényi random graphs, and the growth model (continuous addition of nodes + **preferential attachment**) that reproduces them. [[source.barabasi-albert-1999]] **[confirmed]**

**codification** — Réka Albert & Albert-László Barabási, "Statistical mechanics of complex networks," *Reviews of Modern Physics* 74(1):47–97 (2002) — the canonical review that consolidated the field: degree distributions, clustering, the small-world property, and the statistical-physics treatment of network topology and dynamics. [[source.albert-barabasi-2002]] **[confirmed as attribution]**

**door** — Albert-László Barabási, *Linked: The New Science of Networks* (Perseus, 2002) — the popular entry, framing hubs, the 80/20 concentration of connectivity, and networks as a cross-domain science. [[source.barabasi-linked-2002]] **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — grows out of graph theory ([[field.graph-theory]]) and Erdős–Rényi random-graph theory but is distinct: an empirical/statistical science of *observed* network topology, not the combinatorics of arbitrary graphs. Distinct from [[field.network-flow-theory]] (capacitated s–t flow / max-flow–min-cut), though the two meet where flows concentrate through hubs (hubs as both carriers and control points). Provisional canon (structure — network architecture family beside network-flow-theory); the measuring-vs-structure placement is the operator's to settle at the gate.

*grade s — origin, codification, and door all present.*
