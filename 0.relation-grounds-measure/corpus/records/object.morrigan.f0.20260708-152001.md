**statement (plain)** — the **Morrígan** (Old Irish *Mórrígan* / *Mór-Rígan*) is an Irish goddess of war, fate, and death, counted among the **Tuatha Dé Danann**. She is often figured as a **triple goddess** (variously grouped with **Badb**, **Macha**, and **Nemain** or **Anann**) and appears over the battlefield as a **crow or raven**, foretelling slaughter and shaping the fate of warriors. She is associated with **sovereignty** — the land offered to a rightful king. In the **Táin Bó Cúailnge** she encounters the hero **Cú Chulainn**: offering, then in enmity opposing him, appearing as an eel, a wolf, and a heifer, and finally as an old woman he heals unknowingly.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: war, battle, fate, death, prophecy, sovereignty. Key attributes: the form of a **crow/raven**; shape-shifting; the triple form. Associations: grouped with **Badb**, **Macha**, and **Nemain** (or **Anann**) as a war-triad; consort of the **Dagda** (in Cath Maige Tuired). Text: **Cath Maige Tuired** and the **Táin Bó Cúailnge** (Ulster Cycle). Etymology: the name is read either as *Mór-Rígan*, "phantom queen," or "great queen" — the two readings are debated **[uncertain — the glossing of the name is contested]**. Irish tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Irish mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Cath Maige Tuired and the Táin Bó Cúailnge [[source.ulster-cycle]]; the standard reference treatment is MacKillop, *Dictionary of Celtic Mythology*. The war/crow/triple associations and the Cú Chulainn encounter are attested tradition; the precise membership of the triad and the name-gloss vary across sources and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
