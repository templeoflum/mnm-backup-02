**statement (plain)** — **Tapio** is the Finnish god of the forest, game, and the hunt. He is figured as a tall man covered in moss and bark, with a beard of hanging lichen, the forest itself given a body. With his wife **Mielikki** he rules the woodland — his domain is called Tapiola — and the beasts of the wild are his herds.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: forest, hunt, game. Hunters must please Tapio with offerings and songs to receive game; when honoured he grants the forest's bounty to the respectful, and when slighted he withholds it. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — Tapio is named in Mikael Agricola's 1551 list of pagan gods, appears throughout the Kalevala, and is treated in modern scholarship ([[source.pentikainen-1999]]); this is documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). The Kalevala is Elias Lönnrot's nineteenth-century compilation of oral folk poetry, not an ancient text, but Tapio's cult is independently witnessed by Agricola's sixteenth-century catalogue. **[confirmed as attribution]**
