H. Nyquist, "Certain Topics in Telegraph Transmission Theory," *Transactions of the A.I.E.E.* 47(2) (April 1928), 617–644. Presented at the Winter Convention of the A.I.E.E., New York, NY, February 13–17, 1928.

Establishes that up to **2B independent pulse samples per second** can be carried through a channel of bandwidth B — the rate that the sampling theorem later restates as a sufficient sampling condition. Nyquist did not address the reconstruction of a continuous signal from its samples; that step is Shannon's. The "N" in "Nyquist rate / Nyquist frequency" honors this paper.

Distinct from the 1924 Nyquist paper ("Certain Factors Affecting Telegraph Speed," a precursor to information theory) recorded separately as [[source.nyquist-1924]].
