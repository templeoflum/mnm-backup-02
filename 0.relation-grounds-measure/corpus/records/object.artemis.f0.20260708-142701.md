**statement (plain)** — **Artemis** (Greek *Ἄρτεμις*) is the Olympian goddess of the hunt, wild animals, the wilderness, chastity, and childbirth (and, in later tradition, the moon). She is the daughter of **Zeus** and the Titaness **Leto**, and the twin sister of **Apollo**. A perpetual virgin, she ranges the mountains and forests with her bow and her band of nymphs, mistress of wild creatures and especially the deer. Her myths turn on the fierce guarding of her chastity and honor: she destroys the hunter **Actaeon**, who sees her bathing (in the common version she transforms him into a stag and his own hounds tear him apart), and with Apollo she slays the many children of **Niobe**, who had boasted herself superior to Leto.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: the hunt, wild animals, the wilderness, virginity, and childbirth; in later tradition the moon. Key attributes: the **bow** and arrows, the deer or stag, the hunting hounds, the crescent (later); the epithets *Potnia Therōn* ("mistress of animals") and *Agrotera* ("of the wild"). Parentage: daughter of Zeus and Leto, twin of Apollo, born on the island of Delos. The lunar association is a later development, secondary to her older wilderness character. The Roman *interpretatio* identified her with **Diana**. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and Homer's epics and the Homeric Hymns [[source.homer]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The parentage and the Diana *interpretatio* are well established; the etymology of the name is disputed, and the individual myths (Actaeon, Niobe) vary across sources and should be read as attested tradition, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
