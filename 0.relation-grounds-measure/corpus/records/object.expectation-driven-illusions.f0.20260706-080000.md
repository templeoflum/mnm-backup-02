**statement (plain)** — Expectation-driven illusions constitute a broad class of perceptual phenomena where prior expectations, beliefs, or contextual knowledge systematically bias perception away from the veridical properties of sensory input. These illusions demonstrate that perception is not a passive registration of sensory data but an active inference process that combines bottom-up sensory evidence with top-down predictions derived from past experience and current context. Examples span all sensory modalities and include phenomena such as perceptual set effects, where prior exposure to ambiguous stimuli influences subsequent interpretation; the influence of semantic context on speech perception; and visual illusions where knowledge about typical object properties overrides contrary sensory evidence.

**statement (framework)** — category: cognitive higher order; perceptual mechanism(s): top-down-expectation, bayesian-inference, predictive-coding.
