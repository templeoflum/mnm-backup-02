**statement (plain)** — **Osiris** is the god of the dead, the afterlife, and resurrection, and a god of fertility and vegetation. A king murdered and dismembered by his brother **Set**, his body is gathered and reassembled by his sister-wife **Isis**, who revives him enough to conceive their son **Horus**; Osiris then becomes ruler and judge of the underworld, before whom the dead are judged. His green or black skin marks him as the god of vegetation and of death — the seed that dies and rises. Every deceased Egyptian was identified with Osiris to share in his resurrection.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: the dead, the afterlife, resurrection, judgment, and fertility. Chief cult centre: **Abydos**. Iconography: a mummiform king with green or black skin, the *atef* crown, holding the crook and flail. The Osiris myth — murder and dismemberment by Set, reassembly by Isis, conception of Horus, and his rule and judgment of the underworld (the weighing of the heart, Book of the Dead spell 125) — is central to the funerary tradition. The Greeks by *interpretatio graeca* equated him with **Dionysus** (Herodotus) and, as lord of the dead, with **Hades**. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Egyptian mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested from the Pyramid Texts [[source.pyramid-texts]] onward and central to the Book of the Dead [[source.book-of-the-dead]]; the standard reference treatment is Wilkinson [[source.wilkinson-2003]]. The Abydos cult and the *interpretatio graeca* (Dionysus, Hades) are established; the murder-and-resurrection myth is attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
