**statement (plain)** — **Freyr** (Old Norse *Freyr*) is the foremost of the **Vanir** dwelling among the Aesir, brother of **Freyja** and son of **Njörðr**: god of fertility, prosperity, sunshine, and the good harvest. He owns the ship **Skíðblaðnir**, which always finds a fair wind and can be folded away, and the golden boar **Gullinbursti**. In *Skírnismál* he falls in love with the giantess **Gerðr** and sends his servant **Skírnir** to win her, giving up his sword as the price of the wooing — so that he stands weaponless at **Ragnarök**, where he is slain by the fire-giant **Surtr**.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: fertility, prosperity, peace, sunshine, the harvest, and (in the sources) sacral kingship. Key attributes: the ship **Skíðblaðnir**, the boar **Gullinbursti**, and the sword given up for **Gerðr**. His name means "lord" (Old Norse *freyr*), the counterpart of **Freyja** "lady," and Snorri records his worship as widespread, especially about Sweden and Uppsala. His descent from **Njörðr** among the Vanir and his exchange to the Aesir belong to the Vanir–Aesir tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Norse mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested across the Poetic Edda [[source.poetic-edda]] and systematized in Snorri's Prose Edda [[source.prose-edda]]; the standard reference treatment is Lindow [[source.lindow-2001]]. The Gerðr courtship stands in the eddic *Skírnismál* and the death by Surtr in *Völuspá*; the surrounding biography and cult notices owe much to Snorri and should be read as attested tradition through his 13th-c. Christian framing, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
