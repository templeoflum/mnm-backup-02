**statement (plain)** — **Odin** (Old Norse *Óðinn*) is the chief of the Aesir, the Allfather: god of wisdom, war, death, poetry, and magic (*seiðr*). He is the restless seeker of knowledge who pays for it in sacrifice — he gives an eye at Mímir's well to drink from it, and hangs nine nights wounded on **Yggdrasil**, "given to himself," to seize the runes (*Hávamál* 138–139). He gathers the slain warriors (the *einherjar*) into Valhalla for the last battle, wanders Midgard in a broad hat and blue cloak under many names, and — uniquely among high gods — knows the prophecy of his own death: he will be swallowed by the wolf **Fenrir** at **Ragnarök**.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: sovereignty, wisdom, war, death, poetry, ecstasy, and magic. Key attributes: the spear **Gungnir**, the ring **Draupnir**, the eight-legged horse **Sleipnir**, the ravens **Huginn** and **Muninn** ("thought" and "memory"), the wolves Geri and Freki. Germanic cognates: **Woden** (Anglo-Saxon), **Wotan/Wodan** (continental Germanic) — all from Proto-Germanic *\*Wōdanaz*, root *\*wōð-* "fury, inspiration"; the Roman-era *interpretatio* identified him with **Mercury** (hence Wednesday / *Wodnesdæg* ← *dies Mercurii*). Loose cross-pantheon comparisons (Zeus, Hermes) are made by analogy of function, not descent. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Norse mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested across the Poetic Edda [[source.poetic-edda]] and systematized in Snorri's Prose Edda [[source.prose-edda]]; the standard reference treatment is Lindow [[source.lindow-2001]]. The etymology (*\*Wōdanaz*) and the Mercury *interpretatio* are philologically established; the connected biography owes much to Snorri's 13th-c. Christian framing and should be read as attested tradition, not neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
