**statement (plain)** — **Fūjin** is the god of the wind, among the older Japanese weather deities. He is depicted as a wind-demon bearing a large bag (fukuro) of winds slung over his shoulder. He is classically paired with **Raijin**, the thunder god, the two appearing together as companion guardian figures.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: wind, air, breath. Fūjin is shown carrying a great bag of winds; his iconography — the wind-bearer type — traveled the Silk Road from Greco-Buddhist antecedents, and he is classically paired with Raijin. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — Fūjin is documented in Ashkenazi (2003) and this is documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). Though an old Japanese weather deity, his surviving iconography reached Japan by Buddhist and Silk-Road transmission from Greco-Buddhist wind-bearer antecedents rather than arising purely from the native Shinto chronicles; his pairing with Raijin belongs to the living syncretic tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**
