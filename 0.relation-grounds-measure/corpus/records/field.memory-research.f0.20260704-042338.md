**origin** — F. Bartlett, *Remembering: A Study in Experimental and Social Psychology* (Cambridge, 1932) — memory is reconstruction, not reproduction: the "War of the Ghosts" serial-reproduction studies show remembering governed by schemas ("effort after meaning"). [[source.bartlett-1932]] **[confirmed]**

**codification** — D. Schacter, "The seven sins of memory," *American Psychologist* 54(3):182–203 (1999) — a taxonomy of memory's failures as byproducts of otherwise adaptive features. [[source.schacter-1999]] **[confirmed]**

**door** — K. Nader, G. Schafe & J. LeDoux, "Fear memories require protein synthesis in the amygdala for reconsolidation after retrieval," *Nature* 406:722–726 (2000) — the reconsolidation result opened the modern neuroscience of the labile trace; Schacter's *Searching for Memory* (1996) and *The Seven Sins of Memory* (2001) are the accessible doors. [[source.nader-2000]] **[confirmed]**

*Kin: [[field.synaptic-plasticity]] holds the cellular basis (LTP/LTD); this field holds the systems/cognitive level. The exogram/engram distinction (Donald 1991) bridges to [[field.distributed-cognition]].*
