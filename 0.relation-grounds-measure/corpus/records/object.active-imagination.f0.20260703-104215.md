**statement (plain)** — Active imagination is a deliberately induced, receptive state in which one lowers executive control, lets inner images arise, and then engages the autonomous figures that appear as if they were real interlocutors — writing, painting, or dialoguing with them. Jung distinguished it from passive daydreaming by the conscious participation of the ego *with* the autonomous material. His own practice yielded the figure Philemon, who taught him "that there are things in the psyche which I do not produce, but which produce themselves and have their own life."

**relevance** — the clearest instance of *deliberate, cultivated* inward channeling by a major psychologist, framing the material's autonomy as a psychological fact while bracketing metaphysics — the cultivation invariant made method. Sits beside Myers's [[object.subliminal-self]] (the source reservoir) and the surrealists' automatism (the same mechanism re-licensed as art) [[source.jung-liber-novus-2009]] **[confirmed as attribution]**.
