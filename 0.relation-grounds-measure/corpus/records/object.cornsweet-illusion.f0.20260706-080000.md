**statement (plain)** — The Cornsweet Illusion, described by Tom Cornsweet in 1970 and independently discovered by Kenneth Craik and Vivian O'Brien, is a compelling lightness illusion in which two regions of identical uniform luminance appear markedly different in brightness when separated by a specialized edge profile called a Cornsweet edge. The Cornsweet edge consists of a sharp luminance cusp or step at the boundary between two regions, accompanied by shallow gradients that extend into each region before reaching a uniform plateau. Critically, the luminance values of the two plateau regions are physically identical, yet the region with the darker gradient near the edge appears substantially darker overall, while the region with the lighter gradient appears lighter overall.
