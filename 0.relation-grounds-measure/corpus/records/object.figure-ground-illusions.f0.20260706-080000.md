**statement (plain)** — Figure-Ground Illusions constitute a general class of visual stimuli in which the assignment of image regions as figure (foreground object) versus ground (background) is ambiguous, resulting in multiple possible perceptual organizations of the same visual input. These illusions demonstrate that figure-ground segregation is not a trivial byproduct of the stimulus but rather an active perceptual achievement involving complex neural computations. The most famous example is Rubin's vase-face illusion, in which a single contour can be assigned either to a central vase or to two facing profiles, but never to both simultaneously.

**statement (framework)** — category: ambiguous bistable multistable; perceptual mechanism(s): figure-ground-segregation, contour-integration.
