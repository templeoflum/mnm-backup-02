**statement (plain)** — The Delbœuf Illusion, discovered by Belgian physicist and psychologist Joseph Delbœuf in 1865, is a size contrast illusion in which the perceived size of a central circle is systematically influenced by the size of a surrounding concentric annulus. In the classic configuration, two identical inner circles are each surrounded by outer circles of different diameters. The inner circle surrounded by a slightly larger outer circle appears larger than the identical inner circle surrounded by a much larger outer circle.

**statement (framework)** — category: geometric size length; perceptual mechanism(s): misapplied-size-constancy, spatial-filtering.
