**statement (plain)** — In Hawaiian tradition **Kū** is the god of war and male endeavors, one of the four great gods (with **Kāne**, **Lono**, and **Kanaloa**), worshipped in many "Kū forms" governing the male domains from fishing to war. His war-form **Kūkā'ilimoku** ("Kū the snatcher of land") was the war-god of the chief **Kamehameha**; his rites in the luakini heiau included human sacrifice.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: war, male activities, fishing, sorcery, politics. One of the four great gods (**Kāne**, **Lono**, **Kanaloa**); war-form **Kūkā'ilimoku**, war-god of **Kamehameha**. Cognate with the Māori **Tū** (recorded separately). Hawaiian tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested in Beckwith, *Hawaiian Mythology* (1940) [[source.beckwith-1940]]; documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). These myths come through Hawaiian oral tradition collected and recorded in the 19th–20th c.; the many "Kū forms" and their rites vary across the islands. NOTE the cognate: **Kū ≈ the Māori Tū** (recorded separately). **[confirmed as attribution]**
