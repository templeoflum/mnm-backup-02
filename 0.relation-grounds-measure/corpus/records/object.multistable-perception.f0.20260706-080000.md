**statement (plain)** — Multistable perception refers to the general phenomenon where visual stimuli support three or more mutually exclusive perceptual interpretations that spontaneously alternate over time despite a constant physical input. Unlike bistable stimuli that alternate between two states, multistable displays permit multiple competing percepts, each temporarily dominating conscious awareness before giving way to another interpretation. This phenomenon reveals fundamental principles about how the visual system resolves perceptual ambiguity when sensory evidence equally supports multiple organizational schemas.
