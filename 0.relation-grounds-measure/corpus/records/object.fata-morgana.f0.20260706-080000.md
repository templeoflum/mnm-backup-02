**statement (plain)** — Fata Morgana is a complex and elaborate form of superior mirage in which distant objects appear dramatically distorted, often stretched vertically, inverted, or multiplied into stacked images that can resemble castles, towers, or fantastical structures. Named after Morgan le Fay, the sorceress from Arthurian legend who was said to create illusions to lure sailors to their doom, this phenomenon occurs when multiple layers of air at different temperatures create multiple refracting surfaces that bend light rays in complex paths. Unlike simple mirages that produce a single inverted or displaced image, Fata Morgana mirages involve thermal inversion layers that create multiple alternating upright and inverted images, often compressed or stretched in the vertical dimension, producing bizarre and rapidly changing distortions.
