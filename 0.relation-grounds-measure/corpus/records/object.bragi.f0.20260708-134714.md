**statement (plain)** — **Bragi** (Old Norse *Bragi*) is the god of poetry and eloquence, the husband of **Iðunn**. Snorri names him foremost in skaldship, and the word *bragr* denotes poetry and the best or foremost of a kind. In *Lokasenna* he first meets **Loki**'s arrival at the feast, offering and then being refused peace, and is taunted for cowardice; in Snorri's *Skáldskaparmál* he is the one who tells **Ægir** the tales of the gods. Some scholars connect the god to the historical 9th-century skald **Bragi Boddason** ("Bragi the Old"), one of the earliest named Norse poets — a scholarly connection, not a settled point of the tradition.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: poetry, eloquence, and skaldship. Key relations: husband of **Iðunn**; the divine poet who addresses **Ægir** in *Skáldskaparmál* and speaks with **Loki** in *Lokasenna*. His name is bound to the noun *bragr* "poetry, the foremost." The proposed identity or derivation from the earliest attested skald **Bragi Boddason** is a matter of scholarly argument, carried here as a connection and not as an attested equation. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Norse mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested across the Poetic Edda [[source.poetic-edda]] and systematized in Snorri's Prose Edda [[source.prose-edda]]; the standard reference treatment is Lindow [[source.lindow-2001]]. The link between the god and the skald Bragi Boddason is a scholarly hypothesis and should be read as such, not as attested tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**
