**statement (plain)** — Mark each photon in a double-slit setup with which-path information and the interference vanishes. Then *erase* that information before reading the results — and the interference returns. What destroys interference is the mere availability of which-path information; what restores it is informational erasure, not any physical un-disturbance.

**statement (framework)** — an **empirical** refutation of the intuition that which-path detection kills interference via Heisenberg *disturbance*: Scully–Drühl showed one can mark the path without uncontrolled momentum transfer, and erasure (even delayed) restores interference — foregrounding **information** in quantum foundations. Realized (Kim et al. 2000). Empirical; interpretation **open**.

**basis** — Scully & Drühl's quantum-eraser proposal (1982) [[source.scully-druhl-1982]]; delayed-choice eraser, Kim et al. (2000). **[confirmed as attribution]**
