**statement (plain)** — **Athena** (Greek *Ἀθηνᾶ*; Attic *Athēnā*, Ionic *Athēnē*) is the goddess of wisdom, craft, and strategic warfare. In Hesiod she is born fully armed from the head of **Zeus**, after Zeus swallows the pregnant **Metis**, so that she springs from her father alone. A virgin goddess (*Parthenos*), she is the patron and namesake of **Athens**, whose citadel she wins in her contest with **Poseidon** by giving the olive tree. In Homer she is the constant helper of favoured heroes — above all **Odysseus**, whom she guides through the *Odyssey* — and of Diomedes and Achilles in the *Iliad*. She bears the **aegis** and is attended by the **owl**.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: wisdom and counsel, handicraft (weaving, pottery, metalwork), and strategic, disciplined war (distinct from the raw slaughter of Ares). Key attributes: the **aegis** (often bearing the Gorgon's head), helmet, spear and shield, the **owl**, and the **olive**; epithets *Pallas*, *Parthenos*, *Glaukōpis* ("bright-" or "owl-eyed"), and *Polias* ("of the city"). Born from the head of Zeus after he swallowed Metis; principal cult the Parthenon on the Athenian Acropolis. The Roman *interpretatio* identified her with **Minerva**; a title *a-ta-na po-ti-ni-ja* ("Lady of Athana") appears in Mycenaean Linear B. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and Homer's epics [[source.homer]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The Minerva *interpretatio* is established and the Linear B title is generally read as referring to her; the etymology and the god-or-city priority of the name are debated. The genealogies and narratives are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
