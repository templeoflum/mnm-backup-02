**statement (plain)** — The Chevreul Illusion, described by French chemist Michel Eugène Chevreul in 1839, is a fundamental lightness contrast phenomenon in which adjacent bands of uniform luminance appear to have scalloped gradients, with enhanced contrast at their boundaries. When viewing a series of abutting stripes that each have uniform luminance but differ in luminance from their neighbors, observers perceive each band as being darker on the edge adjacent to a lighter band and lighter on the edge adjacent to a darker band. This creates an illusory impression of gradients within each physically uniform region, with a scalloped or Mach band-like appearance.

**statement (framework)** — category: brightness lightness contrast; perceptual mechanism(s): lateral-inhibition, spatial-filtering, lightness-computation.
