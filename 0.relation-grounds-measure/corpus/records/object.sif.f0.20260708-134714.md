**statement (plain)** — **Sif** (Old Norse *Sif*) is the goddess wife of **Thor**, famed above all for her golden hair. Snorri tells in *Skáldskaparmál* that **Loki**, out of mischief, shears off all her hair; when Thor threatens him, Loki is forced to go to the dwarves and have them make new hair of gold that will grow like true hair. In the same errand the dwarves fashion the other great treasures of the gods — among them **Thor**'s hammer **Mjöllnir**, **Odin**'s spear **Gungnir** and ring **Draupnir**, and **Freyr**'s boar and ship — so that the shearing of Sif's hair is the occasion of the forging of the gods' chief treasures.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: named as Thor's wife and marked by her golden hair. Key relations: wife of **Thor**; the golden hair made by the dwarves after **Loki**'s shearing. Snorri's *Skáldskaparmál* preserves the shearing and the ensuing forging of the treasures; her name appears in the Poetic Edda in *Lokasenna* and *Hárbarðsljóð*. The golden hair is commonly read as a harvest or fertility image, a scholarly interpretation rather than a stated point of the sources. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Norse mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested across the Poetic Edda [[source.poetic-edda]] and systematized in Snorri's Prose Edda [[source.prose-edda]]; the standard reference treatment is Lindow [[source.lindow-2001]]. The hair-shearing and the forging of the treasures come from Snorri's *Skáldskaparmál* and should be read as attested tradition through his 13th-c. Christian framing, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
