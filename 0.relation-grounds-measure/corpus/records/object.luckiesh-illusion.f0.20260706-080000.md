**statement (plain)** — The Luckiesh Illusion refers to geometric visual distortions documented by Matthew Luckiesh, an American physicist and illumination engineer who made significant contributions to visual perception research in the early-to-mid 20th century. Luckiesh's work at the General Electric laboratories focused on the relationship between lighting conditions and visual perception, and he catalogued numerous geometric illusions in his comprehensive texts on visual phenomena. The illusion typically involves configurations where straight lines or geometric shapes appear distorted when placed against specific background patterns or in particular spatial arrangements.
