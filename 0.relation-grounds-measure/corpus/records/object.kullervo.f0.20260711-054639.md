**statement (plain)** — **Kullervo** is the tragic hero of the Kalevala. Born into slavery after his family is destroyed in a clan feud, he grows up warped by wrong and vengeance. Not knowing her, he seduces a young woman who proves to be his own lost sister; when the truth is discovered she drowns herself, and Kullervo, after taking his revenge, throws himself upon his own sword. He is the archetype of the fate-cursed, doomed hero.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: tragedy, fate, vengeance. His cycle is the darkest in the epic, a chain of injury and retribution ending in incest and suicide, and it later inspired works by Sibelius and others. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — Kullervo is attested in the Kalevala and treated in modern scholarship ([[source.pentikainen-1999]]); this is documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). The Kalevala is Elias Lönnrot's nineteenth-century compilation of oral folk poetry, not an ancient text; Lönnrot assembled the Kullervo cycle from several independent oral songs into one connected tragedy. **[confirmed as attribution]**
