**statement (plain)** — In supervisory control the machine, not the human, closes the fast control loops moment-to-moment; the human works one level up — setting goals and targets, programming the automation, monitoring how it is doing, stepping in for exceptions, and learning the system's behavior. The operator's roles cycle through planning, teaching, monitoring, intervening, and learning. The relationship is defined along a **levels-of-automation** continuum from fully manual to fully autonomous, with most real systems in the middle.

**relevance** — the structural description of the control-room / dispatch operator's job: they supervise a process that largely runs itself and act at the seams. Frames what the interface must convey and what the handover must transfer, and sets up the ironies ([[object.ironies-of-automation]]) that arise when the human is left only the exceptions [[source.sheridan-1992]] **[confirmed as attribution]**.
