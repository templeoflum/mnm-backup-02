**statement (plain)** — **Heimdall** (Old Norse *Heimdallr*) is the watchman of the Aesir, guardian of **Bifröst**, the rainbow bridge to Asgard. He is said to be born of nine mothers; his senses are famously keen — he needs less sleep than a bird, sees far by night and day, and can hear grass grow on the earth and wool on sheep. He holds the horn **Gjallarhorn**, which he will sound to rouse the gods at the onset of **Ragnarök**, where he and **Loki** meet as enemies and slay one another. In *Rígsþula* he wanders under the name **Ríg** and, through three households, is made the ancestor of the human social classes — thrall, farmer, and noble.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: watching and warding, the boundary of Asgard, and (in *Rígsþula*) the origin of the human orders. Key attributes: the horn **Gjallarhorn**, the post at **Bifröst**, the birth of nine mothers, and the heightened sight and hearing. His hall is **Himinbjörg** ("heaven-cliffs") at the bridge's edge. The name's etymology is uncertain and much debated; the *Rígsþula* attribution of the class-founding to Heimdall rests on a prose header naming Ríg as Heimdall, which some scholars treat as a later editorial identification. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Norse mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested across the Poetic Edda [[source.poetic-edda]] and systematized in Snorri's Prose Edda [[source.prose-edda]]; the standard reference treatment is Lindow [[source.lindow-2001]]. The keen senses, Gjallarhorn, and the mutual death with Loki are gathered chiefly by Snorri from eddic sources; the class-founding stands in *Rígsþula*. The connected material should be read as attested tradition through the medieval framing, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
