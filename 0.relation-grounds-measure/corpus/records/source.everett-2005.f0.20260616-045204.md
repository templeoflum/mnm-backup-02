**verbatim citation** — Everett, Daniel L. "Cultural Constraints on Grammar and Cognition in Pirahã: Another Look at the Design Features of Human Language." *Current Anthropology* 46, no. 4 (2005): 621–646. DOI: 10.1086/431525.

**what it contains** — reports that Pirahã (an Amazonian language) lacks clausal embedding and recursive possession, alongside the absence of number, counting, color terms, and creation myths. Attributes these to an "immediacy-of-experience principle" — a cultural constraint limiting grammar to assertions about directly witnessed events. If sound, the claim is a putative counterexample to recursion as a universal of human language and bears directly on the Hauser–Chomsky–Fitch hypothesis. Heavily contested (e.g., Nevins, Pesetsky & Rodrigues 2009); carried here as a live, disputed datum, not a settled finding.
