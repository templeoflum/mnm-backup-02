**statement (plain)** — Rainbows are atmospheric optical phenomena appearing as circular arcs displaying the spectral sequence of colors, caused by refraction, internal reflection, and dispersion of sunlight within spherical water droplets. The primary rainbow, appearing at approximately 42 degrees from the antisolar point, results from one internal reflection within droplets, displaying red on the outer edge and violet on the inner edge. Secondary rainbows, appearing at approximately 51 degrees with reversed color sequence, result from two internal reflections and are typically fainter than primary rainbows due to additional light loss at each reflection.
