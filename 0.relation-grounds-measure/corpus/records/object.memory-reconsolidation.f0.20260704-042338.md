**statement (plain)** — a memory once "consolidated" was thought fixed. Nader showed otherwise: reactivate it and it goes soft again, needing fresh protein synthesis to set once more. Block that synthesis in the right moment and the reactivated memory does not re-stabilize. Every recall is a chance to rewrite — the biological echo of reconstruction.

**basis** — Nader, Schafe & LeDoux (2000, *Nature*): reactivated fear memories require de novo protein synthesis in the amygdala to reconsolidate; anisomycin at reactivation impairs the memory. `empirical` — a well-replicated finding with boundary conditions, not a proven theorem. The cellular-level counterpart of [[object.reconstructive-memory]]; kin of [[field.synaptic-plasticity]]. [[source.nader-2000]] **[confirmed]**
