**statement (plain)** — **Poseidon** (Greek *Ποσειδῶν*) is the god of the sea, of earthquakes, and of horses. He is a son of the Titans **Kronos** and **Rhea** and a brother of **Zeus**; in the division of the cosmos after the Titanomachy he receives the sea as his domain. He wields the **trident**, with which he stirs storms and shatters the earth ("Earth-shaker," *Ennosigaios* / *Enosichthōn*). In the *Odyssey* his wrath drives the central action: enraged at the blinding of his son the Cyclops **Polyphemus**, he harries **Odysseus** across the sea and delays his homecoming for years.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: the sea, earthquakes, storms, and horses. Key attributes: the **trident**, the horse, the bull, and the dolphin; epithets *Enosichthōn* / *Ennosigaios* ("Earth-shaker") and *Hippios* ("of horses"). Consort **Amphitrite**; offspring include the Cyclops **Polyphemus**, the winged horse **Pegasus** (by Medusa), the giant **Antaeus**, and the hero **Theseus** by one tradition. His rivalry with **Athena** over the patronage of Athens is a standard myth. The Roman *interpretatio* identified him with **Neptune**; the name is attested already in Mycenaean Linear B as *po-se-da-o*. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and Homer's epics [[source.homer]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The Linear B attestation and the Neptune *interpretatio* are established; the internal etymology of the name is debated, and the genealogies and narratives are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
