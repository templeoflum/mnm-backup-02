**statement (plain)** — Contingent aftereffects represent a sophisticated class of visual adaptation phenomena in which the perceptual aftereffect is dependent on the simultaneous presence of two or more stimulus dimensions. Unlike simple aftereffects that occur along a single perceptual dimension, contingent aftereffects demonstrate that the visual system can adapt to specific conjunctions of features, revealing the multidimensional nature of neural coding in visual cortex. The most famous example is the McCollough effect, where adaptation to colored gratings of different orientations produces color aftereffects that are contingent on orientation.

**statement (framework)** — category: aftereffect adaptation; perceptual mechanism(s): neural-adaptation, orientation-selectivity.
