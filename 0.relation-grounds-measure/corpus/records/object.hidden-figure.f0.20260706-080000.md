**statement (plain)** — Hidden figure illusions present observers with images that initially appear as random or meaningless patterns of light and dark regions but that suddenly resolve into recognizable objects once the appropriate perceptual organization is achieved. The classic example is a high-contrast photograph of a Dalmatian dog sniffing the ground in dappled sunlight, where the irregular black spots on a white background initially resist coherent interpretation. Once observers successfully parse the figure from ground and integrate the fragmented contours into a unified object representation, the dog becomes immediately apparent, and the image is subsequently difficult to see as anything other than the animal.
