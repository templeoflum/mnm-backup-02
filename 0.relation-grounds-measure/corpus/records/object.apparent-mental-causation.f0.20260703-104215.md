**statement (plain)** — The sense that "I consciously willed that" is not a direct readout of causation but an inference the mind constructs. It arises when a thought (a) precedes the action by the right interval (**priority**), (b) matches the action (**consistency**), and (c) seems to be its only cause (**exclusivity**). Because will is inferred, it can be *felt* when one didn't cause the act, and *absent* when one did — which is exactly the report in automatic writing, the Ouija planchette, and trance channeling: real action, no felt authorship.

**relevance** — the cognitive account of felt involuntariness that requires no fraud and no spirit. Complements the motor layer ([[object.ideomotor-action]]) and the metacognitive layer ([[object.cold-control]]): ideomotor supplies unwilled movement, cold control removes the higher-order thought of intending, and apparent mental causation explains why the whole thing feels authored-by-another [[source.wegner-wheatley-1999]] **[confirmed]**.
