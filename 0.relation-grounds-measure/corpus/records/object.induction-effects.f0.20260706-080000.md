**statement (plain)** — Induction effects constitute a broad class of contextual phenomena in which the perceptual appearance of a target stimulus is systematically altered by the properties of surrounding or adjacent stimuli. These effects span multiple perceptual dimensions including brightness, color, size, orientation, and motion, and demonstrate that perception is fundamentally relational rather than absolute. Classical examples include simultaneous brightness contrast, where a gray patch appears lighter on a dark background than on a light background, and color contrast, where complementary surrounds induce opponent hues in neutral targets.
