**statement (plain)** — **Ogun** (**Ogum** in Candomblé) is the fierce **Orisha** of iron, war, and labor, who clears the path with his machete. He cut the first path from heaven to earth, and is the patron of blacksmiths, warriors, and hunters — of all who work with metal. In modern times his patronage extends to drivers, soldiers, and surgeons, and it is by Ogun that oaths are sworn.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: iron, war, labor, technology, oaths, clearing paths. Patron of the forge and of all metalworkers; associated with iron tools, the machete, and the colours green and black; the Orisha by whom oaths are sworn in court and in ritual. Diaspora name: **Ogum** (Candomblé). **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — documented in Idowu (1962) and Karade (1994); this is documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). It is an oral tradition carried in the **Ifá** corpus (the *odu*) and recorded by scholars; his attributes and the trades under his patronage vary between Yorubaland and the diaspora traditions of Santería and Candomblé. **[confirmed as attribution]**
