**statement (plain)** — **Zeus** (Greek *Ζεύς*) is the king of the gods and the ruler of Mount Olympus: god of the sky, thunder, and sovereignty. He is the youngest son of the Titans **Kronos** and **Rhea**; saved by Rhea from Kronos, who swallowed his children, he grows to force his father to disgorge his siblings, leads the Olympians in the ten-year **Titanomachy**, and after the victory casts lots with his brothers **Poseidon** and **Hades**, taking the sky as his domain (*Iliad* 15.187–193). Wielder of the **thunderbolt**, he presides as father of gods and men and as guardian of oaths, hospitality, and justice; he is also the great begetter, father of many gods and heroes by many consorts.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: sky, weather and thunder, sovereignty, order, justice, oaths, and hospitality. Key attributes: the **thunderbolt**, the **aegis**, the eagle, the oak; the epithets *nephelēgeretēs* ("cloud-gatherer") and *Xenios* / *Horkios* (of hospitality / of oaths). Consorts and offspring include Hera, and Metis, Leto, Maia, Semele, and mortals such as Alcmene, producing Athena, Apollo and Artemis, Hermes, Dionysus, Heracles, and others. Etymology: from Proto-Indo-European *\*Dyēus*, "sky, day-sky father," cognate with Latin *Iuppiter* (< *Dyeus-pater*), Vedic *Dyaus*, and Germanic *Tīwaz* — the "sky father" is philologically established; the Roman *interpretatio* identified him with **Jupiter**. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and Homer's epics [[source.homer]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The etymology (*\*Dyēus*) and the Jupiter *interpretatio* are philologically established; the genealogies and the succession myth are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
