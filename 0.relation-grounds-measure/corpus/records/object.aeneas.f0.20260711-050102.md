**statement (plain)** — **Aeneas** is the Trojan hero and mythic ancestor of the Romans, son of the goddess **Venus** (Greek Aphrodite) and the mortal **Anchises**. A Trojan prince, he escapes the sack of Troy carrying his father and the household gods (the *Penates*), and — after long wanderings and the tragic love of **Dido** at Carthage — reaches Latium in Italy. There his line leads down to **Romulus** and **Remus** and the founding of Rome. His defining virtue is *pietas*, duty to gods, father, and people.

**statement (attestation)** — son of Venus and Anchises; Trojan prince and refugee of the fallen city; bearer of the Penates; wanderer whose love of Dido ends in her death; settler in Latium and ancestor of the Roman line. His hallmark is *pietas*; in the tradition he is later honored and deified as **Jupiter Indiges**. Though Trojan in origin, his myth is the Roman national foundation and his cult is Roman — his place in the distinctly Roman set. Associated figures: Venus, Anchises, Dido, Ascanius/Iulus, Latinus, Turnus. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested centrally in Virgil's *Aeneid*, with Ovid's *Metamorphoses* Book 14 and Livy's *Ab Urbe Condita* Book 1; this is documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted fact. The wanderings, the Dido episode, and the deification as Jupiter Indiges are held as attested tradition, elaborated variously across the sources. **[confirmed as attribution]**
