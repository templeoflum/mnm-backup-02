**statement (plain)** — **Iris** (Greek *Ἶρις*, "Rainbow") is the goddess of the rainbow and the swift messenger of the gods. In Hesiod she is a daughter of **Thaumas** and the Oceanid **Electra**, and sister of the Harpies (*Theogony* 265–269). In Homer she is winged and carries the errands of the gods, above all those of **Hera**, moving between Olympus, the earth, and the sea to bear their words.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: the rainbow and divine messaging. Roles: winged herald of the gods, especially of Hera, bridging Olympus and the mortal world. Etymology: Greek *Îris*, "rainbow," also the ordinary word for the rainbow itself; she has no fixed Roman *interpretatio* and is Latinized as **Iris**. Her defining attributes are her wings and her role as swift runner between the divine and mortal realms. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and Homer's epics [[source.homer]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The parentage from Thaumas and Electra and her wings and messenger role are attested; her particular service to Hera and other details are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
