**statement (plain)** — **Tuoni** is the lord of **Tuonela**, the Finnish realm of the dead that lies beneath the earth and waters, reached across a black river. He is death personified as a ruler, and with his wife **Tuonetar** he holds sway over the shadowy realm and its inhabitants. Tuoni is the figure who rules; Tuonela is the place he rules.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: death, the underworld. Heroes who journey to Tuonela — as Väinämöinen does in search of lost words of magic — face great danger, for the realm and its keepers try to hold fast all who cross the river and enter. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — Tuoni and Tuonela are attested throughout the Kalevala and treated in modern scholarship ([[source.pentikainen-1999]]); this is documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). The Kalevala is Elias Lönnrot's nineteenth-century compilation of oral folk poetry, not an ancient text; the underworld imagery it carries draws on older Finnic conceptions of the land of the dead preserved in oral song. **[confirmed as attribution]**
