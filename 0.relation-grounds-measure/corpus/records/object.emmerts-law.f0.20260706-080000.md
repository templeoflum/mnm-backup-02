**statement (plain)** — Emmert's Law states that the apparent size of an afterimage is proportional to the perceived distance of the surface onto which it is projected. When an observer fixates a bright stimulus to generate a retinal afterimage and then views surfaces at different distances, the afterimage appears larger when projected onto more distant surfaces and smaller when projected onto nearer surfaces, despite maintaining a constant retinal size. Formally, S = k × D, where S is perceived size, D is perceived distance, and k is the constant visual angle subtended by the afterimage on the retina.
