**statement (plain)** — **Oya** (**Iansã** in Candomblé) is the warrior **Orisha** of wind, storms, and transformation. The wife and battle-companion of **Shango**, she guards the gates of the cemetery, the boundary between life and death. She brings the sudden storm and the winds of change, and is associated with the River Niger.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: wind, storms, death, transformation, cemetery gates, warfare. Tempest Orisha and battle-companion of **Shango**; guardian of the boundary between life and death at the cemetery gate; associated with the River Niger and with the buffalo; brings the whirlwind and the winds of change. Diaspora name: **Iansã** (Candomblé). **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — documented in Idowu (1962) and Karade (1994); this is documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). It is an oral tradition carried in the **Ifá** corpus (the *odu*) and recorded by scholars; her attributes and her association with river or cemetery vary between Yorubaland and the diaspora traditions of Santería and Candomblé. **[confirmed as attribution]**
