**statement (plain)** — **Baldr** (Old Norse *Baldr*) is the beautiful and beloved god of the Aesir, son of **Odin** and **Frigg**. He is troubled by prophetic dreams of his own death, and the gods try to guard him; **Frigg** wins oaths from all things not to harm him, but overlooks the mistletoe. **Loki** learns of the omission, and at his guidance the blind god **Höðr** casts a mistletoe dart that kills Baldr. He is given a great ship-funeral aboard **Hringhorni**, and the gods send to **Hel** to ransom him: she will release him only if all things weep, but one giantess — **Thökk** (widely taken to be Loki in disguise) — refuses, and Baldr must remain. He returns to the renewed world after **Ragnarök**.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: light, beauty, innocence, the beloved god whose death sets Ragnarök in motion. Key elements: his prophetic death-dreams (*Baldrs draumar*), the mistletoe that alone was not sworn, the blind **Höðr** as unwitting killer, the funeral ship **Hringhorni**, the failed weeping-out of **Hel** and the refusal of **Thökk**, and his survival to the world after the end. The etymology of the name is disputed — variously connected to a word for "lord/prince" or to "bold, brave" — and is not settled. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Norse mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested across the Poetic Edda [[source.poetic-edda]] and systematized in Snorri's Prose Edda [[source.prose-edda]]; the standard reference treatment is Lindow [[source.lindow-2001]]. The full death narrative — the oaths, the mistletoe, Hringhorni, and the ransom from Hel — comes chiefly from Snorri and should be read as attested tradition through his 13th-c. Christian framing, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
