**statement (plain)** — The Dual-Axis Illusion is a bistable or multistable motion perception phenomenon in which a rotating three-dimensional object appears to spin around two different axes either simultaneously or in alternation, despite having only a single physical axis of rotation. This illusion typically occurs when viewing ambiguous motion displays, such as silhouettes or wireframe objects, where depth information is absent or impoverished, leaving the rotation axis underspecified. Observers report spontaneous perceptual switches between different interpretations of the rotation axis, with periods of stable perception alternating with sudden transitions between distinct rotational configurations.
