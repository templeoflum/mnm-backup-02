**statement (plain)** — The Pyramid of Giza Illusion, also known as the Hollow Column illusion, refers to the phenomenon whereby the Great Pyramid and other ancient pyramids appear to have concave rather than flat faces when photographed or viewed under specific lighting and atmospheric conditions. This striking visual effect occurs when each triangular face of the pyramid exhibits a subtle inward curvature, making the structure appear hollowed despite its actual planar geometry. The illusion arises from the interaction of several factors: the pyramid's immense scale, differential weathering patterns along the face centerlines, shadows cast by structural irregularities, and atmospheric perspective effects that alter contrast and luminance distributions across the vast surface.
