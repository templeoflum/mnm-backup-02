William James, *The Principles of Psychology* (Henry Holt and Company, 1890), 2 vols.; chapter IV, "Habit."

Contains — the first systematic psychological treatment of habit: habit as the physiological "plasticity" of neural matter hardened by repetition; its role in economizing attention and effort (once habitual, an action needs little conscious guidance); and its conservative social function ("the enormous fly-wheel of society"). James's practical maxims (launch a new habit with strong initiative, never suffer an exception, seize the first opportunity to act) prefigure modern habit engineering.

Access — public domain; widely reprinted (Dover 1950, Harvard 1981 critical edition). **[confirmed]**
