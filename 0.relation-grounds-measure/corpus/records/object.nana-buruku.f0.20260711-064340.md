**statement (plain)** — **Nana Buruku** (Nanã) is a primordial-mother **Orisha** of swamps and mud in Yoruba tradition, associated with the passage between life and death. In tradition she provided the primordial clay from which **Obatala** moulded the bodies of humankind. Some traditions rank her among the very oldest of the Orisha, though this and her origins are debated.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: swamps, primordial mud, death, ancestors, creation, the deep feminine. Giver of the clay from which human bodies were formed; ruler of swamp and mud and of the boundary of life and death; held in some traditions to be among the most ancient deities. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — documented in Idowu (1962) and Karade (1994); this is documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). It is an oral tradition carried in the **Ifá** corpus (the *odu*) and recorded by scholars; some traditions rank her among the very oldest deities (a claim variously stated) and her origins are debated, and her attributes vary between Yorubaland and the diaspora traditions. **[confirmed as attribution]**
