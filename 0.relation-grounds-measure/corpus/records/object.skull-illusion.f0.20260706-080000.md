**statement (plain)** — The Skull Illusion refers to a class of ambiguous images that can be perceived either as a human skull or as an alternative scene, typically depicting two human figures, a landscape, or other objects arranged such that their contours and shading create the appearance of a skull when viewed as a unified whole. This type of double image demonstrates sophisticated perceptual organization where the same visual elements participate in two radically different interpretations depending on the scale and grouping strategy applied by the observer. When perceived as a skull, specific features such as eye sockets, nasal cavity, and teeth emerge from the global configuration, while the alternative interpretation requires parsing the image into separate figural elements at a finer spatial scale.

**statement (framework)** — category: ambiguous bistable multistable; perceptual mechanism(s): bistable-competition, figure-ground-segregation, top-down-expectation, spatial-filtering.
