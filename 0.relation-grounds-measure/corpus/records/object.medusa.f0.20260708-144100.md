**statement (plain)** — **Medusa** (Greek *Μέδουσα*) is the one **mortal** of the three **Gorgons** (with her immortal sisters Stheno and Euryale), a monster whose **gaze turns to stone** any who look upon her. She is slain by **Perseus**, who — guided by Athena and using his shield as a mirror to avoid her direct gaze — cuts off her head. From her severed neck spring the winged horse **Pegasus** and the warrior **Chrysaor**, her children by Poseidon. Perseus carries her head as a weapon and finally gives it to **Athena**, who sets it upon her shield or breastplate, the **aegis**, where its petrifying face (the *gorgoneion*) wards off enemies.

**statement (attestation)** — domain: petrifying terror, the apotropaic (warding) face, and the monstrous transformed. Genealogy: daughter of the sea-deities Phorcys and Ceto; sister of Stheno and Euryale; by Poseidon mother of Pegasus and Chrysaor. Etymology: Greek *Médousa*, "guardian, ruler" (from *medō*, "to protect, rule over"). She has no distinct Roman name, being carried over as *Medusa*. In **Ovid's Metamorphoses** (book 4) a later version holds that she was once a beautiful maiden whose hair Athena transformed into snakes as punishment after Poseidon violated her in Athena's temple — this transformation account is the later, Roman-era version and is not present in the earliest Greek sources. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]] (the beheading by Perseus, the birth of Pegasus and Chrysaor); the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The mortality among the three Gorgons and the beheading are attested; the beauty-and-transformation account derives from Ovid and should be read as the later tradition, variable across sources, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
