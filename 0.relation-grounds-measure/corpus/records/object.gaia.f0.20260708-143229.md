**statement (plain)** — **Gaia** (Greek *Γαῖα*, "Earth") is the primordial Earth and the mother of all: in Hesiod she comes into being among the very first, after **Chaos**, as the broad ground on which everything stands (*Theogony* 116–118). From herself she brings forth **Ouranos** (Sky), the mountains, and Pontos (Sea), then lying with Ouranos bears the twelve **Titans**, the three **Cyclopes**, and the three hundred-handed **Hecatoncheires**. When Ouranos imprisons her children, she fashions the sickle and prompts her son **Kronos** to castrate him; later she stands against the new gods as well, mother of the **Giants** (from the blood of Ouranos) and, by Tartarus, of the monster **Typhon** who challenges Zeus.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: earth, the fertile and generative ground, and the primordial order that precedes and underlies the gods. Roles: first mother, source of the Titans and the pre-Olympian races, giver of oracles (associated with Delphi before Apollo in some traditions), and prophetic counselor. Etymology: Greek *Gaîa* / *Gê*, "earth," of disputed further derivation; the Roman *interpretatio* identified her with **Terra** / **Tellus**. She is invoked as a witness to oaths and as the nurse of all living things. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and Homer's epics [[source.homer]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The order of the first beings and the Terra/Tellus *interpretatio* are attested; the genealogies and the succession myth are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
