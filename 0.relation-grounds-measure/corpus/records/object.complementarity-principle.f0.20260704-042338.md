**statement (plain)** — the first wave said an external tool counts as mind if it does what an inner process would do (parity). The second wave drops that demand: a notebook, a diagram, a writing system need not resemble any inner process — they can be "radically unlike" it — and still be genuinely part of cognition precisely because they contribute something the brain cannot, working in complement.

**basis** — Sutton (2010) names the two waves and the complementarity principle; Menary's "cognitive integration" (2007/2010) is its sibling framing (integration of manipulating external vehicles); Kirchhoff's third wave (2012) pushes further, denying that internal/external properties are fixed at all.
