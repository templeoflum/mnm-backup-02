Lars Rune Christensen, "Stigmergy in Human Practice: Coordination in Construction Work," *Cognitive Systems Research* 21 (2013): 40–51.

Contains — 14 months of ethnographic fieldwork on Copenhagen building sites. Defines human stigmergy as distributed cooperative tasks "partly integrated by virtue of individuals paying heed to the material evidence of work previously accomplished by others while performing their own tasks" — framed as a "heed concept" (Ryle), deliberately dissociated from the entomological stimulus-response model. Central case: sequential trades building a partition wall, each paying heed to the prior state without talking to the next; notes a *forward-looking* variant (an electrician hangs a cable conspicuously so the carpenter heeds it). Draws the key distinction: **stigmergy** (first-order, coordination inside the doing) vs. **articulation work** (Strauss/Schmidt; second-order coordination — meetings, schedules), which together with **awareness** form a "trinity." Grounds why: plans are underspecified (Suchman 1987) [[source.suchman-1987]] and pace leaves no time to talk.
