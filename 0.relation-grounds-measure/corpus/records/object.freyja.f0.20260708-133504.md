**statement (plain)** — **Freyja** (Old Norse *Freyja*, "Lady") is the foremost goddess of the **Vanir**, daughter of **Njörðr** and sister of **Freyr**, dwelling among the Aesir. She is associated with love, fertility, war, and death: of the warriors slain in battle she receives half into her hall **Fólkvangr**, the other half going to Odin's Valhalla. She owns the necklace **Brísingamen** and a **feather cloak** that grants flight, and rides a chariot drawn by cats. She is the practitioner of **seiðr** (sorcery), which — Snorri says — she taught to the Aesir. In *Þrymskviða* the giant Þrymr demands her as the price of Thor's stolen hammer.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: love, fertility, war, death, and magic (*seiðr*). Key attributes: the necklace **Brísingamen**, the flight-granting feather cloak, the cat-drawn chariot, the hall **Fólkvangr**, the boar **Hildisvíni** (*Hyndluljóð*). She belongs to the **Vanir**, given to the Aesir after the Aesir–Vanir war; Snorri's *Gylfaginning* names her the most renowned of the goddesses. Loose cross-pantheon comparisons (Venus/Aphrodite) are made by analogy of function, not descent. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Norse mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested across the Poetic Edda [[source.poetic-edda]] and systematized in Snorri's Prose Edda [[source.prose-edda]]; the standard reference treatment is Lindow [[source.lindow-2001]]. The connected biography owes much to Snorri's 13th-c. Christian framing and should be read as attested tradition, not neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
