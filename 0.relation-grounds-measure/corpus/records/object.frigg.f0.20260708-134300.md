**statement (plain)** — **Frigg** (Old Norse *Frigg*) is the highest of the goddesses of the Aesir, wife of **Odin** and queen of Asgard: she is associated with marriage, motherhood, and foreknowledge. Snorri names her foremost among the goddesses; she knows all fates but says nothing of them. She is the mother of **Baldr**, and when his death is foreshadowed she extracts oaths from all things — fire, water, metals, stones, sicknesses, beasts, and every other thing — not to harm him, but overlooks the young mistletoe as too slight to bind; that omission is the opening through which Baldr is killed.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: marriage, motherhood, the household, foreknowledge of fate. Key associations: her hall **Fensalir**, her handmaid **Fulla**, her role as Odin's counterpart-queen. Germanic cognates: **Frīg** (Anglo-Saxon), from Proto-Germanic *\*Frijjō* "beloved, lady"; her name lies behind Friday (*Frīgedæg*), which the Roman-era *interpretatio* aligned with *dies Veneris* / Venus. The long-debated question of whether Frigg and **Freyja** derive from a single earlier goddess is a scholarly hypothesis, not a matter of the attested texts. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Norse mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested across the Poetic Edda [[source.poetic-edda]] and systematized in Snorri's Prose Edda [[source.prose-edda]]; the standard reference treatment is Lindow [[source.lindow-2001]]. The Baldr episode, with the oaths and the overlooked mistletoe, is preserved most fully by Snorri; it should be read as attested tradition through his 13th-c. Christian framing, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
