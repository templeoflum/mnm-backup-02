**statement (plain)** — **Ullr** (Old Norse *Ullr*) is a god associated with archery, skiing and snowshoes, hunting, and single combat. Snorri names him in *Gylfaginning* and *Skáldskaparmál* as the son of **Sif** and stepson of **Thor**, so fine an archer and skier that no one can rival him, fair to look on and warlike, and a good one to call on in single combat. *Grímnismál* calls his dwelling Ýdalir ("yew-dales" — the yew being the bow-wood), and *Atlakviða* refers to swearing an oath on "Ullr's ring." Little continuous narrative about Ullr survives, yet he was clearly a major god in the older layer of belief: he is among the most common god-elements in Norwegian and Swedish place-names, which points to a wide cult now largely lost to the surviving stories.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: archery, skiing and snowshoes, the hunt, single combat, and oath-swearing. Key relations: son of **Sif**; stepson of **Thor**; his hall Ýdalir. His dwelling appears in the Poetic Edda in *Grímnismál* and the oath-ring in *Atlakviða*; Snorri's *Gylfaginning* and *Skáldskaparmál* supply his parentage and his skill in archery, skiing, and single combat. His narrative attestation is unusually thin, and his standing rests largely on the abundant place-name evidence rather than on told myth. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Norse mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested across the Poetic Edda [[source.poetic-edda]] and systematized in Snorri's Prose Edda [[source.prose-edda]]; the standard reference treatment is Lindow [[source.lindow-2001]].
