**statement (plain)** — the Tree of Life (*ilan*) arranges the ten [[object.sefirot]] in a fixed configuration joined by 22 paths (the Hebrew letters), forming a cosmological map read both as the descent of divine emanation and as a path of ascent and return. The diagram itself is a later medieval development (in and after the *Bahir*).

**basis** — Scholem; later scholarship on the *ilan* diagrams (Chajes). [[source.scholem-kabbalah-1974]] **[confirmed]** *(status `doctrinal`.)*

**extension (research07 re-mine, 20260704)** — Chajes's ilan scholarship: the parchment *ilanot* are "maps of God," "at once maps, mandalas, and memory palaces," and their use is **performative** — "scrolling an ilan parchment in contemplative study, the kabbalist participated mimetically in *tikkun*" — the diagram is USED, not merely viewed. Provenance: the form is "primarily indebted to the Porphyrian tree and maps of the celestial spheres" (not ancient Assyrian sources); the most widely reproduced modern Tree derives from Kircher's *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652), a Christian-Hermetic adaptation, with a Reuchlin-line variant (Malkuth 1-path vs 3-path). [[source.chajes-2022]] [[source.chajes-2020]] **[confirmed]** The claim "the paths, not the nodes, carry the system" is a modern Hermetic-Qabalah emphasis, not a documented medieval thesis — the flag rides [[object.tarot-path-correspondences]] and [[object.pathworking]].
