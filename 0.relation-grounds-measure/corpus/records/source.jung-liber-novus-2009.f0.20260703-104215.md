C. G. Jung, *The Red Book: Liber Novus*, ed. Sonu Shamdasani, trans. Shamdasani, Kyburz & Peck (W. W. Norton, 2009); composed c. 1913–1930.

Contains — the primary record of Jung's **active imagination**: deliberately "dropping" into a fantasizing state and dialoguing with autonomous inner figures (notably Philemon), from which he concluded "there are things in the psyche which I do not produce, but which produce themselves and have their own life." The pivotal case of *deliberate, cultivated* inward channeling by a major psychologist who framed the material's autonomy as a psychological fact while bracketing metaphysics. Shamdasani's critical apparatus makes it the scholarly edition.

Access — ISBN 978-0-393-06567-1. Method also set out in Jung, "The Transcendent Function" (CW 8). **[confirmed as attribution]** — edition/publisher confirmed from knowledge; primary text, read as testimony-of-practice not doctrine.
