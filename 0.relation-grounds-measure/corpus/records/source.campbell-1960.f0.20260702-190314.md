Donald T. Campbell, "Blind variation and selective retention in creative thought as in other knowledge processes," *Psychological Review* 67, no. 6 (1960): 380–400.

Contains — the **blind-variation-and-selective-retention (BVSR)** model: creative and other knowledge processes proceed by generating variations *blindly* (without foresight of which will succeed) and then selectively retaining the useful ones — a Darwinian "generate broadly, then select" account. Elaborated across exceptional creativity by Dean Keith Simonton; genuinely disputed (Gabora, Weisberg argue creative variation is not truly "blind" and the Darwinian framing is inapt).
