**statement (plain)** — The Bourdon Illusion, discovered by French psychologist Benjamin Bourdon in 1902, is a geometric distortion illusion in which objectively straight edges of a figure appear curved or bent. The illusion typically occurs when a rectilinear figure, such as a rectangle or square, is viewed in the context of other geometric patterns or when specific internal features create local orientation conflicts. Observers consistently perceive deviations from straightness along edges that are physically linear, demonstrating systematic misperception of geometric properties.
