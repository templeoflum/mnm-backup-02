**statement (plain)** — The phenakistoscope is an early animation device invented by Belgian physicist Joseph Plateau in 1832 that demonstrates the visual system's capacity to perceive continuous motion from a rapid sequence of discrete static images. The device consists of a spinning disc with sequential animation frames arranged radially around its circumference, interspersed with regularly spaced viewing slits. When the disc is spun and viewed in a mirror through the slits, the sequential images blend together perceptually to create the appearance of smooth, continuous motion.
