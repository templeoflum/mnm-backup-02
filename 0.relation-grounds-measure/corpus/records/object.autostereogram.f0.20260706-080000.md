**statement (plain)** — Autostereograms, popularly known as Magic Eye images, are single two-dimensional images containing repeating patterns that, when viewed with decoupled vergence and accommodation, reveal hidden three-dimensional scenes. Unlike traditional stereograms requiring side-by-side image pairs, autostereograms encode depth information within a single image through carefully controlled horizontal shifts in the repeating pattern. When observers diverge or converge their eyes to fuse non-adjacent repetitions of the pattern, the visual system interprets the varying horizontal disparities as depth variations, creating vivid perception of surfaces floating at different distances.
