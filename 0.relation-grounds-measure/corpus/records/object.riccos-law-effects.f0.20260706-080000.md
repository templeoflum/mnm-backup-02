**statement (plain)** — Ricco's Law Effects describe the spatial summation behavior at visual detection threshold, stating that within a critical area, stimulus area and intensity exhibit perfect reciprocal tradeoff: the product of area and intensity required for threshold detection remains constant. Discovered by Italian astronomer Annibale Ricco in 1877 during investigations of stellar visibility, this principle reveals fundamental constraints on early visual processing. Ricco's Law operates within a limited spatial region called Ricco's area, beyond which summation becomes less efficient and follows Piper's Law or other partial summation models.
