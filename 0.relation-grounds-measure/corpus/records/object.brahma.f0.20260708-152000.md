**statement (plain)** — **Brahma** (Sanskrit *Brahmā*, masculine) is the creator, the first god of the **Trimurti** beside **Vishnu** the preserver and **Shiva** the destroyer: the maker of the worlds and of beings at the beginning of each cosmic cycle. He is pictured with **four faces** looking to the four quarters, and is said to be born from the **lotus** that rises from the navel of the sleeping **Vishnu**. His consort is **Saraswati**, goddess of speech, learning, and the arts. Though creator, he is **comparatively little-worshipped** — few temples are dedicated to him — the living cults belonging to Vishnu and Shiva.

**statement (attestation)** — role: creator; the first of the Trimurti. Key attributes: **four faces** (chaturmukha) and four arms; the mount **hamsa** (goose/swan); the sacred texts (the Vedas) and the water-pot and rosary; his birth from the navel-lotus of Vishnu. Texts: distinct from the neuter absolute **Brahman** of the Upanishads and continuous with the Vedic creator **Prajapati**; the personal creator god of the classical **Trimurti** as set out in the **Mahabharata** and the **Puranas**. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Hindu mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in the **Mahabharata** [[source.mahabharata]] and the Puranas as the personal creator of the Trimurti; developed from the Vedic/Upanishadic **Brahman** (the neuter absolute) and the creator **Prajapati** — the masculine god Brahma is to be distinguished from the neuter Brahman. His minor cultic standing relative to Vishnu and Shiva is attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
