**statement (plain)** — Sarcone's Müller-Lyer illusion is a contemporary variant of the classic Müller-Lyer illusion created by visual artist and cognitive researcher Gianni Sarcone. Rather than using traditional line segments with inward- or outward-pointing arrowheads, Sarcone's version employs filled shapes, solid geometric forms, or area-based elements to produce comparable length distortions. The innovation lies in demonstrating that the Müller-Lyer effect does not depend critically on the presence of explicit linear fins or angles, but emerges from more general spatial relationships involving boundary configuration and figural extent.
