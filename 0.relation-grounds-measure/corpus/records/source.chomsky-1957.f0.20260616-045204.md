**verbatim citation** — Chomsky, Noam. *Syntactic Structures*. The Hague: Mouton, 1957.

**what it contains** — the first systematic presentation of transformational-generative grammar. A grammar is a finite device that generates (specifies) the infinite set of grammatical sentences of a language and no others. Introduces the inadequacy argument against finite-state (Markov) models and against simple phrase-structure grammars for natural language, motivating transformations. Famous illustration "Colorless green ideas sleep furiously" — grammatical yet meaningless — separates syntax from semantics. The generative move (finite means, infinite output) is the seedbed from which recursion-in-language is later named.

**access notes** — widely reprinted; 2nd edition Mouton de Gruyter 1957/2002 with an introduction by David W. Lightfoot. Scanned copies on Internet Archive and university course pages.
