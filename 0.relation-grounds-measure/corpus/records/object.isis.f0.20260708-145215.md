**statement (plain)** — **Isis** is the great goddess of magic, motherhood, and kingship. Sister-wife of **Osiris**, she gathers the scattered pieces of his murdered body and revives him with her magic enough to conceive their son **Horus**, whom she then protects and raises in secret to claim his father's throne. She is the archetypal grieving widow and devoted mother, a mistress of magic and healing, and a protector of the dead. In the later periods her cult spread across the Greco-Roman world, where she became one of the most widely worshipped deities.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: magic, motherhood, kingship, protection, and healing. Iconography: a woman crowned with the throne-sign (her name) or with cow's horns and the solar disk (assimilating **Hathor**); often winged, or shown nursing the infant Horus. Her role in the Osiris myth — mourning, the magical revival of Osiris, and the protection of Horus — is central; she is one of the four protective goddesses of the dead. The Greeks by *interpretatio graeca* commonly equated her with **Demeter**; her Greco-Roman cult (with Serapis) was widespread. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Egyptian mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested from the Pyramid Texts [[source.pyramid-texts]] onward and central to the Book of the Dead [[source.book-of-the-dead]]; the standard reference treatment is Wilkinson [[source.wilkinson-2003]]. The Osiris-cycle role and the wide Greco-Roman cult are established; the *interpretatio* and the myths are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
