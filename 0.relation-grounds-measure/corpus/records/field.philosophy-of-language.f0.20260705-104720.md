**origin** — Bertrand Russell, "Knowledge by Acquaintance and Knowledge by Description" (1910–11) and *The Problems of Philosophy* (1912): direct, non-inferential *acquaintance* vs. mediated, inferential *description*; the acquaintance principle (every proposition we understand is composed of constituents we are acquainted with). [[source.russell-1912]] **[confirmed]**

**codification** — Ludwig Wittgenstein, *Philosophical Investigations* (1953) — the private-language argument (§§243–315): a language whose words refer to what only the speaker can know is impossible; the beetle-in-a-box (§293) "drops out of consideration." [[source.wittgenstein-1953]] **[confirmed]**

**door** — SEP "Private Language"; Wittgenstein *PI* Part I; Russell *Problems of Philosophy* (short, public-domain).

**lineage / fault-line** — Saul Kripke, *Wittgenstein on Rules and Private Language* (1982) recasts the core as a skeptical paradox about rule-following (the "quus" example) with a communal "skeptical solution"; the "Kripkenstein" reading is disputed as the historical Wittgenstein's. [[source.kripke-1982]] Peer to [[field.formal-semantics]] (the compositional / Montague side of meaning) — this is the private-meaning / philosophy-of-mind side, seeded from the private-language angle. **[confirmed]**
