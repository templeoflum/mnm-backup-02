**statement (plain)** — **Vidar** (Old Norse *Víðarr*) is the silent god, a son of **Odin**, and Snorri says his strength is second only to **Thor**'s. He is famed for a great thick shoe, which — Snorri relates in *Skáldskaparmál* — is fashioned from all the leather scraps that men have cut from their own shoes at toe and heel. At **Ragnarök** he avenges his father: when the wolf **Fenrir** has swallowed Odin, Vidar comes forward and sets his shod foot on the wolf's lower jaw, grips the upper jaw, and tears the wolf apart (Snorri's *Gylfaginning*; *Vafþrúðnismál* has him pierce the heart with a sword). He is among the gods who outlive the end and dwell in the renewed world.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: silence, vengeance, and survival past the end of the world. Key relations: son of **Odin**; the avenger who slays **Fenrir**; one of the survivors of **Ragnarök**. His slaying of the wolf and his survival appear in the Poetic Edda in *Völuspá* and *Vafþrúðnismál*, and his dwelling Víði in *Grímnismál*; Snorri's *Gylfaginning* and *Skáldskaparmál* supply the great thick shoe and the manner of the killing — the foot on the lower jaw and the tearing apart of the wolf. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Norse mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested across the Poetic Edda [[source.poetic-edda]] and systematized in Snorri's Prose Edda [[source.prose-edda]]; the standard reference treatment is Lindow [[source.lindow-2001]]. The great shoe and the manner of his killing Fenrir come chiefly from Snorri and should be read as attested tradition through his 13th-c. Christian framing, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
