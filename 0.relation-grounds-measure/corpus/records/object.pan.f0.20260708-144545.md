**statement (plain)** — **Pan** (Greek *Πάν*) is the god of the wild, of shepherds and their flocks, and of rustic music. He is goat-horned and goat-legged, and in the *Homeric Hymn to Pan* is called a son of **Hermes**. He haunts the mountains and woodlands of **Arcadia**, playing the panpipes (*syrinx*) — an instrument named, in the tradition, for the nymph **Syrinx** whom he pursued and who was turned into reeds. His sudden and terrifying cry in lonely places is said to be the source of "panic."

**statement (attestation)** — domains: the wild, shepherds, flocks, and pastoral music. Roles: rustic god of Arcadia, piper of the *syrinx*, and rouser of sudden fear ("panic") in wild solitudes. Etymology: Greek *Pán*, popularly linked to *pân* ("all") but of disputed derivation; the Roman *interpretatio* identified him with **Faunus**. His characteristic form is half-goat, and his instrument is the panpipe. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in the Homeric Hymns [[source.homeric-hymns]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The Arcadian domain, the goat form, the *syrinx*, and the Faunus *interpretatio* are attested; the parentage from Hermes, the Syrinx tale, and the "panic" etymology are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
