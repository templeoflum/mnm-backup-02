**statement (plain)** — the developing brain makes far more synapses than it keeps, then removes the ones that go unused — a structural "overproduce, then reinforce by use." The removal is active: **microglia**, the brain's immune cells, physically engulf weaker synapses, guided by neural activity and molecular signals — complement proteins (C1q/C3) tag synapses for removal, "don't-eat-me" signals (CD47) protect active ones. Less-active synapses are preferentially pruned.

**statement (mechanism)** — the developmental complement to Hebbian strengthening [[object.hebbian-plasticity]]: use-dependent *elimination*, which lets the system sculpt rather than merely accrete. Structurally parallel to other reinforce-by-use/decay processes (pheromone evaporation, slime-mold tube thinning [[object.slime-mold-externalized-memory]]) — a convergent instance of a common dynamic, not the same mechanism.

**basis** — microglial pruning shown in [[source.paolicelli-2011]] and subsequent Schafer/Stevens work. *Support* — established in developmental neuroscience; implicated in neurodevelopmental disorders when dysregulated. *Limits* — "use it or lose it" is not universal: some cortical regions lose more spines than they grow after experience onset, so decay can dominate regardless of use. **[confirmed]**
