**statement (plain)** — The curvature aftereffect is a shape adaptation phenomenon first systematically described by James J. Gibson in 1933, in which prolonged exposure to curved contours causes subsequently viewed straight lines to appear curved in the opposite direction. After adapting to a convex curve, for instance, a straight line appears concave, and vice versa.

**statement (framework)** — category: aftereffect adaptation; perceptual mechanism(s): neural-adaptation, contour-integration.
