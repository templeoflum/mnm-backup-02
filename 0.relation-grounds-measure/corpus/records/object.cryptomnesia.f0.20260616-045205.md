**statement (plain)** — cryptomnesia ("hidden memory") is recalling something previously learned but experiencing it as a new, self-generated idea — a tune, a name, a fact, a foreign phrase — with no sense that it is a memory at all. In mediumship it is the standard psychological explanation for trance "communications" that contain real but obscure information: the medium had encountered it, forgotten the source, and the subliminal mind served it back, often disguised.

**statement (theoretical)** — Flournoy coined the term studying Hélène Smith, attributing her "Hindu," "Martian," and historical-romance material to memories of books, conversations, and imagery, reworked by subliminal imagination and re-presented as revelation [[source.flournoy-1900]]. Myers folds cryptomnesia into the subliminal-self framework: the subliminal stratum stores and elaborates material below the threshold and returns it through automatisms, severed from its source [[source.myers-1903]]. It is a failure of **source monitoring** — content is retrieved, its origin is not.

**basis (empirical / method)** — *support*: cryptomnesia is a documented and experimentally studied memory phenomenon (unconscious plagiarism paradigms; source-monitoring research), and is the consensus deflationary account of "evidential" mediumistic content and of inadvertent plagiarism (e.g., the Helen Keller "Frost King" case). *limits*: invoking cryptomnesia for a specific séance utterance is usually a post-hoc inference (the original exposure is rarely independently documented); it explains *how* veridical-seeming content can arise without a paranormal source, but cannot by itself prove that source in any given case.

**lineage** — first noted in 1874 (Stainton Moses case); named by Flournoy (1900); taken up by Jung (his 1905 dissertation, later the Nietzsche/Kerner case) and threaded into psychoanalysis; reframed in modern cognitive psychology as a source-monitoring / unconscious-plagiarism effect. Operates on the content carried by automatisms [[object.automatism]].
