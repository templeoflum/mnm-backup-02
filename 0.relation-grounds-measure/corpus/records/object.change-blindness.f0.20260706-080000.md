**statement (plain)** — Change blindness refers to the striking failure to detect substantial changes in a visual scene when those changes occur during a visual disruption such as a saccade, blink, brief blank screen, or other transient. Despite the often dramatic nature of the changes, which can include objects appearing, disappearing, changing color, or changing location, observers frequently remain unaware of these alterations even after multiple presentations. This phenomenon challenges intuitive assumptions about the completeness and stability of visual representation, suggesting that detailed scene information is not stored across disruptions as comprehensively as subjective experience implies.
