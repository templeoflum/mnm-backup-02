**statement (plain)** — Suppose the colour you see looking at a tomato is the colour I see looking at the sky, and vice versa. Having both learned the words by pointing at the same objects, our speech and behaviour match perfectly — no test could detect the difference. Our inner colour-worlds might differ in a way permanently hidden from outside.

**statement (framework)** — if genuine spectrum inversion is possible, **functionalism** fails (functionally identical states, different qualia) and **qualia realism** is vindicated; if the scenario is incoherent, functionalism survives. Locke raised it (*Essay* II.xxxii.15, 1689); Shoemaker and Block gave it modern rigour. Draws on empirical colour science. Established; its coherence stays **open**.

**basis** — Locke's *Essay* (1689) [[source.locke-1689]] and Shoemaker's "The Inverted Spectrum" (1982) [[source.shoemaker-1982]]. **[confirmed as attribution]**
