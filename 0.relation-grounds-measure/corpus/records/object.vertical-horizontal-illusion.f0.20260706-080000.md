**statement (plain)** — The vertical-horizontal illusion is one of the oldest and most extensively studied geometric illusions, in which a vertical line appears longer than a horizontal line of equal physical length. The illusion manifests across diverse configurations, including isolated line pairs, L-shapes, T-shapes, and inverted-T arrangements, with typical overestimation of vertical extent ranging from 10 to 30 percent depending on stimulus parameters and viewing conditions. The effect is robust across cultures, age groups, and species, suggesting deep roots in visual system architecture.
