**statement (plain)** — **Indra** (Sanskrit *Indra*) is the Vedic **king of the gods**: god of the **storm**, **thunder**, and **war**, the most invoked deity of the Rigveda. His central myth is the slaying of the serpent-dragon **Vritra**, who had held back the waters — Indra, strengthened by the drink **Soma**, smites Vritra with his thunderbolt and releases the waters (or the rivers, or the light of dawn) upon the world. He wields the **vajra**, the thunderbolt-weapon. In the later epic and Puranic tradition he is **diminished** — still king of the gods and lord of heaven (Svarga), but a lesser figure beneath Vishnu and Shiva, often humbled in myth.

**statement (attestation)** — role: king of the Vedic gods; god of storm, rain, thunder, and war. Key attributes: the **vajra** (thunderbolt); the mount, the white elephant **Airavata**; the drink **Soma**; his heaven **Svarga** and its court. Central myth: the slaying of **Vritra** and the release of the waters (the *Vritrahan*, "Vritra-slayer"). Texts: the foremost god of the **Rigveda**, addressed in more hymns than any other deity; diminished in the **Mahabharata**, **Ramayana**, and Puranas to a lesser sky-king. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Hindu (Vedic) mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested chiefly in the **Rigveda** [[source.rigveda]], where he is the pre-eminent god and the slayer of Vritra; his later diminishment beneath the great gods of the Trimurti is attested tradition across the epics and Puranas, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
