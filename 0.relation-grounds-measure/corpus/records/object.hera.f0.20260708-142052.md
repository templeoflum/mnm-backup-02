**statement (plain)** — **Hera** (Greek *Ἥρα*) is the queen of the gods and goddess of marriage and the wedded state. She is a daughter of the Titans **Kronos** and **Rhea**, and both the sister and the wife of **Zeus**. In the epic tradition she is the jealous and vindictive protector of marriage: she persecutes Zeus's lovers and the children born of them — pursuing **Heracles** across his life, tormenting **Leto** in her labour, and driving her hostility against Troy in the *Iliad*. She is honoured as the patron of women, wives, and lawful wedlock, worshipped at great sanctuaries such as the Heraion of Argos and on Samos.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: marriage, the wedded state, women and childbirth, and queenship. Key attributes: the diadem or crown, the sceptre, the peacock (a later, chiefly Greco-Roman association), the pomegranate, and the cuckoo; sacred cities Argos, Mycenae, and Samos; the epithet *Boōpis* ("ox-eyed"). Children by Zeus include **Ares**, **Hebe**, and **Eileithyia**, and by tradition she bears **Hephaestus**. The Roman *interpretatio* identified her with **Juno**. The etymology of the name *Hērā* is disputed and remains unresolved. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and Homer's epics [[source.homer]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The Juno *interpretatio* is established; the etymology of the name is uncertain, and the genealogies and narratives are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
