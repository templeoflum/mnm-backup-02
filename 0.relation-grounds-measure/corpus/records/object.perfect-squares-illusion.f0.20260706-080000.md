**statement (plain)** — The Perfect Squares Illusion demonstrates systematic misperceptions of geometric regularity, in which objectively perfect squares appear distorted when embedded in specific background patterns or contextual arrangements. The squares may appear to have curved edges, non-right angles, or unequal side lengths, despite their precise mathematical construction. This phenomenon is closely related to other context-dependent geometric illusions such as the Orbison, Hering, and Zöllner illusions, all of which reveal how surrounding elements can profoundly distort the perceived shape of foreground figures.
