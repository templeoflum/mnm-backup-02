**statement (plain)** — The No Curved Lines Illusion represents a class of geometric patterns in which physically straight line segments arranged in specific configurations produce a compelling perception of curvature. Despite being composed entirely of horizontal, vertical, or diagonal straight elements, the overall pattern appears to contain smooth curves, arcs, or wave-like distortions. This phenomenon is related to several well-documented illusion families, including the café wall illusion and the Fraser spiral illusion, all of which demonstrate how local orientation signals can be integrated into incorrect global shape percepts.
