**statement (plain)** — The Darwin illusion, also known as the afterimage gestalt phenomenon, demonstrates that afterimages generated from complex visual scenes preserve not only the retinotopic arrangement of stimulus elements but also their perceptual organization according to Gestalt principles of grouping and figure-ground segregation. When observers adapt to a structured scene containing multiple elements organized by proximity, similarity, good continuation, or other grouping cues, the resulting afterimage maintains these organizational properties rather than appearing as a mere collection of independent local afterimages. This preservation of global structure in afterimages reveals that adaptation and afterimage generation involve neural representations that encode relational and configurational information, not just local luminance and chromatic properties.
