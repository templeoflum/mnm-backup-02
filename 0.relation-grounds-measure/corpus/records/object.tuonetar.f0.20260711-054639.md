**statement (plain)** — **Tuonetar** is the hostess of **Tuonela**, wife of **Tuoni**, who offers the newcomer a deadly hospitality. To the traveller who reaches the land of the dead she brings a tankard of beer that is full of frogs and worms, and a bed from which none who lie down ever rise. She is named as a mother of the diseases that afflict the living.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: death, disease. Her welcome figures death as a seductive rest — the dangerous comfort of giving up the struggle of life — and marks the boundary a living visitor must refuse if they are to return. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — Tuonetar is attested in the Kalevala and treated in modern scholarship ([[source.pentikainen-1999]]); this is documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). The Kalevala is Elias Lönnrot's nineteenth-century compilation of oral folk poetry, not an ancient text; the figures of the death-realm's keepers are drawn from older oral song. **[confirmed as attribution]**
