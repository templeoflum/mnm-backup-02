**statement (plain)** — **Tyr** (Old Norse *Týr*) is the war-god of the Aesir, associated with war and with law and justice, and named the bravest of the gods. When the gods sought to bind the wolf **Fenrir** with the fetter **Gleipnir**, the wolf would consent only if a god laid a hand in its mouth as a pledge; Tyr alone did so, and when the wolf found itself bound and could not break free, it bit off his right hand. At **Ragnarök** he fights and falls against the hound **Garmr**, each slaying the other.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: war, martial courage, and law/justice; he is the one-handed god, marked by the pledge given to **Fenrir**. Germanic cognates: **Tīw / Tīg** (Anglo-Saxon), from Proto-Germanic *\*Tīwaz*, ultimately from the Indo-European sky-god root *\*dyeus* (cognate with Latin *deus / Iuppiter*, Greek Zeus); the Roman-era *interpretatio* identified him with **Mars** (hence Tuesday / *Tīwesdæg* ← *dies Martis*). His prominence in the surviving myths is slight relative to what the philology and the day-name suggest of an earlier, larger role. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Norse mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested across the Poetic Edda [[source.poetic-edda]] and systematized in Snorri's Prose Edda [[source.prose-edda]]; the standard reference treatment is Lindow [[source.lindow-2001]]. The binding of Fenrir and the loss of the hand are related in full by Snorri; the etymology (*\*Tīwaz*) and the Mars *interpretatio* are philologically established. The connected material should be read as attested tradition through Snorri's 13th-c. Christian framing, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
