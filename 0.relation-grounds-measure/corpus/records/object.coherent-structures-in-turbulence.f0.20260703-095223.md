**statement (plain)** — Although turbulence looks random, it is not structureless. Embedded in it are organized, recurring patterns of motion — coherent structures — such as rolls, hairpin vortices, and large-scale eddies that persist long enough to matter dynamically. Recognizing them means a turbulent flow can be partly understood, modeled with few degrees of freedom, and even re-directed by acting on the structures rather than the mean flow.

**characterizations** — three complementary handles: Townsend's **attached-eddy hypothesis** (wall turbulence as a self-similar hierarchy of eddies scaling with wall distance) [[source.townsend-1976]] **[confirmed as attribution]**; Lumley's **Proper Orthogonal Decomposition** (the structures defined objectively as the most energetic eigenfunctions of the two-point velocity correlation) [[source.lumley-1967]] **[candidate]**; and Hussain's **definition** of a coherent structure as a connected region over which vorticity is instantaneously phase-correlated [[source.hussain-1986]] **[confirmed as attribution]**.

**relevance** — the bridge between BREAKDOWN (turbulence) and re-channeling: reactive flow control targets coherent structures. See [[object.flow-control-taxonomy]].
