**statement (plain)** — The Rhombille Tiling Illusion arises from periodic arrangements of rhombi (diamond-shaped parallelograms) that the visual system spontaneously interprets as three-dimensional cube structures, despite the pattern being objectively flat. This geometric tiling can be perceived in at least three distinct orientations, each presenting the cubes as stacked in a different direction, with the pattern appearing to shift between these interpretations through multistable perception. The illusion demonstrates how the brain preferentially interprets certain two-dimensional geometric configurations as three-dimensional objects, particularly when the stimulus contains angles and adjacency relationships consistent with familiar polyhedra viewed in parallel or isometric projection.
