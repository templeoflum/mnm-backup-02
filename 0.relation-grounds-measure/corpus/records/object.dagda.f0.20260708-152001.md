**statement (plain)** — the **Dagda** (Old Irish *In Dagda*, "the good god") is the great father-god of the **Tuatha Dé Danann**, the divine race of Irish myth: a god of abundance, druidry, and wisdom, and a chief among the gods. He wields a great **club** (*lorg mór*) so vast it is dragged on wheels — one end kills, the other restores life — and owns a **cauldron** (*coire ansic*) that never empties and leaves no company unsatisfied, and a living **harp** (*Uaithne*) that orders the seasons and moves men to weep, laugh, or sleep. He is associated with the tribe of gods in the Irish tradition (as distinct from the Welsh Mabinogi), figuring in the Second Battle of Mag Tuired against the **Fomorians**.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: abundance and plenty, druidry and magic, wisdom, life and death, kingship among the gods. Key attributes: the great **club** of life and death, the inexhaustible **cauldron**, the magic **harp**. Associations: father of **Brigit** and (in tradition) of **Aengus** by **Boann**; a chief of the Tuatha Dé Danann. Text: the Irish mythological cycle — the **Lebor Gabála Érenn** and **Cath Maige Tuired** (the [Second] Battle of Mag Tuired). Etymology: the name is standardly glossed "the good god," in the sense of *good-at-all-things*, not morally good. Irish tradition (no Welsh counterpart is asserted here). **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Irish mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in the Lebor Gabála Érenn [[source.lebor-gabala]] and Cath Maige Tuired; the standard reference treatment is MacKillop, *Dictionary of Celtic Mythology*. The name-gloss "good god" is well established; the attributes (club, cauldron, harp) and genealogies are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
