**statement (plain)** — The Knill and Kersten Illusion demonstrates how the visual system integrates three-dimensional shape information with lighting interpretation to determine surface lightness. In this illusion, two regions of identical luminance appear dramatically different in perceived lightness based on their position within a three-dimensionally interpreted scene. The effect arises when the visual system infers different lighting conditions or surface orientations for the two regions, leading to different reflectance estimates despite identical retinal stimulation.
