**statement (plain)** — **Vishnu** (Sanskrit *Viṣṇu*) is the preserver, the god who sustains cosmic order (**dharma**) and, whenever it decays, descends into the world in a succession of **avatars** (incarnations) to restore it — among them **Rama** and **Krishna**. In the classical **Trimurti** he stands beside **Brahma** the creator and **Shiva** the destroyer as the preserver. He is pictured reclining in cosmic sleep on the coils of the great serpent **Shesha** (Ananta) upon the ocean of milk, between the dissolutions and creations of the world, with the lotus bearing Brahma rising from his navel. His consort is **Lakshmi**, goddess of fortune.

**statement (attestation)** — role: preserver and sustainer of dharma; the supreme god of the **Vaishnava** traditions. Key attributes: four arms bearing the **conch** (shankha), **discus** (chakra / Sudarshana), **mace** (gada), and **lotus**; the mount **Garuda**, the eagle-being; the serpent-couch Shesha; the ten classical **avatars** (Dashavatara: Matsya, Kurma, Varaha, Narasimha, Vamana, Parashurama, Rama, Krishna, [Buddha], Kalki). Texts: a minor solar deity in the **Rigveda**, remembered for his three world-spanning strides; risen to supreme preserver in the **Mahabharata**, **Ramayana**, and the Vaishnava **Puranas** (Vishnu Purana, Bhagavata Purana). **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Hindu mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested from the **Rigveda** [[source.rigveda]] (as a minor Vedic solar god of the three strides) through the epics — the **Mahabharata** [[source.mahabharata]] and the **Ramayana** [[source.ramayana]], where he is the god who incarnates as Krishna and Rama — and given fullest form in the Vaishnava Puranas. The rise from minor Vedic deity to supreme preserver is attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
