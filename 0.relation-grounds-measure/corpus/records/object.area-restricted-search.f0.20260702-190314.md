**statement (plain)** — a forager that finds food intensifies local search (turning more, moving less) to exploit the patch, then straightens out and travels to explore when the patch depletes — **area-restricted search**. Strikingly, human *memory* retrieval does the same: naming animals, people cluster within a semantic patch (pets, then farm animals, then African animals) and switch patches when the current one runs dry, following the same optimal patch-leaving policy (marginal-value theorem [[object.marginal-value-theorem]]).

**statement (framework)** — evidence that internal cognitive search and external spatial foraging share mechanisms and an evolutionary origin ("goal-directed cognition as an evolutionary descendant of spatial foraging") [[source.hills-2015]] — the concrete backbone of the explore/exploit account of thinking.

**basis** — Hills, Jones & Todd 2012 (semantic-memory foraging) [[source.hills-2012]]; broader synthesis [[source.hills-2015]]. *Support* — well-replicated in memory-fluency tasks; common dopaminergic modulation reported. *Limits* — the space↔mind mapping is an analogy grounded in shared dynamics, not identical substrate. **[confirmed]**
