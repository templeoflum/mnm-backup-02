**statement (plain)** — Lewin models any behavior or social situation as a quasi-stationary equilibrium held in place by two opposing sets of forces: *driving* forces pushing toward change and *restraining* forces holding it back. To move the equilibrium you can add driving force or remove restraining force — but adding drive raises tension and provokes resistance, while removing restraints changes the level with less tension. Change should therefore work primarily by *reducing restraints*.

**relevance** — the psychological statement of channeling as **negative work**: don't push harder on the person, remove what blocks the intended path. The direct ancestor of choice architecture ([[object.nudge]], [[object.choice-architecture]]) and of situational **channel factors** ([[object.channel-factors]]) [[source.lewin-1947]] **[confirmed as attribution]**. Widely used as an applied change-management tool.
