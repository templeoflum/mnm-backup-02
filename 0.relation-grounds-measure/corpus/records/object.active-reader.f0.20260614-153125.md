**statement (plain)** — the active reader is the reader an explorable explanation is built for: someone who, while reading, asks their own questions, considers alternatives, questions the author's assumptions, and tries to generalize from specific examples — rather than passively consuming the author's argument. The medium's job is to let those impulses be acted on inside the document.

**statement** — Victor's framing: a typical reading tool "displays the author's argument, and nothing else," so the reader's own questions, alternatives, and doubts stay internal and unanswerable; an explorable explanation exists to "enable and encourage truly active reading." Victor characterizes the active reader as one who "asks questions, considers alternatives, questions assumptions" and "tries to generalize specific examples." **[confirmed]**

**basis** — the design premise underneath the [[object.explorable-explanation]] and the [[object.reactive-document]]: the reader is treated as a participant, not an audience. Victor frames the goal as getting the reader "out of her head" into an external space she can react against. **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — the broader notion of **"active reading" predates Victor**: Mortimer Adler & Charles Van Doren, *How to Read a Book* (1940), define active vs. passive reading and the "demanding reader" who asks questions of the text — carried as lineage **[confirmed as attribution]**. The **"active reader" of interactive documents** — the reader who can act on those questions in the medium itself — is **Bret Victor's framing** (2011) [[source.victor-2011]] **[confirmed]**.
