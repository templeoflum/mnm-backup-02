**statement (plain)** — **Apsu** is the primordial freshwater, the abyss beneath the earth. In the **Enuma Elish** he is the male primordial water whose mingling with **Tiamat**, the salt water, begins creation and brings forth the first generations of gods. Disturbed by the noise of the young gods, Apsu resolves to destroy them so he can rest; the wise **Enki/Ea** learns of the plot, casts a sleep upon him, kills him, and builds his own dwelling upon the body of Apsu — the subterranean freshwater domain that bears his name.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: the primordial freshwater, the subterranean abyss, the source of springs and wells. Culture: Sumerian **Abzu**, Akkadian **Apsu**. In the Enuma Elish he is the male primordial water who mingles with **Tiamat** to begin creation, plots against the young gods, and is slain by **Enki/Ea**, who founds his abode upon him. The apsu is also the name of the cosmic freshwater domain and of a sacred water basin in the temple. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Mesopotamian mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested centrally in the Enuma Elish [[source.enuma-elish]] and treated in the standard reference by Black & Green [[source.black-green-1992]]. His role as primordial freshwater and his death at Enki/Ea's hand are established; the creation narrative is attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
