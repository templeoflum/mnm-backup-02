**statement (plain)** — Filling-in refers to perceptual completion processes by which the visual system constructs a continuous and complete representation of the visual field despite discontinuities, gaps, or absent information in the retinal image. The most familiar example is the blind spot, where the absence of photoreceptors at the optic disc creates a region devoid of visual input that is nonetheless imperceptible under normal viewing conditions, appearing filled with the surrounding pattern or texture. Filling-in also occurs for artificial scotomas created by stabilized images, occlusion boundaries where background patterns appear to extend behind occluding objects, and neon color spreading where color appears to fill regions beyond its physical extent.

**statement (framework)** — category: cognitive higher order; perceptual mechanism(s): figure-ground-segregation, predictive-coding, lateral-inhibition.
