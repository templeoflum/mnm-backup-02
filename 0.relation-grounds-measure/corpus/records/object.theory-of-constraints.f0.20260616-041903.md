statement (plain): every goal-directed system has at least one constraint — a limiting factor that caps the rate at which it achieves its goal. Because the chain is only as strong as its weakest link, improving any non-constraint yields little or no gain in system output; the framework directs management to find the constraint and organize the whole system around managing and elevating it.

basis: introduced in narrative form in *The Goal* (1984), where a plant's output is shown to be governed by a single bottleneck machine, and improvements elsewhere fail to raise throughput until the bottleneck is addressed [[source.goldratt-cox-1984]] [confirmed]. Codified as a named management methodology in Goldratt's 1990 monograph, which frames TOC as a process of ongoing improvement [[source.goldratt-1990]] [confirmed], and surveyed as a mature body of knowledge with its applications and components in the 2010 Handbook [[source.cox-schleier-2010]] [confirmed].

limits: TOC is a managerial framework, not a proved theorem — its claims are operational and validated by practice and case studies rather than formal proof. Its sharpest results assume a system with a small number of stable, identifiable constraints; in highly variable systems, or where the binding constraint shifts rapidly or is a policy/market rather than a physical resource, the "single bottleneck" picture is an idealization. [confirmed]
