**statement (plain)** — The Leaning Tower Illusion is a compelling geometric illusion discovered by Frederick Kingdom and colleagues in 2007, in which two identical side-by-side photographs of a receding object—such as the Leaning Tower of Pisa—appear to diverge, making one tower seem to lean more dramatically than the other. The illusion occurs because the visual system expects that two parallel structures receding into the distance should converge in the retinal image due to linear perspective. When presented with two identical images showing parallel vertical edges, the brain interprets them as representing two different viewing angles or physical configurations rather than identical copies.
