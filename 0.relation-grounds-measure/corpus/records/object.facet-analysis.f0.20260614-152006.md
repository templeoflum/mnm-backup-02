**statement (plain)** — facet analysis is the method of breaking a subject down into independent groups of terms (facets), where each facet collects the terms produced by one single characteristic of division. Instead of forcing every subject onto one fixed branching tree, you sort its concepts into separate, non-overlapping piles — one pile per question you can ask about the subject (its kind, its material, its action, its place, its time) — and later recombine them.

**statement** — facet analysis is "the sorting of terms in a given field of knowledge into homogeneous, mutually exclusive facets, each derived from [a] single characteristic of division" (Vickery). A facet is "a group of terms derived by taking each term and defining it, *per genus et differentiam*, with respect to its parent class." Each facet isolates one characteristic of division so that the facets are mutually exclusive and jointly cover the field. **[confirmed]**

**basis** — the method rests on per-genus-et-differentiam division applied facet by facet rather than once over the whole field: a subject is analysed into its constituent facets (analysis), which are then synthesized into a compound class. This is the analytical half of the analytico-synthetic approach (see [[object.analytico-synthetic-classification]]). The facet-analytic paradigm is widely held to be the most distinct approach to knowledge organization within library and information science and has dominated modern classification theory. **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — originated by S. R. Ranganathan and first embodied in his [[object.colon-classification]] (1st ed. 1933) and theorised in *Prolegomena to Library Classification* (1937) [[source.ranganathan-1933]] [[source.ranganathan-1937]]. The crisp definition above is B. C. Vickery's, in *Faceted Classification* (1960) [[source.vickery-1960]]. The method was consolidated and generalised beyond library shelving by the Classification Research Group (CRG), London, from 1952 [[source.crg-london]]; its most theoretically developed product is the second edition of the *Bliss Bibliographic Classification* (BC2). **[confirmed]**
