Brigitte C. Madrian and Dennis F. Shea, "The Power of Suggestion: Inertia in 401(k) Participation and Savings Behavior," *The Quarterly Journal of Economics* 116(4):1149–1187 (November 2001).

Contains — the empirical anchor of the default-effect literature. A natural experiment at one large U.S. firm: switching the 401(k) plan from opt-in to automatic enrollment (opt-out) sharply raised participation, even though no economic feature of the plan changed. A large fraction of automatically enrolled participants stuck to the **default contribution rate and default fund** — outcomes almost no one chose under opt-in — attributed to inertia and to reading the default as implicit advice. The result is the canonical demonstration that defaults move behavior.
