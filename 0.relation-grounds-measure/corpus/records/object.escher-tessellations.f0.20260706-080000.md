**statement (plain)** — Escher tessellations are interlocking tile patterns developed by Dutch artist M.C. Escher in which recognizable figures such as birds, fish, reptiles, or other forms completely cover a plane without gaps or overlaps, often with adjacent tiles representing different or complementary figures. These mathematical artworks exploit principles of symmetry, periodic tiling, and figure-ground organization to create visually compelling demonstrations of how the perceptual system segments complex scenes into meaningful objects.
