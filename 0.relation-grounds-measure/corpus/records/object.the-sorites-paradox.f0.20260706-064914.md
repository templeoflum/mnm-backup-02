**statement (plain)** — A million grains make a heap. Removing one grain still leaves a heap. Apply that step repeatedly and you reach one grain, or none — still a 'heap', absurdly. Each step looks impeccable, yet the conclusion is false.

**statement (framework)** — the paradox of **vagueness**: predicates like 'heap', 'bald', 'tall' lack sharp boundaries, breaking classical logic under tolerance. Responses: epistemicism (Williamson — sharp but unknowable boundaries), supervaluationism, fuzzy/many-valued logic, contextualism. Established; the correct semantics for vagueness stays **open**.
