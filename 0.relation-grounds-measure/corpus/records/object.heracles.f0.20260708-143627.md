**statement (plain)** — **Heracles** (Greek *Ἡρακλῆς*) is the greatest of the Greek heroes: son of **Zeus** and the mortal **Alcmene**, born at Thebes and hated from birth by **Hera**, whose jealousy drives his persecution (his very name, "glory of Hera," carries the irony). Maddened by Hera, he kills his own children; in expiation the Delphic oracle sends him into servitude to **Eurystheus** of Tiryns, for whom he performs the **Twelve Labours** — among them slaying the **Nemean lion** and the **Lernaean Hydra**, capturing the Erymanthian boar and the Ceryneian hind, cleansing the Augean stables, and fetching **Cerberus** from the underworld. He dies through the **poisoned robe of Nessus** — the centaur's tainted blood, given to him by his wife Deianira as a supposed love-charm, burns him beyond bearing — and is consumed on a pyre on Mount Oeta, after which he is taken up to Olympus and deified (apotheosis).

**statement (attestation)** — domains: strength, heroic labour, expiation, protection, and (after death) apotheosis. Key attributes: the **lion-skin**, the **club**, the bow. Parentage: Zeus and Alcmene of Thebes; his mortal twin Iphicles is son of Amphitryon. The canonical set of **Twelve Labours** in its fixed order and numbering is largely the schema of Apollodorus' Bibliotheca; earlier sources attest many of the deeds without the closed count. The death by the robe of Nessus is dramatized fullest in **Sophocles' *Trachiniae*** (not among the four cited sources — see report). The Roman *interpretatio* identified him with **Hercules**. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Homer's epics [[source.homer]] and the Hesiodic corpus, and systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The fixed canon of the Twelve Labours is the Apollodoran arrangement, and the death-and-apotheosis narrative owes its fullest form to Sophocles' *Trachiniae*; these are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
