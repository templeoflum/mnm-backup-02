**statement (plain)** — Glaucon tells of a shepherd, Gyges, who finds a ring of invisibility and uses it to seize the kingdom. He challenges Socrates: given such a ring — immune to punishment, shame, or reputation — would anyone still act justly? If not, justice is merely instrumental, obeyed only for fear of consequences.

**statement (framework)** — the demanding test of whether **justice is intrinsically valuable** or only instrumentally useful; anticipates social-contract theory and moral psychology's question of the relation between behaviour and motive. Newly sharp under internet anonymity and the online-disinhibition effect. Established; its answer (Socrates argues the just soul is happier) stays **open**.
