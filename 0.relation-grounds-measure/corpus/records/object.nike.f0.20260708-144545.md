**statement (plain)** — **Nike** (Greek *Νίκη*, "Victory") is the goddess and personification of victory. In Hesiod she is a winged daughter of the Titan **Pallas** and the Oceanid **Styx**, sister of Zelos (Rivalry), Kratos (Strength), and Bia (Force) (*Theogony* 383–385). When the gods war against the Titans, Styx brings her children to stand with Zeus, and Nike thereafter attends him; she is likewise a companion of **Athena**.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: victory, in war and in contest. Roles: winged personification of triumph, companion and standing attendant of Zeus and of Athena. Etymology: Greek *Níkē*, "victory"; the Roman *interpretatio* identified her with **Victoria**. Her defining attribute is her wings, and she is regularly shown crowning or bearing the tokens of the victor. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The parentage from Pallas and Styx, the siblings, and the Victoria *interpretatio* are attested; her wings and her attendance on Zeus and Athena are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
