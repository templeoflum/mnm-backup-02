**statement (plain)** — **Tlaloc** is the ancient goggle-eyed rain god of Mesoamerica, giver of the rains that feed the crops and sender of storms, lightning, and drought. Depicted with goggle eyes and jaguar fangs, he is lord of **Tlalocan**, a paradise of the well-watered dead, and is served by the **Tlaloque**, the rain-helpers who pour water and hurl lightning. His cult predates the Aztecs across Mesoamerica; in Tenochtitlan he shared the summit of the Templo Mayor with **Huitzilopochtli**. This is Aztec/Nahua tradition.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: rain, water, storms, lightning, fertility, drought. Nahuatl *Tlaloc*, associated with the earth and with that which lies on its surface. Consort of **Chalchiuhtlicue**; commands the Tlaloque; his goggle-eyed image is one of the oldest and most widespread in Mesoamerica (cf. the Teotihuacan and Maya Chaac storm gods). **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested in the cited sources; documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). The Aztec sources are chiefly the colonial-era codices (Sahagún's *Florentine Codex*), recorded after the conquest; Tlaloc's deep antiquity and pan-Mesoamerican forms mean attributes vary across periods and sources. **[confirmed as attribution]**
