**statement (plain)** — The Motion Aftereffect (MAE), also known as the Waterfall Illusion, is one of the most robust and extensively studied phenomena in visual perception. First formally documented by Robert Addams in 1834 following his observation of stationary rocks appearing to move upward after prolonged viewing of a waterfall, the MAE occurs when a stationary stimulus appears to move in the direction opposite to previously viewed motion. The illusion demonstrates the adaptive nature of motion-sensitive neurons in the visual cortex, which become selectively fatigued during sustained exposure to unidirectional motion.
