**origin** — the oral religious tradition of the **Yoruba** of West Africa: the supreme being **Olodumare**, source of **ashé** (divine power), and the **Orisha** who mediate between the divine and humankind, carried in the **Ifá** divination corpus (the *odu*). **[confirmed]**

**codification** — the scholarly recording of Yoruba religion — E. Bọlaji Idowu's *Olódùmarè: God in Yoruba Belief* (1962) and William Bascom's studies of Ifá divination (1969). [[source.idowu-1962]] [[source.bascom-1969]] **[confirmed]**

**door** — Baba Ifa Karade, *The Handbook of Yoruba Religious Concepts* (1994), a widely-used modern reference to the Orisha. [[source.karade-1994]]

*grade a — origin (oral Ifá tradition), codification (Idowu / Bascom), door (Karade) present. A single-tradition field under [[field.comparative-mythology]]. Field-home for the Yoruba Orisha intake (20260711). NOTE: the ACP "African" roster's one non-Yoruba figure — Anansi (Akan/Ghana) — is a distinct tradition, held out of this field for separate disposition. The Orisha tradition also lives in the Americas as Santería (Cuba) and Candomblé (Brazil).*
