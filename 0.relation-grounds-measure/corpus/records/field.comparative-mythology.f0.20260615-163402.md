**origin (comparative mythology)** — Friedrich Max Müller, "Comparative Mythology," in *Oxford Essays* (1856); coined the "science of religion" (*Religionswissenschaft*, 1873). Also in the lineage: Georges Dumézil's trifunctional hypothesis (1929 *Flamen-Brahman*; *Mitra-Varuna* 1940). [[source.muller-1856]] **[confirmed on attribution; Dumézil's validity heavily contested — carried as lineage, not asserted.]**

**codification (oral tradition)** — Albert B. Lord, *The Singer of Tales* (Harvard, 1960) — the Parry–Lord oral-formulaic theory, built on Milman Parry's (d. 1935) Balkan *guslar* fieldwork; Walter J. Ong, *Orality and Literacy* (Methuen, 1982). [[source.lord-1960]] [[source.ong-1982]] **[confirmed]**

**door** — *The Singer of Tales* free via Harvard's Center for Hellenic Studies; Müller's "Comparative Mythology" public-domain on archive.org; Ong (Routledge reprint).
