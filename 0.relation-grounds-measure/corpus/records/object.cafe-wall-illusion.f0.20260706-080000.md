**statement (plain)** — The Café Wall Illusion is a striking geometric distortion effect in which physically parallel horizontal lines appear to converge and diverge in alternating wedge-shaped patterns. The illusion was brought to prominence by psychologist Richard Gregory in 1979 after observing it on the tiled wall of a café in Bristol, England, though earlier versions had been described by Hugo Münsterberg. The canonical stimulus consists of alternating rows of black and white rectangular tiles, with each row offset by half a tile width relative to adjacent rows, separated by thin gray mortar lines.
