**statement (plain)** — **Dionysus** (Greek *Διόνυσος*) is the Olympian god of wine, ecstasy, ritual madness, and the theatre. He is the son of **Zeus** and the mortal **Semele**, daughter of Cadmus of Thebes — the one great Olympian born of a mortal woman. He is called "twice-born": Semele, tricked by the jealous **Hera** into demanding that Zeus appear in his full divinity, is destroyed by his lightning while pregnant, whereupon Zeus takes the unborn child and sews him into his own **thigh** to complete the gestation, so that he is born a second time from Zeus himself. He is attended by the **maenads** (frenzied women) and the **satyrs**, and his rites bring both joyous release and dangerous madness — as in **Euripides'** *Bacchae*, where he drives the women of Thebes to destroy King Pentheus for denying his divinity.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: wine and the vine, ecstasy and intoxication, ritual madness, fertility of nature, and the theatre (his festivals being the setting of Athenian drama). Key attributes: the **thyrsus** (fennel staff tipped with a pine cone), grapevines and ivy, the wine-cup (*kantharos*), the panther or leopard; his followers the maenads and satyrs. Parentage: son of Zeus and the mortal Semele — the "twice-born" thigh-birth is the standard account, though other birth and identity traditions circulated. The Roman *interpretatio* identified him with **Bacchus** (also Liber). **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]], Homer and the Homeric Hymns and Euripides' Bacchae [[source.homer]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The parentage and the Bacchus *interpretatio* are well established; the birth-narratives and cult traditions vary considerably across sources and should be read as attested tradition, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
