**statement (plain)** — The glory is an atmospheric optical phenomenon appearing as one or more concentric colored rings of light centered on the antisolar point—the point directly opposite the sun from the observer's perspective—typically visible when looking down at clouds or fog from an elevated position such as a mountain or aircraft. The colored rings appear around the observer's own shadow when that shadow is cast onto a cloud layer, creating a halo-like effect that seems to follow the observer's position. The glory results from backscattering of sunlight by cloud droplets through a complex combination of diffraction, reflection, and refraction processes: light enters spherical water droplets, undergoes one or more internal reflections, and exits traveling back toward the source, with different wavelengths being scattered at slightly different angles to produce the characteristic colored ring structure.
