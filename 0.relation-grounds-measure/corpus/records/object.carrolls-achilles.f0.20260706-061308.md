**statement (plain)** — The Tortoise grants "if A then B" and "A" but won't accept "B" until Achilles adds the premise "if (A and if-A-then-B) then B". Achilles obliges — and the Tortoise demands yet another premise licensing *that* step, forever. No stack of premises ever forces the conclusion.

**statement (framework)** — exposes the distinction between **premises** (accepted propositions) and **rules of inference** (acts of inferring): treat a rule as one more premise and inference never gets going — at some point one must *do* the inferring, which is not a further propositional commitment. Anticipates Ryle's knowing-how, Wittgenstein on rule-following, and the tonk problem. Established; the normativity of logic stays **open**.
