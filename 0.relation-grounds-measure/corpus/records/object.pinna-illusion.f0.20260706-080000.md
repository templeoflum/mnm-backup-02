**statement (plain)** — The Pinna Illusion generates a compelling perception of rotation in static concentric circular patterns composed of tilted micropatterns. Discovered by Italian vision scientist Baingio Pinna, the illusion occurs when small oriented elements such as line segments, squares, or chevrons are arranged along concentric rings with systematic tilt variations. Although the pattern contains no physical motion, observers perceive spontaneous rotation or counter-rotation of the rings, with inner and outer rings often appearing to spin in opposite directions.
