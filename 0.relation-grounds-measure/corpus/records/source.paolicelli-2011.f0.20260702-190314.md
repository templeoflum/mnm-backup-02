Rosa C. Paolicelli, Giulia Bolasco, Francesca Pagani, et al., "Synaptic Pruning by Microglia Is Necessary for Normal Brain Development," *Science* 333, no. 6048 (2011): 1456–1458.

Contains — direct evidence that **microglia** — the brain's immune cells — physically engulf and eliminate synapses during development, and that impairing them leaves an excess of immature, weak synapses. With subsequent work (Schafer, Stevens, and colleagues), establishes activity- and complement-guided pruning: less-active synapses are preferentially removed (complement C1q/C3 tag synapses for elimination; molecular "eat-me"/"don't-eat-me" signals). The cellular basis of developmental synaptic pruning.
