**statement (plain)** — **Xipe Totec** ("our lord the flayed one") is the Aztec/Nahua god of seedtime and renewal. At the festival **Tlacaxipehualiztli** his priests wore the flayed skins of sacrificial victims for twenty days, until the skins rotted away. The image is that of the seed shedding its husk and the earth its old skin for new growth.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: spring, renewal, agriculture, sacrifice, the flayed skin. Nahuatl *Xipe Totec*, "our lord the flayed one." Honored at **Tlacaxipehualiztli**, the "flaying of men" festival, where the worn skins figured the earth's renewal. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested in the cited sources; documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). The Aztec sources are chiefly the post-conquest colonial records (Sahagún's *Florentine Codex*); details of the rite and its readings vary across the accounts and should be held loosely. **[confirmed as attribution]**
