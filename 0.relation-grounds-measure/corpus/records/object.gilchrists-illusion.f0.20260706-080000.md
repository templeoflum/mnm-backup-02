**statement (plain)** — Gilchrist's Illusion is a lightness phenomenon that provides strong empirical support for anchoring theory, a framework developed by Alan Gilchrist to explain how the visual system computes surface lightness. The illusion demonstrates that physically identical surfaces can appear dramatically different in lightness depending on which spatial framework or grouping they are perceived to belong to. In typical demonstrations, a gray target surface is presented in a configuration where figural organization cues suggest that it belongs to one of two different frameworks, such as separate illumination fields or depth planes.
