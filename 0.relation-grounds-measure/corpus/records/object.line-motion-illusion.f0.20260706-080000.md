**statement (plain)** — The Line Motion Illusion, first systematically described by Odmar Neumann and Werner Klotz in 1994, occurs when a horizontal or vertical line is presented simultaneously along its entire length, but appears to grow or extend progressively from one end to the other. The key manipulation involves presenting a brief visual cue near one end of the line immediately before the line appears. When observers view this sequence, they report that the line seems to be drawn or painted from the cued end toward the opposite end, despite the fact that all segments of the line are physically present simultaneously.
