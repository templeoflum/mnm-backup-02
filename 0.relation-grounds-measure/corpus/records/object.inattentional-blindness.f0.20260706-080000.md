**statement (plain)** — Inattentional blindness describes the failure to perceive clearly visible and otherwise salient stimuli when attention is engaged by another task or object, even when the unattended stimulus appears at fixation. Systematically documented by Arien Mack and Irvin Rock in 1998, though anticipated by earlier work, the phenomenon was dramatically popularized by Simons and Chabris's "invisible gorilla" demonstration in which observers counting basketball passes failed to notice a person in a gorilla suit walking through the scene. Unlike change blindness, which requires a temporal disruption, inattentional blindness occurs for fully visible, persistent stimuli and reveals that attention is necessary not just for change detection but for conscious perception itself.
