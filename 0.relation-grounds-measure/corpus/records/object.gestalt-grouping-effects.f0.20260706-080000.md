**statement (plain)** — Gestalt grouping effects refer to a set of perceptual organization principles first systematically described by Gestalt psychologists in the early 20th century that determine how visual elements are grouped into coherent objects and patterns. The primary grouping principles include proximity (elements close together are grouped), similarity (elements sharing features are grouped), closure (incomplete contours are perceptually completed), continuity (elements arranged along smooth paths are grouped), common fate (elements moving together are grouped), and symmetry (symmetric elements are preferentially grouped). These principles operate automatically and pre-attentively to segment visual scenes into figures and backgrounds, enabling rapid object recognition despite ambiguous or incomplete sensory input.
