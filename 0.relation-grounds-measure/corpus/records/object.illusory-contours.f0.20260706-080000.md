**statement (plain)** — Illusory contours, also known as subjective contours, are perceived edges and boundaries that appear in regions of an image where no physical luminance gradient or other local image feature directly signals the presence of an edge. These contours arise from the spatial arrangement of inducing elements such as line terminators, angle vertices, or phase-shifted gratings, which suggest the presence of an occluding surface even though no surface is physically present in the stimulus. Observers report clear perception of defined edges with specific orientation and position, and these illusory boundaries can support figure-ground segregation, depth ordering, and other perceptual judgments in much the same way as physically specified contours.
