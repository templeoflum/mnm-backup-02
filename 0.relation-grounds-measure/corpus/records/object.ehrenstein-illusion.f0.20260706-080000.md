**statement (plain)** — The Ehrenstein Illusion, discovered by German psychologist Walter Ehrenstein in 1941, is a multifaceted phenomenon combining illusory contour formation, brightness enhancement, and size distortion. The classic configuration consists of radial black lines arranged like spokes around a central region, with the lines terminating before reaching a common center point. Observers perceive an illusory bright circle or disk at the center where no physical luminance difference exists, demonstrating spontaneous surface completion by the visual system.

**statement (framework)** — category: geometric size length; perceptual mechanism(s): surface-completion, contour-integration, lateral-inhibition.
