**statement (plain)** — **Babalú-Ayé** (the Yoruba **Shopona** / **Sopona**; **Babalú-Ayé** in Santería) is the **Orisha** of disease and healing in Yoruba tradition. He is lord of epidemics and smallpox, held to both send sickness and lift it, and is patron of the suffering and the outcast. He is often depicted covered in sores or hidden beneath raffia and walking with a crutch, and is approached with great respect and euphemism.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: disease, healing, epidemics, smallpox, earth, suffering, outcasts. Sender and healer of pestilence; patron of the sick and the outcast; shown covered in sores or veiled in raffia, leaning on a crutch; addressed with reverence and by euphemism to avoid provoking him. Diaspora name: **Babalú-Ayé** (Santería). **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — documented in Idowu (1962) and Karade (1994); this is documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). It is an oral tradition carried in the **Ifá** corpus (the *odu*) and recorded by scholars; his names, form, and the taboos surrounding him vary between Yorubaland (where he is **Shopona** / **Sopona**) and the diaspora traditions of Santería. **[confirmed as attribution]**
