**statement (plain)** — The Reversing Rotation Illusion refers to a class of ambiguous motion displays where a continuously rotating object spontaneously appears to reverse its direction of rotation despite unchanging physical motion. This phenomenon typically occurs with depth-ambiguous stimuli such as rotating cylinders, spheres with surface markings, or kinetic depth displays where the three-dimensional structure must be inferred from two-dimensional motion patterns. The illusion arises because the projected two-dimensional motion on the retina is compatible with multiple three-dimensional rotation directions—for example, a cylinder rotating clockwise when viewed from one side appears identical to the same cylinder rotating counterclockwise when viewed from the opposite side.
