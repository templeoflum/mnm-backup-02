**statement (plain)** — **Hephaestus** (Greek *Ἥφαιστος*) is the Olympian god of fire, the forge, and craftsmanship — the divine smith and artisan. He is the son of **Hera**: in one tradition she bears him alone, without a father (parthenogenically), in answer to Zeus's solitary birth of Athena; in another he is the son of Zeus and Hera. He is lame, and myth accounts for the fall that cripples him — cast from Olympus, in one version by Hera for his deformity, in another by Zeus for taking Hera's part in a quarrel (*Iliad* 1.590–594). Master of metal, he forges the arms and wonders of the gods — most famously the **shield of Achilles** (*Iliad* 18). He is the husband of **Aphrodite**, whose adultery with **Ares** he exposes by trapping the pair in an invisible net.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: fire, the forge, metalworking, and craftsmanship. Key attributes: the hammer, anvil, and tongs; his automaton assistants and the golden handmaidens he fashions; his workshop and its bellows. Parentage: son of Hera — the parthenogenic birth and the birth by Zeus stand as variant traditions. Two distinct accounts explain his lameness and fall from Olympus. The Roman *interpretatio* identified him with **Vulcan**. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and Homer's epics [[source.homer]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The Vulcan *interpretatio* is well established; the parentage (parthenogenic vs. by Zeus) and the cause of his fall vary across sources and should be read as attested tradition, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
