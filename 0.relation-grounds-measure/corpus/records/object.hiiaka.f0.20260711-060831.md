**statement (plain)** — In Hawaiian tradition **Hi'iaka** (**Hi'iakaikapoliopele**, "Hi'iaka in the bosom of Pele") is the youngest and most beloved sister of **Pele**, said to have been born as an egg that Pele carried in her bosom on the voyage to Hawai'i. She is patron of the hula dancers, of healing, and of chant. Her great epic is the perilous journey she undertakes at Pele's command to fetch Pele's lover **Lohi'au**, passing through many trials of sorcery and healing along the way.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: hula, healing, sorcery, chant, loyalty. Youngest sister of **Pele**; sent to fetch **Lohi'au**. Aliases: **Hi'iakaikapoliopele** (Hawaiian). Hawaiian tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested in Beckwith, *Hawaiian Mythology* (1940) [[source.beckwith-1940]]; documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). These myths come through Hawaiian oral tradition collected and recorded in the 19th–20th c.; the episodes of the Lohi'au journey vary across the islands and among tellers. **[confirmed as attribution]**
