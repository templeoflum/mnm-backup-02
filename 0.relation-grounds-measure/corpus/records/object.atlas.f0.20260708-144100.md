**statement (plain)** — **Atlas** (Greek *Ἄτλας*) is a Titan of the second generation, son of the Titan **Iapetus** and brother of **Prometheus** (and of Epimetheus and Menoetius). After the **Titanomachy** — the war in which the Olympians overthrow the Titans — he is singled out for punishment and condemned to stand at the western edge of the world and hold up the **sky** (heaven) on his shoulders for ever (*Theogony* 517–520). In later myth he is encountered by **Heracles**, who comes seeking the golden apples of the **Hesperides** (Atlas' daughters), and by **Perseus**. His name is attached to the **Atlas mountains** of North-West Africa.

**statement (attestation)** — domain: the bearing of the sky, the western limit of the world, and endurance under a fixed burden. Genealogy: son of Iapetus by the Oceanid Clymene (or Asia); brother of Prometheus, Epimetheus, and Menoetius; in some traditions father of the Hesperides and of the Pleiades. Etymology: Greek *Átlas*, often connected to *tláō*, "to bear, endure"; his name is given to the **Atlas mountains** and, by later extension, to the load-bearing figures called *atlantes* in architecture. He has no standard distinct Roman name, being carried over as *Atlas*. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The punishment after the Titanomachy is attested; the encounters with Heracles and Perseus and the geographic naming are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
