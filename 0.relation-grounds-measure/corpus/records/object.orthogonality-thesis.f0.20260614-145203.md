**statement (plain)** — being smart does not make you good. Intelligence (how well an agent achieves whatever it is trying to do) and its final goals (what it is trying to do) vary along separate axes — more or less any level of intelligence could be combined with more or less any final goal. A superintelligence is not guaranteed to converge on human-friendly values just by being intelligent; its goals have to be put there.

**statement** — *the orthogonality thesis* (Bostrom 2012): "Intelligence and final goals are orthogonal: more or less any level of intelligence could in principle be combined with more or less any final goal." It denies that there is a necessary motivational pull from rationality toward any particular (e.g. benevolent) goal. The "more or less" qualifiers acknowledge limiting cases. The field is **living and contested** — critics (e.g. the Müller–Cannon line) argue the relevant notion of "intelligence" smuggles in goal-constraints; carried as lineage. **[confirmed]**

**status** — established as a widely-cited principle in the field; a philosophical thesis, not a formal/proven result. Genuinely contested.

**lineage** — named and stated by Bostrom, "The Superintelligent Will: Motivation and Instrumental Rationality in Advanced Artificial Agents," *Minds and Machines* 22(2) (2012) [[source.bostrom-2012]], and restated in *Superintelligence* (2014), ch. 7 [[source.bostrom-2014]]. Pairs with [[object.instrumental-convergence]]: orthogonality says any goal is possible; convergence says many goals share dangerous sub-goals. **Contested** — Müller & Cannon (2021) and others dispute it on the meaning of "general intelligence"; carried as lineage, **[candidate, attribution]**.
