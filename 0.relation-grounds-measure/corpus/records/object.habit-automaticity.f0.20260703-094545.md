**statement (plain)** — A habit is not merely a frequent behavior; it is one that has become *automatic*. After enough repetition in a consistent setting, the setting itself (time, place, preceding action, emotional state) cues the behavior directly, so it launches without a deliberate decision and demands little attention. This is why habits are efficient (they free conscious capacity) and why they are hard to break by intention alone (the cue fires before the will engages).

**characterization** — automaticity has several features: the behavior is initiated by situational cues rather than goals, unfolds with reduced awareness and control, is efficient (low cognitive load), and is difficult to inhibit once cued [[source.wood-neal-2007]] **[confirmed as attribution]**. James located the mechanism in the "plasticity" of neural tissue that repetition wears into channels [[source.james-1890]] **[confirmed]**. Because control has been ceded to the cue, changing a habit means changing the cue/context, not trying harder — the link to [[object.context-dependent-repetition]] and to environmental design.
