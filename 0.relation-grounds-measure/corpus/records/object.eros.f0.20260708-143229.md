**statement (plain)** — **Eros** (Greek *Ἔρως*, "desire") is the god of love and sexual desire, and the tradition holds him in two distinct forms. In **Hesiod** he is a **primordial** power, one of the first beings to come into being — with **Chaos** and **Gaia** — "fairest among the deathless gods," who loosens the limbs and overmasters the mind of gods and men alike; here he is the cosmogonic force that draws things together and makes generation possible (*Theogony* 116–122). In **later tradition** — lyric, then Hellenistic poetry — he is instead the youthful son of **Aphrodite**, a winged boy armed with **bow and arrows** whose shafts kindle desire in whomever he strikes. These two traditions are not reconciled: the primordial Eros and the son of Aphrodite stand side by side in the sources.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: love, sexual desire, and the generative force that joins. Roles: in Hesiod, a primordial cosmogonic power coeval with Chaos and Gaia; in later tradition, the mischievous son and agent of Aphrodite. Key attributes (later form): wings, the **bow and arrows**, sometimes a torch. Etymology: Greek *Érōs*, "desire," the common noun and the god being one; the Roman *interpretatio* identified him with **Cupid** (*Cupido*) / **Amor**. The two genealogies — primordial versus son of Aphrodite — are a genuine divergence in the tradition, not an error to be resolved. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and in later lyric and Hellenistic tradition [[source.homer]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The primordial Eros and the son-of-Aphrodite Eros are both attested and mutually divergent; the Cupid/Amor *interpretatio* is established, and the genealogies are attested tradition, variable across sources, to be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
