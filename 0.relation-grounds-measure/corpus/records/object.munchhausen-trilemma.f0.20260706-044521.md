**statement (plain)** — To justify a belief you cite a reason — which itself needs justifying, and so on. There are only three ways the chain can go: (1) it regresses forever (never completed), (2) it circles (A justified by B … justified by A — viciously circular), or (3) it stops at a brute, unjustified assertion (dogmatism). Each looks fatal.

**statement (framework)** — the **regress problem** (Agrippa's trilemma), formalized by Albert as the Münchhausen trilemma (after the baron who pulled himself from a swamp by his own hair). If the three exhaust the options and all fail, justified belief — and JTB knowledge — is impossible. Responses reject one horn: foundationalism (defensible stopping points), coherentism (virtuous circles), infinitism. Established; **open**.
