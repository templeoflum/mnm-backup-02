William G. Chase & Herbert A. Simon, "Perception in chess," *Cognitive Psychology* 4, no. 1 (1973): 55–81.

Contains — building on de Groot, the demonstration that chess masters reconstruct real (but not random) board positions far better than novices because they perceive the board in larger, meaningful **chunks** — configurations grasped as units — rather than having larger raw memory. The pauses between glances reveal chunk boundaries. The template result for all expert perception: experience restructures what is literally perceived. (Simon & Gilmartin 1973 estimated 10,000–100,000 chunks for mastery; ~50,000 commonly cited, Gobet & Simon 1998.)
