**statement (plain)** — a language whose words refer to what only the speaker can know — his immediate private sensations — is impossible: no criterion of correctness can be established for a private ostensive definition. The diarist who writes "S" for a recurring sensation (§258) fails, because "whatever is going to seem correct to me is correct. And that only means that here we can't talk about 'correct'." The beetle-in-a-box (§293): if everyone has a private "beetle" no one else can see, "the thing in the box doesn't belong to the language-game at all… it cancels out" — public sensation-words do not get their meaning from a private inner object.

**basis** — Wittgenstein, *PI* (1953) §§243, 258, 265, 293. The current SEP "Private Language" entry does not expound the beetle directly; the §293 text is from *PI* itself, corroborated across expository sources. [[source.wittgenstein-1953]] **[confirmed]**
