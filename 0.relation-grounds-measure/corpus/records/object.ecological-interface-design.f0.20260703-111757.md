**statement (plain)** — Ecological interface design (EID) builds the display around the deep structure of the work domain — its **abstraction hierarchy**, from physical components up through functions to overall purpose — rather than around individual sensors. By making the process's real constraints and relationships directly visible, the operator can *see* why the system is in the state it is in and reason about situations the designer never anticipated, instead of mentally reconstructing the whole plant from scattered readings.

**relevance** — the design answer to the knowledge-based end of [[object.skill-rule-knowledge-framework]]: support skill- and rule-based work with directly perceptible cues, and support knowledge-based problem-solving by exposing domain constraints. The principled alternative to single-sensor-single-indicator control panels, and the design-theory counterpart to alarm management ([[object.alarm-management]]) [[source.vicente-rasmussen-1992]] **[confirmed as attribution]**.
