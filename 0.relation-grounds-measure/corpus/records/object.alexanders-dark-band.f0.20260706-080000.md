**statement (plain)** — Alexander's Dark Band is a distinctive dark region visible in the sky between the primary and secondary rainbows during rainbow displays. This atmospheric optical phenomenon appears as a noticeably darker band of sky, typically spanning approximately 8 degrees in angular width, positioned between the bright arc of the primary rainbow at approximately 42 degrees from the antisolar point and the secondary rainbow at approximately 51 degrees. The darkening effect results from the geometry of light ray paths through water droplets: light rays that would illuminate this region are instead refracted and reflected into the angles occupied by the primary and secondary bows, creating a relative deficit of scattered light in the intermediate zone.
