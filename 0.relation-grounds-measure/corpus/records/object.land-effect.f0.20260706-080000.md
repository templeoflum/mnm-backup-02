**statement (plain)** — The Land effect, discovered by Polaroid founder Edwin Land in 1959, demonstrates that observers perceive a surprisingly rich and differentiated range of colors when viewing images projected through only two wavelength bands rather than the full visible spectrum. In Land's original demonstrations, he superimposed two black-and-white photographs of the same scene taken through different color filters, then projected them using long-wavelength (red) and middle-wavelength (green or white) light. Despite the impoverished spectral information available, observers reported seeing blues, yellows, greens, and other hues that were not physically present in the projection.
