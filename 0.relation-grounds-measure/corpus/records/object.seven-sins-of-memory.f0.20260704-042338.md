**statement (plain)** — memory fails in seven characteristic ways: it fades (transience), slips when attention is elsewhere (absent-mindedness), and blocks on the tip of the tongue (blocking) — the sins of forgetting; it mis-sources, bends to suggestion, and skews to present belief (misattribution, suggestibility, bias) — the sins of distortion; and it intrudes with what we would forget (persistence). Schacter's claim: these are byproducts of features that mostly serve us well.

**basis** — Schacter (1999, *American Psychologist*; 2001 book). Rests on the reconstructive view ([[object.reconstructive-memory]]): the distortion sins are exactly what a schema-driven rebuild predicts. [[source.schacter-1999]] **[confirmed]**
