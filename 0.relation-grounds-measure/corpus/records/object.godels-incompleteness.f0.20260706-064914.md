**statement (plain)** — Gödel encodes statements about a formal system as numbers within the system, then constructs a sentence that in effect says 'I am not provable.' If the system is consistent, that sentence is true but unprovable (first theorem); and the system cannot prove its own consistency (second theorem).

**statement (framework)** — **proven** limits on formal systems: no consistent, sufficiently strong, recursively axiomatized theory is complete, and none proves its own consistency. Ended Hilbert's program for a complete, self-certifying foundation; the Gödel-numbering/self-reference technique is kin to Cantor's diagonal and Turing's halting. Proven.
