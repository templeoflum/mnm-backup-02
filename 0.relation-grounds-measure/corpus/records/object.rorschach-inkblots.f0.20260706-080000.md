**statement (plain)** — The Rorschach Inkblots are a set of symmetrical, ambiguous inkblot images developed by Swiss psychiatrist Hermann Rorschach in 1921, originally designed for personality assessment but representing a powerful demonstration of how observers impose meaningful interpretations on inherently ambiguous stimuli. Unlike other bistable illusions that alternate between a small number of specific percepts, Rorschach inkblots support virtually unlimited interpretations, with different observers perceiving different objects, scenes, or figures based on their individual cognitive styles, expectations, and past experiences. The bilateral symmetry of the inkblots provides organizational structure while leaving semantic content radically underdetermined, making them maximally projective stimuli.
