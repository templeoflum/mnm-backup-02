**statement (plain)** — The Chromatic Motion Illusion refers to illusory motion percepts that arise from purely chromatic modulation in the absence of luminance contrast, or conversely, the distinctive characteristics of motion perception when stimuli are defined solely by chromatic differences. At equiluminance (isoluminance), where stimuli differ only in chromaticity but not in brightness, the human visual system exhibits severely degraded motion sensitivity compared to luminance-defined motion. However, under certain conditions, purely chromatic stimuli can still produce compelling motion percepts, albeit with slower apparent speeds and coarser spatial resolution.
