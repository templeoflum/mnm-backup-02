**statement (plain)** — **Jörmungandr** (Old Norse *Jörmungandr*), the **Midgard Serpent** or World Serpent, is the son of **Loki** and the giantess **Angrboða** and brother of the wolf **Fenrir** and of **Hel**. Snorri tells in *Gylfaginning* that **Odin** cast him into the deep sea that surrounds all lands, where he grew so vast that he lies encircling the whole world and bites his own tail. He is the great adversary of **Thor**: in *Hymiskviða* Thor goes fishing with the giant Hymir and hooks the serpent, hauling him up until the terrified giant cuts the line. At **Ragnarök** the serpent comes ashore in fury, and he and Thor kill one another — Thor slays the serpent but staggers back nine paces and falls dead from its venom.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: the encircling sea-serpent, the world-boundary, the doom-adversary of Thor. Key relations: son of **Loki** and **Angrboða**; sibling of **Fenrir** and **Hel**; cast into the sea by **Odin**; the foe **Thor** hooks in the fishing episode and dies against at the end. The fishing episode is told in the Poetic Edda in *Hymiskviða* and the mutual slaying at Ragnarök in *Völuspá*; Snorri's *Gylfaginning* supplies Odin's casting him into the sea and the image of the serpent encircling the world with its tail in its mouth. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Norse mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested across the Poetic Edda [[source.poetic-edda]] and systematized in Snorri's Prose Edda [[source.prose-edda]]; the standard reference treatment is Lindow [[source.lindow-2001]]. Odin's casting him into the sea and the encircling of the world come chiefly from Snorri's *Gylfaginning* and should be read as attested tradition through his 13th-c. Christian framing, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
