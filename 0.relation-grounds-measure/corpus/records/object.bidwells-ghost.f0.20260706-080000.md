**statement (plain)** — Bidwell's Ghost is a chromatic afterimage phenomenon in which a colored ghost image appears when a moving visual stimulus is followed by a bright achromatic flash. The classic demonstration involves a dark disk rotating against a white background, with a sector removed to allow a brief flash of light to reach the retina. When timed appropriately, observers perceive a vivid colored afterimage (the "ghost") in the region previously occupied by the moving disk, despite the disk itself being achromatic.

**statement (framework)** — category: color; perceptual mechanism(s): photoreceptor-adaptation, neural-adaptation, retinal-persistence.
