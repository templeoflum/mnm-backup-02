**origin** — the Eleusinian Mysteries and kindred ancient cults; the secret content reconstructed (via Clement of Alexandria and others) as a tripartite structure — *dromena* (things done/enacted), *legomena* (things said), *deiknymena* (things shown) — with no ancient source describing the exact content: the incommunicable, by design. [[source.mylonas-1961]] **[confirmed on attribution]**

**codification** — Walter Burkert, *Ancient Mystery Cults* (Harvard, 1987) — the standard scholarly treatment; initiation as a personal, optional, secret transformation distinct from civic religion. [[source.burkert-1987]] **[confirmed]**

**door** — Burkert, *Ancient Mystery Cults* (1987); Mylonas, *Eleusis and the Eleusinian Mysteries* (1961).

**lineage** — the incommunicable, worked out: the *arrheton*/*aporrheton* philology (the unspeakable vs. the forbidden; Bielawski 2016) [[object.arrheton-aporrheton]]; the *pathein*/*mathein* debate — initiation induced an experience, not doctrine (Aristotle fr. 15 Rose *ap.* Synesius) [[object.pathein-not-mathein]]; and the *myesis*/*telete*/*epopteia* grade-scheme, its sequence genuinely debated (Simms) [[object.myesis-telete-epopteia]]. The attested benefit is a better hope for the afterlife (*Homeric Hymn to Demeter* 480–482). The ergot/psychedelic-*kykeon* hypothesis (Wasson–Hofmann–Ruck 1978; Muraresku 2020) is carried as contested/unproven; Burkert's non-psychedelic reading is the scholarly default.
