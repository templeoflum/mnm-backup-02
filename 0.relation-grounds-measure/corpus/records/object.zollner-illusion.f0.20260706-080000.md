**statement (plain)** — The Zöllner Illusion, first described by German astrophysicist Johann Karl Friedrich Zöllner in 1860, is a classic geometric illusion in which parallel lines appear to converge or diverge when crossed by short oblique lines (hatching). The illusion was discovered accidentally when Zöllner noticed the effect on a fabric pattern and subsequently communicated it to physicist Johann Christian Poggendorff, whose own eponymous illusion arose from the same correspondence. In the standard configuration, long parallel lines are overlaid with arrays of short diagonal lines at alternating angles, typically 20–45 degrees from perpendicular.
