**statement (plain)** — You have first-person access to your own pains and thoughts, but never to another's. All you observe of others is behaviour — words, gestures, brain scans — and every such datum is logically compatible with there being no inner experience at all. The gap between behaviour and subjective experience is conceptual, not empirical, so no technology can close it.

**statement (framework)** — an **epistemological** problem, not practical doubt: it concerns the structure of the evidence for attributing minds. The classic response is Mill's **argument from analogy** (others resemble me in body and behaviour, so likely in mind); Wittgenstein's private-language argument and beetle-in-the-box recast the whole question. Bears directly on animal, patient, and machine consciousness. Established; unresolved (**open**).

**basis** — the argument from analogy (Mill, Ayer) and Wittgenstein's *Philosophical Investigations* (1953) [[source.wittgenstein-1953]]. **[confirmed as attribution]**
