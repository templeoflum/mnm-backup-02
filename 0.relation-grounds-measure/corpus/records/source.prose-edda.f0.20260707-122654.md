The **Prose Edda** (Younger Edda, Snorra Edda) — written c. 1220 by the Icelandic chieftain and historian **Snorri Sturluson** (1179–1241) as a handbook for poets. Its *Gylfaginning* ("the beguiling of Gylfi") gives the connected narrative of the mythology from creation to Ragnarök; *Skáldskaparmál* catalogues the kennings and the myths behind them; the *Prologue* frames the gods euhemeristically as deified men (Snorri writing as a Christian). The single richest systematic source, though later and more shaped than the Poetic Edda. Public-domain trans. Brodeur (1916) on archive.org; standard modern trans. Anthony Faulkes. **[confirmed as attribution]**
