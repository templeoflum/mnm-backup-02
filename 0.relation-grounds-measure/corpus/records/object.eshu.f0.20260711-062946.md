**statement (plain)** — **Eshu** (**Elegba** / **Eleggua** / **Exu**) is the divine messenger and trickster **Orisha**, guardian of the crossroads and doorways, who carries words between humans and the Orisha. He must be saluted first in any rite so that offerings reach their mark, and he holds the **ashé** of communication. As a trickster he exposes folly and teaches through the reversals he sets in a person's path.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: crossroads, communication, trickery, fate, doorways, language. Guardian of thresholds and master of chance and of language; saluted first in ritual so that sacrifice reaches the Orisha; a moral teacher who exposes pride and folly. Diaspora names: **Elegba / Eleggua** (Santería), **Exu** (Candomblé). IMPORTANT: Eshu is NOT the Christian devil — that equation is a colonial and missionary misidentification, and is recorded here only to be rejected, not adopted. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — documented in Idowu (1962), Bascom (1969), and Karade (1994); this is documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). It is an oral tradition carried in the **Ifá** corpus (the *odu*) and recorded by scholars; his names and his role in ritual vary between Yorubaland and the diaspora traditions of Santería and Candomblé, and the devil-equation is a distortion introduced from outside the tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**
