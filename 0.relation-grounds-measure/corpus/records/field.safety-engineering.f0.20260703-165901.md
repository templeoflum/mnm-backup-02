**origin** — the fail-safe / safe-by-design tradition: system-safety engineering (aerospace & nuclear, MIL-STD-882) and, for the protection branch, Jerome Saltzer & Michael Schroeder's design principles ("The Protection of Information in Computer Systems," 1975) — fail-safe defaults, separation of privilege, least privilege. [[source.saltzer-schroeder-1975]] **[confirmed]**

**codification** — James Reason, *Human Error* (Cambridge, 1990) — the **Swiss-cheese model** of accident causation (layered defenses, latent vs. active failures) and the human-error taxonomy that organizes defense-in-depth thinking. [[source.reason-1990]] **[confirmed]**

**door** — Nancy Leveson, *Engineering a Safer World: Systems Thinking Applied to Safety* (MIT Press) — the modern systems-theoretic treatment of accident causation and safe design. [[source.leveson-2009]] **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — split out from the former `high-reliability-orgs-safety-engineering` field (20260703 field census — a mashup of two disciplines). This is the *technical / design* half: how to build systems that fail safe. Its sibling [[field.high-reliability-organizations]] is the *organizational* half (how organizations operating hazardous technology stay safe); [[field.human-factors]] is the *cognitive* half. The three share the high-hazard-systems concern from different angles.
