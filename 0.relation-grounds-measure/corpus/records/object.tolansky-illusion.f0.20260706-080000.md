**statement (plain)** — The Tolansky illusion, described by physicist Samuel Tolansky in 1964, is a geometric illusion involving systematic misperception of line alignment, orientation, or length in specific spatial configurations. Tolansky, known primarily for his work in optical physics and interferometry, contributed several observations on visual perception anomalies, particularly those involving periodic patterns, alignment judgments, and interactions between line elements. While less widely known than canonical illusions such as the Müller-Lyer or Poggendorff, the Tolansky illusion exemplifies how subtle variations in spatial arrangement can induce robust perceptual distortions.
