**statement (plain)** — **Rhea** (Greek *Ῥέα*) is a **Titaness**, daughter of **Ouranos** and **Gaia**, and the sister and wife of **Kronos**. By him she bears the first generation of Olympians — **Hestia**, **Demeter**, **Hera**, **Hades**, **Poseidon**, and **Zeus** — each of whom Kronos swallows at birth to forestall the prophecy that a child will overthrow him. Grieving, Rhea appeals to Gaia and Ouranos and, when Zeus is due, is sent to Crete, where she bears him in secret and gives Kronos a stone wrapped in swaddling clothes to swallow in the infant's place (*Theogony* 468–491). Through this deception the child survives to grow, disgorge his siblings, and overthrow his father.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: motherhood and the continuity of the divine line across the succession. Roles: Titaness, mother of the first Olympian generation, and the agent of Zeus's rescue — the deception of the swaddled stone. In later tradition Rhea is assimilated to the Phrygian mother-goddess **Cybele** (the *Magna Mater*), and the Roman *interpretatio* identified her with **Ops**; these identifications are cultic overlays, not part of the Hesiodic core. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and Homer's epics [[source.homer]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The swaddled-stone rescue is attested; the Cybele assimilation and the Ops *interpretatio* are later cultic identifications, and the genealogies are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
