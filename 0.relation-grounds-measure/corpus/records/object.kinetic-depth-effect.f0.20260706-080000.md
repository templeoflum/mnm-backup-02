**statement (plain)** — The Kinetic Depth Effect, first systematically described by Hans Wallach and D.N. O'Connell in 1953, refers to the perception of three-dimensional structure from two-dimensional motion patterns. When observers view the shadow or silhouette projection of a rotating three-dimensional object, they spontaneously perceive the object as having volumetric depth and three-dimensional form, even though the stimulus itself is a flat pattern on a two-dimensional surface.

**statement (framework)** — category: motion movement; perceptual mechanism(s): motion-integration, bayesian-inference, structure-from-motion.
