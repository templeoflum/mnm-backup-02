**statement (plain)** — Floor illusions are painted or printed patterns applied to horizontal surfaces that create compelling perceptual experiences of three-dimensional depth, including apparent holes, chasms, raised platforms, or distorted surfaces, despite being physically flat. These artistic installations exploit the visual system's depth perception mechanisms by manipulating pictorial depth cues such as linear perspective, texture gradients, shading, and occlusion to generate interpretations that conflict with the actual planar geometry of the surface. The illusions are particularly effective because floors are typically viewed from oblique angles where perspective foreshortening naturally occurs, allowing artists to create images that match the expected retinal projections of three-dimensional structures.
