**statement (plain)** — The Hermann Grid Illusion, first described by Ludimar Hermann in 1870, is one of the classic demonstrations of lateral inhibition in the visual system. The illusion consists of a regular grid of black squares separated by white corridors. When viewing this configuration, observers perceive dark or ghostly gray spots at the intersections of the white corridors, despite these intersection regions being physically identical in luminance to the corridor segments.

**statement (framework)** — category: brightness lightness contrast; perceptual mechanism(s): lateral-inhibition, spatial-filtering, orientation-selectivity.
