**statement (plain)** — The incubation effect is the empirical finding that setting a problem aside for a period — rather than working on it continuously — can raise the rate of subsequent solution. It is the testable core of Wallas's "incubation" stage.

**basis** — Sio & Ormerod's meta-analysis of incubation studies reports a positive overall incubation effect, with **divergent-thinking tasks benefiting more** than linguistic or visual insight tasks; effects are larger when the preparation period is longer and when the incubation interval is filled with a **low-cognitive-demand** activity (consistent with attention-withdrawal / unconscious-processing accounts) [[source.sio-ormerod-2009]] [confirmed]. The construct originates with Wallas's stage model [[source.wallas-1926]] [confirmed].

**limits** — Effect sizes are modest and heterogeneous across task type, incubation activity, and study design; the underlying mechanism (unconscious work vs. forgetting of misleading fixation vs. fresh-look effects) is not resolved by the aggregate effect [[source.sio-ormerod-2009]] [confirmed].
