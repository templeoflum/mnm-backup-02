**statement (plain)** — The Pinna-Brelstaff Illusion, discovered by Baingio Pinna and G.J. Brelstaff in 2000, produces a striking perception of circular rotation in a completely static pattern when observers move toward or away from the display. The stimulus consists of concentric rings of small oriented elements, typically tilted line segments or chevrons, arranged with systematic directional biases.

**statement (framework)** — category: motion movement; perceptual mechanism(s): motion-energy-detection, orientation-selectivity, motion-integration.
