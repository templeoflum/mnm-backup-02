**statement (plain)** — In Hawaiian tradition **Lono** (**Lono-i-ka-makahiki**) is the god of peace, agriculture, and rainfall, one of the four great gods (with **Kāne**, **Kū**, and **Kanaloa**). He is the god of the rains that bring the harvest. During the annual **Makahiki** season (roughly October to February) war was set aside, tribute was gathered, and games and feasting honored Lono.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: peace, agriculture, rainfall, sport, the Makahiki festival. One of the four great gods (**Kāne**, **Kū**, **Kanaloa**); honored in the **Makahiki**. Aliases: **Lono-i-ka-makahiki** (Hawaiian). Cognate with the Māori **Rongo** (recorded separately). Hawaiian tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested in Beckwith, *Hawaiian Mythology* (1940) [[source.beckwith-1940]]; documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). These myths come through Hawaiian oral tradition collected and recorded in the 19th–20th c.; the observances of the Makahiki and the forms of Lono vary across the islands. NOTE the cognate: **Lono ≈ the Māori Rongo** (recorded separately). **[confirmed as attribution]**
