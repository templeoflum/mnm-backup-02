**statement (plain)** — the methodological critique of the Eliadean "sui generis religion" claim: that religion is "distinct, unique, and self-caused," an irreducible datum (the sacred, religious experience, hierophany). McCutcheon argues this constitutes the object as "ahistoric, apolitical, fetishized," serving institutional interests ("skyhooks," the "politics of nostalgia"); J. Z. Smith stresses constructed/local place; the charge across these critics is that scholars in the Eliadean mode become "caretakers" and "celebrators," defending the believer's self-understanding rather than explaining it.

**basis** — McCutcheon, *Manufacturing Religion* (1997); J. Z. Smith, *To Take Place* (1987) & *Map Is Not Territory* (1978); Wasserstrom, *Religion after Religion* (1999). April DeConick's parallel charge that Gnosticism scholarship essentializes an ahistorical *sui generis* Gnosis cross-links this to [[field.western-esotericism]].
