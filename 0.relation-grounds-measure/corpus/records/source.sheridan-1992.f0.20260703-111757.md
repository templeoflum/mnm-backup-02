Thomas B. Sheridan, *Telerobotics, Automation, and Human Supervisory Control* (MIT Press, 1992).

Contains — the systematic account of **human supervisory control**: a human operator intermittently programs and monitors an automated system that closes the inner control loops, intervening to set goals, handle exceptions, and take over when needed. Develops the levels-of-automation continuum (from fully manual to fully autonomous), the roles of the human as planner/teacher/monitor/intervenor, and the mental-model and trust issues of supervising machines. The canonical framing for control-room and dispatch work.
