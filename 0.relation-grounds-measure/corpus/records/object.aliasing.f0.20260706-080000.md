**statement (plain)** — Aliasing refers to a class of visual artifacts that arise when continuous spatial or temporal signals are undersampled, violating the Nyquist-Shannon sampling theorem. In visual contexts, aliasing manifests as moiré patterns, staircase edges (jaggies), wagon-wheel effects, and other spurious structures that were not present in the original scene. These artifacts occur when the spatial frequency content of an image exceeds half the sampling rate of the display or imaging system, causing high-frequency components to be misrepresented as lower frequencies.
