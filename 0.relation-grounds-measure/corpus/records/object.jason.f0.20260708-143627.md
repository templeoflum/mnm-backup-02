**statement (plain)** — **Jason** (Greek *Ἰάσων*) is the leader of the **Argonauts**: son of Aeson, the rightful king of Iolcus whose throne has been usurped by **Pelias**. To be rid of him, Pelias sends Jason to fetch the **Golden Fleece** from **Colchis** at the far edge of the Black Sea. Jason gathers a company of heroes, builds the ship **Argo**, and after many trials reaches Colchis. There King Aeëtes sets him lethal tasks — yoking fire-breathing bulls, sowing dragon's teeth from which armed men spring, and taking the Fleece guarded by a sleepless dragon. The king's daughter **Medea**, a sorceress, falls in love with Jason and, through her drugs and craft, enables him to accomplish the tasks and carry off the Fleece. He marries her; but years later, at Corinth, he abandons her to marry Glauce, and Medea takes her terrible revenge.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: heroic quest, seafaring, leadership. Parentage: Aeson of Iolcus. Key elements: the ship **Argo** and the company of **Argonauts**, the **Golden Fleece**, the tasks set by Aeëtes, and the decisive aid of **Medea**. The single most complete connected narrative is **Apollonius of Rhodes' *Argonautica*** (Hellenistic epic, not among the four cited sources — see report); the betrayal and Medea's revenge are dramatized fullest in **Euripides' *Medea*** (likewise not cited). Earlier attestation runs from Homer and Pindar; Apollodorus' Bibliotheca summarizes the whole. **[confirmed as attribution]**
