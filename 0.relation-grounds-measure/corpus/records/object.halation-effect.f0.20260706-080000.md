**statement (plain)** — The halation effect refers to the perceptual phenomenon in which bright or highly luminant regions of a visual scene appear to extend beyond their physical boundaries, causing their color and luminance to bleed or spread into adjacent darker areas. This spreading creates a halo-like appearance around bright objects, reducing perceived contrast and sharpness at luminance boundaries. The term originates from photography, where light scattering within film emulsions or digital sensors produces similar artifacts, though the perceptual halation effect occurs through distinct mechanisms in human vision.

**statement (framework)** — category: color; perceptual mechanism(s): lateral-inhibition, photoreceptor-adaptation.
