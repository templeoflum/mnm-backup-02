Katherine A. MacLean, Emilio Ferrer, Stephen R. Aichele, David A. Bridwell, Anthony P. Zanesco, Tonya L. Jacobs, Brandon G. King, Erika L. Rosenberg, Baljinder K. Sahdra, Phillip R. Shaver, B. Alan Wallace, George R. Mangun & Clifford D. Saron, "Intensive meditation training improves perceptual discrimination and sustained attention," *Psychological Science* 21, no. 6 (2010): 829–839.

Contains — a landmark longitudinal result from the Shamatha Project: three months of intensive shamatha (focused-attention) training (~5 hr/day) improved **perceptual discrimination** (detecting smaller differences in line length) and, through that improved sensitivity, better **sustained attention/vigilance** (reduced vigilance decrement). Gains persisted at follow-up for continued practitioners — evidence of experience-driven perceptual plasticity in adults.
