**statement (plain)** — Pareidolia is the perceptual tendency to perceive meaningful patterns, particularly faces and familiar objects, in random, ambiguous, or unstructured stimuli. This phenomenon represents a fundamental aspect of human perception in which the visual system actively constructs interpretations of ambiguous sensory input by matching it against stored representations and expectations. Pareidolia occurs across multiple sensory modalities, though it is most commonly studied and experienced in vision, where people perceive faces in clouds, religious figures in toast, animals in rock formations, and countless other meaningful patterns in noise.
