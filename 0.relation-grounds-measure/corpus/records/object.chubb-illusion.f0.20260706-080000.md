**statement (plain)** — The Chubb Illusion, discovered by Charles Chubb in 1989, demonstrates that the perceived contrast of a textured region is substantially reduced when that region is embedded within a high-contrast texture surround. In the classic demonstration, a Gaussian blob or circular patch of noise texture appears to have lower contrast when surrounded by high-contrast noise compared to when surrounded by low-contrast noise or a uniform field, even though the physical contrast of the central patch remains constant. This phenomenon reveals important properties of contrast processing in the visual system, suggesting that contrast perception is normalized relative to the local context rather than computed in absolute terms.
