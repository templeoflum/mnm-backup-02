**statement (plain)** — Cognition should be analyzed at the level of the functional system that accomplishes a task — a coordinated assembly of people, tools, representations, and the environment — rather than confined to a single brain. Cognitive work is the creation, transformation, and propagation of representational state across both internal and external media. [[source.hutchins-1995]] **[confirmed]**

**basis (method / support)** — Built from cognitive ethnography: Hutchins's detailed study of ship navigation showed how a fix is computed by a team coordinated with instruments and charts, distributing a task no single navigator performs alone [[source.hutchins-1995]]. Hollan, Hutchins & Kirsh (2000) generalized this into a research foundation for HCI, with the functional system and "propagation of representational state across media" as core analytic commitments [[source.hollan-hutchins-kirsh-2000]]. **[confirmed]** Limits: DCog is a descriptive/methodological framework, not a predictive theory; boundaries of a "functional system" are analyst-chosen, and critics question whether spreading the unit of analysis dilutes the notion of cognition.
