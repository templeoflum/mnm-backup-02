**statement (plain)** — In Hawaiian tradition **Kanaloa** is the god of the ocean and its creatures, the fourth of the four great gods (with **Kāne**, **Kū**, and **Lono**). He is often paired with **Kāne**, his companion in opening the water-springs across the land. Yet in some traditions he stands in dark contrast to Kāne — a god of the sea, the dead, and forbidden magic.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: ocean, ocean creatures, the underworld, healing, sorcery. Fourth of the four great gods (**Kāne**, **Kū**, **Lono**); companion to **Kāne** in opening water-springs. Cognate with the Māori **Tangaroa** (recorded separately). Hawaiian tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested in Beckwith, *Hawaiian Mythology* (1940) [[source.beckwith-1940]]; documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). These myths come through Hawaiian oral tradition collected and recorded in the 19th–20th c.; in some traditions Kanaloa's darker associations with the underworld and forbidden sorcery vary across the islands. NOTE the cognate: **Kanaloa ≈ the Māori Tangaroa** (recorded separately). **[confirmed as attribution]**
