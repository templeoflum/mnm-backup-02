**statement (plain)** — The crowding effect describes the impairment in identifying a peripheral target stimulus when it is flanked by nearby objects, even when the target itself is well above detection threshold. This phenomenon represents a fundamental bottleneck in peripheral object recognition that cannot be explained by simple acuity limitations. Crowding is characterized by a critical spacing that scales with eccentricity, typically requiring flankers to be separated by at least half the target's eccentricity for accurate identification.
