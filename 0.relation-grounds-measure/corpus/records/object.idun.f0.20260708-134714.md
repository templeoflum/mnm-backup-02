**statement (plain)** — **Iðunn** (Old Norse *Iðunn*) is the goddess who keeps the apples that the gods eat to stay young, and the wife of **Bragi**. Snorri tells in *Skáldskaparmál* how **Loki**, under compulsion by the giant **Þjazi**, lures her out of Asgard with her apples and delivers her to the giant, who bears her off. Deprived of the apples, the gods begin to age and grow grey; they force Loki to bring her back, and he flies in **Freyja**'s falcon-shape to Þjazi's hall and carries her home as a nut, the giant pursuing in eagle-form until he burns in the fire the gods kindle at the wall of Asgard.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: rejuvenation and the keeping of the apples of youth. Key relations: wife of **Bragi**; keeper of the apples; abducted by **Þjazi** through **Loki** and recovered. The narrative of the abduction and recovery is preserved by Snorri in *Skáldskaparmál*; her name appears in the Poetic Edda in *Lokasenna*, where Loki taunts her. The apples themselves are the emblem attached to her throughout the tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Norse mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested across the Poetic Edda [[source.poetic-edda]] and systematized in Snorri's Prose Edda [[source.prose-edda]]; the standard reference treatment is Lindow [[source.lindow-2001]]. The apple-abduction narrative comes chiefly from Snorri and should be read as attested tradition through his 13th-c. Christian framing, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
