**statement (plain)** — **Kronos** (Greek *Κρόνος*) is the youngest of the twelve **Titans**, son of **Ouranos** (Sky) and **Gaia** (Earth). At his mother's urging he castrates his father with the sickle and takes rule of the cosmos; his reign is remembered as the **Golden Age**, when mortals lived without toil (Hesiod, *Works and Days* 109–120). Warned by Gaia and Ouranos that he is fated to be overthrown by his own child, he swallows each of the children born to him by his sister-wife **Rhea** — Hestia, Demeter, Hera, Hades, and Poseidon — as they are born (*Theogony* 453–467). Rhea hides the sixth, **Zeus**, and gives Kronos a stone wrapped in swaddling to swallow in his place. Grown, Zeus forces Kronos to disgorge the swallowed children and leads them in the ten-year **Titanomachy**, which ends with Kronos and the Titans cast down into **Tartarus**.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: the second divine sovereignty and, by tradition, the Golden Age of the world's beginning. Key attribute: the sickle (*harpē*) with which he unmans Ouranos. Roles: son who overthrows the father, Titan-king, and the swallowing father in turn overthrown — the middle term of the succession Ouranos → Kronos → Zeus. Etymology: Greek *Krónos* is of disputed derivation; the popular association with *Chrónos* ("time") is a later conflation, not a philological identity. The Roman *interpretatio* identified him with **Saturn**. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and Homer's epics [[source.homer]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The swallowing myth and the Saturn *interpretatio* are attested; the etymology is contested, and the genealogies and the succession myth are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
