**statement (plain)** — Ishihara plates are pseudoisochromatic color vision test stimuli consisting of colored dots arranged to form numbers or patterns that are visible to observers with normal color vision but invisible or different to observers with color vision deficiencies. Developed by Shinobu Ishihara in 1917, the test uses the principle that individuals with red-green color blindness cannot discriminate certain hue combinations that differ primarily in the red-green chromatic channel while maintaining similar luminance. Each plate contains a mosaic of dots varying in hue and luminance, with the target figure composed of dots that differ from the background primarily in chromaticity rather than brightness.
