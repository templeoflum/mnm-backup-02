**statement (plain)** — Modal completion refers to perceptual phenomena in which observers experience visible illusory surfaces possessing definite brightness, color, or texture properties in regions where no such physical stimulation exists. In contrast to amodal completion, where occluded object portions are inferred but not consciously seen, modal completion involves the vivid subjective experience of a surface with specific visual qualities filling a region of space. Classic examples include the Kanizsa triangle, where a white triangular surface appears to float above inducing elements with a brightness that observers consistently report as greater than the physical background luminance, and the Ehrenstein illusion, where a circular disc is seen within a pattern of radiating lines despite being absent from the physical stimulus.
