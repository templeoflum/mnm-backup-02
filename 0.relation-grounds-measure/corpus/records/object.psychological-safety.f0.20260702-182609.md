**statement (plain)** — psychological safety is a team's shared sense that speaking up will not be punished or humiliated — that you can ask a "dumb" question, admit a mistake, challenge a decision, or halt the work without being made to regret it. Where it holds, people surface problems early and learn; where it is absent, they stay silent and errors hide. It is not niceness or lowered standards — it is the felt safety to take *interpersonal* risk.

**statement (framework)** — Edmondson (1999): "a shared belief held by members of a team that the team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking"; empirically it drives learning behavior (question-asking, error-admission, feedback-seeking), which improves performance. The decisive enabling condition behind distributed stop-authority — andon [[object.andon]], deference to expertise [[object.deference-to-expertise]] — which go silent without it.

**basis** — defined and measured across manufacturing teams in [[source.edmondson-1999]]. *Support* — extensively replicated; central to teaming, healthcare safety, and (popularly) Google's Project Aristotle. *Limits* — team-level and relational; does not by itself supply competence or accountability, and can be misread as comfort. **[confirmed]**
