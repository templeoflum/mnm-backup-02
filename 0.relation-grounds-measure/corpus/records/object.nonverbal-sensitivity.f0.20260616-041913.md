**statement (plain).** People differ stably in how accurately they read affective nonverbal cues, and this decoding skill can be measured. The Profile of Nonverbal Sensitivity (PONS) operationalizes it as accuracy at identifying portrayed affect from short clips across multiple channels (face, body, voice tone, content-masked speech).

**basis.** The PONS — a 220-item multi-channel film test — established nonverbal sensitivity as a reliable individual-difference variable and mapped its demographic and personality correlates [[source.rosenthal-1979-b]] **[confirmed as attribution]**. The downstream thin-slicing program rests on the same premise: observers extract valid affective/interpersonal information from brief nonverbal samples, and accuracy is quantifiable as an effect size [[source.ambady-rosenthal-1992]] **[confirmed]**. Limits: the PONS measures decoding of *portrayed* (posed) affect and was built for group research, not individual diagnosis; channel-specific scores can diverge, and "sensitivity" indexes accuracy against an intended-meaning key, not ground-truth mind-reading.
