**statement (plain)** — The Stroop effect, discovered by John Ridley Stroop in 1935, is one of the most robust and widely studied phenomena in cognitive psychology, demonstrating the automaticity of reading and the difficulty of selective attention. The classic task requires participants to name the ink color of color words while ignoring the word's semantic meaning. When the word and ink color are incongruent (for example, the word "red" printed in green ink), reaction times are substantially slower and error rates higher compared to congruent trials (the word "red" in red ink) or neutral baselines.
