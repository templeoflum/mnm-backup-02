**statement (plain)** — **Selene** (Greek *Σελήνη*, "Moon") is the Titan goddess of the moon, child of the Titans **Hyperion** and **Theia** and sister of **Helios** (Sun) and **Eos** (Dawn) (*Theogony* 371–374). By night she drives her **moon-chariot** across the sky, drawn by horses (or, in some accounts, oxen), a crescent upon her brow. She is best known for her love of the beautiful mortal shepherd **Endymion**, for whom she asks — and is granted — that he be laid in an **eternal sleep**, ageless and unchanging, so that she may visit him for ever.

**statement (attestation)** — domain: the moon and its nightly course, the light of night. Genealogy: daughter of Hyperion and Theia; sister of Helios and Eos. Attributes: the chariot, the crescent, a torch or veil. Etymology: Greek *Selēnē*, from *selas*, "light, brightness." The Roman *interpretatio* identified her with **Luna**. In later tradition Selene is associated or partly assimilated with **Artemis** and **Hecate** as a lunar triad, a development beyond the earliest attestations. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The lunar chariot and the Endymion myth are attested; the assimilation with Artemis and Hecate is attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
