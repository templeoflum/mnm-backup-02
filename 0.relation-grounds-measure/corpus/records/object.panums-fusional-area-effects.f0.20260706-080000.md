**statement (plain)** — Panum's Fusional Area Effects describe the range of binocular disparities over which the visual system can maintain single, fused vision rather than experiencing diplopia (double vision). Discovered by Danish physiologist Peter Ludvig Panum in 1858, this phenomenon reveals fundamental constraints on binocular integration and stereoscopic depth perception. Within Panum's fusional area, corresponding retinal images from the two eyes are sufficiently similar that they are perceptually unified into a single cyclopean representation, despite measurable positional differences.
