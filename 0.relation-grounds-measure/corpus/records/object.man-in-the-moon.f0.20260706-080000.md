**statement (plain)** — The Man in the Moon is a classic example of face pareidolia in which observers perceive a human face in the pattern of dark and light regions on the lunar surface visible from Earth. The dark regions, known as lunar maria (Latin for "seas"), are vast basaltic plains formed by ancient volcanic eruptions, while the lighter areas are heavily cratered highlands. When viewed from Earth, particularly during a full moon, these contrasting regions create a pattern that many cultures have interpreted as a human face, with two dark maria forming eyes and other features suggesting a nose and mouth.
