**statement (plain)** — The color afterimage is one of the most fundamental and readily demonstrable visual aftereffects, occurring when prolonged viewing of a colored stimulus produces a subsequent perception of its complementary color when viewing a neutral surface. This phenomenon manifests in two primary forms: negative afterimages, where the perceived color is complementary to the adapting stimulus, and positive afterimages, where the perceived color matches the original stimulus. Negative afterimages arise from photoreceptor and neural adaptation in the retina and early visual cortex, where prolonged exposure to specific wavelengths of light causes selective fatigue of the corresponding cone photoreceptors and chromatic opponent channels.
