**statement (plain)** — Lateral inhibition effects encompass a family of perceptual phenomena that arise from the inhibitory interactions between neighboring neurons in sensory systems, particularly in the visual pathway. This neural mechanism, in which active neurons suppress the activity of adjacent neurons, serves to enhance contrast, sharpen spatial boundaries, and improve edge detection by emphasizing differences in stimulation between neighboring regions. Classic manifestations include Mach bands, where illusory bright and dark bands appear at luminance gradients; the Hermann grid illusion, where dark spots appear at intersections of white bars on a black background; and simultaneous brightness contrast, where the perceived brightness of a region depends on the luminance of surrounding areas.
