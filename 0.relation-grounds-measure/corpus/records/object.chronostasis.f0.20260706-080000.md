**statement (plain)** — Chronostasis is a temporal illusion in which the first visual stimulus fixated immediately following a saccadic eye movement appears to persist for a longer duration than it objectively does. The phenomenon is most commonly experienced when glancing at an analog clock with a second hand; the first second after the eye movement seems to last noticeably longer than subsequent seconds, creating the impression that the clock has momentarily stopped. This illusion demonstrates that subjective time perception is not veridical but instead reflects active neural construction involving motor efference copies, predictive coding, and postdictive temporal binding.
