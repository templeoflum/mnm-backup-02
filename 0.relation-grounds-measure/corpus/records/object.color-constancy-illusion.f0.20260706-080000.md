**statement (plain)** — Color Constancy Illusions are a class of phenomena in which the visual system's mechanisms for achieving stable color perception across varying illumination conditions are systematically fooled, causing physically identical spectral compositions to appear as dramatically different colors. Under natural viewing, color constancy allows observers to perceive a banana as yellow whether viewed in bluish daylight or reddish incandescent light, by discounting the illumination and extracting surface reflectance properties. However, carefully constructed displays can exploit these same mechanisms to produce striking illusions where identical pixel values appear as entirely different hues depending on surrounding context.
