**statement (plain)** — 5S is a set of five workplace disciplines, each a Japanese word beginning with S: **seiri** (sort — keep only what is needed, remove the rest), **seiton** (set in order — a designated place for everything, everything in its place), **seiso** (shine — clean, and in cleaning, inspect), **seiketsu** (standardize — make the first three a routine, not a one-off), and **shitsuke** (sustain — hold the discipline over time). The point is a *visual workplace*: when everything has a place and a standard state, anything out of place or abnormal is immediately obvious.

**relevance** — the foundation discipline beneath the visible TPS tools, and the manufacturing form of **close-of-shift** order: leaving the workplace sorted, ordered, and clean at handover so the next shift starts from a known, legible state and any deviation stands out. The ordering practice that makes a clean-floor / clean-index handover natural [[source.hirano-1995]] **[confirmed as attribution]**.
