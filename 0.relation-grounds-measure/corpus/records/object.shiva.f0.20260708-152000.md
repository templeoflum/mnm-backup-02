**statement (plain)** — **Shiva** (Sanskrit *Śiva*) is the destroyer and transformer, the third god of the **Trimurti** beside **Brahma** the creator and **Vishnu** the preserver: the power by which the cosmos is dissolved so that it may be made anew. He is the great **ascetic**, lord of **yogis**, seated in meditation on Mount Kailasa, and also **Nataraja**, the lord of the dance whose dance sustains and destroys the world. He is worshipped aniconically as the **linga**. His consort is **Parvati** (also manifest as Durga and Kali); their sons are Ganesha and Skanda (Kartikeya).

**statement (attestation)** — role: destroyer and transformer; the supreme god of the **Shaiva** traditions; the ascetic and the lord of the dance. Key attributes: the **third eye** in his forehead (whose glance burns); the **trident** (trishula); the crescent moon in his matted hair, which bears the descending river **Ganges** (Ganga); the serpent about his neck; the ash-smeared body; the bull **Nandi** as his mount; the drum (damaru). Texts: developed from the Vedic **Rudra** of the **Rigveda**; risen to supreme destroyer-transformer in the **Mahabharata** and the Shaiva **Puranas** (Shiva Purana, Linga Purana). **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Hindu mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested from the Vedic **Rudra** of the **Rigveda** [[source.rigveda]], the fierce storm-god, through the **Mahabharata** [[source.mahabharata]] and given fullest form in the Shaiva Puranas. The identification of Rudra with the classical Shiva and his rise to supreme destroyer is attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
