**statement (plain)** — The rubber hand illusion is a powerful demonstration of multisensory integration in bodily self-consciousness, first systematically described by Botvinick and Cohen in 1998. The illusion is induced by simultaneously stroking a visible rubber hand and the participant's own hidden hand with synchronous tactile stimulation. After a period typically lasting one to two minutes, participants report experiencing the tactile sensations as arising from the rubber hand and often feel a sense of ownership over the artificial appendage.

**statement (framework)** — category: cognitive higher order; perceptual mechanism(s): cross-modal-integration, bayesian-inference, predictive-coding.
