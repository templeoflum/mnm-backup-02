**statement (plain)** — The Todorovic Illusion demonstrates that the perceived lightness of target regions is determined more by the local junction structure and contour configuration around their edges than by the total area or luminance of the surrounding region. In this illusion, identical gray target squares appear different in lightness depending on whether they share extended contours with dark or light regions, even when the overall luminance of the surround is equated between conditions. The phenomenon was described by Dejan Todorovic and challenges theories that explain lightness solely in terms of spatial averaging, area ratios, or total surround luminance.
