**statement (plain)** — The Crater Illusion, also known as the Concave/Convex Illusion, is a depth-from-shading phenomenon in which the same surface appears either as a protruding convex dome or a recessed concave crater depending on the assumed direction of illumination. This bistable percept arises from the visual system's learned prior assumption that illumination comes from above, a statistical regularity reflecting the position of the sun in natural environments. When viewing photographs of cratered surfaces such as lunar landscapes, craters with shadows on the bottom edge are typically perceived as depressions, while identical images rotated 180 degrees appear as raised bumps, because the shadow positions have been inverted relative to the assumed overhead light source.
