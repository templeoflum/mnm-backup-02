**statement (plain)** — Figure-Ground Ambigrams are sophisticated visual designs in which both the figure (foreground) and ground (background) regions contain meaningful content, typically text or recognizable shapes, with different interpretations emerging depending on which region is assigned as figure versus ground. Unlike simple ambigrams that can be read in multiple orientations, figure-ground ambigrams exploit the fundamental perceptual process of figure-ground segregation to create designs where the negative space is as semantically meaningful as the positive space. Classic examples include designs where one word appears in the black regions while a different word or image appears in the white regions, often with the two meanings being semantically related or contrasting.

**statement (framework)** — category: ambiguous bistable multistable; perceptual mechanism(s): figure-ground-segregation, contour-integration.
