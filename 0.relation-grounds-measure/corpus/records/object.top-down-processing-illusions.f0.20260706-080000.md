**statement (plain)** — Top-down processing illusions represent a diverse collection of perceptual phenomena in which higher-level cognitive factors such as knowledge, expectations, context, attention, and beliefs systematically override or modulate the processing of bottom-up sensory information, leading to systematic misperceptions. These illusions provide compelling evidence that perception is not a simple feed-forward process from sensation to cognition but involves extensive bidirectional interactions in which cortical areas associated with memory, language, and executive function send predictions and constraints downward to earlier sensory areas. Examples span all sensory modalities and include the phonemic restoration effect, where missing speech sounds are perceptually filled in based on context; object recognition biases where ambiguous images are resolved according to prior knowledge; and attention-mediated phenomena where task demands determine which aspects of sensory input reach awareness.

**statement (framework)** — category: cognitive higher order; perceptual mechanism(s): top-down-expectation, bayesian-inference, predictive-coding, attentional-modulation.
