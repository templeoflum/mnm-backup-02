**statement (plain)** — you can't know what "buy" means without the whole commercial scene: buyer, seller, goods, money. That scene is the frame; its named slots are the frame elements; the verb evokes the whole even when the sentence fills only part of it. Meaning is relational to a structured background, not atomic.

**basis** — the antecedent is Fillmore's case frame (1968:27) — the set of deep cases a verb requires, with the subject-selection hierarchy Agent > Instrumental > Objective; "Frame Semantics" (1982) generalizes cases-of-a-verb to scenes-in-understanding; FrameNet (1998–present) builds the frame inventory lexicographically at scale.
