**statement (plain)** — In a finite, bounded, volume-preserving (Hamiltonian) system, take any region of phase space. The theorem proves that almost every trajectory starting there returns to that region infinitely often — the system revisits any neighborhood of any earlier state, though the recurrence times can be unimaginably long.

**statement (framework)** — a **proven theorem** (measure-theoretic) with vast philosophical fallout: it sits in tension with the thermodynamic arrow of time (recurrence vs. irreversible entropy increase — the Zermelo objection), and underlies eternal-return arguments. Recurrence times for macroscopic systems exceed the age of the universe. Proven.

**basis** — Poincaré's 1890 three-body-problem memoir [[source.poincare-1890]]. **[confirmed as attribution]**
