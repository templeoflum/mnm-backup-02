**statement (plain)** — The Baldwin Illusion is a geometric size illusion in which identical lines appear to differ in length depending on the size of surrounding contextual elements. First described by James Mark Baldwin in 1895, the illusion typically presents two parallel horizontal lines of equal length, each positioned between pairs of squares or other geometric shapes. The line flanked by larger squares appears shorter than the line flanked by smaller squares, despite their objective equality.

**statement (framework)** — category: geometric size length; perceptual mechanism(s): misapplied-size-constancy, spatial-filtering.
