**statement (plain)** — Habits are built by doing the same behavior in the same context repeatedly. Each repetition strengthens the context→response association until the context alone triggers the response ([[object.habit-automaticity]]). The practical corollaries: keep the context stable (habits transfer poorly across settings), lower the friction on the wanted behavior and raise it on the unwanted one, and rely on repetition rather than motivation — motivation is what gets the first repetitions done, not what sustains the habit.

**evidence** — habit formation is gradual and its rate varies widely by behavior and person; in Lally et al.'s field study (2010) automaticity rose along an asymptotic curve with a median of ~66 days to plateau (range ~18–254), and a missed occasion did not derail the process [[source.wood-2019]] **[confirmed]**. Disrupted contexts (a move, a new job) are where old habits break and new ones can be seeded, because the cue structure is temporarily open [[source.wood-neal-2007]] **[confirmed as attribution]**.
