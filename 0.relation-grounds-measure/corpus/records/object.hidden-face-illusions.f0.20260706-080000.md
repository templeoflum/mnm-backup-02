**statement (plain)** — Hidden Face Illusions are a class of visual stimuli in which human faces are concealed or camouflaged within complex scenes, becoming apparent only after the observer discovers the appropriate perceptual organization. Unlike bistable figures where multiple interpretations alternate, hidden face illusions typically produce a one-way perceptual transition: once the face is discovered, it becomes difficult to unsee it, demonstrating the powerful influence of face-detection mechanisms on visual perception. These illusions exploit the human visual system's heightened sensitivity to face-like configurations, which is mediated by specialized neural circuitry including the fusiform face area.
