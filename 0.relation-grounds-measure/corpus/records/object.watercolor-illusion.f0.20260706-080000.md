**statement (plain)** — The watercolor illusion, discovered by Baingio Pinna in 1987, is a chromatic completion phenomenon in which a thin colored contour flanked by a darker or differently colored contour induces an illusory tint that spreads across the entire enclosed region, resembling watercolor paint wash on paper. The effect is strongest when the lighter or more saturated color is positioned on the inner side of the contour pair, and the magnitude of spreading can extend over remarkably large spatial areas, sometimes spanning several degrees of visual angle. Unlike simultaneous contrast, which produces complementary color shifts, the watercolor illusion creates assimilative color spreading that matches the hue of the inducing contour.

**statement (framework)** — category: color; perceptual mechanism(s): surface-completion, figure-ground-segregation, contour-integration.
