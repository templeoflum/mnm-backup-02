Full citation — C.-L. M. H. Navier, "Mémoire sur les lois du mouvement des fluides," *Mémoires de l'Académie des sciences de l'Institut de France*, tome VI (1827), pp. 389–440. The memoir was read at the Académie Royale des Sciences in Paris on 18 March 1822; the volume nominally for 1823 was issued in 1827.

Contains — the first derivation of equations of motion for a viscous (incompressible) fluid, extending Euler's equations to include internal friction. Navier's molecular-force derivation was later judged flawed in its physical basis, but the resulting equation is correct; it was re-derived on continuum grounds by Cauchy (1823), Poisson (1829), Saint-Venant (1837), and Stokes (1845). The pairing Navier (1822) + Stokes (1845) gives the equations their name.
