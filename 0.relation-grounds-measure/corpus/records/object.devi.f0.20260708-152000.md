**statement (plain)** — **Devi** (Sanskrit *Devī*, "the Goddess"), as **Mahadevi** ("the great Goddess"), is the supreme feminine divine — the one Goddess of whom all goddesses are forms, and the **shakti**, the active divine energy or power, without which the male gods are inert. She is manifest in many forms: as **Parvati**, the gentle consort of Shiva; as **Durga**, the warrior-goddess mounted on a lion who slays the buffalo-demon **Mahishasura** when the gods cannot; and as **Kali**, the fierce, dark, garlanded goddess of destruction and time. In the **Shakta** traditions she is worshipped as the supreme deity.

**statement (attestation)** — role: the great Goddess; supreme feminine divine and **shakti**; consort-energy of Shiva. Key manifestations: **Parvati** (Uma), the consort of Shiva; **Durga** (Mahishasuramardini), slayer of the buffalo-demon Mahishasura, lion-mounted and many-armed; **Kali**, the black goddess of time and destruction, garlanded with skulls; and lesser forms and the Mahavidyas. Texts: gathered into a single supreme Goddess most fully in the **Devi Mahatmya** (the "Glorification of the Goddess"), embedded in the **Markandeya Purana**, and in the later Shakta Puranas; goddess figures and the shakti concept also appear in the **Mahabharata** and the epics. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Hindu mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. The great-Goddess theology is set out most fully in the **Devi Mahatmya** of the Markandeya Purana; goddess figures and shakti also appear in the **Mahabharata** [[source.mahabharata]] and the epics. The gathering of Parvati, Durga, Kali, and the rest into one supreme Mahadevi is attested Shakta tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
