**statement (plain)** — In Māori tradition **Rongo** is the god of cultivated plants, peace, and fertility, and a son of **Rangi** and **Papa**. When the parents were parted and **Tāwhirimātea** loosed his storms, Rongo (with **Haumia**, the wild foods) hid within the body of their mother Papa, and so he became the god of the tilled crops that grow from the earth. He is above all the god of the kūmara (sweet potato) and of peace-making.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: peace, agriculture, fertility, harvest. Son of **Rangi** and **Papa**; brother of **Tāne**, **Tū**, **Tangaroa**, and **Tāwhirimātea**; god of the kūmara. Cognate with the Hawaiian **Lono** (recorded separately). Māori tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested in Grey, *Polynesian Mythology* (1855) [[source.grey-1855]]; documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). These myths come through Māori oral tradition collected and recorded in the 19th–20th c. (Grey and later scholars); the exact roster of the cultivated-food gods and their names varies across iwi. NOTE the cognate: **Rongo ≈ the Hawaiian Lono** (recorded separately). **[confirmed as attribution]**
