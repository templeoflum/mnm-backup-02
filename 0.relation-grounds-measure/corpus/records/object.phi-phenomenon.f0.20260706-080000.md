**statement (plain)** — The Phi Phenomenon, systematically investigated by Max Wertheimer in 1912, represents a foundational discovery in Gestalt psychology and motion perception research. Unlike beta movement, in which observers perceive a discrete object moving between spatial locations, the phi phenomenon produces an experience of pure, objectless motion—a percept of movement through empty space without any identifiable object traversing the path. This striking dissociation between motion and form perception occurs when two spatially separated light sources are alternately illuminated with precise temporal parameters, typically with interstimulus intervals between 30 and 200 milliseconds.
