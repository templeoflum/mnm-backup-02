**statement (plain)** — Weber's Illusion describes a tactile distance perception phenomenon in which a moving stimulus traced across the skin is perceived as covering significantly more distance than an objectively equal static spatial extent presented simultaneously or sequentially. Discovered by Ernst Heinrich Weber, a pioneering figure in experimental psychology and psychophysics, this illusion reveals fundamental properties of somatosensory spatial processing and the integration of tactile and kinesthetic information. The effect demonstrates that perceived spatial extent in touch is not simply determined by the spatial separation of stimulated receptors, but is enhanced by the temporal dynamics and motion trajectory of stimulation.
