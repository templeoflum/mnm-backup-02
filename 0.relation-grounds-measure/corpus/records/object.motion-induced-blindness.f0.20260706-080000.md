**statement (plain)** — Motion-Induced Blindness (MIB) is a striking perceptual phenomenon in which salient, stationary visual targets spontaneously and repeatedly disappear from conscious awareness when superimposed on a moving background pattern. Discovered by Yoram Bonneh and colleagues in 2001, MIB demonstrates that physical salience does not guarantee perceptual persistence, and that motion processing can actively suppress static feature detection. The illusion typically manifests when observers fixate centrally while attending to high-contrast static targets overlaid on a rotating or translating pattern of distractors.
