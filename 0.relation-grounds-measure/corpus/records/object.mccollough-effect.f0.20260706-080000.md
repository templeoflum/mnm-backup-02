**statement (plain)** — The McCollough effect, discovered by Celeste McCollough in 1965, is a remarkable orientation-contingent color aftereffect in which adaptation to colored gratings of different orientations causes achromatic gratings to appear tinted in complementary colors depending on their orientation. The classic demonstration involves alternately viewing vertical red-and-black gratings and horizontal green-and-black gratings for several minutes. Subsequently, vertical black-and-white gratings appear greenish-cyan, while horizontal black-and-white gratings appear pinkish-red.

**statement (framework)** — category: aftereffect adaptation; perceptual mechanism(s): neural-adaptation, orientation-selectivity.
