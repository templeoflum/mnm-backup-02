**statement (plain)** — **Horus** is the falcon-headed god of the sky and of kingship. Son of **Osiris** and **Isis**, he avenges his murdered father and, after a long contest with his uncle **Set**, defeats him to win the throne of Egypt. The living pharaoh is Horus incarnate; the dead king becomes Osiris and his heir becomes the new Horus. His right eye is the sun and his left the moon — the **Eye of Horus** (*wedjat*), wounded by Set and restored, becomes a great symbol of protection, healing, and wholeness.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: the sky, kingship, and protection. Iconography: a falcon or a falcon-headed man wearing the Double Crown; the **wedjat** eye. The Contendings of Horus and Set — the legal and physical struggle for the throne, resolved in Horus's favour before the tribunal of the gods — is a central myth. The king's titulary included the Horus name, identifying the reigning pharaoh with the god. The Greeks by *interpretatio graeca* equated him (in the form Harpocrates, Horus-the-child, and elsewhere) with **Apollo**. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Egyptian mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested from the Pyramid Texts [[source.pyramid-texts]] onward and central to the Book of the Dead [[source.book-of-the-dead]]; the standard reference treatment is Wilkinson [[source.wilkinson-2003]]. The royal identification with Horus and the *interpretatio* with Apollo are established; the contest with Set and the several forms of Horus are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
