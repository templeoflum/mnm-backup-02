**statement (plain)** — **Coatlicue** ("serpent skirt") is the terrifying Aztec/Nahua earth-mother, mother of the gods. She wears a skirt of woven serpents and a necklace of hearts, hands, and a skull, and is both devourer and womb of all life. Impregnated by a ball of feathers that fell as she swept, she bore **Huitzilopochtli**, which provoked her daughter **Coyolxauhqui** to lead an attack against her.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: the earth, life and death, fertility, motherhood. Nahuatl *Coatlicue*, from *coatl* (serpent) and *cueitl* (skirt) — "serpents-her-skirt." Mother of Huitzilopochtli, Coyolxauhqui, and the Centzon Huitznahua; her colossal statue in Mexico City shows the serpent skirt and the necklace of hearts and hands. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested in the cited sources; documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). The Aztec sources are chiefly the colonial-era codices (Sahagún's *Florentine Codex*), recorded after the conquest; the earth-mother's names and aspects (Coatlicue, Tonantzin, and related mothers) overlap and vary across the sources. **[confirmed as attribution]**
