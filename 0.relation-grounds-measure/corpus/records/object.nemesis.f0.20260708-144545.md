**statement (plain)** — **Nemesis** (Greek *Νέμεσις*) is the goddess of retribution against **hubris** and against undeserved good fortune, who redresses what exceeds due measure. In Hesiod she is a daughter of **Nyx** (Night), born among the grim powers (*Theogony* 223–224). In one tradition she is, by **Zeus**, the mother of **Helen**, whom she bears in the form of an egg after fleeing his pursuit through changing shapes.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: retribution, the redress of excess, and indignation at undeserved fortune. Roles: divine leveler who punishes hubris and pride, and (in one tradition) the mother of Helen. Etymology: Greek *Némesis*, "indignation / due apportionment," from *némein*, "to distribute what is due"; the Roman name is likewise **Nemesis**, at times glossed as **Invidia**. Her office is the restoration of measure against those who rise or prosper beyond their due. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The descent from Nyx and her function against hubris are attested; the Zeus–Helen tradition and the Invidia gloss are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
