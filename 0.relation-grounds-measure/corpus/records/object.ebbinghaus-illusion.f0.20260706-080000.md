**statement (plain)** — The Ebbinghaus Illusion, also known as Titchener Circles, is one of the most studied size contrast illusions in perceptual psychology. Discovered by German psychologist Hermann Ebbinghaus in 1902, the illusion presents two identical central circles, one surrounded by an array of large circles and the other by small circles. The central circle surrounded by large circles appears substantially smaller than the identical central circle surrounded by small circles, demonstrating powerful contextual modulation of perceived size.
