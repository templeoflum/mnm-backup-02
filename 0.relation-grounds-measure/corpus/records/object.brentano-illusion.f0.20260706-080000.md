**statement (plain)** — The Brentano Illusion is a geometric distortion phenomenon closely related to the Müller-Lyer illusion, named after philosopher and psychologist Franz Brentano. In this configuration, two line segments share a common vertex, forming an angle similar to the classic Müller-Lyer arrowhead arrangement, but the task involves judging the location of the bisection point rather than overall line length. Observers systematically misperceive where the vertex divides the composite figure, with the apparent midpoint shifted away from its true geometric position.
