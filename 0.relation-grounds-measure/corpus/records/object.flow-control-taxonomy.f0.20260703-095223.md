**statement (plain)** — Engineering methods for controlling a fluid flow fall into three families by how much they intervene. **Passive** control changes the geometry or surface and adds no energy: vortex generators, riblets, screens, honeycombs, streamlining. **Active** control adds energy in a preset way: steady or unsteady suction, blowing, synthetic jets. **Reactive** (feedback) control adds energy *in response* to a measured flow state: sensors detect the flow, actuators correct it in real time.

**mapping** — the taxonomy lines up with the phases of directing any flow: passive control is pure SHAPE (build the resistance landscape once); reactive control is pure HOLD (sense, compare, correct — a feedback loop); active control sits between. Turbulence and its coherent structures are the usual targets [[source.gad-el-hak-2000]] **[confirmed]**.
