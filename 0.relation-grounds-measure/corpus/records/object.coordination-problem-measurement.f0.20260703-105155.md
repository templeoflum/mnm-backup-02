**statement (plain)** — To measure a quantity you need a standard, but how do you know the standard is correct? Checking it requires an instrument, whose correctness requires a standard — a circle. Chang's case study is temperature: mercury and alcohol thermometers disagree once you leave the fixed points, and there is no prior "true" temperature scale to adjudicate between them. The coordination problem is this threat of circular or regressive justification at the base of any measurement.

**resolution — epistemic iteration** — Chang's answer is not a foundation but a process: start with an imperfect standard, use it to build knowledge that then exposes and corrects the standard's defects, and iterate — "successive stages of knowledge, each building on the preceding one," progressing within a continuous tradition. The same circularity recurs in the modern SI, where units are fixed by constants whose values are expressed in those very units [[source.chang-2004]] **[confirmed]**.
