**statement (plain)** - when a skilled person acts - grips a tool, catches a ball - the body is directed at the task without a prior mental representation of it. Merleau-Ponty calls this motor intentionality: a bodily "I can" distinct from the reflective "I think" of act-intentionality. The intentional arc unifies sensing and moving so that "the body is a knowing subject engaging with the world." Tools extend it - a chisel lets one feel the wood's resistance.

**basis** - Merleau-Ponty (1945), illustrated via the Schneider case (a brain-injured patient whose motor intentionality is disturbed while abstract movement is not); "operative intentionality" derives from Husserl/Fink. **[confirmed]** Rests on [[object.body-schema]]; prose kin [[object.craft-as-attunement]], [[object.indwelling]].
