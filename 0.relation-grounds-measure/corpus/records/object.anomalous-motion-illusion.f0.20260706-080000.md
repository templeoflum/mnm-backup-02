**statement (plain)** — The Anomalous Motion Illusion refers to the compelling perception of motion in objectively static visual patterns. This phenomenon typically arises from repeated asymmetric luminance profiles arranged in specific spatial configurations, where gradients of brightness or contrast create the impression of continuous movement despite the absence of any physical displacement. The illusion is particularly robust when viewing patterns composed of sawtooth or stepped luminance transitions, with the direction of perceived motion corresponding to the orientation of these gradients.
