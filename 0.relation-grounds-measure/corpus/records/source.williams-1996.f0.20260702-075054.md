Michael Allen Williams, *Rethinking "Gnosticism": An Argument for Dismantling a Dubious Category* (Princeton University Press, 1996). Argues that "Gnosticism" is a modern scholarly construct that misdescribes the diverse sources (Nag Hammadi and others) grouped under it, testing the standard cliches — anti-cosmism, elitism, libertinism/asceticism — against the texts and finding them wanting. Foundational because post-1996 scholarship on the "gnostic" corpus is largely framed for or against this argument.
