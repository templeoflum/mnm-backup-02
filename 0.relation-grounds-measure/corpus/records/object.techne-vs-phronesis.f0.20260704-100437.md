**statement (plain)** - Aristotle separates two kinds of practical knowing. Techne (craft) is "a state of capacity to make, involving a true course of reasoning" - it produces something beyond the activity. Phronesis (practical wisdom) is knowing how to act well, where "good action itself is its end." The tell: "in art he who errs willingly is preferable, but in practical wisdom... he is the reverse" - so virtue is a state, not a craft, and "you can forget a craft but there is no forgetting of wisdom."

**basis** - *NE* VI.3-5: techne distinguished from episteme (the necessary/eternal), from empeiria (experience without the "account"), and from phronesis; "craft knowledge pertains to production (poiesis), thereby distinguishing it from practical wisdom, which is concerned with action (praxis)" (Angier). [[source.aristotle-ne6]] **[confirmed]** Sharpens the existing [[object.techne]] and complements [[object.poiesis-praxis-distinction]] (making vs doing) with the knowledge-vs-wisdom cut.
