**statement (plain)** — Day's Cross Illusion is a geometric length distortion effect in which the perceived length of the arms of a cross figure varies systematically with their relative proportions, despite objective equality. Identified by Australian psychologist Ross Day, the illusion demonstrates that identical line segments forming the horizontal and vertical arms of a cross appear unequal when the overall proportions of the cross are manipulated. Specifically, when one arm of the cross is extended or shortened relative to the perpendicular arm, the apparent length of both arms shifts in a predictable manner.

**statement (framework)** — category: geometric size length; perceptual mechanism(s): misapplied-size-constancy, orientation-selectivity.
