**statement (plain)** — **Prometheus** (Greek *Προμηθεύς*) is a **Titan**, son of **Iapetus**, and the great champion of humankind. At **Mekone** he arbitrates the division of the sacrificial ox and tricks **Zeus** into choosing the portion of bones wrapped in fat, leaving the meat for mortals (*Theogony* 535–557). When Zeus in anger withholds fire from men, Prometheus steals it and carries it down hidden in a **fennel-stalk** (*narthēx*). In some traditions — later than Hesiod — he also moulds the first humans from clay. For his defiance Zeus has him bound to a rock (in the Caucasus in later tellings), where an eagle devours his liver each day, which grows whole again each night; he is at last freed when **Heracles** shoots the eagle (*Theogony* 521–534).

**statement (attestation)** — domains: forethought and cunning craft, the benefaction of humankind, and defiance of Zeus. Key attributes: the stolen **fire** and the fennel-stalk that hides it; the rock and the eagle of his punishment. Etymology: Greek *Promētheús* is popularly read as "forethought" (*pro-* + *mēthos/mathein*), contrasted with his brother **Epimetheus** ("afterthought"); this reading is traditional and folk-etymological rather than securely established. The clay-moulding of humanity belongs to later tradition, not to Hesiod. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony and Works and Days [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and Homer's epics [[source.homer]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The Mekone trick, the fire-theft, and the punishment are attested; the "forethought" etymology is folk-traditional and the clay-moulding is a later accretion, and the genealogies are attested tradition, variable across sources, to be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
