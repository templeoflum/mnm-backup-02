**statement (plain)** — The Flash Lag Illusion is a temporal mislocalization phenomenon in which a briefly flashed stationary stimulus appears to spatially lag behind a continuously moving object, even when the two are physically aligned at the moment of the flash. When observers view a moving object and a brief flash is presented at the same spatial location as the moving object, they consistently report that the flash appears behind the moving object in the direction opposite to motion. This perceptual offset typically ranges from several degrees of visual angle and occurs reliably across diverse stimulus configurations and motion parameters.

**statement (framework)** — category: motion movement; perceptual mechanism(s): motion-integration, predictive-coding, neural-adaptation.
