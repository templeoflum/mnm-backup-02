**statement (plain)** — Subjective colors refer to a diverse set of chromatic percepts that arise from physically achromatic stimuli through various temporal, spatial, or configurational manipulations. Unlike veridical color perception, which results from differential activation of cone photoreceptors by varying wavelength compositions, subjective colors emerge through neural interactions that occur when non-chromatic patterns are presented under specific conditions. Major categories include temporal subjective colors such as Fechner colors (perceived during flicker of black-and-white patterns), Benham's top (concentric arcs appearing colored during rotation), and sequential metacontrast; spatial subjective colors arising from specific geometric arrangements; and those induced by eye movements across stationary patterns.
