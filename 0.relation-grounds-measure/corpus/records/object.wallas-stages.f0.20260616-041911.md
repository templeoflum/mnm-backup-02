**statement (plain)** — Wallas's model describes the creative process as four phases: **preparation** (conscious, effortful work defining and investigating the problem), **incubation** (setting the problem aside, allowing unconscious processing), **illumination** (the sudden emergence of the idea — the "aha"), and **verification** (conscious testing, evaluation, and elaboration of the idea). The model separates the *process* of creation from the *ability* tradition that grew up around Guilford.

**basis** — Stated by Wallas in *The Art of Thought* (1926) [[source.wallas-1926]] [confirmed]; presented as a standard process model in Sawyer's survey of the field [[source.sawyer-2012]] [confirmed].

**limits** — The model is a descriptive, phenomenological framework rather than a tested mechanistic theory; the stages are not strictly sequential and overlap in practice. Of its four phases, *incubation* is the one with substantial later quantitative support (see [[object.incubation-effect]]); the framework as a whole remains a heuristic rather than a validated stage sequence [[source.sawyer-2012]] [candidate, partially confirmed].
