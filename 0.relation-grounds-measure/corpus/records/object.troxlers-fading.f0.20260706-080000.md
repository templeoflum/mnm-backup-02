**statement (plain)** — Troxler's fading, discovered by Swiss physician Ignaz Paul Vital Troxler in 1804, is a phenomenon in which peripheral visual stimuli gradually fade from conscious awareness and may disappear entirely during steady central fixation, only to reappear when eye movements occur or attention shifts. The effect is most pronounced for stimuli presented in the visual periphery while the observer maintains fixation on a central point without moving the eyes. The fading is not simply a reduction in perceived contrast but can result in complete perceptual filling-in, where the region previously occupied by the stimulus becomes perceptually indistinguishable from the background.
