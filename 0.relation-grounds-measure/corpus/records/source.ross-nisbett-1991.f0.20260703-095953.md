Lee Ross & Richard E. Nisbett, *The Person and the Situation: Perspectives of Social Psychology* (McGraw-Hill / Temple University Press, 1991).

Contains — the canonical statement of situationism: the power and subtlety of situational influence, the importance of subjective construal, and the treatment of psyches and groups as Lewinian "tension systems" in equilibrium between impelling and restraining forces. Popularizes Lewin's **channel factors** — small situational details that open or close the channel for a behavior and so exert influence out of all proportion to their size (their example: a map plus an appointment time turning tetanus-inoculation intentions into action).
