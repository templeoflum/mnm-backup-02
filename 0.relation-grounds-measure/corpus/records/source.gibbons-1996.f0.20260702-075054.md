B. J. Gibbons, *Gender in Mystical and Occult Thought: Behmenism and its Development in England* (Cambridge, 1996). Study of how Boehme's theosophy — with its androgynous Adam and divine Sophia — was received and developed in England (Pordage, Leade, the Philadelphians), read through the gendered symbolism at the heart of Behmenist thought.
