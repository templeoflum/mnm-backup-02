Edward O. Wilson, *Sociobiology: The New Synthesis* (Harvard University Press, 1975).

Contains — the founding synthesis of sociobiology; relevant here for naming **sematectonic** stigmergy (from Greek *sēma* "sign" + *tektōn* "builder/craftsman"): coordination via modification of the physical work product itself, distinguished from **sign-/cue-/marker-based** stigmergy, where a dedicated signal is deposited apart from the work. This orthogonal pairing to Grassé's original notion is the distinction the stigmergy literature has used since.
