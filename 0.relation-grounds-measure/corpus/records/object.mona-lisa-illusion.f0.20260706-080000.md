**statement (plain)** — The Mona Lisa Illusion, also known as the gaze-following effect, refers to the perceptual phenomenon in which the eyes in a portrait or photograph appear to follow the viewer as they move relative to the image. The effect is named after Leonardo da Vinci's famous Mona Lisa painting, whose subject's gaze famously seems to track observers as they walk past. This illusion occurs because flat, two-dimensional representations of faces lack the binocular disparity and motion parallax cues that normally signal changes in relative position.
