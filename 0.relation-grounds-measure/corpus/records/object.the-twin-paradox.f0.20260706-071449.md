**statement (plain)** — One twin rockets to a distant star and back at near light speed; on return she has aged far less than her stay-at-home sibling. The 'paradox': if motion is relative, why isn't it symmetric? Answer: only the traveler accelerates and changes frames (turns around), so the situations are not symmetric — the asymmetry, and the age difference, are real.

**statement (framework)** — the classic test of understanding **time dilation** and the frame-dependence of elapsed proper time; the resolution turns on the traveler's non-inertial turnaround (or the relativity of simultaneity on the outbound/inbound legs), not a contradiction. Confirmed by atomic-clock experiments (Hafele–Keating). Established.
