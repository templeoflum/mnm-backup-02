**statement (plain)** — **Anu** (Sumerian **An**) is the sky god and supreme head of the Mesopotamian pantheon, the father of the gods. As the highest authority he embodies the sky and legitimate rule, but in most of the surviving myths he is remote and inactive — an *otiose* head of the pantheon who delegates action to the younger gods such as **Enlil**, **Enki**, and **Marduk**. His name is also the Sumerian word for "sky."

**statement (attestation)** — domains: the sky, supreme divine authority, kingship. Culture: Sumerian **An**, Akkadian **Anu**. Chief cult centre: the temple **Eanna** at **Uruk** (shared with Ishtar/Inanna). In the pantheon he heads the great triad with Enlil and Enki/Ea, and in the Enuma Elish he stands among the elder gods before Marduk's exaltation. He is characteristically remote and otiose in the narrative myths, granting authority rather than acting. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Mesopotamian mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in the Enuma Elish [[source.enuma-elish]] and treated in the standard reference by Black & Green [[source.black-green-1992]]. His headship of the pantheon and Uruk cult are established; his remoteness in the myths is attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
