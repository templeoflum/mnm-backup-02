Karl E. Weick and Karlene H. Roberts, "Collective Mind in Organizations: Heedful Interrelating on Flight Decks," *Administrative Science Quarterly* 38, no. 3 (1993): 357–381.

Contains — building on the Berkeley carrier flight-deck fieldwork, theorizes reliable performance as a pattern of **heedful interrelating** — individuals acting while attending to how their actions fit the imagined whole of the joint activity — and coins **collective mind** as the emergent property. Its claim: "as heedful interrelating and mindful comprehension increase, organizational errors decrease." Quotes the "lowest rating on the deck" stop-authority passage from the 1987 carrier study [[source.rochlin-1987]].
