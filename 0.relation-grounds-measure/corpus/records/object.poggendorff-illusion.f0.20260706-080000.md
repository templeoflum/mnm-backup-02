**statement (plain)** — The Poggendorff illusion is a geometric illusion in which a diagonal line passing behind an occluding vertical rectangle appears misaligned with its continuation on the other side, despite being collinear. First described by Johann Christian Poggendorff in 1860, the illusion demonstrates systematic errors in extrapolating contour trajectories across occluded regions. The magnitude of misalignment depends on the angle of the transversal line relative to the occluding edges, with maximum effect occurring at intermediate angles (typically 20-70 degrees from vertical).
