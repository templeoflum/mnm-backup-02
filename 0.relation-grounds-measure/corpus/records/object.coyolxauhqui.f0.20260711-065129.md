**statement (plain)** — **Coyolxauhqui** ("bells on her cheeks") is the Aztec/Nahua moon goddess, daughter of the earth-mother **Coatlicue**. She led the four hundred star-gods, the **Centzon Huitznahua**, in an attack against their mother at the mountain of **Coatepec**. There she was struck down by the newborn **Huitzilopochtli** and cut to pieces, her body flung down the mountain (the great round stone at the Templo Mayor shows her dismembered).

**statement (attestation)** — domains: the moon, the stars, warfare. Nahuatl *Coyolxauhqui*, from *coyolli* (bell) — "painted with bells" / "bells-on-her-cheeks." Daughter of Coatlicue, sister and adversary of Huitzilopochtli, leader of the Centzon Huitznahua; often read as the moon dismembered by the rising sun. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested in the cited sources; documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). The Aztec sources are chiefly the colonial-era codices (Sahagún's *Florentine Codex*), recorded after the conquest; her identification with the moon and the details of Coatepec vary across the sources. **[confirmed as attribution]**
