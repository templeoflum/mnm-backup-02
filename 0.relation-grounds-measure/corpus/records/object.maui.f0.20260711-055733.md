**statement (plain)** — **Māui** (Māui-tikitiki-a-Taranga) is the great trickster and culture-hero told throughout Polynesia. Born premature and cast into the sea wrapped in his mother's topknot, he was raised by the gods and returned to work his wonders: he fished up islands with his magic hook, snared the sun with a noose to slow its course, and won fire for humankind from the fire-goddess. At the last he sought to defeat death itself and was crushed within the body of **Hine-nui-te-pō** while trying to win immortality for people.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: trickery, culture-hero, fire, sun, island-fishing, death. Son of **Taranga**; his deeds are told across the Pacific (Hawaiian **Māui**, Samoan **Ti'iti'i**); slain by **Hine-nui-te-pō**. Aliases: **Māui-tikitiki-a-Taranga** (Māori). Pan-Polynesian. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested in Grey, *Polynesian Mythology* (1855) [[source.grey-1855]] for the Māori Māui and gathered comparatively in Luomala, *Maui-of-a-Thousand-Tricks* (1949) [[source.luomala-1949]]; documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). These myths come through oral tradition collected and recorded in the 19th–20th c. (Grey, Luomala, and later scholars); the roster of Māui's deeds, his name-forms, and the manner of his death vary widely across islands. **[confirmed as attribution]**
