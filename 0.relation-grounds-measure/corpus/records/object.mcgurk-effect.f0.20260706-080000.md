**statement (plain)** — The McGurk effect is a striking cross-modal illusion demonstrating the integration of auditory and visual information in speech perception. Discovered by Harry McGurk and John MacDonald in 1976, the effect occurs when an auditory speech sound (for example, the syllable "ba") is dubbed onto a video of a person articulating a different sound (for example, "ga"), resulting in the perception of a third, intermediate phoneme (for example, "da") that was neither seen nor heard. This fusion demonstrates that speech perception is fundamentally multimodal, with the visual system's analysis of articulatory gestures contributing obligatorily to phonetic categorization even when auditory information is unambiguous.
