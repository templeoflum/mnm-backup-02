**statement (plain)** — Rasmussen's SRK model distinguishes three modes of cognitive control. **Skill-based** behavior is smooth, automatic, sensorimotor (a practiced operator's routine actions). **Rule-based** behavior applies stored if-then procedures to recognized situations. **Knowledge-based** behavior is slow, effortful reasoning from first principles, invoked when no rule fits — the novel or unforeseen case. Each level has a characteristic failure: slips at the skill level, misapplied rules at the rule level, and flawed reasoning or an incomplete mental model at the knowledge level.

**relevance** — the cognitive backbone of the field: it explains what an operator is doing at each moment, why the hardest failures (knowledge-based, in novel situations) are exactly the residue automation leaves ([[object.ironies-of-automation]]), and it drives interface design that supports all three levels ([[object.ecological-interface-design]]). Underpins modern human-error taxonomies (Reason) as well [[source.rasmussen-1983]] **[confirmed]**.
