**statement (plain)** — **Lemminkäinen** is the handsome, reckless warrior-lover of the Kalevala, all charm and impetuousness without wisdom. In pursuit of a bride from the North he is set the task of shooting the swan of **Tuonela**, the river of the dead, where he is killed and his body cut to pieces and cast into the black water. His mother rakes the river, gathers his scattered body, and by her magic restores him to life.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: passion, war, recklessness. He is the impulsive counterpart to the wise Väinämöinen and the steady Ilmarinen, and his death-and-resurrection is one of the epic's most vivid episodes. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — Lemminkäinen is attested throughout the Kalevala and treated in modern scholarship ([[source.pentikainen-1999]]); this is documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). The Kalevala is Elias Lönnrot's nineteenth-century compilation of oral folk poetry, not an ancient text, and Lönnrot drew the Lemminkäinen cycle together from several distinct oral song-figures. **[confirmed as attribution]**
