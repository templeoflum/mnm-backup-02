William Ickes (ed.), *Empathic Accuracy* (Guilford Press, 1997).

Contains — the founding edited volume on **empathic accuracy**: how accurately a perceiver infers the actual content of another's thoughts and feelings, measured with a video-replay paradigm (perceivers guess a target's recorded thoughts at marked moments and are scored against the target's own report). Ickes (who coined the term with Tooke, 1988) calls it "everyday mind reading." Signature findings: friends outperform strangers, women's advantage is largely motivational, and some groups (e.g., abusive husbands reading their wives) show specific deficits.
