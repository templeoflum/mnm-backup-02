**statement (plain)** — **Tiamat** is the primordial goddess of the salt sea, the embodiment of chaos and of the undifferentiated waters at the beginning of the world. With **Apsu**, the fresh water, she gives rise to the first gods. In the **Enuma Elish**, roused to war against the noisy young gods, she spawns a host of monsters and champions and leads them into battle. She is met and slain by **Marduk**, who splits her body in two — forming the heavens from one half and the earth from the other — so that the ordered cosmos is made from the body of chaos itself.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: the primordial salt sea, chaos, the pre-creation waters. Culture: Babylonian **Tiamat**. In the Enuma Elish she and **Apsu** are the primordial pair from whom the gods descend; she spawns eleven monsters to war on the young gods, is defeated by **Marduk** in single combat, and her divided body becomes **heaven and earth**. She has no cult; she is a cosmogonic figure of the creation epic. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Mesopotamian mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested centrally in the Enuma Elish [[source.enuma-elish]] and treated in the standard reference by Black & Green [[source.black-green-1992]]. Her role as primordial sea and her defeat by Marduk and the making of the world from her body are established; the cosmogony is attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
