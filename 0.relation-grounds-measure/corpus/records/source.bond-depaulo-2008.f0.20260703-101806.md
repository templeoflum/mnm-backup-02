Charles F. Bond Jr. & Bella M. DePaulo, "Individual differences in judging deception: Accuracy and bias," *Psychological Bulletin* 134, no. 4 (2008): 477–492.

Contains — a meta-analytic/statistical case that individual differences in deception-detection *accuracy* are very small, that judges vary far more in bias (tendency to call things truthful/deceptive) than in skill, and that apparent high-accuracy "wizards" can be explained by chance across large samples. The principal scientific counterweight to strong micro-expression / Wizards claims, and to nonverbal-cue-to-deception reliability generally (cf. DePaulo et al. 2003).
