The study of use-dependent change at the synapse — how experience physically alters the strength and number of neural connections. The substrate of learning and memory, and of how the developing brain wires itself.

**origin** — Donald Hebb, *The Organization of Behavior* (1949): the postulate that a synapse strengthens when the presynaptic cell repeatedly helps fire the postsynaptic one. [[source.hebb-1949]] **[confirmed as attribution]**

**codification** — the mechanism was made precise as **long-term potentiation / depression** and **spike-timing-dependent plasticity** [[object.spike-timing-dependent-plasticity]] (strengthening or weakening set by the millisecond order of pre- and post-synaptic firing), balanced by homeostatic mechanisms that keep pure Hebbian rules from running away. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**door** — the developmental side (overproduction then activity- and microglia-guided **synaptic pruning** [[object.synaptic-pruning]]) is opened by Paolicelli et al., *Science* (2011). [[source.paolicelli-2011]]
