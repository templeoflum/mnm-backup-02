**statement (plain)** — reflections are the atoms of rigid geometry: any isometry can be built by reflecting in a small number of mirrors. In n dimensions, n mirrors always suffice (n+2 if the motion has no fixed point). Two reflections make a rotation (mirrors meeting) or a translation (mirrors parallel) — so screw motions and Euclidean isometries are all just short products of reflections.

**statement (formal)** — every non-identity isometry of the n-dimensional Euclidean space is a composition of at most n reflections through hyperplanes (Cartan–Dieudonné); affinely (Gallier), an isometry of Is(n) with a fixed point is a composition of ≤ n reflections, otherwise ≤ n+2 — generalizing Chasles' screw-motion result. **[confirmed]**

**basis** — attributed to É. Cartan (1938) and J. Dieudonné (1948); exact primary years not independently verified here. Composition of two reflections = rotation (intersecting mirrors, twice the angle) or translation (parallel mirrors). SE(n) is generated by even numbers of reflections.
