**statement (plain)** — In Māori tradition **Tū** (Tūmatauenga) is the god of war and of humankind — the fierce brother who, when the children debated how to escape the darkness between **Rangi** and **Papa**, alone counselled killing their parents rather than merely parting them. When **Tāwhirimātea** loosed his storms in revenge, Tū alone stood his ground and fought, and afterward subdued his brothers, becoming ancestor and patron of warriors.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: war, humanity, courage, hunting, fishing. Son of **Rangi** and **Papa**; brother of **Tāne**, **Tangaroa**, **Rongo**, and **Tāwhirimātea**; ancestor and patron of humankind. Aliases: **Tūmatauenga** (Māori). Cognate with the Hawaiian **Kū** (recorded separately). Māori tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested in Grey, *Polynesian Mythology* (1855) [[source.grey-1855]]; documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). These myths come through Māori oral tradition collected and recorded in the 19th–20th c. (Grey and later scholars); the war-god's role and epithets vary across iwi. NOTE the cognate: **Tū ≈ the Hawaiian Kū** (recorded separately). **[confirmed as attribution]**
