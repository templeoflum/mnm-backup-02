**statement (plain)** — Gestalt completion effects encompass a broad class of perceptual phenomena in which the visual system spontaneously organizes fragmentary or incomplete visual elements into coherent wholes based on organizational principles identified by Gestalt psychologists in the early twentieth century. These principles include proximity, similarity, good continuation, closure, common fate, and prägnanz (the tendency toward simple, regular forms). When presented with scattered dots, broken lines, or partially occluded shapes, observers automatically perceive organized patterns, continuous contours, and complete objects rather than experiencing the stimulus elements as independent, unrelated fragments.
