**statement (plain)** — Apparent Motion, also known as Beta Movement, is a fundamental perceptual phenomenon in which the sequential presentation of static stimuli at different spatial locations produces the compelling impression of smooth, continuous motion. First systematically investigated by Max Wertheimer in 1912, this illusion forms one of the cornerstone observations of Gestalt psychology and has profound implications for understanding visual motion perception. When two stationary images are flashed in rapid succession at nearby locations, observers do not perceive two separate events but rather a single object moving seamlessly from the first position to the second.
