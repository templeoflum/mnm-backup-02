**statement (plain)** — The Fraser-Wilcox Illusion, discovered by James Fraser and J.E.H. Wilcox in 1979, produces a compelling perception of rotary motion in static images composed of repeated asymmetric luminance profiles arranged in concentric circular patterns. The illusion typically consists of rings containing sequences of luminance elements that transition from light to dark through intermediate gray values in a spatially asymmetric manner.

**statement (framework)** — category: motion movement; perceptual mechanism(s): motion-energy-detection, orientation-selectivity, spatial-filtering.
