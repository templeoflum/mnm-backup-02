Karl E. Weick, "The Collapse of Sensemaking in Organizations: The Mann Gulch Disaster," *Administrative Science Quarterly* 38, no. 4 (1993): 628–652.

Contains — reads the 1949 Mann Gulch wildfire, in which 13 smokejumpers died, as a collapse of **sensemaking**: the crew's role structure and shared understanding disintegrated — famously when foreman Wagner Dodge lit an "escape fire" and ordered the crew into it, an instruction so far outside their frame that they ignored it and ran. Weick draws from it sources of *resilience*: improvisation, "virtual role systems," the attitude of wisdom, and norms of respectful interaction.

Lineage — the account is influential but contested: Thomas Basbøll and others allege scholarship problems, and a 2023 reanalysis argues the crew did *not* in fact lose its structure. Cite as an influential interpretation, not settled fact.
