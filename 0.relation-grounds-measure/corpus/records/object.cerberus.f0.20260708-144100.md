**statement (plain)** — **Cerberus** (Greek *Κέρβερος*) is the monstrous, **many-headed dog** that guards the gate of the **underworld** (Hades). Offspring of **Typhon** and **Echidna** (*Theogony* 310–312), he keeps watch at the entrance, fawning on the dead who enter but permitting **none to leave** — letting the shades in and none out. Hesiod gives him fifty heads; the common later tradition gives him **three**, often with a serpent for a tail and snakes about his neck. His **capture** — dragging Cerberus up alive from Hades into the light — is the **twelfth and last Labour** imposed on **Heracles**.

**statement (attestation)** — domain: the guarding of the threshold between the living and the dead, and the boundary of the underworld. Genealogy: offspring of Typhon and Echidna; brother of Orthrus, the Hydra, and the Chimera. Attributes: multiple heads (three in the standard tradition), serpent-tail. Etymology: Greek *Kérberos*, of disputed derivation. The Roman *interpretatio* carries him over as **Cerberus**. His single mythic role of note beyond guarding the gate is his forced capture in the last Labour of Heracles. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The parentage, the gate-keeping role, and the capture as Heracles' final Labour are attested; the number of heads varies across sources and should be read as attested tradition, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
