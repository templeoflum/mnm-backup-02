**statement (plain)** — Two drivers are equally reckless. One gets home; the other kills a child who runs into the road. We judge the second far more harshly — though the only difference is luck, an outcome outside either's control. Yet the Kantian principle says moral worth depends only on the will, not on consequences.

**statement (framework)** — **moral luck** threatens the control principle at the heart of ethics. Nagel distinguishes *resultant*, *circumstantial*, *constitutive*, and *causal* luck; Williams argues the aspiration to a luck-free morality is confused. Excising the phenomenon seems to make moral judgment incoherent. Established; unresolved (**open**).
