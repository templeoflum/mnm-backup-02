**statement (plain)** — Persistence of vision refers to the phenomenon in which visual percepts continue briefly after the physical stimulus has been removed, creating a temporal continuity that enables the perception of smooth motion from rapidly presented discrete images. This property of the visual system has been historically invoked to explain the apparent motion seen in cinema, television, animation, and early optical devices such as the phenakistoscope and zoetrope. The persistence duration is typically estimated at approximately 50-100 milliseconds, though this varies with stimulus intensity, duration, and retinal location.
