**statement (plain)** — Pepper's Ghost is a theatrical illusion technique that creates the appearance of transparent, ethereal figures on stage through the strategic use of angled glass and controlled lighting. Developed and popularized by John Henry Pepper in 1862, though based on earlier principles described by Henry Dircks, the illusion employs a large sheet of glass positioned at a 45-degree angle between the audience and the stage. A brightly lit actor or object positioned off-stage reflects off this transparent barrier, appearing to the audience as a ghostly, semi-transparent apparition that can seemingly interact with performers on the main stage.
