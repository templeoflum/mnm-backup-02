**statement (plain)** — The Müller-Lyer Illusion, first reported by German psychiatrist Franz Carl Müller-Lyer in 1889, stands as perhaps the most extensively studied geometric illusion in perceptual science. The classic configuration presents two equal-length horizontal line segments, one terminated by outward-pointing arrow fins (wings-out) and the other by inward-pointing fins (wings-in). Observers consistently perceive the wings-out shaft as approximately 15-25% longer than the wings-in shaft, despite their objective equality.
