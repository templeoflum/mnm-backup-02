**statement (plain)** — The Sander's parallelogram illusion, first described by Friedrich Sander in 1926, is a geometric size illusion in which the diagonal of a larger parallelogram appears considerably longer than an objectively equal-length diagonal of a smaller parallelogram placed adjacent to or overlapping it. The illusion is typically constructed using two parallelograms of different sizes that share a common vertex or edge, with their diagonals serving as the critical comparison elements. Despite being physically identical in length, the diagonal spanning the larger parallelogram is consistently perceived as longer.

**statement (framework)** — category: geometric size length; perceptual mechanism(s): misapplied-size-constancy, spatial-filtering.
