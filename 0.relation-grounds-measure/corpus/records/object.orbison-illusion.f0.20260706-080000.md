**statement (plain)** — The Orbison Illusion, first reported by William Dillard Orbison in 1939, occurs when regular geometric shapes such as squares, circles, or rectangles are superimposed on backgrounds containing radiating lines or concentric patterns. Under these conditions, the target shapes appear systematically distorted, with straight edges appearing curved and right angles appearing acute or obtuse. The magnitude of distortion depends on the relationship between the target shape's orientation and the background pattern's radial structure.
