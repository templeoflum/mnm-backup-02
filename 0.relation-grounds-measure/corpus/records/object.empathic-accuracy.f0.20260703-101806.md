**statement (plain)** — Empathic accuracy is the measurable degree to which one person correctly infers *what another is actually thinking or feeling* — not warmth or concern, but hit-rate on content. It is operationalized with a video-replay paradigm: a target's recorded thoughts at marked moments are the ground truth, and a perceiver's inferences are scored against them. Ickes calls it "everyday mind reading."

**findings** — friends outperform strangers (shared history supplies priors); the female advantage is largely a matter of motivation, not fixed ability; and specific deficits appear in some dyads (e.g., abusive husbands reading their own wives). Distinct from decoding accuracy on standardized nonverbal tests ([[object.nonverbal-sensitivity]]) — empathic accuracy scores *content inference in a real interaction*. A key outcome measure in whether inner stillness improves reading others (Mascaro 2013, Tan 2014) [[source.ickes-1997]] **[confirmed as attribution]**.
