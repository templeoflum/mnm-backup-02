**statement (plain)** — **Njörðr** (Old Norse *Njörðr*) is one of the **Vanir** dwelling among the Aesir: god of the sea, seafaring, wind, and wealth, father of **Freyr** and **Freyja**. Snorri tells that he came to the Aesir as a hostage after the war between the Aesir and the Vanir, and that men should invoke him for seafaring and for fishing. His marriage to the giantess **Skaði** is ill-matched: he loves his shore-home **Nóatún** ("ship-enclosure") by the sea, while she loves her father's mountain hall **Þrymheimr** — neither can bear the other's country, so they part between the two.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: the sea, seafaring, wind, fishing, and wealth. Key relations: father of **Freyr** and **Freyja** among the Vanir; his seat **Nóatún**; his contested marriage to **Skaði**. Snorri records his exchange to the Aesir as a hostage sealing the Aesir–Vanir war, and preserves the account of the mismatched marriage between shore and mountain. His name is philologically linked to the goddess **Nerthus** named by Tacitus in the *Germania*, an established comparison of the reflex, not a claim of identity. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Norse mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested across the Poetic Edda [[source.poetic-edda]] and systematized in Snorri's Prose Edda [[source.prose-edda]]; the standard reference treatment is Lindow [[source.lindow-2001]]. The hostage exchange and the Skaði marriage come chiefly from Snorri and should be read as attested tradition through his 13th-c. Christian framing, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
