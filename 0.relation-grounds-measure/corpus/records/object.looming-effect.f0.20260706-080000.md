**statement (plain)** — The looming effect is an atmospheric optical phenomenon in which distant objects appear larger, closer, and vertically elevated above their actual geometric positions, caused by downward-curving refraction of light rays in the atmosphere. This effect occurs when a temperature inversion exists, with warmer air overlying cooler air near the surface, creating a vertical gradient in atmospheric refractive index. Light rays traveling from distant objects curve downward as they pass through this gradient, reaching the observer's eye at a steeper angle than would occur in a homogeneous atmosphere.
