**statement (plain)** — once a familiar solution method works, the mind keeps applying it even when a simpler or better one is available — the *Einstellung* ("set") effect. Luchins's water-jar experiments established it; the sharpest evidence for *why it is dangerous* is Bilalić's chess work: masters shown a position with a familiar solution and a better one took the familiar one, none found the better, and eye-tracking showed they kept looking at squares relevant to the *first* idea while sincerely believing they were searching for something better. The block operates below awareness.

**statement (mechanism)** — **schema-driven attention**: recognizing a familiar pattern activates a schema that directs attention toward its own features and away from others — including the better solution. The cost is large: a familiar solution cut expert chess performance by ~600 Elo (≈3 SD) [[source.bilalic-2008]].

**basis** — Luchins 1942 [[source.luchins-1942]]; mechanism and cost quantified by Bilalić et al. 2008 [[source.bilalic-2008]]. *Support* — robust across domains (arithmetic, chess, programming). *Limits* — moderated by expertise and by whether the familiar route is cued; countered by incubation and defixation. **[confirmed]**
