**statement (plain)** — The Beuchet Chair Illusion is a three-dimensional installation in which a chair is divided into separate components positioned at different distances from the viewer, yet appears to form a complete, normal-sized chair when viewed from a specific vantage point. Developed by Jean Beuchet, this demonstration creates dramatic misperceptions of human body size when individuals interact with the apparently unified chair structure. The illusion exploits depth cue conflicts and size constancy mechanisms by carefully positioning the chair's back, seat, and legs at varying depths while maintaining the correct angular projections from the designated viewing position.
