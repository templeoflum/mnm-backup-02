**statement (plain)** — The attentional blink is a temporal limitation in visual attention characterized by the failure to detect a second target stimulus (T2) when it appears within approximately 200-500 milliseconds after successful detection of a first target (T1) in a rapid serial visual presentation (RSVP) stream. First systematically described by Raymond, Shapiro, and Arnell in 1992, this phenomenon reveals fundamental constraints on the temporal dynamics of conscious perception and attentional resource allocation. During the attentional blink period, observers typically report being unaware of T2 even when it is clearly visible and would be easily detected outside this critical temporal window.
