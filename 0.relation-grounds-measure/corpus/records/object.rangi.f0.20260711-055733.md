**statement (plain)** — In Māori tradition **Rangi** (Ranginui, the Sky Father) lay locked in embrace with **Papa** (Papatūānuku, the Earth Mother), and between their pressed bodies their many children were trapped in darkness. When **Tāne** braced his legs and thrust his parents apart, sky and earth were separated and light first entered the world. Rangi rose above; ever after his tears fall as rain and his sighs rise as the mist, in longing for Papa below.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: sky, heaven, rain, light, fatherhood. Consort of **Papa**; father of **Tāne**, **Tū**, **Tangaroa**, **Rongo**, **Tāwhirimātea**, and the other atua. Aliases: **Ranginui** (Māori). Cognate with the Hawaiian **Wakea** and the Tahitian **Atea** (recorded, where relevant, separately). Māori tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested in Grey, *Polynesian Mythology* (1855) [[source.grey-1855]]; documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). These myths come through Māori oral tradition collected and recorded in the 19th–20th c. (Grey and later scholars); the details of the separation and the order of the children vary across iwi and island traditions. **[confirmed as attribution]**
