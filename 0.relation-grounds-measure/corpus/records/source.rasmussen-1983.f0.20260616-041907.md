Jens Rasmussen, "Skills, Rules, and Knowledge; Signals, Signs, and Symbols, and Other Distinctions in Human Performance Models," *IEEE Transactions on Systems, Man, and Cybernetics* SMC-13, no. 3 (1983): 257–266 — the foundational statement of the **skill-, rule-, knowledge-based (SRK) framework** of human cognitive control.

Contains — three qualitatively different levels of human performance: **skill-based** (smooth, automated sensory-motor patterns driven by *signals*, below conscious control), **rule-based** (stored if-then procedures cued by familiar *signs*), and **knowledge-based** (deliberate analytical problem-solving over *symbols* in unfamiliar situations). The framework supplies the cognitive substrate for Reason's error taxonomy (slips/lapses at the skill/rule level, mistakes at the rule/knowledge level) and for human-reliability and human-factors safety analysis generally.
