**statement (plain)** — **Cú Chulainn** (also spelled **Cuchulainn** / **Cuchulain**) is the greatest hero of the **Ulster Cycle**: son of the god **Lugh** (and, in the mortal frame, of Sualtam), born **Sétanta** and renamed "the Hound of **Culann**" after he slays the smith's guard-dog and takes its place. Trained by the warrior-woman **Scáthach**, he is famed for his boyhood deeds (*macgnímrada*), for his single-handed defense of **Ulster** against the army of **Medb** in the **Táin Bó Cúailnge** — holding the ford while the men of Ulster lie under a debilitating curse — and for his monstrous battle-frenzy, the **ríastrad** ("warp-spasm"), in which his body contorts. He wields the barbed spear **Gáe Bolg**, and dies young, binding himself upright to a standing stone so as to die on his feet.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: heroism, single combat, martial prowess, the warrior's frenzy. Key attributes: the spear **Gáe Bolg**; the **ríastrad** (battle-distortion); his charioteer **Láeg**; his geasa (taboos), whose breaking brings his death. Associations: son of **Lugh**; fostered/king Conchobar's champion; slays his own son **Connla** unknowingly, and his friend **Ferdiad** at the ford. Text: the **Táin Bó Cúailnge** and its fore-tales (Ulster Cycle), preserved in the **Book of the Dun Cow** (c. 1100). Etymology: *Cú Chulainn*, "the Hound of Culann." Irish tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Irish mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested throughout the Ulster Cycle — the Táin Bó Cúailnge and its remscéla [[source.ulster-cycle]]; the standard reference treatment is MacKillop, *Dictionary of Celtic Mythology*. The naming, the Táin defense, the ríastrad, and the divine paternity by Lugh are attested tradition, variable across recensions, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
