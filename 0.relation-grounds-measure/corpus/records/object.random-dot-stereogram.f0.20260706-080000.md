**statement (plain)** — Random Dot Stereograms, pioneered by Béla Julesz in 1960, are pairs of images composed entirely of randomly distributed dots that reveal vivid three-dimensional shapes only when viewed with proper binocular fusion, despite containing no monocular depth cues whatsoever. Each stereogram pair presents statistically identical random dot patterns to the two eyes, with the critical manipulation being horizontal shifts of specific dot regions between the left and right images, creating controlled binocular disparities. When these disparities are correctly fused by the visual system, regions with different disparity values appear at different depths, causing hidden shapes such as squares, circles, or complex forms to emerge dramatically from the background.

**statement (framework)** — category: depth 3d stereoscopic; perceptual mechanism(s): depth-cue-conflict, bayesian-inference.
