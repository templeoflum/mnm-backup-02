**statement (plain)** — **Akka** is the earth-goddess and consort of **Ukko**, the sky-god. Her union with Ukko brings the fertilizing rain, joining sky and earth to make the crops grow. She is a protector of women in childbirth and of the harvest, the female ground answering the male sky.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: earth, fertility, childbirth, harvest. Her name means "old woman," the earthly counterpart to Ukko's "old man." She is associated with the rowan tree (Finnish *rauni*), which some scholars link to her by-name **Rauni** — an etymology that is reconstructed and contested, not securely attested. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — Akka is named in Mikael Agricola's 1551 catalogue of pagan gods and treated in modern scholarship ([[source.pentikainen-1999]]); this is documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). The proposed identity of Rauni as Akka's rowan-associated by-name rests on disputed etymology and should be held as scholarly-reconstructed rather than settled. Where the tradition reaches us through later compilations such as Lönnrot's nineteenth-century Kalevala, that material is compiled from older oral runo-song, not an ancient text. **[confirmed as attribution]**
