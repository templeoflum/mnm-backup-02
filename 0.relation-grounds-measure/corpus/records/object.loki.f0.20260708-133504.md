**statement (plain)** — **Loki** (Old Norse *Loki*) is the trickster of Norse myth, an ambiguous figure of giant parentage (son of **Fárbauti** and **Laufey**) who dwells among the Aesir. He is a shapeshifter who moves between forms and sexes, by turns helper and saboteur of the gods — devising their treasures and rescues, and also their disasters. By the giantess **Angrboda** he fathers three monsters: the wolf **Fenrir**, the world-serpent **Jormungandr**, and **Hel**, mistress of the dead. He engineers the death of **Baldr**, guiding the blind **Höðr** to cast the mistletoe, and afterward blocks his return from Hel. For this the gods bind him beneath a venom-dripping serpent until **Ragnarök**, when he breaks free and leads the giants and the dead against the gods, falling in mutual slaughter with **Heimdall**.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: trickery, deceit, transformation, strife, and (through his offspring and Ragnarök role) the undoing of the cosmic order. Key associations: shapeshifting (mare, salmon, fly, seal, falcon-cloak flights), the monstrous brood (Fenrir, Jormungandr, Hel), and the eight-legged **Sleipnir**, whom he bears as a mare. Attested chiefly in *Lokasenna* (his flyting of the gods), *Þrymskviða*, and Snorri's *Gylfaginning*. Cross-pantheon "trickster" comparisons are made by analogy of function, not descent. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Norse mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested across the Poetic Edda [[source.poetic-edda]] and systematized in Snorri's Prose Edda [[source.prose-edda]]; the standard reference treatment is Lindow [[source.lindow-2001]]. The connected biography (Baldr's death, the binding, the Ragnarök role) owes much to Snorri's 13th-c. Christian framing and should be read as attested tradition, not neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
