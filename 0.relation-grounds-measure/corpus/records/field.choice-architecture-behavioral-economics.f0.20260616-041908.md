**origin** — Daniel Kahneman & Amos Tversky, "Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk," *Econometrica* 47(2):263–291 (1979) — the descriptive alternative to expected-utility theory that grounds the field: reference-dependence, loss aversion, and probability weighting. Preceded by Tversky & Kahneman's heuristics-and-biases program ("Judgment under Uncertainty," *Science* 185:1124–1131, 1974). [[source.kahneman-tversky-1979]] **[confirmed]**

**codification** — Richard H. Thaler & Cass R. Sunstein, *Nudge: Improving Decisions About Health, Wealth, and Happiness* (Yale University Press, 2008) — names "choice architecture" and "libertarian paternalism," turning the descriptive findings into a design program for steering choices while preserving freedom to opt out. The principle was first stated in Thaler & Sunstein, "Libertarian Paternalism," *AER* 93(2):175–179 (2003). [[source.thaler-sunstein-2008]] **[confirmed]**

**door** — Daniel Kahneman, *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2011) — the standard popular synthesis of the heuristics/biases and prospect-theory tradition (System 1 / System 2), widely used as the field's entry text. [[source.kahneman-2011]] **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — two converging streams: the descriptive decision science of Kahneman & Tversky (1974, 1979, 1981) and the behavioral-economics-of-policy program of Thaler & Sunstein (2003, 2008). The empirical default-effect literature — Madrian & Shea (2001) — supplies the field's signature applied result. [[source.madrian-shea-2001]] **[confirmed]**

*grade [s] — origin, codification, and door all present.*
