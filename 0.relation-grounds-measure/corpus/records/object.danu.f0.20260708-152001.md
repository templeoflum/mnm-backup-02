**statement (plain)** — **Danu** is the mother goddess of the Irish gods, who are named after her: the **Tuatha Dé Danann**, "the peoples (or tribes) of the goddess **Danu**." She is an ancestral, primordial mother figure — the divine matriarch from whom the race of gods descends. She is, however, attested **more by name than by narrative**: she has almost no surviving story of her own, appearing chiefly through the collective name of her people. **[uncertain — Danu is essentially a name behind a patronymic; the very nominative form "Danu" is a scholarly reconstruction, and she is sometimes conflated with the figure Anu/Anann; she has no developed myth]**

**statement (attestation)** — domains: motherhood, ancestry, the divine race (traditionally, and by inference, fertility/earth). Key attribute: the eponymy of the **Tuatha Dé Danann**. Associations: sometimes identified or conflated with **Anu / Anann** (the reconstructed genitive *Danann* underlies the tribal name). Text: the **Lebor Gabála Érenn** (chiefly as the eponym of the god-race). Etymology: the name is standardly reconstructed from the genitive *Danann* in *Tuatha Dé Danann*; the nominative "Danu" is inferred, and a connection to river-goddess names is proposed but debated. Irish tradition. **[uncertain — as noted, attestation is by name, not narrative]**

**basis** — a figure of Irish mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Known chiefly through the collective name in the Lebor Gabála Érenn [[source.lebor-gabala]]; the standard reference treatment is MacKillop, *Dictionary of Celtic Mythology*. The eponymy of the Tuatha Dé Danann is attested; the nominative form "Danu," her identity with Anu/Anann, and any developed maternal narrative are scholarly inference and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[uncertain]**
