Oskar Pfungst, *Das Pferd des Herrn von Osten (Der Kluge Hans)* (Leipzig: J. A. Barth, 1907); English: *Clever Hans (The Horse of Mr. von Osten): A Contribution to Experimental Animal and Human Psychology* (Holt, 1911).

Contains — the investigation showing the "counting" horse was not doing arithmetic but reading tiny, involuntary changes in the questioner's posture, breathing, and facial tension to know when to stop tapping. Hans succeeded (~89%) only when the questioner knew the answer and was visible, and failed (~6%) otherwise. Pfungst found he himself produced the cues involuntarily even when trying to suppress them — cues leaked nonconsciously by the sender and read nonconsciously by the receiver. The origin of the observer-expectancy (Clever Hans) effect and a foundation for double-blind method.
