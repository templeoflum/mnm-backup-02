**statement (plain)** — When people suppress or falsify emotion, true affect can leak out: through very brief involuntary facial displays (micro-expressions) and through the less-controllable channels (feet, hands, torso) that "leak" states the face hides. Skilled readers use clusters of these leakage cues, interpreted against a baseline and context, rather than any single tell. The rare high performers ("Truth Wizards") reportedly detect deception well above chance.

**the controversy (flagged)** — this is genuinely disputed. Bond & DePaulo (2008) argue individual differences in *lie-detection accuracy* are small and the "wizards" may be chance across large samples; the broader DePaulo literature finds nonverbal cues to deception are generally weak. So: leakage of emotion is real and the coding is well-founded ([[object.facial-action-coding-system]]), but micro-expressions as a reliable *deception detector* are not settled — recorded here with the dispute intact, not asserted [[source.bond-depaulo-2008]] **[confirmed as attribution]**.
