**statement (plain)** — Face pareidolia is the perceptual phenomenon in which observers perceive faces in non-face objects, patterns, or textures. This represents the most common and well-studied form of pareidolia, reflecting the human visual system's exceptional sensitivity to face-like configurations. Face pareidolia occurs across a wide range of stimuli, including natural objects such as rocks, clouds, and wood grain, as well as manufactured items like electrical outlets, cars, and buildings.
