**statement (plain)** — **Chalchiuhtlicue** ("she of the jade skirt") is the Aztec/Nahua goddess of the flowing waters of the earth's surface — rivers, lakes, streams, and springs. She is the wife or sister of the rain god **Tlaloc** and patron of birth and of the water-rite that named a newborn. In one tradition she ruled and was the Sun (world-age) that ended in a great flood.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: flowing water, rivers, lakes, springs, birth. Nahuatl *Chalchiuhtlicue*, from *chalchihuitl* (jade / greenstone) and *cueitl* (skirt) — "jade-her-skirt." Consort of Tlaloc; presides over the Aztec water-naming rite; associated with the flood-destroyed world-age. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested in the cited sources; documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). The Aztec sources are chiefly the colonial-era codices (Sahagún's *Florentine Codex*), recorded after the conquest; her relation to Tlaloc (wife vs. sister) and her role among the world-ages vary across the sources. **[confirmed as attribution]**
