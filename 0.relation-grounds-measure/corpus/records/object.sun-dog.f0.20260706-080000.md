**statement (plain)** — Sun dogs, also known as parhelia or mock suns, are atmospheric optical phenomena appearing as bright spots or patches of light flanking the sun at approximately 22 degrees to either side, caused by refraction of sunlight through hexagonal plate-shaped ice crystals with their principal axes oriented vertically. These crystals act as 60-degree prisms, refracting light that passes through their vertical side faces with a minimum deviation angle of approximately 22 degrees. Sun dogs are most commonly observed when the sun is relatively low in the sky, as this geometry maximizes the number of appropriately oriented ice crystals contributing to the phenomenon.
