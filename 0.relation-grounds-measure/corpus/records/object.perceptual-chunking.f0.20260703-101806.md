**statement (plain)** — With experience, an expert stops seeing a domain as many separate elements and starts seeing it in a few large, meaningful configurations — chunks — each grasped as a unit. A chess master reproduces a real position at a glance not by superior memory but because a handful of chunks encodes what a novice sees as twenty scattered pieces; on random (meaningless) positions the advantage vanishes.

**relevance** — the template for all expert perception ("it is no mistake of language for the master to say he *sees* the right move"): radiology, wine tasting, tracking, reading people. Mastery is estimated to require on the order of 10,000–100,000 chunks (commonly ~50,000). Explains why trained perception is fast, automatic, and hard to verbalize — the perceptual counterpart of [[object.tacit-knowing]] and the mechanism under [[object.perceptual-learning]] fluency [[source.chase-simon-1973]] **[confirmed]**.
