**statement (plain)** — a person who knows no Chinese sits in a room with a rulebook mapping incoming Chinese symbols to outgoing ones. They pass answers indistinguishable from a native speaker's — yet understand nothing; they only shuffle symbols by shape. Searle's claim: a running program is in exactly this position. Syntactic manipulation, however fluent, never adds up to *understanding* or *intentionality* (mental states being genuinely *about* something).

**statement (framework)** — a challenge to **strong AI** / computational functionalism ("the right program is *sufficient* for a mind"). Searle grants **weak AI** (the computer as a model of cognition) but denies strong AI: *syntax is not sufficient for semantics*. The classic replies — Systems, Robot, Brain-Simulator, Other-Minds — and Searle's rejoinders form one of the longest-running debates in philosophy of mind; large language models have made it live again.

**basis** — Searle's thought experiment and argument in *Minds, Brains, and Programs* [[source.searle-1980]]. A conceptual/philosophical device, not an empirical result: **established** as a canonical argument, while the questions it poses (what is understanding? is computation sufficient for cognition?) remain **open**. **[confirmed as attribution]**
