**statement (plain)** — **Hermes** (Greek *Ἑρμῆς*) is the Olympian messenger of the gods and the god of travel, trade, boundaries, thieves, and eloquence, and the **psychopomp** — the guide who leads the souls of the dead down to the underworld. He is the son of **Zeus** and the nymph **Maia**. The *Homeric Hymn to Hermes* tells of his precocious first day: born in a cave, he steals the cattle of **Apollo**, invents the lyre from a tortoise shell, and talks his way to reconciliation and a place among the gods. Swift and cunning, he crosses every boundary — between gods and men, between the living and the dead — carrying Zeus's messages and escorting travelers and souls.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: messages and heralding, travel and roads, trade and commerce, boundaries, thievery and cunning, eloquence, and the guiding of souls (as *psychopompos*). Key attributes: the **caduceus** (*kērykeion*, the herald's staff), the **winged sandals** (*talaria*) and winged cap (*petasos*), and the lyre he invents; the *herm* boundary-markers. Parentage: son of Zeus and Maia. The Roman *interpretatio* identified him with **Mercury**. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and Homer's epics and the Homeric Hymns [[source.homer]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The parentage and the Mercury *interpretatio* are well established; the etymology of the name and its link to the *herm* stones are debated, and the birth-day narrative is attested tradition and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
