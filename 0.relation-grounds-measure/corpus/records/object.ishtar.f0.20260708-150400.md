**statement (plain)** — **Ishtar** (Sumerian **Inanna**) is the goddess of love, sexuality, and war — the most prominent goddess of the pantheon, ambivalent and powerful, joining desire and violence. She is the goddess of the famous **descent to the underworld**, where she passes the seven gates, is stripped and killed, and is later restored. In the **Epic of Gilgamesh** she offers herself to the hero, and when he spurns her — reciting the fates of her former lovers — she demands and looses the **Bull of Heaven** upon Uruk in revenge. She is identified with the planet **Venus**, as both morning and evening star.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: love, sexuality, fertility, war, and the planet Venus. Culture: Sumerian **Inanna**, Akkadian **Ishtar**. Chief cult centre: the temple **Eanna** at **Uruk** (shared with Anu). Her chief myths: the **Descent to the Underworld** and, in Gilgamesh Tablet VI, her spurning by the hero and the loosing of the **Bull of Heaven**. She is astrally identified with **Venus**. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Mesopotamian mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in the Epic of Gilgamesh [[source.epic-of-gilgamesh]] and treated in the standard reference by Black & Green [[source.black-green-1992]]. Her Uruk cult, Venus association, and the Descent and Bull-of-Heaven myths are established; the myths are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
