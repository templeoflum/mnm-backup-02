**statement (plain)** — when generating many ideas, quantity peaks early but *quality climbs*: the later ideas in a session are, on average, more original than the earlier ones. The obvious responses come first and are the least creative; the good ones are structurally later. The practical rule: don't stop at the first viable answer.

**statement (framework)** — Beaty & Silvia (2012): two mechanisms combine — spreading activation gradually reaching remote associates on the fringe of the semantic network, and executive control suppressing obvious early responses and switching categories. Directly motivates idea-quota practices (generate 15–20 before adopting one).

**basis** — one of the oldest, most robust findings in the field; the executive interpretation from a 10-minute unusual-uses task (133 adults) [[source.beaty-silvia-2012]]. *Support* — replicated widely. *Limits* — the rise is partly gated by executive function and fluid intelligence; lower working-memory capacity switches categories less. **[confirmed as attribution]**
