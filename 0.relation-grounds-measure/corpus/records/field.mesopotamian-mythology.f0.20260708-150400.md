**origin** — the earliest myths of ancient Mesopotamia, recorded in cuneiform on clay tablets from the 3rd millennium BCE — the world's oldest recorded mythology — telling of the **Anunnaki** gods, the creation of the world and of humankind, and the great Flood, across the Sumerian, Akkadian, Babylonian, and Assyrian traditions. [[source.black-green-1992]] **[confirmed]**

**codification** — the **Enuma Elish** (Babylonian creation epic, on seven tablets, c. late 2nd millennium BCE) and the **Epic of Gilgamesh** (standard Babylonian version c. 1200 BCE) — the two great texts through which the creation and flood traditions are most often drawn. [[source.enuma-elish]] [[source.epic-of-gilgamesh]] **[confirmed]**

**door** — Jeremy Black and Anthony Green, *Gods, Demons and Symbols of Ancient Mesopotamia* (British Museum Press, 1992) — the standard illustrated reference dictionary (with the primary translations of the epics for the myths). [[source.black-green-1992]]

*grade s — origin (3rd-millennium cuneiform myth), codification (Enuma Elish + Epic of Gilgamesh), door (Black & Green 1992) present.
