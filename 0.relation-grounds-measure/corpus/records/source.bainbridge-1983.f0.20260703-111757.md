Lisanne Bainbridge, "Ironies of Automation," *Automatica* 19, no. 6 (1983): 775–779 (Dept. of Psychology, University College London).

Contains — the founding critique of the "classic" approach to automation: automating the routine tasks leaves the human operator only the tasks that could not be automated (the hardest), lets the operator's manual and cognitive skills decay through disuse, yet still expects that operator to monitor vigilantly and take over precisely during abnormal, high-stress conditions. Names the central ironies and the design tension they create — a pioneering statement of why more automation does not simply mean fewer human problems.
