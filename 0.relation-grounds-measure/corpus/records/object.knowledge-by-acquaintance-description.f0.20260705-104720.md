**statement (plain)** — *knowledge by acquaintance* is direct, unmediated, non-inferential (sense-data, memory, introspection, the self "to an extent," universals); *knowledge by description* is indirect, mediated, inferential, "secondhand." The acquaintance principle: "every proposition which we can understand must be composed wholly of constituents with which we are acquainted."

**basis** — Russell, *The Problems of Philosophy* (1912, pp. 58, 78) and the 1910–11 Aristotelian Society paper. Predecessors named in the literature: John Grote ("knowledge of acquaintance"), Helmholtz (1868: *das Kennen* vs. *das Wissen* — the *Kennen* kind "can not be expressed in words, even to ourselves"), and William James (1890).
