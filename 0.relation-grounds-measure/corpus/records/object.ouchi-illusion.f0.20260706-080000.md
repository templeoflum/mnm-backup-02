**statement (plain)** — The Ouchi Illusion, discovered by Japanese artist and vision scientist Hajime Ouchi in 1977, produces a compelling perception of motion and depth from a purely static geometric pattern. The classic configuration consists of a central circular or elliptical region filled with horizontal line segments surrounded by a background of vertical line segments, or vice versa. When observers make small eye movements or shift their gaze across the pattern, the central disk appears to slide, float, or move independently from the background, creating an unstable perception of layered surfaces at different depths.
