**statement (plain)** — White's Illusion is a striking lightness phenomenon in which identical gray patches embedded in a square-wave grating appear different in brightness depending on whether they replace segments of the black or white bars of the grating. Crucially, the direction of the lightness difference is opposite to what classical simultaneous contrast predicts: gray patches replacing white bars appear lighter than those replacing black bars, even though the former have more black in their immediate surround and should therefore appear lighter by contrast. Discovered by Michael White in 1979, this illusion has become one of the most theoretically significant challenges to simple contrast-based accounts of lightness perception.
