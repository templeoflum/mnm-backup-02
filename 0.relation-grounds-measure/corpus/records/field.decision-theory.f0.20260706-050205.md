**origin** — Blaise Pascal's wager (*Pensées*, 1670) — the first application of expected-value reasoning to a practical decision under uncertainty. [[source.pascal-1670]]

**codification** — John von Neumann & Oskar Morgenstern, *Theory of Games and Economic Behavior* (1944) — the expected-utility theorem axiomatizing rational choice over lotteries [[source.von-neumann-morgenstern-1944]]; extended to subjective probability by Savage's *Foundations of Statistics* (1954).

**door** — Martin Peterson, *An Introduction to Decision Theory* (Cambridge) — expected utility, decision under ignorance, Bayesian and causal decision theory.

*grade s — origin (Pascal), codification (von Neumann–Morgenstern; Savage), door (Peterson) present. New field-home for the thought-experiments intake (quarantine, 20260706); homes the rational-choice puzzles (Pascal's wager, Kavka's toxin).*
