**statement (plain)** — **Ares** (Greek *Ἄρης*) is the Olympian god of war, specifically war in its violent, bloodthirsty, and destructive aspect — the tumult of battle and slaughter — as against **Athena**, who embodies war as strategy and disciplined skill. He is the son of **Zeus** and **Hera**. Homer presents him as little loved among the gods: in the *Iliad* Zeus calls him the most hateful of the gods to him, and he is wounded and humiliated in the fighting. He is the lover of **Aphrodite**; in the tale sung in the *Odyssey*, her husband **Hephaestus** traps the pair in an unbreakable net and exposes them to the laughter of the Olympians.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: war, battle-tumult, slaughter, and courage in its raw and violent form. Key attributes: spear, helmet, and shield; the attendants **Deimos** ("terror") and **Phobos** ("fear"), often named as his sons, and the goddess **Enyo**. Parentage: son of Zeus and Hera. His character is markedly unfavorable in Greek treatment, contrasted with the honored Athena. The Roman *interpretatio* identified him with **Mars** — though Mars held a far more central and dignified place in Roman religion than Ares did in Greek. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and Homer's epics [[source.homer]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The parentage and the Mars *interpretatio* are well established; the differing valuations of Ares in Greek versus Mars in Roman religion, and the individual episodes (the net, the wounding), are attested tradition and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
