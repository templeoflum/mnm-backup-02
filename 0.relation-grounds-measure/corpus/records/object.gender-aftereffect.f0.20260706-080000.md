**statement (plain)** — The gender aftereffect is a face adaptation phenomenon in which prolonged viewing of faces of one gender systematically biases the perceived gender of subsequently viewed faces in the opposite direction. After adapting to male faces, androgynous or ambiguous faces are more likely to be categorized as female, while adaptation to female faces biases perception toward male categorization. The effect demonstrates that gender perception operates via opponent coding mechanisms analogous to those in color and orientation perception, where neural populations selective for male and female facial characteristics mutually inhibit one another.
