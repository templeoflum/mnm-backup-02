**statement (plain)** — The Hyperboloid Illusion is a geometric depth phenomenon in which straight rods or lines arranged in a circular configuration with systematic rotation appear to form a curved hyperboloid surface rather than remaining in a flat plane or simple cylindrical arrangement. This illusion demonstrates how the visual system integrates local orientation information across space to construct global three-dimensional surface percepts. When viewing the display, observers perceive a smoothly curved saddle-shaped or hourglass-shaped surface despite the physical straightness of individual elements.
