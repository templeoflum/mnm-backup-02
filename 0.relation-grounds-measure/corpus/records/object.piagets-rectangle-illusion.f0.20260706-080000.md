**statement (plain)** — Piaget's Rectangle Illusion refers to geometric misperceptions studied by Swiss developmental psychologist Jean Piaget as part of his extensive investigations into the ontogeny of perceptual and cognitive abilities in children. Piaget examined how children of different ages perceive spatial relationships, proportions, and geometric properties, documenting systematic biases in size, length, and area judgments. The rectangle illusion specifically involves configurations where rectangles of equal area but different aspect ratios are judged as having different sizes, or where rectangles in different orientations or contextual arrangements produce biased perceptual comparisons.
