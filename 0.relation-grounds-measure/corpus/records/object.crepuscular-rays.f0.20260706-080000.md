**statement (plain)** — Crepuscular rays, also known as Jacob's Ladder or sunbeams, are visible shafts of sunlight that appear to radiate outward from the sun when it is partially obscured by clouds, mountains, or other obstacles, creating alternating light and shadow streaks across the sky. The name derives from the Latin word "crepusculum," meaning twilight, as these rays are most commonly observed during dawn and dusk when the sun is near the horizon and atmospheric scattering is enhanced. The rays appear to diverge from the sun's position, but this apparent divergence is purely a perspective illusion: the light rays are actually traveling in parallel paths from the distant sun, and their apparent divergence results from the same geometric perspective that makes parallel railroad tracks appear to converge toward the horizon.
