**statement (plain)** — **Janus** is the Roman god of doorways, beginnings, and transitions, depicted with two faces looking in opposite directions — one to the past, one to the future. He is one of the oldest and most distinctly Roman gods, native to Italy with no counterpart in the Greek pantheon. Because he presides over openings and beginnings, he is invoked first in any prayer or rite, before even **Jupiter**, and gives his name to the month of January (*Ianuarius*).

**statement (attestation)** — god of the *ianua* (door, gateway), of beginnings and endings, of transitions and of time. His two faces (*bifrons*) look to past and future; his attributes are the key and the staff. The doubled Gates of Janus (Ianus Geminus) in the Roman Forum stood open in war and shut in peace. He receives first mention in prayers and the first month of the year. As a native Italic god he has no Greek equivalent — the mark of his place in the distinctly Roman set. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested chiefly in Ovid's *Fasti* Book 1, where Janus answers the poet, and in later antiquarians such as Macrobius; this is documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted fact. His absence of a Greek counterpart and his priority in invocation are reported as tradition and are variously elaborated across the sources. **[confirmed as attribution]**
