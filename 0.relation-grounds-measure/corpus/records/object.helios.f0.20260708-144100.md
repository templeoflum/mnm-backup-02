**statement (plain)** — **Helios** (Greek *Ἥλιος*, "Sun") is the Titan sun-god, child of the Titans **Hyperion** and **Theia** and brother of **Selene** (Moon) and **Eos** (Dawn) (*Theogony* 371–374). Each day he drives his blazing **sun-chariot**, drawn by fiery horses, across the sky from east to west, and because he looks down on everything he is invoked as the god who **sees all things** — it is Helios who tells Demeter of Persephone's abduction. He is father of **Phaethon**, who begs to drive the chariot and, losing control, scorches the earth until Zeus strikes him down, and father of the sorceress **Circe** and of **Aeetes** of Colchis. In the **Odyssey** (book 12) his sacred **cattle** graze on the island of Thrinacia; Odysseus' starving men slaughter them, and for this sacrilege all are destroyed at sea. In later tradition Helios is increasingly identified with **Apollo**.

**statement (attestation)** — domain: the sun, its daily course, all-seeing sight, and oaths sworn by the light. Genealogy: son of Hyperion and Theia; brother of Selene and Eos; father of Phaethon, Circe, Aeetes, and (in some accounts) the Heliades and Pasiphae. Attributes: the four-horse chariot, the radiate crown. Etymology: Greek *Hēlios*, "sun." The Roman *interpretatio* identified him with **Sol**. The later identification with Apollo (Phoebus) is a development of the classical and post-classical period, not original to the earliest sources. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and Homer's Odyssey [[source.homer]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The daily chariot-course and the cattle episode are attested; the Phaethon story and the Apollo identification are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
