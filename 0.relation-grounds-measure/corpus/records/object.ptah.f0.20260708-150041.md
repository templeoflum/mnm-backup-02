**statement (plain)** — **Ptah** is the creator god of **Memphis** and the patron of craftsmen and artisans. In the **Memphite Theology** he creates the world through the thought of his **heart** and the word of his **tongue** — conceiving each thing in his mind and bringing it into being by speaking it. He is depicted mummiform, holding a **composite staff** combining the *was*, *ankh*, and *djed* signs. His consort is the lioness goddess **Sekhmet**, and his son is **Nefertem**.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: creation, craftsmen, and artisans. Chief cult centre: **Memphis**. Iconography: a mummiform figure with a straight beard, holding a composite staff (*was* / *ankh* / *djed*). Cosmology: in the **Memphite Theology** he creates through the heart (thought) and the tongue (word). Family: husband of **Sekhmet**, father of **Nefertem**. The Greeks by *interpretatio graeca* equated him with **Hephaestus**. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Egyptian mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested from the Pyramid Texts [[source.pyramid-texts]] onward and present in the Book of the Dead [[source.book-of-the-dead]]; the standard reference treatment is Wilkinson [[source.wilkinson-2003]]. The Memphis cult and the Hephaestus *interpretatio graeca* are established; the Memphite Theology's creation-by-heart-and-tongue and his family relations are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
