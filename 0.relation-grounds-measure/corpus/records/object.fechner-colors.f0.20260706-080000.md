**statement (plain)** — Fechner colors are subjective chromatic percepts that arise when viewing rotating black-and-white patterns at specific angular velocities, typically between 5 and 10 revolutions per second. Named after Gustav Fechner, who first systematically described the phenomenon in 1838, these illusory colors appear despite the complete absence of chromatic information in the stimulus. The colors perceived vary with rotation speed, pattern configuration, and individual observer characteristics, often manifesting as concentric rings or radial bands of pastel hues including reds, greens, blues, and yellows.
