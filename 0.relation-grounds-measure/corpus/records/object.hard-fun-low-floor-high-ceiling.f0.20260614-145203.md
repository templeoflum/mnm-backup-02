**statement (plain)** — two linked design rules of thumb. "Hard fun": children enjoy challenging work *because* it is hard, not in spite of it — the goal is the right kind of difficulty, not ease. "Low floor, high ceiling": a good tool lets a complete beginner do something meaningful right away (low floor) while still rewarding years of increasingly ambitious work (high ceiling). Mitchel Resnick later added "wide walls": there should be many different paths from floor to ceiling, so learners with different interests can all find their own project.

**statement** — *hard fun* names the engagement learners report when work is fun *because* it is demanding and connected to their interests. *low floor / high ceiling* is the design criterion that an environment (a [[object.microworld]], a programming language) be easy to enter yet open-ended in depth; Resnick's *wide walls* adds breadth of possible projects as a third axis. Together they articulate what makes a constructionist environment work. These are design heuristics, not formal results. **[confirmed]**

**basis** — both express what a [[object.constructionism]] environment must offer: the low floor lets the learner begin building immediately; the high ceiling means the same world still holds genuine ideas worth years of effort; hard fun is the affective signature of a learner inside such a world doing something that matters to them. Logo is the worked example — start by drawing a square, end at recursive fractals. Established practice in educational-technology design. **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — "hard fun": Papert credits the phrase to a first-grader at the Gardner Academy, San Jose, California (mid-1980s) who described Logo work as "It's fun. It's hard. It's Logo"; Papert took up the term thereafter [[source.papert-hard-fun]]. "low floor / high ceiling": attributed to Papert (from the Logo design work of the 1970s); his student and MIT colleague Mitchel Resnick credits Papert with the pair and adds "wide walls" [[source.resnick-2016]]. The core engagement design is already present in *Mindstorms* (1980) [[source.papert-1980]]. **[confirmed as attribution]** *(the first-grader provenance for "hard fun" rests on Papert's own retelling, archived at The Daily Papert; the "low floor / high ceiling" pair is a community-standard attribution to Papert rather than a single coined-in-print quotation.)*
