**statement (plain)** — stigmergic traces come in two kinds by how they relate to the work. **Sematectonic**: the coordinating stimulus *is* the work product or its physical state — the height of a termite pillar, a worn footpath, a partly-built wall, tension in a rope. **Sign/marker-based**: agents deposit a dedicated signal that is *not* part of the work — ant pheromone, a kanban card, a flag, a status field. The difference matters because it tells you whether coordination can ride the medium itself or needs a separate channel.

**statement (framework)** — Wilson (1975) named the sematectonic variant (Greek *sēma* "sign" + *tektōn* "builder") against sign/cue/marker-based stigmergy. This axis is *orthogonal* to Heylighen's quantitative-vs-qualitative axis [[object.quantitative-vs-qualitative-stigmergy]]: a case is one of each (e.g. pheromone is quantitative-marker-based; a wasp's staged funnel is qualitative-sematectonic).

**basis** — introduced in [[source.wilson-1975]], standard in the stigmergy literature. *Caution* — real systems blend both: Grassé's termites turned out partly pheromone-driven (marker), so the textbook "pure sematectonic" example is mixed [[source.facchini-2024]]; and some linguists dislike the coinage "sematectonic" while accepting the distinction it marks. **[confirmed]**
