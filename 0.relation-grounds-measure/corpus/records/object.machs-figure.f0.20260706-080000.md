**statement (plain)** — Mach's Figure, created by physicist and philosopher Ernst Mach, is a classic depth-ambiguous stimulus depicting a book-like or folded-paper configuration that spontaneously reverses between two three-dimensional interpretations: appearing either as opening toward the observer or opening away from the observer. The figure consists of two rectangular surfaces meeting at a central fold or hinge, rendered with shading gradients that are consistent with both interpretations. This ambiguity arises because the same pattern of light and shadow can result from either a convex fold (mountain fold) with surfaces angling toward the viewer or a concave fold (valley fold) with surfaces angling away.

**statement (framework)** — category: ambiguous bistable multistable; perceptual mechanism(s): depth-cue-conflict, bistable-competition.
