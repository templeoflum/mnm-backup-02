**statement (plain)** — The face distortion effect, also known as flashed face distortion, is a striking phenomenon in which sequentially presented faces, when aligned at the eyes and presented in peripheral vision at rapid rates, appear grotesquely distorted with exaggerated or monstrous features. Normal faces that appear unremarkable when viewed individually or in central vision become perceptually warped when viewed under these specific presentation conditions. The effect requires peripheral viewing, alignment of facial features (particularly the eyes), and rapid alternation between different face images, typically at rates of 3-5 faces per second.
