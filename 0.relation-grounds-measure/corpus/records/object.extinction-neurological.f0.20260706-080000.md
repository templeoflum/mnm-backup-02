**statement (plain)** — Neurological extinction is a phenomenon typically observed following unilateral brain damage, most commonly to the right parietal cortex, in which a patient fails to perceive a contralesional stimulus when a competing ipsilesional stimulus is presented simultaneously, despite being able to detect the contralesional stimulus when it is presented in isolation. This selective failure of awareness under bilateral stimulation reveals a competitive attentional mechanism in which the lesioned hemisphere is unable to adequately represent stimuli contralateral to the damage when faced with interhemispheric competition. Extinction can occur in any sensory modality, including visual, tactile, and auditory domains, and can even be demonstrated for stimuli that are physically identical in their sensory properties but differ in their spatial location.
