**statement (plain)** — Binocular lustre is a perceptual phenomenon in which presenting different luminance levels to the two eyes produces the appearance of a lustrous, shiny, or metallic surface quality that cannot be achieved through monocular viewing alone. When one eye views a lighter region while the other eye simultaneously views a darker region of the same spatial location, observers report a distinctive glossy or luminous surface appearance rather than simple binocular rivalry or fusion to an intermediate brightness. This effect demonstrates that the visual system uses interocular luminance differences as a cue to surface properties, particularly specularity and reflectance characteristics.
