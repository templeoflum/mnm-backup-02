**origin** — the **Lebor Gabála Érenn** ("Book of the Taking of Ireland") — the medieval Irish pseudo-history of the successive settlings of Ireland, including the coming of the **Tuatha Dé Danann**, compiled in the 11th century from older material — the framing source for the Irish mythological cycle and its pantheon. [[source.lebor-gabala]] **[confirmed]**

**codification** — the **Ulster Cycle** and the **Four Branches of the Mabinogi** — the Old/Middle Irish heroic tales centered on **Cú Chulainn** and the **Táin Bó Cúailnge** (preserved in the Book of the Dun Cow, c. 1100), and the medieval Welsh prose tales of the Mabinogi (Red Book of Hergest / White Book of Rhydderch, c. 12th–13th c.) — the two great narrative bodies of the tradition. [[source.ulster-cycle]] [[source.mabinogi]] **[confirmed]**

**door** — James MacKillop, *Dictionary of Celtic Mythology* (Oxford) — the standard modern reference (with Miranda Green, *Dictionary of Celtic Myth and Legend*, for the wider material) — named as the door; no source-record cut yet (settle-pass owes a `source.mackillop`).

*grade s — origin (Lebor Gabála Érenn), codification (Ulster Cycle + Mabinogi), door (MacKillop) present. A single-tradition field under [[field.comparative-mythology]] (the cross-tradition study above it). Field-home for the Celtic pantheon/hero figure intake (20260708); homes the Irish gods (Dagda, Lugh, Morrígan, Danu), the Ulster hero Cú Chulainn, and the Fenian hero Fionn.*
