**statement (plain)** — The Wundt illusion, first described by pioneering psychologist Wilhelm Wundt in 1898, is a geometric distortion illusion in which two parallel horizontal lines appear to bow inward (become concave) when superimposed on a background of converging radial lines. The illusion is the inverse of the Hering illusion, in which parallel lines appear to bow outward against a background of diverging lines. In the Wundt configuration, the radial lines typically converge toward a central point beyond the parallel test lines, creating a visual context that systematically distorts perceived straightness.
