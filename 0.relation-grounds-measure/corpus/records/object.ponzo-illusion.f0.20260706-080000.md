**statement (plain)** — The Ponzo illusion is a classic size-contrast illusion in which two identical horizontal lines placed between converging lines appear to be different lengths, with the upper line appearing substantially longer than the lower line. First reported by Italian psychologist Mario Ponzo in 1911, the illusion is typically attributed to the misapplication of linear perspective depth cues. The converging lines mimic the appearance of parallel lines receding into the distance, such as railroad tracks or a road vanishing toward the horizon.

**statement (framework)** — category: geometric size length; perceptual mechanism(s): misapplied-size-constancy, depth-cue-conflict.
