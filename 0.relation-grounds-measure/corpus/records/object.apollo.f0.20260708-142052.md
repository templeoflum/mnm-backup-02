**statement (plain)** — **Apollo** (Greek *Ἀπόλλων*) is the god of prophecy, music, healing, and archery, and — chiefly in later tradition — of light and the sun. He is the son of **Zeus** and the Titaness **Leto**, and the twin brother of **Artemis**, born on the island of **Delos** after Hera's persecution of Leto. He is lord of the great oracle at **Delphi**, which he wins by slaying the serpent **Python**; from there his Pythia delivers prophecy. He leads the Muses in music and song (*Mousagetēs*), sends and averts plague with his silver bow — as in the opening of the *Iliad*, where his arrows fall on the Greek camp — and is a god of healing, father of **Asclepius**.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: prophecy and oracles, music and poetry, archery, healing and plague, purification, and (increasingly from the classical period) the light of the sun. Key attributes: the silver **bow**, the **lyre** (or kithara), the **laurel**, the tripod, and the swan; oracular seats **Delphi** and **Delos**; epithets *Phoebus* ("bright/pure"), *Loxias*, *Pythios*, and *Musagetēs*. His identification with **Helios**, the sun, is largely a later, post-Homeric development. The Romans took him over under his own name, **Apollo**. The etymology of the name is disputed and remains unresolved. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Homer's epics and the Homeric Hymns [[source.homer]] and in Hesiod [[source.hesiod-theogony]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The etymology of the name and his origins (Anatolian, Dorian, and other hypotheses) are debated; the sun-god identification is a later development. The genealogies and narratives are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
