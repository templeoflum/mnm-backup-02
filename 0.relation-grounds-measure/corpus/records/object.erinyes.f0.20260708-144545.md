**statement (plain)** — the **Erinyes** (Greek *Ἐρινύες*; the **Furies**) are goddesses of vengeance for crimes against kin — bloodguilt, oath-breaking, and offenses against parents. In Hesiod they are born when the blood of the castrated **Ouranos** falls upon **Gaia** (*Theogony* 183–185). Tradition names three — **Alecto**, **Megaera**, and **Tisiphone** — and calls them euphemistically the **Eumenides** ("Kindly Ones"), a placating name for powers too dreadful to name plainly.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: vengeance and the pursuit of bloodguilt, oath-breaking, and crimes against parents and kin. Roles: relentless avengers who hound the guilty, especially the shedders of kindred blood. Etymology: Greek *Erinýes*, of disputed derivation; their three names are **Alēktṓ** ("unceasing"), **Mégaira** ("grudging"), and **Tīsiphónē** ("avenger of murder"), and they are euphemistically the **Eumenides**. The Roman *interpretatio* identified them with the **Furiae** / **Dirae**. Their office is inescapable pursuit of the polluted. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The birth from the blood of Ouranos and their office against bloodguilt are attested; the fixed count of three, the three names, and the Eumenides euphemism are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
