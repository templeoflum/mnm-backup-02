**statement (plain)** — Hybrid images are visual stimuli that combine high and low spatial frequency information from two different source images to create a single composite image whose perceived content changes systematically with viewing distance. The technique involves extracting high spatial frequencies from one image and low spatial frequencies from another, then superimposing them. When viewed up close, the high-frequency details dominate perception and observers see one image.

**statement (framework)** — category: miscellaneous specialized; perceptual mechanism(s): spatial-filtering, multi-scale-processing.
