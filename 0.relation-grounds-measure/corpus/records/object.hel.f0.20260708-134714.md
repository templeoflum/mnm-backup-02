**statement (plain)** — **Hel** (Old Norse *Hel*) is the being who rules the underworld realm that bears the same name, the daughter of **Loki** and the giantess **Angrboða** and sister of the wolf **Fenrir** and the serpent **Jörmungandr**. Snorri describes her in *Gylfaginning* as half one color and half dark or livid, and says **Odin** cast her down into the lower world and gave her authority over those who die of sickness and old age, who come to her hall. In the myth of **Baldr**'s death, when the gods send to ransom him from her, Hel answers that she will release him only if all things in the world, living and dead, weep for him — and one giantess refuses, so he stays.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: rule over the realm of the dead who die of sickness and old age. Key relations: daughter of **Loki** and **Angrboða**; sibling of **Fenrir** and **Jörmungandr**; the keeper who names the weeping-condition in the **Baldr** myth. Snorri's *Gylfaginning* supplies the parentage, the half-dark appearance, and Odin's casting her down; the realm **Hel** and its road appear in the Poetic Edda in *Völuspá* and *Baldrs draumar*. The name denotes both the place and its ruler, and is cognate with the concealing sense of the root "to hide." **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Norse mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested across the Poetic Edda [[source.poetic-edda]] and systematized in Snorri's Prose Edda [[source.prose-edda]]; the standard reference treatment is Lindow [[source.lindow-2001]]. The parentage, the two-colored appearance, and the Baldr-release condition come chiefly from Snorri's *Gylfaginning* and should be read as attested tradition through his 13th-c. Christian framing, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
