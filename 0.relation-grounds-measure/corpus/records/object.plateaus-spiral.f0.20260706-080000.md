**statement (plain)** — Plateau's spiral, discovered by Belgian physicist Joseph Plateau in 1850, is a motion aftereffect phenomenon in which adaptation to a rotating spiral induces a compelling illusion of expansion or contraction in subsequently viewed stationary objects. When a spiral pattern rotates clockwise (appearing to contract toward the center), adaptation causes stationary patterns to appear to expand outward; counterclockwise rotation (apparent expansion) produces aftereffects of contraction. The phenomenon is a specific instance of the motion aftereffect, but unlike simple translational motion aftereffects, the spiral aftereffect involves radial or rotational motion components.
