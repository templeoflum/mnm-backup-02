**statement (plain)** - Aristotle explains nature by analogy to craft: a natural thing comes to be as if a craftsman were making it, aiming at an end. "If a house had been a thing made by nature, it would have been made in the same way as it is now by art; and if things made by nature were made also by art, they would come to be in the same way as by nature" (*Physics* II.8). Art "in some cases completes what nature cannot bring to a finish, and in others imitates nature."

**basis** - the craft-analogy language (nature "crafting," *demiourgein*) is pervasive in Aristotle's biology, physics, and metaphysics; tied to teleology ("nature does nothing in vain," *he phusis ouden poiei maten*). Rests on the four-cause scheme, especially the final cause.
