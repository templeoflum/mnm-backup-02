**statement (plain)** — Mirages are atmospheric optical phenomena in which displaced, distorted, or inverted images of distant objects appear due to refraction of light through vertical gradients in atmospheric temperature and thus refractive index. Inferior mirages, the most common type, occur when the ground surface is significantly hotter than the overlying air, causing light rays to curve upward and creating inverted images appearing below the true object position, often interpreted as water reflections on hot roads or desert sand. Superior mirages occur with temperature inversions where warmer air overlies cooler air, causing downward ray curvature and producing upright or inverted images appearing above the true object position, sometimes allowing observation of objects beyond the geometric horizon.
