**statement (plain)** — What makes a property of a mathematical (or physical) object *structural* rather than an accident of a particular presentation? Two natural criteria: (a) **definability** — it can be defined purely from the primitive relations of the theory; (b) **invariance** — it is preserved under isomorphism, i.e., it holds of an object iff it holds of every structurally identical copy. Korbmacher & Schiemer show the two criteria do not pick out the same class of properties, so "structural property" is not a single well-defined notion.

**relevance** — sharpens what structuralist and ontic-structural-realist claims about "structure" actually assert (the field's core term needs this precision). Connects to the **Carnap thesis** (structural ⇔ isomorphism-invariant extension) and the **Tarski–Sher thesis** (logical ⇔ invariant under all permutations of the domain), so logicality appears as a limit case of structurality [[source.korbmacher-schiemer-2018]] **[confirmed]**.
