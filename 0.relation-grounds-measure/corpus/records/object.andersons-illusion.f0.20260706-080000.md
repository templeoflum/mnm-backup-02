**statement (plain)** — Anderson's Illusion is a lightness illusion that demonstrates the critical role of perceived transparency in determining surface lightness. In this illusion, two regions of identical luminance appear dramatically different in their perceived lightness when one is interpreted as being viewed through a transparent filter or overlay while the other is perceived as an opaque surface. The effect reveals how the visual system performs sophisticated computational inferences about scene geometry and material properties when determining surface reflectance.
