**statement (plain)** — reliability on a dangerous, fast-moving deck is not in any one head; it lives in the *interrelating*. People act "heedfully" — carrying out their own task while attending to how it fits what everyone else is doing and to the imagined whole of the joint activity. When that mutual attentiveness is high, the group behaves as if it had a single mind, and errors fall; when it degrades, the group fragments and accidents rise.

**statement (framework)** — Weick & Roberts (1993): "collective mind" as a *pattern of heedful interrelations of actions in a social system"; "as heedful interrelating and mindful comprehension increase, organizational errors decrease." A near neighbour of collective mindfulness [[object.high-reliability-organization]] and the counter-case to the collapse of sensemaking [[object.collapse-of-sensemaking]].

**basis** — theorized from the Berkeley carrier fieldwork [[source.weick-roberts-1993]]. *Support* — widely used in safety, crew resource management, and teamwork research. *Limits* — "mind" is a metaphor for an interaction pattern, not a literal group cognition; measurement is qualitative. **[confirmed]**
