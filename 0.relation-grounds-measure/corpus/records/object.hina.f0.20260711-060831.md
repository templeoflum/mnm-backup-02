**statement (plain)** — In Hawaiian and wider Polynesian tradition **Hina** (**Hina-i-ka-malama** in Hawai'i, **Sina** in Sāmoa) is the great goddess of the moon, found throughout the islands and associated with women's arts, the making of **tapa** (bark cloth), and the tides. In various traditions she is the mother, grandmother, or companion of the trickster-hero **Māui**. Wearied of earthly labor, she is said to have climbed to the moon, where she can still be seen beating her tapa.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: moon, tapa-making, womanhood, tides. Kin to **Māui** (mother, grandmother, or companion, by tradition). Aliases: **Hina-i-ka-malama** (Hawaiian), **Sina** (Samoan). Pan-Polynesian (Hawaiian / Māori / Samoan); here recorded in her Hawaiian and pan-Polynesian form. Hawaiian tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — attested in Beckwith, *Hawaiian Mythology* (1940) [[source.beckwith-1940]]; documented tradition (`status: doctrinal`). These myths come through Hawaiian and wider Polynesian oral tradition collected and recorded in the 19th–20th c.; in some traditions Hina's relation to Māui and her ascent to the moon vary across the islands. **[confirmed as attribution]**
