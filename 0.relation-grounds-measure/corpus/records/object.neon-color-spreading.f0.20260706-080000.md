**statement (plain)** — Neon color spreading is a striking chromatic completion phenomenon first systematically described by Dario Varin in 1971, in which illusory color appears to spread from colored line segments into adjacent achromatic regions, creating a translucent, neon-like wash of color. The classic configuration consists of a grid of black lines interrupted by colored segments at their intersections; the colored segments appear to induce a diffuse colored surface that extends across the enclosed spaces while maintaining perceptual transparency. The effect is strongest when the colored segments are collinear with implied contours and when the background and enclosed regions share similar luminance.

**statement (framework)** — category: color; perceptual mechanism(s): surface-completion, figure-ground-segregation, lateral-inhibition.
