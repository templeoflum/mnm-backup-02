**statement (plain)** — every stigmergic trace does two jobs at once: it *records* what has already happened and it *cues* what should happen next. Because the state of the work lives in the environment rather than in any agent's head, a swarm — or a single worker — can tackle a project too large to hold internally: the medium carries the memory and the plan. Heylighen frames individual cognition itself as an "interiorization" of this external stigmergy.

**statement (framework)** — connects stigmergy to distributed cognition and the extended mind [[object.extended-mind-thesis]]: Marsh & Onof (2008) [[source.marsh-onof-2008]] propose "social stigmergy as the extra-cranial analog of an artificial neural network." The cleanest non-neural demonstration is slime-mold externalized memory [[object.slime-mold-externalized-memory]].

**basis** — Heylighen's universal-coordination account [[source.heylighen-2016]]; the epistemic bridge in [[source.marsh-onof-2008]]. *Limits* — the extended-mind reading is philosophically contested (Adams & Aizawa's coupling-constitution objection [[object.parity-principle]]): causal coupling to an external store need not make it *part of* cognition. A strong interpretive frame, not settled fact. **[confirmed]**
