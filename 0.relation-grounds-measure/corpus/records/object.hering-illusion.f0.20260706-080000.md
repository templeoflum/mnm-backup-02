**statement (plain)** — The Hering Illusion is a classical geometric distortion illusion in which two parallel straight lines appear to bow outward when superimposed on a background of radiating lines emanating from a central point. Discovered by German physiologist Ewald Hering in 1861, this illusion demonstrates how contextual orientation information can systematically distort the perception of straightness and parallelism. The radiating background lines create a strong impression of depth or perspective, resembling the visual pattern produced when looking down a corridor or tunnel.

**statement (framework)** — category: geometric size length; perceptual mechanism(s): orientation-selectivity, spatial-filtering, contour-integration.
