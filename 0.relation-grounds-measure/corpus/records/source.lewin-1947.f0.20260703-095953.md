Kurt Lewin, "Frontiers in Group Dynamics II: Channels of Group Life; Social Planning and Action Research," *Human Relations* 1, no. 2 (1947): 143–153. Field-theory synthesis collected in *Field Theory in Social Science* (Harper, 1951).

Contains — the origin of **channel theory** (behavior flows through channels with **gatekeeper**-controlled sections — Lewin's food-to-table example), and of **force-field analysis**: behavior sits at a quasi-stationary equilibrium between *driving* and *restraining* forces, and durable change comes preferentially from *reducing restraining forces* rather than increasing driving ones — the negative-work principle. The foundational text linking physical-flow language to human and group behavior.
