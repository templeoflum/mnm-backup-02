Nicholas P. Spanos, Wendy P. Cross, Mark Lepage & Marjorie Coristine, "Glossolalia as learned behavior: An experimental demonstration," *Journal of Abnormal Psychology* 95, no. 1 (1986): 21–23.

Contains — the experimental case that glossolalia is an ordinary, learnable verbal skill, not necessarily a dissociative state: ~20% of naïve subjects produced fluent glossolalia on a baseline trial, and 70% did after two training sessions (taped samples, live modeling, instruction). "Our findings are more consistent with social learning than with altered state conceptions of glossolalia." The empirical anchor of the **state-vs-learned-skill** divergence (against Goodman/Newberg's trance/neural findings).
