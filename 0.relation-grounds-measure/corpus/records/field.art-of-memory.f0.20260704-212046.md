**origin** — the three Latin sources of the classical art: the anonymous *Rhetorica ad Herennium*, Cicero's *De oratore* (the Simonides invention story), and Quintilian — a technique of impressing "places" (*loci*) and "images" on memory, forming an internal architecture one moves through. [[source.yates-1966]] **[confirmed]**

**codification** — Frances A. Yates, *The Art of Memory* (1966; Warburg Institute tradition) — the field-defining monograph; medieval transmission via Albertus Magnus and Aquinas, who placed memory under the virtue of Prudence (the puzzle that drove Yates's reinterpretation of the art as more than mnemotechnics). [[source.yates-1966]] **[confirmed]**

**door** — Yates, *The Art of Memory* (1966); Rossi, *Logic and the Art of Memory* (2000). [[source.rossi-2000]]

**lineage** — Yates's thesis that Renaissance occult memory (Camillo's theatre, Bruno's *De umbris idearum*) was stimulated by the Hermetic tradition is her own framing, adjacent to the contested "Yates thesis" — carried as lineage, not asserted. The psychological memory literature is a separate lineage entirely ([[field.memory-research]]).
