**origin** — *Sefer Yetzirah* ("Book of Formation"), the earliest extant work of Jewish esoteric speculation (compiled between the 2nd and 6th centuries CE; dating contested) — creation through "32 paths of wisdom," the 10 *sefirot* plus the 22 Hebrew letters: a system constituted by *relations* among letters and numbers, not by units in isolation. [[source.sefer-yetzirah]] **[confirmed]**

**codification** — Gershom Scholem founded the modern academic study of Kabbalah (*Major Trends in Jewish Mysticism*, 1941; *Kabbalah*, 1974), reading the sefirot as a closed, mutually-defined emanative structure ("their end is in their beginning"); the *Sefer ha-Bahir* and the *Zohar* (13th c.) are the central texts. [[source.scholem-kabbalah-1974]] **[confirmed]**

**door** — Scholem, *Kabbalah* (1974); Moshe Idel, *Kabbalah: New Perspectives* (1988) for the revisionist reading. [[source.idel-1988]]

**lineage** — **Scholem vs. Idel**: theosophical-theurgical (Scholem) vs. ecstatic (Idel) as the primary character of Kabbalah — a live scholarly dispute, carried as lineage, not resolved.
