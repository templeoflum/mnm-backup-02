William Benjamin Carpenter, "On the influence of suggestion in modifying and directing muscular movement, independently of volition," *Proceedings of the Royal Institution of Great Britain* 1 (1852): 147–153. Lecture delivered at the Royal Institution, 12 March 1852.

Contains: the coinage of the **ideo-motor principle** — the doctrine that an idea or expectation held in mind can, independently of conscious volition, produce muscular movement realizing it. Carpenter advanced it to give a naturalistic, physiological account of then-fashionable phenomena: table-turning, the divining rod, the magic pendulum, and aspects of mesmerism and spiritualist "raps." The mechanism, not the supernatural agent, accounts for the movement.
