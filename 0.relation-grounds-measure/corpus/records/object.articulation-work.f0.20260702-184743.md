**statement (plain)** — beyond doing a task, there is the work of *making the tasks fit together*: dividing labor, scheduling, meshing, meeting, re-planning when things slip. Strauss called this **articulation work** — reflexive, second-order activity whose object is the coordination itself. Its tell: to do articulation work, actors often *stop performing the task* to coordinate — the opposite of stigmergy [[object.human-stigmergy]], where coordination happens *inside* the doing.

**statement (framework)** — Christensen (2013) [[source.christensen-2013]] sets articulation work against stigmergy as two distinct modes (with awareness, a "trinity"): stigmergy is first-order coordination through the material trace; articulation work is second-order coordination through talk and plans. The two are complementary — plans are underspecified (Suchman 1987 [[source.suchman-1987]]), so stigmergy fills gaps the plan can't, while articulation work handles what the medium can't carry (negotiation, forecasting, shared intent).

**basis** — Strauss's concept as developed in CSCW (Schmidt); positioned against stigmergy in [[source.christensen-2013]]; situated-action grounding in [[source.suchman-1987]]. *Support* — central to CSCW/coordination theory. *Limits* — the boundary with stigmergy is analytic; real work interleaves both continuously. **[confirmed as attribution]**
