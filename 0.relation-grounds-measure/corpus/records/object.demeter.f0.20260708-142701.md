**statement (plain)** — **Demeter** (Greek *Δημήτηρ*) is the Olympian goddess of grain, agriculture, and the harvest — the giver of the cultivated crops on which human life depends. She is a daughter of **Kronos** and **Rhea** and thus a sister of **Zeus**, by whom she is the mother of **Persephone**. The *Homeric Hymn to Demeter* tells the central myth: **Hades** abducts Persephone to be his queen in the underworld, and Demeter, grieving and searching over the earth, withholds all growth until the ground is barren; a settlement returns Persephone for part of the year but binds her to the underworld for the rest (having eaten the pomegranate seeds), and her descent and return account for the cycle of the **seasons**. In the same hymn Demeter establishes her worship at Eleusis, the origin of the **Eleusinian Mysteries**.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: grain, agriculture, the cultivated harvest, and the fertility of the tilled earth; the Mysteries of Eleusis. Key attributes: sheaves of grain and ears of wheat, the torch (carried in her nocturnal search), the poppy. Parentage: daughter of Kronos and Rhea, sister of Zeus; mother of Persephone. Her name is often glossed as "grain-mother" or "earth-mother" (*mētēr*, "mother"), but the first element is disputed. The Roman *interpretatio* identified her with **Ceres**. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and the Homeric Hymns [[source.homer]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The parentage and the Ceres *interpretatio* are well established; the etymology of the name is disputed, and the abduction-and-seasons myth is attested tradition and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
