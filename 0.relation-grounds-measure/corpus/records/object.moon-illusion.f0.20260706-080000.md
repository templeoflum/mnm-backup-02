**statement (plain)** — The Moon Illusion is one of the oldest documented perceptual phenomena, in which the moon appears substantially larger when positioned near the horizon compared to when it is elevated high in the sky, despite subtending essentially the same visual angle (approximately 0.5 degrees) in both positions. This dramatic size difference has been noted since antiquity, with references appearing in ancient Greek, Chinese, and Arabic texts. The illusion persists even when observers are aware of the objective equality of the moon's angular size, demonstrating its resistance to cognitive override.
