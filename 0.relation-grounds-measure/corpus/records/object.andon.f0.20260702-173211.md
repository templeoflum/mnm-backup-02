**statement (plain)** — andon (行灯, "paper lantern") is the shop-floor stop signal. Any worker who spots a defect, abnormality, or improvement opportunity pulls the andon cord or presses the button; a team leader comes immediately to "go-see," and if the problem is not resolved within the cycle time, the line stops. Authority to stop is pushed all the way to the front line, it is an *obligation* not merely a permission, and a good-faith stop is thanked, never punished.

**statement (mechanism)** — andon is the human expression of jidoka [[object.jidoka]]: the same "stop on abnormality" principle, with a person as the detector. It instantiates distributed stop-authority — decentralized, instant, obligatory, blame-free.

**basis** — the load-bearing empirical finding is that *the cord is trivial and the culture is everything*. **NUMMI** (the 1984 Toyota–GM joint venture in Fremont) is the standard evidence: roughly 85% of the same workforce GM had rated its worst reached Toyota-level defect rates within a year, because Toyota trained supervisors to answer a pull with help and kept explicit promises about what a stop would trigger — not because a cord was installed; GM's attempts to copy the cord alone failed [[source.liker-2004]]. The fear-vs-trust signature is quantitative: Toyota's Georgetown plant pulled the cord ~2,000×/week while Ford's Dearborn plant pulled ~2×/week (2007 BBC report via LeanBlog) — a near-silent cord is a symptom of fear, not quality. The general condition is genuine **psychological safety** (Edmondson 1999) plus visible management commitment; bolted onto a blame culture the signal goes silent. *(NUMMI worker testimony popularized on *This American Life* ep. 403, 2010; treat the journalism as illustration, the pattern as the finding.)* **[confirmed]**
