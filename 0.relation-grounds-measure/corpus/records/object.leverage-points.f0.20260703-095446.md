**statement (plain)** — Meadows argues that not all interventions in a system are equally powerful, and that people habitually push on the weakest ones. She ranks intervention points roughly from least to most effective: numbers/parameters (subsidies, taxes, standards); buffer sizes; stock-and-flow structure; delays; the strength of balancing feedback loops; the gain of reinforcing loops; information flows (who has access to what); the rules of the system; the power to self-organize; the goals of the system; the mindset or paradigm the system arises from; and the power to transcend paradigms. Counterintuitively, the high-leverage points (goals, paradigms) are the hardest to shift and the ones most resisted.

**relevance** — the general grammar of *where* to act on a flow system — the systems-thinking counterpart of "act at the bottleneck." Its own framework rather than a derivation from other objects [[source.meadows-2008]] **[confirmed]**.
