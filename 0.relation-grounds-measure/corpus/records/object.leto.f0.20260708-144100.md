**statement (plain)** — **Leto** (Greek *Λητώ*) is a Titaness, daughter of the Titans **Coeus** and **Phoebe** (*Theogony* 404–408). By **Zeus** she becomes the mother of the twin gods **Apollo** and **Artemis**. Pregnant, she is persecuted by the jealous **Hera**, who forbids any land under the sun to give her a place to give birth; Leto wanders in search of refuge until the barren, **floating island of Delos** — itself an outcast — agrees to receive her. There, clinging to a palm tree, she brings forth the twins, and Delos becomes fixed and sacred as the birthplace of Apollo.

**statement (attestation)** — domain: motherhood, and (as mother of the twin archers) the bearing of Apollo and Artemis. Genealogy: daughter of Coeus and Phoebe; sister of Asteria; consort of Zeus; mother of Apollo and Artemis. Etymology: Greek *Lētō*, of disputed derivation. The Roman *interpretatio* identified her with **Latona**. She also figures in the punishment of those who insult her — the destruction of **Niobe's** children by her twins and the transformation of the Lycian peasants into frogs. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and the Homeric Hymn to Apollo, systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The parentage and the birth on Delos are attested; the wandering, the persecution by Hera, and the punishments are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
