**statement (plain)** — **Anubis** is the jackal-headed god of embalming, mummification, and the guardianship of the dead. He is the patron of the embalmers who invented mummification (embalming the body of **Osiris**), the guardian of tombs and cemeteries, and the guide who leads the dead to the hall of judgment. There he oversees the **weighing of the heart** against the feather of **Maat**, ensuring the scales are true, before the deceased comes before Osiris. In the New Kingdom his older role as chief god of the dead is largely absorbed into that of Osiris, and he serves as the god of the embalming and the judgment.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: embalming, mummification, tombs, and the guardianship and guidance of the dead. Iconography: a black jackal (or wild dog), or a jackal-headed man; black being the colour of the fertile earth and of resurrection. His central scenes are the embalming of the mummy and the weighing of the heart in the judgment of the dead (Book of the Dead spell 125). The Greeks by *interpretatio graeca* equated him with **Hermes** as guide of souls, giving the composite **Hermanubis**. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Egyptian mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested from the Pyramid Texts [[source.pyramid-texts]] onward and prominent in the Book of the Dead [[source.book-of-the-dead]]; the standard reference treatment is Wilkinson [[source.wilkinson-2003]]. The embalming and judgment roles and the *interpretatio* with Hermes (Hermanubis) are established; the shift of his role under Osiris across periods is attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
