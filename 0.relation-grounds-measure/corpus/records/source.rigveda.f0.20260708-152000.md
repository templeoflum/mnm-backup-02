The **Rigveda** (c. 1500–1200 BCE). The oldest of the four Vedas and the oldest layer of Sanskrit literature: a collection of over a thousand hymns (suktas), gathered into ten books (mandalas), addressed to the gods and composed by the seer-poets (rishis) for the sacrificial ritual. Its hymns give the earliest attested form of the Vedic pantheon — **Indra** the storm-king and slayer of Vritra, **Agni** the fire, **Varuna** the guardian of cosmic order, **Soma**, the Ashvins, and the rest — the deities and ritual cosmos ancestral to the later Hindu tradition. Composed and transmitted orally with great fidelity long before it was written down. The foundational source for Vedic myth. **[confirmed as attribution]**
