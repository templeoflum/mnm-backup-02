Jukka Keränen, "The Identity Problem for Realist Structuralism," *Philosophia Mathematica* 9, no. 3 (2001): 308–330.

Contains — the founding statement of the identity objection: ante rem structuralism treats mathematical objects as nothing but their structural relations, yet in *non-rigid* structures (those with non-trivial automorphisms) distinct positions share all structural properties — e.g., the complex roots i and −i, or the two square roots — so the view seems committed to identifying patently distinct objects. Anticipated in John Burgess's 1999 review of Shapiro. The problem that drives the weak-discernibility literature.
