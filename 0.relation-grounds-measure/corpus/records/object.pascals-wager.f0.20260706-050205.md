**statement (plain)** — Pascal does not argue God exists; he computes payoffs. Believe and God exists: infinite gain. Disbelieve and God exists: infinite loss. If God doesn't exist, the difference is merely finite. Any nonzero probability times an infinite payoff is infinite expected value — so rationally, wager on God.

**statement (framework)** — the first application of **expected-value reasoning** to a practical decision, introducing dominance reasoning and reframing belief as choice under uncertainty. Objections: the many-gods problem, the mixed-strategy and infinite-utility problems, doxastic voluntarism, Pascal's Mugging. Recurs in existential-risk and AI-safety arguments. Established; its soundness stays **open**.
