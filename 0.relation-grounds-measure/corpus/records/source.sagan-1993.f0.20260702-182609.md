Scott D. Sagan, *The Limits of Safety: Organizations, Accidents, and Nuclear Weapons* (Princeton University Press, 1993).

Contains — the decisive empirical test of Normal Accident Theory [[object.normal-accident-theory]] against High-Reliability Theory, using the U.S. nuclear-weapons safety record (Cuban Missile Crisis near-misses, the 1968 Thule B-52 crash, NORAD false alarms). Sagan concludes the evidence tilts toward Perrow — "good fortune played a greater role than good design" — and that redundancy and tighter control sometimes *created* hazards. Sparked the 1994 NAT-vs-HRO symposium (La Porte, Rochlin, Perrow, Sagan) in the *Journal of Contingencies and Crisis Management*.
