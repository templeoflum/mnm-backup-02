**statement (plain)** — The spiral aftereffect is a motion adaptation phenomenon in which prolonged viewing of a rotating spiral pattern induces a vivid impression of opposite motion in subsequently viewed stationary patterns, causing them to appear to expand, contract, or rotate. When a spiral rotates to create an impression of inward motion (contraction), stationary test patterns subsequently appear to expand outward; conversely, adaptation to outward spiral motion produces aftereffects of contraction. The phenomenon is closely related to Plateau's spiral and represents a specific instance of the broader class of motion aftereffects.

**statement (framework)** — category: aftereffect adaptation; perceptual mechanism(s): neural-adaptation, motion-energy-detection.
