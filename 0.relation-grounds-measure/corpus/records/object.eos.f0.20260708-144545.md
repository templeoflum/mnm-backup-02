**statement (plain)** — **Eos** (Greek *Ἠώς*, "Dawn") is the goddess of the dawn: in Hesiod she is a Titan, daughter of **Hyperion** and **Theia**, and sister of **Helios** (the Sun) and **Selene** (the Moon) (*Theogony* 371–374). Each morning she rises before the sun, and Homer names her with the standing epithet "rosy-fingered" (*rhododaktylos*). Among her mortal loves is **Tithonus**, for whom she wins immortality from Zeus but forgets to ask for eternal youth, so that he withers away endlessly into old age.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: the dawn and the daily breaking of light before sunrise. Roles: Titan sister of Helios and Selene, herald of the day, and a goddess given to carrying off mortal youths as lovers. Etymology: Greek *Ēṓs* / *Ēōs*, "dawn," cognate with Latin *aurora* and Sanskrit *Uṣas*; the Roman *interpretatio* identified her with **Aurora**. Her defining epithet in Homer is "rosy-fingered." **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Greek mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Hesiod's Theogony [[source.hesiod-theogony]] and Homer's epics [[source.homer]], systematized in Apollodorus' Bibliotheca [[source.apollodorus-bibliotheca]]; the standard reference treatment is Hard [[source.hard-2004]]. The Titan parentage, the "rosy-fingered" epithet, and the Aurora *interpretatio* are attested; the Tithonus tale and other genealogies are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
