**statement (plain)** — The missing square puzzle is a geometric dissection paradox in which a square or rectangular figure is divided into several polygonal pieces that are then rearranged to form an apparently identical shape with a different total area. The most common version involves a triangle divided into four pieces that can be reconfigured into a similar-looking triangle with an additional unit square of area mysteriously appearing or disappearing in the rearrangement. The resolution of the paradox lies in the fact that the hypotenuse of the reassembled triangle is not actually a straight line but consists of two line segments with slightly different slopes, creating a very thin parallelogram-shaped gap or overlap that accounts for the missing or extra area.
