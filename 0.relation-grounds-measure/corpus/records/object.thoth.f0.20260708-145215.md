**statement (plain)** — **Thoth** is the ibis-headed god of writing, wisdom, magic, the moon, and reckoning. He is the scribe of the gods and the inventor of writing, the keeper of knowledge and the master of magic and calculation who measures time and the seasons. In the judgment of the dead he stands by the scales and records the verdict of the **weighing of the heart**. He arbitrates disputes among the gods, and heals and restores — as in the healing of the **Eye of Horus**. His chief cult was at **Hermopolis**.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: writing, wisdom, knowledge, magic, the moon, and reckoning (time, mathematics, measurement). Chief cult centre: **Hermopolis** (Egyptian *Khemenu*). Iconography: an ibis-headed man, often with scribe's palette and reed, or a baboon; both the ibis and the baboon are sacred to him; associated with the moon and the lunar crescent. His scribal role in the judgment of the dead is central to the funerary texts. The Greeks by *interpretatio graeca* equated him with **Hermes** (whence *Hermes Trismegistus* of the later Hermetic tradition). **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Egyptian mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested from the Pyramid Texts [[source.pyramid-texts]] onward and prominent in the Book of the Dead [[source.book-of-the-dead]]; the standard reference treatment is Wilkinson [[source.wilkinson-2003]]. The Hermopolis cult and the *interpretatio* with Hermes are established; the myths of his scribal and lunar roles are attested tradition, variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
