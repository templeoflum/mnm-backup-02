Donald O. Hebb, *The Organization of Behavior: A Neuropsychological Theory* (Wiley, 1949).

Contains — the **Hebbian postulate**: "When an axon of cell A is near enough to excite a cell B and repeatedly or persistently takes part in firing it, some growth process or metabolic change takes place in one or both cells such that A's efficiency, as one of the cells firing B, is increased." The founding proposal of use-dependent synaptic strengthening — popularly flattened to "neurons that fire together wire together" (a phrase due to Carla Shatz), though Hebb's wording implies *causal ordering*, not mere co-activation.
