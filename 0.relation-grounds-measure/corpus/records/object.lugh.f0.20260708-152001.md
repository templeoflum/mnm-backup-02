**statement (plain)** — **Lugh** (Old Irish *Lug*, epithet *Samildánach*, "skilled in many arts together" / "master of all arts") is an Irish god of the **Tuatha Dé Danann**: a youthful warrior-god of skill and craft who wins entry to the court of **Nuada** by being master of every art at once. He wields a great **spear** (one of the four treasures of the Tuatha Dé) and leads the Tuatha Dé Danann to victory over the **Fomorians** at the **Second Battle of Mag Tuired**, where he slays his own grandfather, the Fomorian **Balor** of the baleful eye. In the Ulster Cycle he is the divine father of the hero **Cú Chulainn**.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: skill and craft in all arts, warriorhood, oaths, kingship, light (traditionally). Key attributes: the great **spear**; the epithets *Samildánach* ("master of all arts") and *Lámfada* ("of the long arm"). Associations: son of **Cian** (Tuatha Dé) and **Ethniu** (daughter of the Fomorian Balor); father of **Cú Chulainn**. Text: **Cath Maige Tuired** (the Second Battle of Mag Tuired) and the **Lebor Gabála Érenn**. Etymology: from a proposed Proto-Celtic **\*Lugus**; a Welsh counterpart **Lleu Llaw Gyffes** of the Mabinogi is often proposed but debated **[uncertain — the Lugh/Lleu equation is a scholarly proposal, not textbook-certain]**. Irish tradition. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Irish mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in Cath Maige Tuired and the Lebor Gabála Érenn [[source.lebor-gabala]]; the standard reference treatment is MacKillop, *Dictionary of Celtic Mythology*. The many-skills epithet, the spear, and the slaying of Balor are attested tradition; the Welsh Lleu equation and the "light-god" reading are scholarly proposals and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
