**statement (plain)** — **Enlil** is the god of wind, air, and storms and the executive king of the gods — the active ruler who carries out the decisions of the divine assembly while **Anu** remains its remote head. He holds the **Tablet of Destinies**, the emblem of supreme authority that fixes the fates. In the flood tradition it is Enlil who, angered by the noise of humankind, sends the great Flood to destroy them. As chief god of the Sumerian pantheon his word both raises and destroys.

**statement (attestation)** — domains: wind, air, storm, kingship, the fixing of destinies. Culture: Sumerian and Akkadian **Enlil**. Chief cult centre: the temple **Ekur** at **Nippur**, the religious heart of Sumer. He holds the **Tablet of Destinies**; he is the god who sends the Flood in the Mesopotamian flood tradition (the Atrahasis story, echoed in Gilgamesh Tablet XI). In the myths he acts as the effective executive of the divine assembly. **[confirmed as attribution]**

**basis** — a figure of Mesopotamian mythic tradition, documented as *held* (`status: doctrinal`), not asserted. Attested in the flood narrative of the Epic of Gilgamesh [[source.epic-of-gilgamesh]] and treated in the standard reference by Black & Green [[source.black-green-1992]]. His Nippur cult, the Tablet of Destinies, and his role as executive king are established; the flood tradition is attested and variable across sources, and should be read as such, not as neutral report. **[confirmed as attribution]**
