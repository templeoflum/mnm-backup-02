Full citation — J. L. Doob, *Stochastic Processes* (New York: John Wiley & Sons, 1953).

Role — the founding systematic treatment of **martingale theory**: Doob developed martingales (a name and partial notion introduced by Ville, 1939, after Lévy's 1934 inequalities) into a full theory, proving the **martingale convergence theorem** (an L¹-bounded martingale converges almost surely) and the **optional stopping / optional sampling theorem**, and giving the Doob decomposition and maximal inequalities. The book also presents conditional expectation given a σ-algebra (Radon–Nikodym definition) as the foundation.

Access — standard reference; widely held; reissued (Wiley Classics). Attribution corroborated by Wikipedia ("Martingale (probability theory)", "Doob's martingale convergence theorems") and standard texts (Williams, *Probability with Martingales*). **[confirmed]**

Lineage — "martingale" as a probabilistic concept: Lévy (1934, inequalities), named and extended by J. Ville (*Étude critique de la notion de collectif*, 1939); theory developed by Doob (1953).
