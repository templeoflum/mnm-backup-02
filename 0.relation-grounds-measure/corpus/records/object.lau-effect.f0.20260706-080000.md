**statement (plain)** — The Lau Effect is a visual phenomenon discovered by Ernst Lau in 1948, characterized by the appearance of visible fringes or patterns when two incoherent gratings are superimposed. Unlike traditional moiré patterns that arise from geometric interference of periodic structures, the Lau Effect emerges from the interaction of spatially incoherent illumination with periodic structures, producing intensity modulations that the visual system interprets as distinct patterns. The effect demonstrates the visual system's sensitivity to second-order spatial correlations and statistical regularities in the visual field, even when first-order luminance information appears uniform or random.
