**statement (plain)** — The Duck-Rabbit Illusion is one of the most iconic and widely recognized examples of bistable perception, in which a single ambiguous line drawing can be perceived as either a duck facing left or a rabbit facing right. While the image appeared in German magazines in the late 19th century, it was popularized by American psychologist Joseph Jastrow in 1899 as a demonstration of the active role of perception in organizing sensory information. The same visual features serve dual purposes: what appears as the duck's bill becomes the rabbit's ears, and vice versa.

**statement (framework)** — category: ambiguous bistable multistable; perceptual mechanism(s): bistable-competition, top-down-expectation.
