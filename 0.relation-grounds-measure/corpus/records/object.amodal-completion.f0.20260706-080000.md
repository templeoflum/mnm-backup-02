**statement (plain)** — Amodal completion refers to the perceptual phenomenon whereby observers automatically perceive complete object shapes and surfaces even when portions of those objects are physically occluded from view. Unlike modal completion, where illusory surfaces appear visible with specific brightness or color properties, amodal completion involves the perceptual inference of hidden structure without conscious visual experience of the occluded regions. When a circle is partially hidden behind a rectangle, for instance, observers spontaneously perceive a complete circular object continuing behind the occluder rather than a truncated arc abutting the rectangle's edge.
