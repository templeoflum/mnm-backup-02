**statement (plain)** — before deciding *how much* deliberation a decision deserves, ask whether you can walk back through the door. If the choice is reversible — a **two-way door** — make it fast, cheaply, alone or in a small group, and be willing to undo it. If it is irreversible — a **one-way door** — slow down, consult, and weigh it carefully. The failure modes are symmetric: heavyweight process on a reversible choice yields paralysis; a fast, casual process on an irreversible one yields regret.

**statement (framework)** — Bezos (2016): Type 2 decisions "are changeable, reversible… can and should be made quickly," while Type 1 decisions "must be made methodically, carefully, slowly." The governing variables are **cost-to-undo** and **coupling** (how many downstream choices the decision locks in).

**basis** — articulated in [[source.bezos-2016]]; consonant with the broader reversible/irreversible-decision literature in management and decision-making. *Support* — widely adopted as a management heuristic. *Limits* — reversibility is often a judgment, not a fact, and can be misjudged in both directions; a practitioner heuristic, not a validated law. **[confirmed as attribution]**
