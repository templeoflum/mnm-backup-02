**statement (plain)** — *Physarum polycephalum*, a single-celled organism with no brain or neurons, coats wherever it has been with extracellular slime and then *avoids* the coated areas — using the trail as an external map. This lets it solve the U-shaped trap (a standard robot-navigation test): masking the trail (pre-coating the dish) cripples it — only about a third reach the goal, slower, re-crossing explored ground ten times as often. Earlier, the same organism reorganizes into the shortest tube through a maze. The "memory" is not learned; it is simply laid down and read back from the world.

**statement (framework)** — a clean instance of externalized (non-neuronal) cognition and **memory-without-learning**; the authors suggest external spatial memory may be "the functional precursor to the internal memory of higher organisms." An empirical anchor for stigmergy-as-external-memory [[object.stigmergy-as-external-memory]] and the extended-mind thesis [[object.extended-mind-thesis]].

**basis** — Reid et al. 2012 (external memory / U-trap) [[source.reid-2012]]; Nakagaki et al. 2000 (maze-solving) [[source.nakagaki-2000]]. *Support* — replicated, widely cited across biology, robotics, philosophy of mind. *Limits* — "memory" here is a functional analogy (an externalized avoidance trace), not internal representation. **[confirmed]**
