# expert-intuition

**statement (plain):** Expert intuition is the capacity to recognize a situation and arrive at a judgment or action rapidly, without conscious step-by-step reasoning. NDM treats it as learned pattern recognition rather than mystical insight. Its trustworthiness is not unconditional: an intuition is likely to be valid only when two conditions both hold — (1) the environment is sufficiently regular and predictable (high validity) that there are stable cues to learn, and (2) the person has had prolonged opportunity to learn those regularities through practice with accurate, timely feedback. Where either condition fails, subjectively confident intuitions can be the product of bias and overconfidence rather than skill.

**basis (method / support):** The two-condition account is the convergent conclusion of the adversarial collaboration between Kahneman (skeptical of intuition, from heuristics-and-biases) and Klein (NDM): evaluating an intuitive judgment requires assessing the predictability of the environment and the individual's opportunity to learn its regularities [[source.kahneman-klein-2009]]. The NDM grounding of intuition as experience-based recognition combining intuition with analysis is laid out in *Sources of Power* [[source.klein-1998]]. [confirmed]

**limits:** The validity/regularity of an environment is often hard to quantify in advance, so the criteria are easier to apply retrospectively than prospectively. Subjective confidence is not a reliable signal of which condition is met; high confidence occurs in both valid and invalid domains.
