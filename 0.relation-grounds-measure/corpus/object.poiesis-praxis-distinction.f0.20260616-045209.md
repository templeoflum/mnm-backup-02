**statement (plain)** — Aristotle distinguishes two kinds of doing. *Poiesis* (making/production) aims at an end *beyond* the activity — the house, the shoe, the cured patient; the activity is a means and the product is what matters. *Praxis* (action) has its end *in itself* — acting well, playing the flute, living well; there is no separate product. The crafts (techne) govern poiesis; practical wisdom (phronesis) governs praxis. "For while making has an end other than itself, action cannot; for good action itself is its end" (*NE* VI.5).

**statement (formal)** — *NE* VI.4–5 (Bekker ~1140a1–1140b7): poiesis (ποίησις) is activity whose *telos* is *para* (beyond) the activity, in the ergon/product; praxis (πρᾶξις) is activity whose *telos* is *en* (within) the activity (*eupraxia*, doing-well, is its own end). Techne is the disposition correlated with poiesis; phronesis with praxis.

**basis** — a foundational distinction in the history of philosophy; "established" marks it as a real and central position, not a fact asserted true. Documented at *NE* VI.4–5 [[source.aristotle-ne6]] and discussed in the SEP survey of episteme/techne [[source.sep-episteme-techne]]. Support: the distinction underlies the whole craft/ethics divide in Aristotle and is taken up across later thought (Marx's labour, Arendt's *work* vs *action* in *The Human Condition*, Gadamer on phronesis). Limits: the sharpness of the line is contested — whether any rich practice is purely poiesis or purely praxis, whether craftsmanship collapses the two, and how to read borderline cases (medicine, teaching, performance) are live debates in the secondary literature. **[confirmed]**
