Eugene T. Gendlin, *Focusing* (Everest House, New York, 1978; revised Bantam edition 1981). ISBN of the Bantam ed. 0-553-27833-9.

Contains — the concept of the **felt sense**: a bodily, holistic, initially vague and pre-verbal sense of a situation or problem, located in the body (often the chest/abdomen), distinct from emotion or thought. *Focusing* presents a six-step process (clearing a space; getting a felt sense; finding a handle; resonating; asking; receiving) for attending to and allowing the felt sense to shift ("felt shift"). Grounded in Gendlin's earlier philosophy of *Experiencing and the Creation of Meaning* (1962) and in psychotherapy-process research linking clients' bodily-referent attention to therapeutic outcome.

Role — origin of the experiential/clinical strand of interoception; the felt sense is a primary phenomenological account of attending to internal bodily state.

Access — in print (Bantam, and later Rider/Random House editions); widely available. The felt-sense definition is the canonical citation. **[confirmed]**
