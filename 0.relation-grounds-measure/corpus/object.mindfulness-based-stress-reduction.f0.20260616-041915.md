**statement (plain)** — Mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR) is a standardized, secular, group-based training program — typically eight weekly sessions plus a day-long retreat and daily home practice — that teaches mindfulness meditation (body scan, sitting meditation, mindful movement) to help participants relate differently to stress, pain, and illness. It is the prototype from which later mindfulness-based interventions (e.g. MBCT) were derived.

**basis** — Developed by Kabat-Zinn at the University of Massachusetts Medical Center's Stress Reduction Clinic and codified as a curriculum in *Full Catastrophe Living* [[source.kabat-zinn-1990]] [confirmed]. Its clinical effects have been assessed in randomized trials; meta-analytic evidence finds small-to-moderate reductions in anxiety, depression, and pain for mindfulness-meditation programs [[source.goyal-2014]] [confirmed].

**limits** — Reported as studied: meta-analysis finds the effects small-to-moderate, with low/insufficient evidence that meditation programs outperform active controls (exercise, medication), and variable trial quality [[source.goyal-2014]] [confirmed]. MBSR is a defined program/design, status established, not a claim of large or specific efficacy.
