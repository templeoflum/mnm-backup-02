Ernest R. Hilgard, *Divided Consciousness: Multiple Controls in Human Thought and Action* (New York: John Wiley & Sons, 1977). (Expanded edition 1986.)

Contains: the **neodissociation theory** of mind — multiple cognitive control systems operating in parallel under a hierarchical "executive ego," able to be functionally split so that subsystems run with partial autonomy and partial mutual amnesia. Marshals data on hypnotic analgesia, automatic writing, fugue, multiple personality, posthypnotic amnesia, and possession states. Introduces the **"hidden observer"** — a concurrently registering stream, reportable under hypnosis, that records (e.g.) pain not consciously experienced. The hidden-observer finding is itself contested (critics argue it can be an artifact of suggestion), carried as lineage in the dependent object.

The cognate journal statement: Hilgard, "The problem of divided consciousness: A neodissociation interpretation," *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences* 296 (1977): 48–59.
