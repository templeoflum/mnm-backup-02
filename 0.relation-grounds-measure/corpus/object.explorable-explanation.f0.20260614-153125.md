**statement (plain)** — an explorable explanation is a piece of informational media in which an interactive simulation of an idea is presented together with guidance, so the reader can play with the model and develop intuition for how the system works — instead of only reading the author's account of it.

**statement** — Bret Victor describes it as his "umbrella project for ideas that enable and encourage truly active reading," with the aim of changing people's relationship with text so that text becomes "an environment to think in" rather than "information to be consumed." The common Wikipedia gloss: "a form of informational media where an interactive computer simulation of a given concept is presented, along with some form of guidance." **[confirmed]**

**basis** — rests on the [[object.active-reader]] premise (a reader asks questions, considers alternatives, questions assumptions) and is realized through forms such as the [[object.reactive-document]] and reader-controlled visualizations (e.g. Victor's "[[source.victor-2011-ladder|Up and Down the Ladder of Abstraction]]"). Codified not by a treatise but by a community body of work — the explorabl.es collection assembled by Nicky Case. [[source.case-explorables]]

**lineage** — term **coined / popularized by Bret Victor** in his 2011 essay "Explorable Explanations" [[source.victor-2011]] **[confirmed]**. The phrase appears in passing in a 1994 academic paper by Peter Brusilovsky, so Victor is the populariser rather than sole originator — carried as lineage **[candidate, earliest-use Brusilovsky 1994]**. Deep precursors: Alan Kay's Dynabook / "active essays" [[source.kay-1972]] and Ted Nelson's hypertext [[source.nelson-hypertext]] **[confirmed as attribution]**.
