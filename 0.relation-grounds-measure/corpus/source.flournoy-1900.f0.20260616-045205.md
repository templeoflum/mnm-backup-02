Théodore Flournoy, *Des Indes à la planète Mars: Étude sur un cas de somnambulisme avec glossolalie* (Genève: Eggimann; Paris: F. Alcan, 1900). English: *From India to the Planet Mars*, trans. D. B. Vermilye (Harper & Brothers, 1900); reissued Princeton University Press (Princeton Legacy / 1994) with intro. by Sonu Shamdasani.

Contains: a sustained psychological study of the Geneva medium "Hélène Smith" (Catherine-Élise Müller, 1861–1929) — her "Martian," "Hindu," and "Marie-Antoinette" cycles. Flournoy analyzes the trance productions (including invented "Martian" language / glossolalia) as **subliminal romances** built by a dissociated creative imagination, and introduces / deploys **cryptomnesia** (forgotten memories resurfacing as apparently novel material) to explain the seemingly evidential content — declining the spirit hypothesis.

Two registers: Flournoy's psychology of subliminal creativity and cryptomnesia is the empirical contribution; the spiritualist reading he refutes is recorded as a tradition-tenet, attested not endorsed.

Access: French original on Internet Archive / Classiques UQAM; English translation public-domain and in Princeton reissue.
