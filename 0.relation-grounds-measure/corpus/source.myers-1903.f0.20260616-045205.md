Frederic W. H. Myers, *Human Personality and Its Survival of Bodily Death*, 2 vols. (London: Longmans, Green, and Co., 1903) — published posthumously (Myers d. 1901).

Contains: Myers's fullest statement of the **subliminal self** — a stratum of mentation below the threshold of ordinary awareness — and his taxonomy of its expressions: **sensory and motor automatisms** (automatic writing, crystal-gazing, "messages"), hypnotism, sleep, trance, possession, genius, and the survival material gathered through the Society for Psychical Research (which Myers co-founded, 1882). Coins / popularizes "subliminal," "supraliminal," and applies a single framework to normal, abnormal, and putatively supernormal functioning.

Two registers: Myers's *psychology* of automatism and the subliminal (studied phenomena, an empirical program) is distinct from his *survival thesis* (the contested interpretive claim that some automatisms evidence post-mortem agency). This record attests the text; the survival thesis is recorded as held, not asserted.

Access: full text public domain — Project Gutenberg #38492; original Longmans 2-vol. set widely catalogued.
