Full citation — É. Borel, "Les probabilités dénombrables et leurs applications arithmétiques," *Rendiconti del Circolo Matematico di Palermo* **27** (1909), 247–271.

Role — first formulation and proof of a **strong law of large numbers**, for the Bernoulli scheme in its number-theoretic ("normal numbers") interpretation: almost every real number is normal, i.e. the digit frequencies of almost every number tend to their expected values (almost-sure, not merely in-probability, convergence). Borel's proof was not fully rigorous by later standards; a rigorous proof for Bernoulli trials was given by Hausdorff (1914) and Hardy–Littlewood, with the general theory completed by Kolmogorov.

Access — historical; Rendiconti digitized. Attribution corroborated by Encyclopedia of Mathematics ("Strong law of large numbers") and Seneta's tricentenary history (*Bernoulli* 19(4), 2013). **[confirmed]**

Note — distinct from [[source.borel-1905]] (approximation theory, *Leçons sur les fonctions de variables réelles*) and [[source.borel-1895]].
