**statement (plain)** — records that come from one creator (an office, agency, family, or person) are kept together and are not mixed with records from any other creator. Where a record came from is part of what it means, so the archive preserves origin rather than re-sorting everything by subject, date, or place.

**statement** — the principle of provenance (respect des fonds) holds that archival records must be grouped according to the entity that created or accumulated them, and that the records of distinct creators (distinct [[object.fonds]]) must be kept separate and never interfiled. It has two limbs: respect des fonds proper (separation by origin) and [[object.original-order]] (preservation of the creator's internal arrangement). Per the [[source.saa-dictionary]]: "records originating in one office, agency, or individual must be grouped together" without mixing those of different creators. **[confirmed]**

**basis** — the archival significance of a record is contextual: it derives from the record's place within the organic whole produced by its creator's functions and activities. Intermingling fonds destroys that evidential context "irreparably." Arrangement and description are therefore built on provenance rather than on imposed classification. **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — first formulated by Natalis de Wailly, head of the Administrative Section of the Royal Archives, in the French Ministry of the Interior circular of 1841 (dated 24 April 1841), directing archivists "to assemble the different documents by fonds." The concept spread through Belgium and France around 1840, was refined by Prussian archivists into the Provenienzprinzip (also Registraturprinzip), promoted by the Dutch Manual ([[source.muller-feith-fruin-1898]], 1898), and ratified at the First International Congress of Archivists and Librarians, Brussels 1910. Reinforced by Jenkinson ([[source.jenkinson-1922]]) and Schellenberg ([[source.schellenberg-1956]]). The 1841 attribution is confirmed by both the [[source.saa-dictionary]] and standard reference works. **[confirmed]**
