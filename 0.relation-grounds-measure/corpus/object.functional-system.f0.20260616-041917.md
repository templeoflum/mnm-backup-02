**statement (plain)** — A functional system is the configuration of people, artifacts, and representational media — together with their coordinating activity — that accomplishes a cognitive task. Its cognitive properties are not reducible to those of any member; they depend on how representational state is propagated and transformed across the physical media that implement the system. [[source.hutchins-1995]] **[confirmed]**

**basis (method / support)** — Introduced through the navigation case in *Cognition in the Wild*, where computing a ship's position is a property of the bridge team-plus-instruments, not any one navigator [[source.hutchins-1995]]; carried forward as the central unit of analysis in the HCI programmatic paper, where "the cognitive properties of the system depend on the physical properties of the representational media" [[source.hollan-hutchins-kirsh-2000]]. **[confirmed]** Limits: the system's boundary is set by the analyst's question, so the construct is methodological rather than a natural kind; it inherits DCog's general critiques.
