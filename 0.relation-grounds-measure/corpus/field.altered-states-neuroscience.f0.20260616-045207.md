**origin** — A. Dietrich, "Functional neuroanatomy of altered states of consciousness: The transient hypofrontality hypothesis," *Consciousness and Cognition* 12(2), 231–256 (2003) — names a unifying neural account (transient hypofrontality) and pulls the heterogeneous catalogue of altered states under one neuroscientific question. [[source.dietrich-2003]] **[confirmed]**

**codification** — D. Vaitl et al., "Psychobiology of Altered States of Consciousness," *Psychological Bulletin* 131(1), 98–127 (2005) — the consortium review that systematizes the field: a four-dimension phenomenology (activation, awareness span, self-awareness, sensory dynamics) cross-cut with the psychobiology of each induction route. [[source.vaitl-2005]] **[confirmed]**

**door** — Vaitl et al. (2005) doubles as the standard survey entry point; the resting-state / default-mode-network strand that now organizes much of the imaging work opens with Raichle et al. (2001) [[source.raichle-2001]], with the psychedelic-state evidence anchored by Carhart-Harris et al. (2012) [[source.carhart-harris-2012]]. **[confirmed]**

lineage — the field has no single founder: it assembles older traditions (sleep/dream neuroscience, hypnosis research, meditation studies, psychopharmacology) under a shared neuroscientific frame. Dietrich (2003) is taken here as the origin for proposing a *common* neural signature; the DMN line (Raichle 2001 → Carhart-Harris 2012) is a parallel, now-dominant explanatory strand rather than a successor. The unifying-mechanism claims remain contested. **[confirmed]**

*grade [s] — origin + codification + door all present.*
