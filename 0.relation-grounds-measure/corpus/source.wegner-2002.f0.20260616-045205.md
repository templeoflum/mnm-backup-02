Daniel M. Wegner, *The Illusion of Conscious Will* (Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 2002). New edition with foreword by Daniel Gilbert (MIT Press, 2017), ISBN 9780262534925; original hardcover ISBN 9780262232227.

Contains: the **apparent mental causation** account — the experience of consciously willing an act is not the act's cause but an *inference* the mind draws when a thought precedes the action with the right **priority, consistency, and exclusivity**; the real causal chain runs unconsciously. Wegner builds the case largely from automatisms and dissociative-feeling phenomena: Ouija boards, table-turning, automatic writing, hypnosis, facilitated communication, spirit possession, dissociative identity, and trance channeling — read as cases where the will-experience and the action come apart. Extends the experimental program of Wegner & Wheatley (1999).

Status note: the strong "illusion" reading is a contested philosophical position; objections (e.g., that apparent causation evidence undercuts only an inflated notion of will) are carried as lineage in the dependent objects.
