**origin** — Edwin Hutchins, *Cognition in the Wild* (MIT Press, 1995) — distributed cognition (DCog): the unit of cognitive analysis is the functional system of people + artifacts + environment, not the lone mind; grounded in ethnographic study of ship navigation. [[source.hutchins-1995]] **[confirmed]**

**codification** — Andy Clark & David Chalmers, "The Extended Mind" (*Analysis* 58.1, 1998) — the extended mind thesis and the parity principle; the Otto-and-his-notebook thought experiment establishes active externalism as a philosophical position. [[source.clark-chalmers-1998]] **[confirmed]**

**door / survey** — Andy Clark, *Supersizing the Mind: Embodiment, Action, and Cognitive Extension* (Oxford, 2008) — book-length development and survey of the situated/embodied/extended program; SEP "Embodied Cognition" gives an accessible encyclopedic entry tying DCog, the extended mind, and embodiment together. [[source.clark-2008]] [[source.sep-embodied-cognition]] **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — DCog and the extended mind are sibling threads, not one founding: Hutchins's anthropological/computational DCog (1995, and the HCI-facing statement in Hollan, Hutchins & Kirsh 2000 [[source.hollan-hutchins-kirsh-2000]]) descends from a cognitive-anthropology and cybernetic lineage; Clark & Chalmers's extended mind is a parallel philosophy-of-mind framing. Both reject the "brainbound" boundary; they are commonly grouped under the 4E (embodied, embedded, extended, enactive) umbrella. **[confirmed]**

*grade [s] — origin + codification + door.*
