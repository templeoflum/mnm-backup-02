Simmons, Nelson & Simonsohn, "False-Positive Psychology: Undisclosed Flexibility in Data Collection and Analysis Allows Presenting Anything as Significant," *Psychological Science* 22(11) (2011):1359–1366.

Coined/popularized "researcher degrees of freedom"; showed that undisclosed analytic flexibility (p-hacking) drives the false-positive rate far above the nominal .05; proposed a six-requirement disclosure remedy for authors plus four reviewer guidelines.

Distinct from Ioannidis 2005 ("Why Most Published Research Findings Are False") — different paper, author, venue, and argument.

Access — abstract free at SAGE (DOI 10.1177/0956797611417632); preprint at SSRN 1850704.
