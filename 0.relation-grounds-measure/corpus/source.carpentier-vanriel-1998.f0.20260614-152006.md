Paul Carpentier and Jan van Riel coined the term "content-addressed storage" (CAS) while working at FilePool in the late 1990s. EMC acquired FilePool in 2001 and shipped the first commercial CAS product, **Centera**, in 2002 (timed with the document-retention demands of the Sarbanes–Oxley Act). The two later reunited at Caringo (CAStor software).

Role — the storage-industry origin of the *term* and product category content-addressable storage, for the content-addressing strand of addressing & namespace design. (Distinct from the data-structure ancestry, which runs through [[source.merkle-1987]] hash trees; and from the much earlier ICL Content Addressable File Store / CAFS hardware of the late 1960s.)

Access — Wikipedia "Content-addressable storage"; eWeek, "Caringo Reunites Inventors of CAS"; StorageNewsletter (2017).

Confidence — coinage attribution (Carpentier & van Riel, FilePool, late 1990s), the 2001 EMC acquisition, and the 2002 Centera release are consistent across Wikipedia and trade press. **[confirmed as attribution]**
