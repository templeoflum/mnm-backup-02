**statement (plain)** — analytico-synthetic classification is the principle behind faceted systems: you do not look a subject up in a fixed, pre-built list of every possible class (enumeration). Instead you analyse the subject into its independent facets and then synthesize a class number by combining notations for those facets. Because the facets are combined at the point of use rather than pre-listed, the same concepts can be recombined freely — this combining-at-use is called post-coordination.

**statement** — analytico-synthetic classification constructs a class notation by relating (synthesizing) elements that have first been isolated by analysis, "as opposed to an enumerative classification system" which supplies an exhaustive top-down list of subjects. Ranganathan named it the analytico-synthetic method explicitly to contrast it with the enumerative method; it rests on two paired innovations — **facet analysis** (the analytical step) and **post-coordination** (combining facets at the point of use rather than pre-coordinating them in a fixed list). **[confirmed]**

**basis** — the concept is the unifying rationale for faceted systems: analysis (decompose into single-characteristic facets, see [[object.facet-analysis]]) + synthesis (combine facet notations into one co-extensive number). Post-coordination — coordinating terms at retrieval/construction time rather than in advance — is what gives the approach its combinatorial reach over enumerative schemes and connects it to later post-coordinate indexing. It is the abstract concept that [[object.colon-classification]] first realised in practice. **[confirmed]**

**lineage** — the analytico-synthetic / post-coordinate framing is Ranganathan's, developed with [[object.colon-classification]] (1933) and *Prolegomena to Library Classification* (1937) [[source.ranganathan-1933]] [[source.ranganathan-1937]]; expounded for a wider audience by B. C. Vickery, *Faceted Classification* (1960) [[source.vickery-1960]], and carried forward by the Classification Research Group. **[confirmed as attribution]** — the "two key innovations: facet analysis and post-coordination" phrasing is from secondary literature (ISKO / *Cataloging & Classification Quarterly*) summarizing Ranganathan; original-text wording not opened here.
