# dream — ux — f0 — 20260608-070000

## the frame

the lattice instrument is the tool we build with. TS is properly parked — the first toy is built, the edge report is written, the breaks are known. now the tool itself needs attention. friction in the instrument is friction in everything that passes through it.

we've traversed the full system through use: 200→201→202→221→220→222, back to 100→101→102, back to 200→201→202 producing 210 frames, back to 100→101→102 producing refined frames, then executing at 211, then jumping to 110→111→112 for discernment. every address across all branches has been visited. the traversal pattern is understood. the instrument should match it.

this dream is about what the lattice instrument becomes. ungrounded. no building. compile feature ideas based on how we actually use the system and where friction lives.

## the territory

- **three view modes** — what is each one? what mindset does it put you in? what do you see there that you don't see elsewhere?
- **per-address contracts** — when you arrive at an address, what's the deal? what document types live there? what verbs are active? what does the instrument show you to put you in the right headspace?
- **the dashboard** — the persistent top bar. pinboard, counter, quick jumps. what stays across views? what travels with you?
- **file operations** — move, copy, fold, timestamp. the user directs all file movement. the instrument handles naming, metadata, fold numbers, timestamps automatically. the user points, the system executes cleanly.
- **verb map** — navigate by asking "what am I doing?" not "what's the address?" verbs as the navigator. the natural flows between addresses made visible.
- **document type awareness** — the instrument knows what a dream is, what a log is, what a sketch is, what an edge is. it knows what types belong at which addresses. it can suggest, warn, organize by type.

## what we know from use

- we always direct file movement manually. the instrument should formalize this — selection, destination, the system handles naming.
- the fold system works. f0 stays, fN layers on. the instrument should know about folds and handle the numbering.
- timestamps sort everything. the instrument should generate them.
- every address has a character. 200 receives. 201 tracks. 210 frames. 211 builds. 101 sketches. 102 decides. 122 dreams. 112 lands edges. the instrument should reflect this character when you arrive.
- we cycle between branches fluidly. the transition should be frictionless — jump from 211 to 101 to 222 without losing context.
- the session log, dream log, manifest, schedule, frame — these are always-open documents. the dashboard should hold them.
- the three views (definition, practice, maintenance) are three lenses on the same structure. you don't switch views to switch work — you switch views to switch how you see the work.

## stream

### ~1 — three views are three mindsets, not three screens

starting with the views because they frame everything else.

right now the three views feel like separate applications sharing an address bar. View 0 has a tree and a file reader. View 1 has a card and some rings. View 2 has a compression tower and a destination grid. switching between them is like switching programs. the address bar is the only thread.

but the structure says something different. 0 is specification — what IS. 1 is operation — what you DO. 2 is configuration — where it GOES. these aren't workspaces, they're lenses. you don't go to View 0 to do different work — you go there to see the same work differently. you look at address 101 in View 0 and you see: here's the structural identity, here are the files, here's what lives here. you look at 101 in View 1 and you see: here's the triad, here's what the verb is asking you to do, here's where you are in the flow. you look at 101 in View 2 and you see: here's the state, what's been archived, what's pending, what needs to move.

same address. three readings. like looking at a room through a blueprint, then through your own eyes, then through an inventory sheet. the room hasn't changed. your relationship to it has.

what this means for the UI: each view should show the SAME information re-organized around its mindset, not DIFFERENT information. right now View 0 has files and View 1 doesn't. View 2 has archiving and the others don't. that's wrong — or at least, it's incomplete. files should be visible from every view. the archive state should be visible from every view. what changes is the emphasis, the arrangement, the primary action.

**View 0 — specification — "what IS here"**
the library mindset. you're surveying. the patchboard shows the whole tree so you can see everything at once. the microscope shows deep detail on one node. files are front and center because the specification of an address IS its contents. the read action dominates — you're reading, comparing, checking what exists. the question this view answers: what does this address contain?

**View 1 — operation — "what do I DO here"**
the practice mindset. you're working. the triad is front and center because the verb tells you what to do. the position visualizations (rings, minimap, ratio line) tell you where you are in the flow — what came before, what comes after, what's adjacent. files should be visible but secondary — what matters is the action, not the inventory. the question this view answers: what is the work at this address?

**View 2 — configuration — "where does this GO"**
the maintenance mindset. you're managing. the state of what's here (archive status, file dates, fold counts, pending movements) dominates. the compression tower is one tool for this but it's not the whole story — file movement, manifest state, routing rules, what's ready to gate. the question this view answers: what is the state of this address and where does its contents go next?

the dashboard travels across all three. it doesn't change when you switch views. the views are below the dashboard — the windshield changes, the instrument panel doesn't.

### ~4 — file operations as first-class actions

this is where the biggest friction lives. every file operation currently happens outside the instrument: in the terminal, in the file browser, through the operator. the instrument is read-only. it can show you what's there but it can't touch anything.

what the user always does manually:
- **move** a file from one address to another (200→101, 211→110)
- **copy** a file to another address (build copy from 211 to 110)
- **fold** a document (f0 stays, create f1 as a new layer)
- **create** a new document at an address (dream log, edge doc, sketch)
- **name** with the convention: type.scope.fN.YYYYMMDD-HHMMSS.ext

what the instrument should handle automatically:
- the timestamp — generated at creation time, UTC
- the fold number — instrument knows what folds exist, offers the next one
- the address path — instrument knows the full folder path for any address
- the document type — if the instrument knows the address contract, it can suggest the type (at 122, suggest "dream"; at 101, suggest "sketch"; at 222, suggest "log")

what the user always controls:
- WHERE something goes — the user selects the destination address
- WHAT moves — the user selects the file(s)
- WHETHER to fold or copy — the user decides if this is a new layer or a new location
- the scope/project name — the user names what it is

the interaction: select a file in the microscope or file list. an action bar appears: move | copy | fold. select the action. if move or copy, a destination picker appears (the address grid from View 2, or a compact version). if fold, the instrument creates fN+1 at the same address with the same name, incremented fold number, new timestamp. confirm. done.

this requires a server that can write. the current `python -m http.server` can't do it. the research operation was supposed to inform the server decision, but the core requirement is clear regardless: the server needs to handle file creation, move, copy, and rename. everything else (what to name, where to put it, what fold number) is client-side logic.

### ~9 — folds as containers

this is the big one for reducing friction. right now at address 101 you might see:

```
sketch.tsnew-trace.f0.20260607-160000.md
sketch.ts-composition.f0.20260607-120000.md
sketch.ts-composition.f1.20260607-150000.md
sketch.ts-cascade.f0.20260607-120000.md
sketch.ts-cascade.f1.20260607-150000.md
sketch.ts-proportion.f0.20260607-120000.md
sketch.ts-proportion.f1.20260607-150000.md
```

seven files. but that's three documents: tsnew-trace (1 fold), ts-composition (2 folds), ts-cascade (2 folds), ts-proportion (2 folds). actually four documents. the fold trail is the version history of one thing, not separate things.

the instrument should parse filenames and group:

```
▸ sketch.tsnew-trace     [f0]          160000
▸ sketch.ts-composition  [f0 → f1]     120000 → 150000
▸ sketch.ts-cascade      [f0 → f1]     120000 → 150000
▸ sketch.ts-proportion   [f0 → f1]     120000 → 150000
```

click the arrow to expand and see individual folds. the fold indicator `[f0 → f1]` tells you how deep the history goes. the latest fold is what you see by default. the earlier folds are accessible but not in the way — they're context, not clutter.

**what this changes:**
- 44 sketch files at 101 become maybe 15 documents with fold trails. suddenly navigable.
- the manifest becomes a document inventory, not a file inventory. "101 has 15 sketches" not "101 has 44 files."
- folding a document becomes a first-class action: select the document, click "fold," the instrument creates fN+1 with the right name, right timestamp, right fold number. you edit the new fold. the old one is untouched.
- comparing folds becomes natural: side-by-side view, or a diff, or just flipping between f0 and f1 to see what changed.

**the naming convention already supports this.** `type.scope.fN.timestamp.ext` — the instrument just needs to parse on `type.scope` as the document identity and `fN` as the fold index. everything after the scope and before the extension is metadata.

**project grouping** takes this further. the scope IS the project: `tsnew-trace`, `ts-composition`, `lattice`. group documents by scope across addresses and you get a project view: "everything related to tsnew-trace, wherever it lives — the sketch at 101, the decisions at 102, the frame at 210, the build at 211, the edge at 112." the naming convention carries project identity across addresses. the instrument just has to read it.

### ~10 — the dashboard tray

the pinboard from the parked idea, sharpened by use.

what gets pinned: the documents you're always reaching for during a work cycle. from the last cycle:
- the frame (frame.tsnew-trace.f0 at 210) — the blueprint guiding the build
- the manifest (manifest.session.f1 at 201) — the count of everything
- the schedule (if one existed) — what's next

- the dream log (dream.ux.f0 at 122) — if dreaming, the current dream

these aren't at one address. they're scattered across the tree. the tray collects them into one persistent row at the top. each pinned document is a compact chip: `[frame · tsnew-trace · f0]` or even shorter `[210: frame]`. click to peek (show the content in a slide-out panel without navigating away). double-click to navigate to its address.

**the key insight:** the tray is how you hold the plan while executing the plan. you're at 211 building. the frame is at 210. without the tray you navigate away, check the frame, navigate back, try to remember. with the tray you glance up. the frame is right there. you never lose the plot.

**what makes it work:**
- drag a document from the file list onto the tray to pin it. or a "pin" button next to each document in the microscope.
- limited slots — maybe 5 or 6. forces curation. you can't pin everything. you pin what matters right now.
- pins persist across view switches and navigation. they DON'T persist across sessions (or do they? sessionStorage would give per-tab persistence. localStorage would give cross-session. both are reasonable. start with sessionStorage.).
- each pin knows its address and document identity. if the document folds (f0 → f1), the pin could update to show the latest fold. or it stays on the fold you pinned. either is defensible.

**the tray combined with fold containers:**
a pin doesn't point at a file — it points at a document. `frame.tsnew-trace` is one document that might have folds f0, f1, f2. the pin shows the document. click to peek shows the latest fold by default, with a fold selector to see earlier ones. the tray understands documents, not files.

**the library (View 0).** quiet. you go there to look things up, to check the index, to understand where things are. you don't make noise in the library. the patchboard is the card catalog. the microscope is the reading desk. the verb map is the reference section. the document type registry is the dewey decimal. this room fills slowly — it fills as the ground fills. right now the shelves are mostly empty because the ground is mostly unwritten. that's correct. a library with empty shelves is a library that hasn't been written yet, not a library that's broken.

**the studio (View 1).** paint on the floor. you go there to make things, to try things, to hold two ideas next to each other and squint. things are pinned to the walls. it's messy but it's YOUR mess. the triad card is the canvas on the easel — what you're looking at right now. the verb flow is the music playing — it sets the rhythm of movement. quick capture, dream streams, side-by-side comparison, free annotation. the structure keeps it from being chaos but it doesn't keep it from being messy. the studio is where mind lives.

**the office (View 2).** filing cabinets. you go there to make sure everything is accounted for, to sign off, to move things to where they belong, to check the numbers. nothing leaves without a signature. file operations, manifest management, fold creation, audit tools, schedule editing, memory management, compression, archiving. the control room. this is where safety lives. the office is where maintenance lives.

**the hallway (dashboard).** the coat hooks where you hang what you're carrying. the pinboard where the current plan is tacked up. the clock that tells you where you are. you pass through it every time you move between rooms. it never changes. the address counter, the triad, the tray, the verb list, the view tabs. the hallway is the constant.

you don't switch rooms to do different work in a different building. you walk from the library to the studio to the office. the home is one structure. the rooms are three characters of that structure. the hallway connects them.

worth renaming the view tabs when we build: library, studio, office. not "0 specification," "1 operation," "2 configuration." the formal names are correct but the room names are what you FEEL when you're there. both can coexist — the tab says "library" and the structural label says "0 specification" in small text underneath. the room name is the human name. the digit is the address.
