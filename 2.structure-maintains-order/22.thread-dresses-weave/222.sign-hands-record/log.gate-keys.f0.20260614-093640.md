## O — observation (why the settled line didn't read right from every angle)

Operator's angle (20260614): **safety protocol, lockout-tagout, startup procedure exist for a reason.** There is a difference between *never enforcing things* and *having a directed flow so traffic doesn't destroy things*. The keystone's word **"enforcement" conflated two different things:**

- **enforcing a decision** — removes agency over the *work* (what you do, the outcome). This is what the system rightly resists.
- **gating a requirement** — names conditions that must hold to pass (the *keys*), agnostic to how you satisfy them or what you do once through. LOTO doesn't decide your job; it says energy must be isolated first. **Directed flow, not dictated outcome.** This is legitimate — even necessary — and is NOT "enforcement of a decision."

The tell that it's the keystone's language that was outdated, not the idea: **the keystone's own routing gate already gates on a key** — "verified → specification" makes *verification* the key; "not ready" means *you don't have the key yet*. So the gate was never pure discipline-without-requirement; the old line mis-described what the gate already does. The re-word is more accurate to the keystone itself.

It also **dissolves the standing tension** between safety-gating and Q5's "ask, never deny": a requirement is not a denial, and a signature is just another key. Nothing is denied; the keys are simply named.
