I have been turning over how the whole paperwork engine should behave once it is wired live, and a few things want surfacing before we commit to them. The core mechanism holds cleanly: a document carries its own control-lines, the dispatch renders those lines as controls, and a signature is just a targeted line-rewrite logged as a decision row. What stays open is how a chat memo becomes a form — the head-symbol already declares whether a memo needs you, which is clean, but we have not decided what happens when a memo carries no clear head, or when two memos point at the same underlying document. There is also the question of what a signed paper turning over to a seat should feel like on the seat's own side, since from here the operator only ever watches it leave his court. My lean is to keep every piece atomic and let the field carry the state rather than any one surface, but I want your eye on these edge cases before I build past the mock. None of this blocks the work in front of us — it is a map of the decisions the build will surface, so that we make them deliberately instead of stumbling into them by accident.
