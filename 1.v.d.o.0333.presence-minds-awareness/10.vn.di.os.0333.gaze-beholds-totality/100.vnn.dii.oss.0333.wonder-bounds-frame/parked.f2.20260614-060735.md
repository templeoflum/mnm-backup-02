## archive the cycle, build forward fresh — don't retroactively fix

Source: naming-grammar dream session, 20260614, while working out the flow of dreams and whether to retroactively fix file names per the new grammar.

**The idea:** Don't retroactively rename or re-grammar the existing tree. Let the current cycle run to its purpose — TS rebuilt, the research mass fully processed, the systems worked many different ways with good observation/decision logging — until everything sits at a settled, well-worked place. Then do the **first archive**: every file is closed-to-purpose, only what we need to carry forward stays, and the **whole old tree is frozen as a reference snapshot.** Cycle 2 starts fresh — a new project, refined templates that already encode whatever the grammar work settled, clean from day one. The grammar is applied *prospectively* (in the new templates), never *retroactively* (to old files).

**Why it surfaced:** the naming-grammar dream kept circling a retroactive-fix knot (its Q7: convert the legacy 54 vs bless them exempt). Both options are work spent fixing the past. The archive-boundary move dissolves the knot — you finish old files to their purpose, then freeze them; nothing gets renamed. It also resolves the "am I micromanaging the dream's exact cell-path (102→112→122 vs 100→101→102→…→122)?" worry: the path is emergent (the dream already found STAGE = PLACE — a doc's address advances as it ripens), so you *record* the path, you don't *prescribe* it. Automation would log the trace, not enforce the route.

**Why it's the operator's own flow-health test, scaled up:** a tree that accumulates forever and never closes is hoarding at the largest scale — the exact failure the flow-health test names. The archive is the system *exhaling*. And it's the dream's "registry is a wear-pattern, not a schema" at macro scale: cycle 1 *wears* the patterns; cycle 2 starts with templates *shaped by* what was worn. You never pre-build the perfect schema; you let one cycle teach the next.

**The real work of the archive = the carry-forward sift.** "Only what we need stays" is a distillation act — the 220 trim at whole-tree scale, the biggest sift of all. Carry too much and cycle 2 inherits the mess; too little and you lose hard-won ground. Likely carry-forward: the keystone (refined), the templates (refined), the rebuilt TS + instruments (lattice/server), and the *distilled findings* — conclusions and the settled grammar, not raw process logs. Everything else freezes into the archive as reference.

**Why it's parked (not built/dreamt now):** the trigger is *cycle completion*, which hasn't happened — TS isn't rebuilt, the research isn't fully processed. The completion condition needs naming too: "complete" = the cycle stopped producing new live threads (the same dry-test we run on a single dream, applied to the whole cycle). Park it until the cycle is actually near that point; pulling the archive early would freeze live work. Mechanically it's likely a git tag (frozen snapshot) + a reset of the working tree to the carry-forward set — but that's design for later, not now.
