02 As a tree

Branch by branch from the current frame. The path you built is inked in; the siblings it passed over are ghosted. A tree costs 3ⁿ to draw — so this panel caps at three levels deep while the line below keeps going for free. That asymmetry is itself a fact about the two readings.

03 As a line

The same frame, relabeled 0 to 1. Tall ticks are the first split into thirds, then thinner ticks split each of those, and so on. Your address is the shaded cell — never an endpoint, always something you could subdivide again.

04 The seam

Here's what the line carries that the tree throws away. The cell 0 2 2 and the cell 1 0 0 sit side by side on the line — touching at exactly ⅓. On the tree they're as far apart as two leaves can be: their only shared ancestor is the root.

On the tree: 6 edges apart — the maximum at this depth.  On the line: 0 apart — they share a wall. The line glues together what the tree holds furthest away.

05 Closure — there is no bottom

Zoom in and the cell you were standing in becomes a fresh 0-to-1, with its own three thirds. Zoom out and this whole interval becomes a single digit of a coarser one. The structure is the same at every scale, so "how far down does it go" turns out to be the wrong question — there's no fixed scale to measure from.

07 The carry law

Step the current cell one place to the right (+1 in base 3). Sometimes the carry stays at the bottom; sometimes it ripples all the way up. The height the carry climbs is exactly what sets how far apart the two neighbors sit on the tree: tree distance = 2 × (carry height + 1). This is the single law that ties the line, the tree, and the seam together.

08 One point, three verdicts

Take the seam pair deeper: 0.02, 0.022, 0.0222… creeps up toward ⅓ from the left, while 0.1 sits at ⅓ exactly. In the limit they don't just touch — they're the same number: 0.0222…₃ = 0.1₃ = ⅓, the base-3 cousin of 0.999… = 1.

So the seam pair gets three answers at once:  tree → maximal distance (6 edges)  ·  line → zero distance (touching)  ·  limit → identical (one point).

09 Cantor, done honestly

A famous object hides one branch away. Cut every middle child — keep only the outer two at each node — and the full interval crumbles into the Cantor set. 10 The line doesn't know the base

The notebook chose three. But slide the branching factor: the tree is entirely defined by the base — two children, three, four, five — and the tick grid on the line gets denser or sparser to match. The line itself never moves. [0,1] is [0,1] in every base. Which is the quiet answer to why three felt arbitrary: the line was base-blind the whole time, and the base lives only in how you choose to address it.

11 Why the primes are the irreducible bases

Pick two bases. Points the first can reach as a finite expansion stick up; points only the second can reach stick down; points both reach are tall bars through the line. Watch when the two combs mesh and when they pass straight through each other. A base can only reach denominators built from its own primes — so two bases share a point only where their primes overlap. The primes are exactly the bases that reduce to nothing smaller.

12 Turning the base knob

Hold the object fixed and change only the base. Three of its parts respond differently. The tree shows whether the branching can be factored — a prime base is one irreducible split, a composite base is nested smaller trees, an irrational base is binary with a grammar. The seam shows the walls: isolated for integer bases, but for an irrational base the cells overlap and the seam fattens into a band. The redundancy measures it: integer bases tile exactly (total length 1), irrational bases cover more than once, and the excess is address-space landing on the same points.

13 The other map: spelling out the address

Panel 07 was +1 — the odometer, the most orderly map the addresses carry. This is its twin, ×3 mod 1: the shift. The rule is the dumbest imaginable — drop the leading digit — yet the orbit scatters across the whole interval. That scatter isn't the map being clever; the complexity was already written into the address, and the shift only reads it out one place per step. So the chaos lives in the number, not the motion. Watch a rational (a seam point) cycle, and an irrational wander forever.
