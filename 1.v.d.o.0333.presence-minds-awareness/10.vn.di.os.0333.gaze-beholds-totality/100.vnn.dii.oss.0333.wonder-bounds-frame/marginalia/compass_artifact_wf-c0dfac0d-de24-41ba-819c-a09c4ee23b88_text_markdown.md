# Archaeological Research Map — Remainder Sweep + Chunk 1 ("The Mind Canon")

## TL;DR
- **Every candidate lineage in the brief was confirmed cleanly** against two or more independent sources, with exact titles, years, and venues nailed down; the few wrinkles are publication-date nuances (Euler's paper presented 1735, dated to the 1736 volume, actually printed 1741) and genuinely shared/contested attributions (thematic roles; second-order cybernetics' debt to Mead; AI-alignment praxis).
- **Free, high-quality DOOR texts exist for most fields** — Diestel's *Graph Theory* (author's electronic editions), Klein's Erlangen Program (English translation on arXiv/UCR), Wiener's *Cybernetics* (MIT Press open access), Papert's "Situating Constructionism" (papert.org/archive.org), public-domain Pseudo-Dionysius (CCEL/Internet Archive), and Lord's *The Singer of Tales* (Harvard CHS online).
- **The origin/codification distinction holds sharply throughout** — e.g., Thorndike's law of effect (1898/1911, origin) vs. Skinner's *Behavior of Organisms* (1938, codification); ZPD (Vygotsky, origin) vs. the term "scaffolding" (Wood, Bruner & Ross 1976); Saussure's structural linguistics (origin) vs. Lévi-Strauss's anthropological extension (codification) — exactly as Nyquist 1928 (origin) was distinguished from Shannon 1949 in the prior dive.

## Key Findings — confirmed cleanly vs. contested

**Confirmed cleanly (well-attested, two+ independent routes):** graph theory (Euler→König→Diestel); coordinate systems & Erlangen (Descartes→Klein); constructionism (Papert); prepared-environment pedagogy (Montessori); ZPD/scaffolding split (Vygotsky vs. Wood-Bruner-Ross 1976); tacit knowledge (Polanyi); deliberate practice (Ericsson et al. 1993); ethology (Tinbergen 1951); operant conditioning (Thorndike→Skinner); cybernetics (Wiener 1948); second-order cybernetics (von Foerster 1974); phenomenology (Husserl); structuralism (Saussure→Lévi-Strauss); semiotics (Peirce/Saussure dual origin→Eco); comparative mythology & oral tradition (Müller; Parry-Lord; Ong); apophatic traditions (Pseudo-Dionysius and the cross-traditional strands).

**Contested / flagged:** the single "originator" of thematic roles (Gruber 1965 vs. Jackendoff 1972 vs. Fillmore 1968 — shared); the precise division of credit in second-order cybernetics (von Foerster formalized; Mead's 1968 paper supplied the title/impulse); AI-alignment praxis (living, fast-moving — philosophical origins vs. practical field genuinely unsettled); Dumézil's trifunctional hypothesis (attribution secure, validity contested); authorship of *The Cloud of Unknowing* (inherently anonymous); whether cross-traditional apophatic strands reflect influence or convergence (debated).

## PART A — REMAINDER SWEEP (two stone fields)

### graph theory [s]
- **ORIGIN —** Leonhard Euler, *"Solutio problematis ad geometriam situs pertinentis"* (Solution of a problem relating to the geometry of position), the Seven Bridges of Königsberg paper, in *Commentarii academiae scientiarum Petropolitanae*, vol. 8. Universally regarded as the first theorem of graph theory (and a foreshadowing of topology). **Confidence: HIGH.** *Date wrinkle to preserve on the card:* presented to the St. Petersburg Academy 26 Aug 1735; carries the date of the 1736 volume; actually printed 1741. Cite as **"1736 (presented 1735; printed 1741)."**
- **CODIFICATION —** Dénes Kőnig, *Theorie der endlichen und unendlichen Graphen*, Leipzig: Akademische Verlagsgesellschaft, 1936 — the first textbook / systematic treatise on graph theory ("From Königsberg to König's book"). **Confidence: HIGH.** (English: *Theory of Finite and Infinite Graphs*, trans. R. McCoart, Birkhäuser, 1990.)
- **DOOR —** Reinhard Diestel, *Graph Theory* (Springer, Graduate Texts in Mathematics 173; 1st ed. 1997, now 6th ed. 2025). The author maintains free electronic editions (2nd/4th/5th); earlier editions are also on archive.org. The standard, freely accessible modern entry point. Alternatives: Bondy & Murty, *Graph Theory*; West, *Introduction to Graph Theory*.

### coordinate systems & the Erlangen program [s]
- **ORIGIN —** René Descartes, *La Géométrie*, published 1637 as an appendix to *Discours de la méthode* (Leiden: Jan Maire). Founds analytic/coordinate geometry by systematically linking algebraic equations to geometric curves; introduces the x/y convention for unknowns. **Confidence: HIGH.** (Public domain; full French text on Wikisource and Internet Archive.)
- **CODIFICATION —** Felix Klein, *Vergleichende Betrachtungen über neuere geometrische Forschungen* ("Erlangen Program"), Erlangen: Andreas Deichert, 1872 (a 48-pp. pamphlet) — defines a geometry by its group of transformations and the invariants preserved under that group. **Confidence: HIGH.** *Note:* commonly confused with Klein's inaugural address; it was a separately printed pamphlet distributed at his Erlangen inauguration. Reprinted in *Mathematische Annalen* 43 (1893), pp. 63–100.
- **Through-line:** Klein's program — a geometry = a transformation group + its invariants — is the conceptual ancestor of the modern view that there is no privileged/canonical coordinate chart, feeding into general covariance in relativity (Élie Cartan later generalized Klein's homogeneous model spaces to Cartan connections).
- **DOOR —** Klein's Erlangen Program in English: the LaTeX/scanned translation hosted at math.ucr.edu (John Baez's page) and the arXiv translation (arXiv:0807.3161, "A comparative review of recent researches in geometry"). For analytic geometry as a learner: the primary source *La Géométrie* (Smith–Latham English translation) is public domain on archive.org.

## PART B — CHUNK 1, "THE MIND CANON"

### SLICE 10 — learning & transmission

#### constructionism & microworlds [s]
- **ORIGIN —** Seymour Papert, *Mindstorms: Children, Computers, and Powerful Ideas*, New York: Basic Books, 1980. Introduces the LOGO-based "mathland"/microworlds vision; the term "constructionism" was not yet coined here. **Confidence: HIGH.**
- **CODIFICATION —** Seymour Papert & Idit Harel, "Situating Constructionism," the opening chapter of *Constructionism: Research Reports and Essays, 1985–1990* (eds. Harel & Papert), Norwood, NJ: **Ablex Publishing Corporation**, 1991, pp. 1–11 — the explicit theoretical statement distinguishing constructionism ("learning-by-making"; people, not computers, as the driving force) from Piaget's constructivism. **Confidence: HIGH.** *Resolved attribution note:* the Ablex/Norwood, NJ citation is confirmed by papert.org, BibSonomy, SCIRP, and The Daily Papert; the occasional "Lawrence Erlbaum, Hillsdale/Mahwah, NJ" citations conflate this with Papert's distinct 1996 chapter in Kafai & Resnick's *Constructionism in Practice* (Erlbaum). Year (1991) and authorship are firm.
- **DOOR —** "Situating Constructionism" is freely available at papert.org and on archive.org (full PDF). *Mindstorms* is the accessible book-length entry.

#### prepared-environment pedagogy [t]
- **ORIGIN —** Maria Montessori, *Il Metodo della Pedagogia Scientifica applicato all'educazione infantile nelle Case dei Bambini*, 1909; translated into English 1912 as *The Montessori Method*. Documents the "prepared environment" and "auto-education" from the Casa dei Bambini (opened 1907). **Confidence: HIGH.**
- **DOOR —** *The Montessori Method* (1912 English translation by Anne E. George) is public domain and on archive.org / Project Gutenberg. For Montessori's fullest late statement of the prepared environment, *The Absorbent Mind* (1949).

#### scaffolding & ZPD [t]
- **ORIGIN (ZPD) —** Lev Vygotsky, the zone of proximal development; conceived in the early 1930s (Vygotsky d. 1934), reaching English readers via *Mind in Society: The Development of Higher Psychological Processes*, Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press, 1978 (posthumous compilation). **Confidence: HIGH.**
- **ORIGIN (term "scaffolding") —** David Wood, Jerome S. Bruner & Gail Ross, "The Role of Tutoring in Problem Solving," *Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry*, 17(2), 1976, pp. 89–100. First defines scaffolding as "a process that enables a child or novice to solve a task or achieve a goal that would be beyond his unassisted efforts" (p. 90). **Confidence: HIGH** (DOI 10.1111/j.1469-7610.1976.tb00381.x). *Distinction:* Vygotsky never used "scaffolding"; the term is Wood-Bruner-Ross's, applied to the ZPD only later.
- **DOOR —** *Mind in Society* (widely available); the 1976 Wood-Bruner-Ross paper circulates freely as PDF.

#### tacit knowledge [s]
- **ORIGIN —** Michael Polanyi, *Personal Knowledge: Towards a Post-Critical Philosophy*, University of Chicago Press / Routledge, 1958. Coins "tacit knowing"; draws partly on Gilbert Ryle's knowing-how/knowing-that distinction (1945). **Confidence: HIGH.**
- **CODIFICATION —** Michael Polanyi, *The Tacit Dimension*, University of Chicago Press, 1966 (the lectures), containing the slogan "we can know more than we can tell." **Confidence: HIGH.**
- **DOOR —** *The Tacit Dimension* (short, accessible; University of Chicago Press reprint). Excerpts freely available online.

#### deliberate practice & apprenticeship [s]
- **ORIGIN —** K. Anders Ericsson, Ralf Th. Krampe & Clemens Tesch-Römer, "The Role of Deliberate Practice in the Acquisition of Expert Performance," *Psychological Review*, 100(3), 1993, pp. 363–406. **Confidence: HIGH** (DOI 10.1037/0033-295X.100.3.363).
- **CODIFICATION / companion —** Cognitive apprenticeship: Allan Collins, John Seely Brown & Susan E. Newman, "Cognitive Apprenticeship: Teaching the Crafts of Reading, Writing, and Mathematics," in *Knowing, Learning, and Instruction: Essays in Honor of Robert Glaser* (ed. L. B. Resnick), Hillsdale, NJ: Lawrence Erlbaum, 1989. **Confidence: HIGH.**
- **DOOR —** The 1993 *Psychological Review* paper circulates widely as PDF; popular synthesis in Ericsson & Pool, *Peak* (2016).

### SLICE 11 — training & feedback
*(Gate note — the user's G·cyb gate: **cybernetics & control theory precedes second-order cybernetics.** First-order cybernetics studies *observed* systems; second-order studies *observing* systems, including the observer in the system.)*

#### ethology & horsemanship lineage [s] — dual lineage
- **ORIGIN (scientific ethology) —** Konrad Lorenz and Niko Tinbergen as co-founders of classical ethology; Tinbergen's "four questions" (causation/mechanism, ontogeny/development, function/adaptation, evolution) articulated in "On Aims and Methods of Ethology," *Zeitschrift für Tierpsychologie*, 1963 (written to honour Lorenz's 60th birthday). **Confidence: HIGH.**
- **CODIFICATION (ethology) —** Niko Tinbergen, *The Study of Instinct*, Oxford: Clarendon / Oxford University Press, 1951 — the foundational, first textbook of ethology. Lorenz, Tinbergen & Karl von Frisch shared the 1973 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine "for their discoveries concerning organization and elicitation of individual and social behaviour patterns." **Confidence: HIGH.**
- **ORIGIN (horsemanship craft) —** *Ancient:* Xenophon, *On Horsemanship* (*Peri Hippikēs* / *The Art of Horsemanship*), c. 4th c. BCE — the earliest surviving horse-training treatise, viewing the horse as a partner. *Modern "natural horsemanship":* Tom Dorrance (1910–2003), *True Unity: Willing Communication Between Horse and Human* (ed. Milly Hunt Porter, 1987) and the "true unity and willing communication" philosophy; his protégé Ray Hunt (1929–2009), *Think Harmony with Horses*; brother Bill Dorrance, *True Horsemanship Through Feel*. **Confidence: HIGH.** Present as a dual lineage: scientific ethology + practical horsemanship craft.
- **DOOR —** *The Study of Instinct* (reprinted; Google Books); Xenophon's *Art of Horsemanship* is public domain (archive.org, Project Gutenberg; Morris H. Morgan translation); Dorrance's *True Unity* for the craft strand.

#### operant conditioning [t]
- **ORIGIN —** Edward L. Thorndike, the law of effect — "Animal Intelligence: An Experimental Study of the Associative Processes in Animals," *Psychological Monographs*, 1898 (the puzzle-box studies); restated in *Animal Intelligence* (1911). **Confidence: HIGH.**
- **CODIFICATION —** B. F. Skinner, *The Behavior of Organisms: An Experimental Analysis*, New York: Appleton-Century, 1938 — systematizes operant conditioning, reinforcement schedules, and the operant ("Skinner") chamber. **Confidence: HIGH.** *Distinction:* Thorndike supplied the law of effect (origin); Skinner introduced "reinforcement," rejected Thorndike's unobservable mental states, and built the comprehensive framework (codification).
- **DOOR —** Skinner, *Science and Human Behavior* (1953) — his accessible synthesis, available free as PDF from the B. F. Skinner Foundation.

#### cybernetics & control theory [s] *(gates second-order cybernetics)*
- **ORIGIN —** Control-theory precursor: James Clerk Maxwell, "On Governors," *Proceedings of the Royal Society of London*, 1868 — the first significant mathematical treatment of feedback (governors). Wiener explicitly credits this paper and notes "governor" shares its Greek root (*kybernetes*, "steersman") with "cybernetics." **Confidence: HIGH.**
- **CODIFICATION —** Norbert Wiener, *Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine*, Cambridge, MA: MIT Press (with Hermann & Cie, Paris, and Wiley), 1948 — the first public use of "cybernetics"; synthesizes the Macy Conferences. **Confidence: HIGH.** *Context note:* the Macy Conferences (1946–1953) were co-organized by neuropsychiatrist Warren S. McCulloch (chair) and Frank Fremont-Smith, medical director of the Josiah Macy, Jr. Foundation; "cybernetics" entered the conference title only at the seventh meeting (March 1950), after Wiener's book.
- **DOOR —** *Cybernetics* (the 1961 2nd edition) is available **open access** from MIT Press (direct.mit.edu). Accessible companion: Wiener, *The Human Use of Human Beings* (1950).

#### second-order cybernetics [s] *(GATED — comes after cybernetics & control theory)*
- **ORIGIN / impulse —** Margaret Mead, "Cybernetics of Cybernetics," in *Purposive Systems: Proceedings of the First Annual Symposium of the American Society for Cybernetics* (eds. von Foerster, White, Peterson & Russell), New York: Spartan Books, 1968 — supplied the title and impulse for self-applied cybernetics (von Foerster titled the paper, as Mead was in the field and uncontactable). **Confidence: HIGH.**
- **CODIFICATION —** Heinz von Foerster, who in 1974 articulated the first-order/second-order distinction (cybernetics of *observed* vs. *observing* systems) and edited *Cybernetics of Cybernetics, or, the Control of Control and the Communication of Communication* (BCL Report 73.38, University of Illinois, 1974). The observer is included in the system. **Confidence: HIGH on von Foerster as primary formalizer; credit is genuinely shared with Mead's 1968 impulse — flagged.**
- **DOOR —** von Foerster, *Understanding Understanding* (Springer, 2003) collects the key essays; *Observing Systems* (1981). Stuart Umpleby's historical-overview papers are freely accessible.

#### AI alignment praxis [s] — living, contested, fast-moving (attribution flagged unsettled)
- **ORIGIN (philosophical) —** Nick Bostrom, *Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, Strategies*, Oxford University Press, 2014; earlier framing by Eliezer Yudkowsky / MIRI (e.g., "Artificial Intelligence as a Positive and Negative Factor in Global Risk," in *Global Catastrophic Risks*, 2008). Stuart Russell reframed alignment/control as central to AI's future (*Human Compatible*, 2019). **Confidence: HIGH on works/years; "who founded the field" is genuinely contested.**
- **CODIFICATION (praxis) —** Dario Amodei, Chris Olah, Jacob Steinhardt, Paul Christiano, John Schulman & Dan Mané, "Concrete Problems in AI Safety," arXiv:1606.06565, 2016 — the agenda that turned alignment into an empirical ML research program (avoiding negative side effects, reward hacking, scalable oversight, safe exploration, distributional robustness). Paul Christiano et al., "Deep Reinforcement Learning from Human Preferences" (NeurIPS 2017) and "Supervising Strong Learners by Amplifying Weak Experts" (iterated amplification, arXiv:1810.08575, 2018) seed RLHF and amplification. **Confidence: HIGH on documents; the field is living and its self-account evolves.**
- **DOOR —** "Concrete Problems in AI Safety" (free on arXiv) is the canonical entry; Victoria Krakovna's continually updated "AI alignment resources" list; Russell's *Human Compatible* for the accessible book treatment. *Flag:* fast-moving, contested domain — treat any "settled" lineage cautiously.

#### phenomenological method [t]
- **ORIGIN —** Edmund Husserl, *Logische Untersuchungen* (*Logical Investigations*), 2 vols., 1900–1901 — establishes descriptive phenomenology and the critique of psychologism. **Confidence: HIGH.**
- **DOOR / mature statement —** Husserl, *Ideen zu einer reinen Phänomenologie und phänomenologischen Philosophie, Erstes Buch* (*Ideas I*), 1913 — introduces the epoché and the phenomenological (transcendental) reduction. Later turns in the lineage: Martin Heidegger (*Being and Time*, 1927) and Maurice Merleau-Ponty (*Phenomenology of Perception*, 1945). **Confidence: HIGH.**
- **DOOR (accessible) —** Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy "Husserl" and "Phenomenology" entries (free); *The Idea of Phenomenology* (1907 lectures) as a short way in.

### SLICE 12 — myth & meaning

#### structuralism [s]
- **ORIGIN —** Ferdinand de Saussure, *Cours de linguistique générale*, Lausanne/Paris: Payot, 1916 (posthumous; compiled by Charles Bally & Albert Sechehaye, with Albert Riedlinger, from 1906–1911 lecture notes). Founds structural linguistics: langue/parole, signifier/signified, language as a system of differences without positive terms. **Confidence: HIGH.** *Flag:* because Saussure did not write it (editors amalgamated three course-years), fidelity to his actual views is debated.
- **CODIFICATION / extension —** Claude Lévi-Strauss, *Anthropologie structurale* (*Structural Anthropology*), Paris: Plon, 1958 — extends the structural method from language to kinship and myth. **Confidence: HIGH.** *Distinction:* linguistic origin (Saussure) vs. anthropological extension (Lévi-Strauss).
- **DOOR —** *Course in General Linguistics* (Wade Baskin trans., 1959; Roy Harris trans., 1983) — widely available.

#### semiotics [t] — dual origin
- **ORIGIN (a) —** Charles Sanders Peirce's triadic theory of signs (sign/representamen — object — interpretant), developed across his writings (key statements c. 1902–1906) and reconstructed from the *Collected Papers of Charles Sanders Peirce*, Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press, 8 vols.: vols. 1–6 ed. Hartshorne & Weiss (1931–1935), vols. 7–8 ed. Burks (1958). **Confidence: HIGH.** (No single canonical "origin" paper; cite the *Collected Papers*.)
- **ORIGIN (b) —** Ferdinand de Saussure's "semiology" (signifier/signified) in the *Cours de linguistique générale* (1916). The two traditions — Peircean "semiotic" and Saussurean "semiology" — developed independently. **Confidence: HIGH.**
- **DOOR / codification —** Umberto Eco, *A Theory of Semiotics*, Bloomington: Indiana University Press, 1976 (Italian original *Trattato di semiotica generale*, Milan: Bompiani, 1975). **Confidence: HIGH.** Accessible alternative: Daniel Chandler, *Semiotics: The Basics* (Routledge), with a free companion site, "Semiotics for Beginners."

#### linguistics: argument structure [s]
- **ORIGIN (thematic relations) —** Jeffrey S. Gruber, *Studies in Lexical Relations*, MIT PhD dissertation, 1965 — first to introduce "theme" and a system of thematic relations (Agent, Theme, Source, Goal, Location). **Confidence: HIGH** (on MIT DSpace; later distributed by Indiana University Linguistics Club, 1970).
- **ORIGIN (parallel "case" tradition) —** Charles J. Fillmore, "The Case for Case," in *Universals in Linguistic Theory* (eds. Emmon Bach & Robert T. Harms), New York: Holt, Rinehart and Winston, 1968, pp. 1–88 — Case Grammar / deep semantic cases. **Confidence: HIGH.**
- **SYSTEMATIZER —** Ray Jackendoff, *Semantic Interpretation in Generative Grammar*, Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 1972 — adopts and popularizes thematic relations (theta-roles). **Confidence: HIGH.**
- **CODIFICATION —** Beth Levin, *English Verb Classes and Alternations: A Preliminary Investigation*, Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1993 — large-scale classification of 3,000+ English verbs by shared meaning/syntactic behavior. **Confidence: HIGH.**
- **Contested:** the single "originator" of thematic roles is genuinely shared — **Gruber (originated, 1965); Jackendoff (systematized/popularized, 1972); Fillmore (parallel "case" tradition, 1968)**; ancient precursor in Pāṇini's *kārakas*. **Flag as contested.**
- **DOOR —** Levin (1993) is the standard reference; Robert Van Valin's introductions and Adele Goldberg's *Constructions* (1995) as accessible modern routes.

#### narratology & story morphology [f] — orientation note
Vladimir Propp's *Morphology of the Folktale* (Russian 1928; English trans. 1958, 2nd ed. 1968) reduced Russian wonder-tales to 31 recurring "functions," seeding structuralist narratology. Tzvetan Todorov coined *narratologie* in *Grammaire du Décaméron* (1969); Gérard Genette (*Narrative Discourse*, Fr. 1972) supplied the dominant analytic vocabulary (order, mood, voice). **Confidence: HIGH.** Entry point: Propp (1928/1968) plus Genette.

#### comparative mythology & oral tradition [s]
- **ORIGIN (comparative mythology) —** Friedrich Max Müller, "Comparative Mythology," essay in *Oxford Essays* (London: John W. Parker and Son, 1856) — applies comparative philology to Indo-European myth ("myth as a disease of language"). Müller is credited with virtually creating the discipline of comparative religion and is often called the "father of Religious Studies," having coined the term "science of religion" (*Religionswissenschaft*) in 1873. **Confidence: HIGH.** Also in this lineage: Georges Dumézil's trifunctional ("tripartite ideology") hypothesis — three functions (sovereign/sacral, martial, productive) — first proposed 1929 (*Flamen-Brahman*), developed in *Mitra-Varuna* (1940). **Confidence: HIGH on attribution; validity heavily contested (Brough, Gonda, Lincoln; possible Gobineau precedent).**
- **CODIFICATION (oral tradition) —** Albert B. Lord, *The Singer of Tales*, Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press, 1960 — the foundational statement of Parry-Lord oral-formulaic theory ("composition in performance"), based on Milman Parry's (d. 1935) Balkan *guslar* fieldwork. Walter J. Ong, *Orality and Literacy: The Technologizing of the Word*, London/New York: Methuen, 1982, synthesizes the consequences of the shift from oral to literate culture. **Confidence: HIGH.**
- **DOOR —** *The Singer of Tales* is freely readable online via Harvard's Center for Hellenic Studies; Müller's "Comparative Mythology" is public domain on archive.org; Upaniṣadic/comparative texts on sacred-texts.com. Ong's *Orality and Literacy* (Routledge reprint) for the accessible synthesis.

#### apophatic traditions [s] — cross-traditional lineage
- **ORIGIN (Christian) —** Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite, *The Mystical Theology* (*De Mystica Theologia*) — the first work in which theology becomes explicitly "mystical," the *via negativa* (God known by negation/unknowing). **Confidence: HIGH.** *Refined dating (per Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy):* because the author draws on Proclus (d. 485 CE) and is first clearly cited by Severus of Antioch between 518 and 528, the *Corpus Areopagiticum* is placed **c. 485–518/528 CE** (i.e., late 5th–early 6th century). Author was pseudonymous, probably a Syrian monk.
- **PRECURSOR —** Plotinus, *The Enneads* (3rd c. CE, ed. Porphyry) — "the One" beyond being and predication; the Neoplatonic *via negativa* / *aphairesis* that Pseudo-Dionysius drew upon. **Confidence: HIGH.**
- **LATER (Christian) —** Meister Eckhart (c. 1260–1328; the "Godhead" beyond God) and the anonymous Middle English *The Cloud of Unknowing* (late 14th c.; God approached through a "cloud of unknowing," known by love not thought). **Confidence: HIGH; authorship of *The Cloud* is inherently anonymous.**
- **EASTERN parallels —** Nāgārjuna's Madhyamaka (*Mūlamadhyamakakārikā*, c. 2nd–3rd c. CE): *śūnyatā* (emptiness) and the *catuṣkoṭi* (tetralemma, negating all four positions); and *neti neti* ("not this, not this") of the Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad (attributed to the sage Yājñavalkya), describing Nirguṇa Brahman by negation. **Confidence: HIGH on attributions.** *Flag:* whether these traditions reflect historical influence or independent convergence is genuinely debated — do not assert a single genealogy.
- **DOOR —** Public-domain Pseudo-Dionysius: C. E. Rolt's translation (*Dionysius the Areopagite: On the Divine Names and the Mystical Theology*, SPCK, 1920) on Internet Archive; John Parker's translation on CCEL. Modern scholarly: Luibhéid & Rorem, *Pseudo-Dionysius: The Complete Works* (Paulist Press, 1987; in copyright).

#### interactive fiction & ludology [f] — orientation note
Interactive fiction begins with *Colossal Cave Adventure* (Will Crowther, written during the 1975–76 academic year — Crowther dated it "1975, give or take a year"; released on the ARPANET in early 1976; the canonical expanded version by Don Woods followed in May 1977), leading to Infocom and *Zork* (1977–80). The ludology-vs-narratology debate frames game studies: Espen Aarseth, *Cybertext: Perspectives on Ergodic Literature* (Johns Hopkins UP, 1997); Gonzalo Frasca coined "ludology" in his 1999 essay "Ludology Meets Narratology" (first published in Finnish in *Parnasso* 1999, no. 3; English at ludology.org); Jesper Juul (*Half-Real*, 2005) advanced the games-as-rules view. **Confidence: HIGH.** Entry point: Aarseth (1997) and Nick Montfort, *Twisty Little Passages* (2003) for IF specifically.

## Caveats
- **Euler "1736" is a convention** — presentation (1735), volume date (1736), and actual printing (1741) differ; preserved on the card.
- **Pseudo-Dionysius dating** is now tightened to c. 485–518/528 CE (late 5th–early 6th c.) on the SEP's Proclus/Severus argument, rather than a flat "c. 500."
- **Eco's *Trattato di semiotica generale*** is occasionally mis-dated 1976; the correct original is **1975** (Italian) → **1976** (English, *A Theory of Semiotics*).
- **AI-alignment praxis** is a living, contested, fast-moving field; named "origins" are provisional and the community's account of its own history remains in flux — flag on the card.
- **Dumézil's trifunctional hypothesis** is securely attributed but its scholarly *validity* is heavily contested.
- **The apophatic strands** (Dionysian, Eckhartian, Madhyamaka, Upaniṣadic, and Sufi *fanāʾ*) are presented as a cross-traditional *family*, not one genealogical lineage; direct-influence vs. independent-convergence is unsettled.
- **Saussure's *Cours*** was assembled posthumously from students' notes; its fidelity to Saussure's own views is a standing scholarly debate — note when citing it as the structuralist origin.
- **"Situating Constructionism" publisher** is now resolved to Ablex (Norwood, NJ), 1991; the Erlbaum citations seen elsewhere conflate it with Papert's separate 1996 chapter.
