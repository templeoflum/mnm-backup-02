## RUNG ZERO · calibration — sampling theory

**the instrument passed.** the 1948/1949 distinction was caught unaided: the sampling theorem's formal statement + proof lives in the **1949** paper; the **1948** paper founds a *different* field (information theory). the three candidates declared on the map all confirm, with the origin-vs-codification seam intact.

- **origin —** H. Nyquist, "Certain Topics in Telegraph Transmission Theory," *Trans. AIEE* 47 (Feb 1928), 617–644. precursor: showed ~2B independent samples per bandwidth B, but did not treat sampling/reconstruction of continuous signals as such. **[confirmed]**
- **codification —** C. E. Shannon, "Communication in the Presence of Noise," *Proc. IRE* 37(1) (Jan 1949), 10–21. sampling theorem as Theorem 1, explicit statement + proof; credits the "Nyquist interval." **[confirmed]**
- **adjacent origin (different field) —** C. E. Shannon, "A Mathematical Theory of Communication," *Bell System Technical Journal* 27 (Jul & Oct 1948), 379–423, 623–656. founds information theory (entropy, channel capacity, the bit) — *not* the sampling-theorem source. the exact error loose sources make. **[confirmed]**
- **lineage carried (WKS) —** the theorem is genuinely multi-claimant: E. T. Whittaker 1915 (cardinal series); V. A. Kotelnikov 1933 (independent, communications framing); J. M. Whittaker 1935; Küpfmüller 1928; Raabe 1939; Someya 1949; Gabor 1946. scholarly name **Whittaker–Kotelnikov–Shannon**. per Meijering, "exactly how/when/why Nyquist's name attached remains obscure." **[confirmed]**
- **calibration takeaway —** the three-tier hunt pattern survives first contact. promote it from FLOATING. distinguish the *implying precursor* from the *formal statement*; carry multi-claimant fields as lineages. this seam is the bench every later card was cut against.

# CHUNK 0 · THE MEASURING CANON (dive 0)

## slice 00 · the patient's mathematics (13 rungs)

**analytic number theory [s]**
- origin — P. G. L. Dirichlet, theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions (1837), introducing Dirichlet characters & L-functions; "father of analytic number theory." **[confirmed]**
- codification — B. Riemann, "Über die Anzahl der Primzahlen unter einer gegebenen Größe," *Monatsber. Berliner Akad.* (1859) — complex-analytic ζ(s). **[confirmed]**
- door — T. M. Apostol, *Introduction to Analytic Number Theory* (Springer, 1976); Davenport's notes circulate free.

**multiplicative number theory [s]**
- origin — Euler product (Euler, "Variae observationes circa series infinitas," 1737) linking ∏(1−p⁻ˢ)⁻¹ to ζ(s); extended by Dirichlet 1837. lineage Euler→Dirichlet. **[confirmed for lineage]**
- codification — H. Davenport, *Multiplicative Number Theory* (Markham, 1967; Springer GTM 74). **[confirmed]**
- door — Davenport; Apostol.

**diophantine approximation [s]**
- origin — Dirichlet's approximation theorem (1842, pigeonhole); Liouville (1844) built the first transcendentals via approximation bounds. present Dirichlet (approximation) + Liouville (transcendence). **[candidate, partially confirmed]**
- codification — J. W. S. Cassels, *An Introduction to Diophantine Approximation* (Cambridge Tracts, 1957). **[candidate, partially confirmed]**
- door — Cassels; Khinchin, *Continued Fractions* (Dover) for the CF route.

**continued fractions [s]**
- origin — Euler, "De fractionibus continuis dissertatio" (1737/1744) — first systematic theory; precursors Bombelli, Wallis, Brouncker. **[candidate, partially confirmed]**
- codification — O. Perron, *Die Lehre von den Kettenbrüchen* (Teubner, 1913); A. Ya. Khinchin, *Continued Fractions* (1935 Russian / 1964 English). **[candidate, partially confirmed]**
- door — Khinchin, *Continued Fractions* (Dover).

**sieve methods [t]**
- origin — sieve of Eratosthenes (ancient) as namesake; the modern combinatorial sieve is Viggo Brun, "Über das Goldbachsche Gesetz und die Anzahl der Primzahlpaare," *Archiv for Math. og Naturvidenskab* B34(8) (1915). **[confirmed for Brun]**
- door — Cojocaru & Murty, *An Introduction to Sieve Methods and Their Applications* (Cambridge, 2005); reference Halberstam & Richert, *Sieve Methods* (1974).

**prime-gap statistics [s]**
- origin — Bertrand's postulate (1845; Chebyshev 1852) and large-gap results (Westzynthius 1931) as precursors; the probabilistic framing is Cramér (1936), "On the order of magnitude of the difference between consecutive prime numbers," *Acta Arithmetica* (Cramér random model). diffuse origin. **[candidate, unconfirmed]** for any single founder
- codification — no single treatise; lives inside ANT monographs (Montgomery & Vaughan, *Multiplicative Number Theory I*, 2007). **[candidate, unconfirmed]**
- door — survey literature (Soundararajan on small gaps; the Polymath8 / Zhang–Maynard–Tao bounded-gaps work, free on arXiv).

**elementary number theory [s]**
- origin — Euclid, *Elements* VII–IX (c. 300 BCE) — infinitude of primes, Euclidean algorithm. **[confirmed, canon]**
- codification — C. F. Gauss, *Disquisitiones Arithmeticae* (1801) — congruences, quadratic reciprocity. **[confirmed]**
- door — Hardy & Wright, *An Introduction to the Theory of Numbers* (1938); gentler: Burton.

**complex analysis [s]**
- origin — A.-L. Cauchy (1814–1825), Cauchy integral theorem/formula; co-founders Riemann (1851) and Weierstrass. **[confirmed]**
- codification — Whittaker & Watson, *A Course of Modern Analysis* (1902); modern standard Ahlfors, *Complex Analysis* (1953). **[confirmed]**
- door — Ahlfors; free MIT/Wisconsin OCW notes; Needham, *Visual Complex Analysis* for intuition.

**special functions & series acceleration [t]**
- origin — Euler (gamma function 1729–30; Euler–Maclaurin summation; Euler transform); 18th–19th c. tradition (Bessel, Legendre, Gauss hypergeometric). field origin diffuse. **[candidate, partially confirmed]**
- door — Abramowitz & Stegun, *Handbook of Mathematical Functions* (NBS, 1964, public-domain PDF); modern open-access **NIST DLMF (dlmf.nist.gov)**.

**asymptotic analysis [s]**
- origin — H. Poincaré, "Sur les intégrales irrégulières des équations linéaires," *Acta Math.* (1886) — rigorous asymptotic series; precursors Stirling (1730), Laplace. **[candidate, partially confirmed]**
- codification — N. G. de Bruijn, *Asymptotic Methods in Analysis* (1958). **[candidate, partially confirmed]**
- door — de Bruijn (Dover); Bender & Orszag, *Advanced Mathematical Methods for Scientists and Engineers*.

**harmonic/Fourier analysis [s]** · *pays G·fourier*
- origin — J. Fourier, *Théorie analytique de la chaleur* (1822; precursor memoir 1807/1811) — trigonometric series for heat. **[confirmed, canon]**
- codification — A. Zygmund, *Trigonometric Series* (1935); for Euclidean harmonic analysis, Stein & Weiss, *Introduction to Fourier Analysis on Euclidean Spaces* (1971). **[confirmed]**
- door — Stein & Shakarchi, *Fourier Analysis: An Introduction* (Princeton, 2003); gentler: Körner, *Fourier Analysis*.

**real analysis & limits [s]**
- origin — Cauchy, *Cours d'analyse* (1821, rigorous limits); Bolzano (1817) and Weierstrass (ε–δ, 1860s) complete the arithmetization. lineage Cauchy/Bolzano/Weierstrass. **[confirmed]**
- codification — W. Rudin, *Principles of Mathematical Analysis* (1953) as the modern codifying text; the rigor program culminated with Weierstrass/Dedekind. **[confirmed]**
- door — free: Tao, *Analysis I*; Abbott, *Understanding Analysis*; canonical "Baby Rudin."

**approximation theory [t]**
- origin — Chebyshev (1850s, best uniform approximation / Chebyshev polynomials) and the Weierstrass approximation theorem (1885). **[confirmed for both results]**
- door — Trefethen, *Approximation Theory and Approximation Practice* (SIAM; draft chapters free); classic Cheney, *Introduction to Approximation Theory*.
