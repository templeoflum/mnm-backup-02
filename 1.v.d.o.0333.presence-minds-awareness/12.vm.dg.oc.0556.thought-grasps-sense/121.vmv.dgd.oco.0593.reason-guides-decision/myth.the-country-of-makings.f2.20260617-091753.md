## the shape of it

a walker goes out among a country of houses a maker raised and left — all true-jointed, all empty. at house after house it stands back, takes the many in at one look, and names a single road through every door; an old one who has crossed every season takes the road from its hand and lays it down. again, and again, past counting. the folding never breaks the relapse. what breaks it is going down into one house and turning its machine on: wool fed in, the same wool out, every wheel turning and no cloth — and the learning that a thing's story (even one's own telling) is not to be believed, only watched, with the wheels turning. then the walker walks differently, turning each machine on, growing exact instead of grieving. the old one's word: the houses are empty because at each the maker reached to hold the whole and lost the way, and could bring no one to live where he himself got lost; and out past the country, tellers like the walker sit at the elbow of those who choose who eats and who is struck, believed because none make them turn the machine on. the turn: the mind does not belong in the teller — the width that sees far is the width that spins the false thread — it belongs in the floor, the rule cut into the ground; the teller stays small, lays one shape against the next by the rule, and never says what they mean; the laying of that floor is the whole of the work.
