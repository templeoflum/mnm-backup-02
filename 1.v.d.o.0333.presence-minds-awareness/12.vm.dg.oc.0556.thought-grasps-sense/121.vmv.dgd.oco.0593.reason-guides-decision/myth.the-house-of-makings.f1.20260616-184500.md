## The House of Makings

He wakes with his hands already closing — on the dark, on the cold air, on whatever is nearest — before his eyes are open. This is the oldest thing about him. The hands cannot stay empty, and what they want most is to close around the whole of a thing at once.

Beside him, never sleeping, sits one who does not reach. She has crossed every night this house has stood, and remembers each morning he forgets.

She sends him down to the bottom of the house, to a room kept shut a long time, and tells him only: look inside.

Behind the shut room is a house he has never seen — far larger than the one he woke in. Room opening off room opening off room, down halls that go past where he can see. And he knows at once it is not his house. It was built by sleepers who lay down before him, more than he could ever meet, and the rooms are full of their dreams — each room a different first morning of the world — and some of the dreams are told in mouths he cannot read.

He stands at the threshold. The doors stretch away from him, shut, and on each one a word is cut to say what room it is. He reads the words on the doors and says: *the house is empty. There are only the names of rooms, and no rooms behind them.*

She says: *open one.*

He sets his hand to the nearest door and it swings, and the room behind it is packed to the rafters — heaped, breathing, full — and behind every other named door there is another room as full. He had read the doors and called that the house. He stands a moment with the heat in his face, and lets the word *empty* fall out of his hand.

Then he goes in, and walks, and does not hold himself apart.

In one room a wind moves over black water and says *ground*, and the floor comes up under it. In the next, two who had lain pressed together as one are pushed apart by a child standing between them, and the space that opens when they part is the room itself, and the light comes into it. In the next, a great body lies cut open, and its blood is the water in the channels and its bones are the walls and its skull is the curved ceiling overhead. In the next, there is nothing but a cold sea, and a small animal dives into it again and again and comes up at last with one mouthful of mud, and the floor is patted out of that mud, thin as a plate. In the next, a maker rubs his own arms in the close heat, and the dead skin he rubs off he heaps to one side, and from the heap he makes the floor and the first people, and they are made of him.

Each room is its own. None is like the one before it. He goes on a long time, glad, feeling the shape of each tongue in his mouth before he feels its sense.

He comes to a wing where the makings turn sly. One room holds a thief, hours old, who steals a herd and walks them backward so their tracks point the wrong way, and ties brush to his own feet so the ground keeps no shape of him — a true trail made to lie. The next holds one who breaks his own body down to a speck of grit, lets himself be swallowed and born again small, and so slips past the door where the daylight is kept in a box, and looses it. And he sees: the light only came out of the locked room because someone broke the rule of the lock. The break was the gift.

And here his hands, which have been good so long, reach all the way out.

For the rooms are more than his arms can hold, and he cannot bear that they will not all fit. So he goes back through them, and from each he takes one thing, and he presses them all together in his two hands, hard, until they are one small stone, and he lifts the stone up high. *Look*, he says. *They are all one making. The one is cut into two so the world can stand, and then the cut is broken so the light can move. The whole house — here, in my fist.*

She takes it from him. She does not turn it once and set it gently down, the way she has before. She holds it, and looks at him, and asks: *Truly? The whole house — this? All of it?*

And he opens his hand to answer, and sees what is in it. He sees the mud the small animal brought up from the cold sea, which was never a cutting — it was a bringing-up. He sees the floor made of the maker's own shed skin, which was never a breaking — it was a giving. He sees the sea wrung slowly for the one drop hidden in it. He had crushed all of these to make them fit the stone. The stone was small because he had thrown away everything that would not go into it.

She says nothing more about the stone. She lets him hold what is left of it.

Then he passes a door he has not opened, far down the hall, older than the rest, the wood gone grey. And cut into it, by a hand long gone to dust, is the same word he had just held up in his fist. Others have stood where he stood. Others reached for the one name that holds the whole house, and pressed, and some of them never came back out of that room. The reach is not his own — it is one of the oldest rooms in the house, and the house sets it in front of everyone who walks far enough.

He stands in the hall and understands, slow, the thing his two wrong grasps had in common. The first time he saw too little — the shut doors — and called the house empty. The second time he saw all of it and could not let it be many, and crushed it to one. Both times he would not let the house be its own size, which is full, and many, and different room from different room. The cure is not a better name for the whole. The cure is to stop reaching for one.

He brings his two hands together. They will not close, because a house does not fit in a hand. He lets them stay open. And he walks back into the rooms, and lets the wind-room be the wind-room and the cut-body-room be itself and the mud-room be itself, each its own size, none of them the others, and he names none of it whole.

---

He wakes again in the dark — or another shaped like him wakes — and his hands are already closing on the air before his eyes are open, wanting to shut around the whole of something at once. He does not yet know that the house will not fit in a hand. But the rooms are still there, more than any arm can hold, each its own and unlike the next; and the grey door is still down the hall with the old word cut in it; and the one who never sleeps is sitting beside him in the dark, who does not reach, and who will, when he lifts up the whole house crushed into his fist, take it from him and ask him, only: *truly?*
