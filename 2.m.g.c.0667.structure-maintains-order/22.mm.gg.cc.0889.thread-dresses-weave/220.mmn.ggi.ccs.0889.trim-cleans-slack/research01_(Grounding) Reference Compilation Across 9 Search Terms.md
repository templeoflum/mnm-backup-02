## AREA 1: Ontic Structural Realism (structure-as-fundamental; identity from relations)

### Point 1.1 — "ontic structural realism identity invariants"

**Canonical/foundational sources:**
- James Ladyman, "What is Structural Realism?" *Studies in History and Philosophy of Science* 29(3), 1998 — the paper that introduced the epistemic/ontic distinction and launched OSR.
- James Ladyman & Don Ross (with David Spurrett & John Collier), *Every Thing Must Go: Metaphysics Naturalized*, Oxford University Press, 2007 — the canonical book-length OSR statement; Ch. 3 ("Ontic Structural Realism and the Philosophy of Physics"). Slogan: "There are no things. Structure is all there is."
- Steven French, *The Structure of the World: Metaphysics and Representation*, Oxford University Press, 2014 — major monograph defending eliminativist OSR.
- Steven French & James Ladyman, "Remodelling Structural Realism: Quantum Physics and the Metaphysics of Structure," *Synthese* 136, 2003.

**Key technical content / definitions:**
- OSR: the view that relational/modal structure is ontologically fundamental and does not supervene on the intrinsic properties of individuals; the identity and individuality of objects depends on the relational structure of the world.
- "Identity invariants" connects to physical invariance features cited as support for OSR: permutation invariance in many-particle QM (Muller 2009), quantum entanglement (Esfeld 2004; Lam 2010a in QFT), gauge invariance (Lyre 2004), and diffeomorphism invariance/background independence in GTR (Pooley 2006; Rickles 2006; Esfeld & Lam 2008).
- "ExtOSR" (Extended OSR), Holger Lyre — treats structural properties (relational + structurally-derived intrinsic properties or "structure invariants") as ontological primitives; designed to accommodate gauge symmetry invariants and "zero-value properties."

**Recent frontier (2024–2026):**
- James Ladyman & Lorenzo Lorenzetti, "Effective Ontic Structural Realism," *British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* (published online December 2023; print forthcoming) — unifies "effective realism" proposals (Fraser & Vickers; Williams; Fraser; Robertson & Wilson) via realism about modal structure and "scale relativity of ontology."

**Active researchers:** James Ladyman, Steven French, Don Ross, Michael Esfeld, Holger Lyre, Vincent Lam, Lorenzo Lorenzetti, Matteo Morganti (metaphysical coherentism alternative), Nora Berenstain (OSR and modality).

### Point 1.2 — "Universal Theory of Structure Hamlin ante rem structuralism"

**VERIFICATION: CONFIRMED REAL.** This is a genuine published work.
- **Colin Hamlin, "The universal theory of structure: a fundamental ontology for ontic structural realism," *Synthese* (2025).** DOI: 10.1007/s11229-025-05425-5. Also on PhilArchive (HAMTUT) and PhilSci-Archive (item 27806).

**Content/definitions:**
- Argues OSR holds structure is ontologically fundamental but lacks a precise metaphysical account of structure. Draws on ante rem structuralism and its formal axiomatizations to define **Structure Theory (ST)**: the view that structures exist sui generis and constitute the subject matter of mathematics.
- ST forces OSR to confront its "collapse problem" (distinguishing physical from mathematical structure). Hamlin embraces the collapse via Tegmark's **Mathematical Universe Hypothesis (MUH)**, identifying the physical universe as an ante rem structure.
- This yields the **Universal Theory of Structure (UTS)** — a fundamental ontology of structures for both mathematical and physical reality, refining OSR's thesis from "physical reality is structural" to "physical reality is an ante rem structure."

**Prior related work by same author:** Hamlin (2017), "Towards a theory of universes: structure theory and the mathematical universe hypothesis" — defended a universal ontology of ante rem structures based on Shapiro's (1997) formalization.

**Active researcher:** Colin Hamlin.

### Point 1.3 — "structural property invariance definability Korbmacher Schiemer Assadian"

**VERIFICATION: ALL THREE CONFIRMED REAL.**

**Canonical paper:**
- **Johannes Korbmacher & Georg Schiemer, "What Are Structural Properties?," *Philosophia Mathematica* 26(3), October 2018, pp. 295–323.** DOI: 10.1093/philmat/nkx011.
  - Presents two formal explications: the **definability account** (structural properties = properties definable purely in terms of the primitive relations of mathematical theories) and the **invariance account** (structural properties = properties invariant under isomorphism / holding of all structurally similar objects).
  - Key finding: the two characterizations do NOT determine the same class of mathematical properties.
  - Connects to the "Carnap thesis" (a property is structural iff its extension is invariant under isomorphic copies) and the analogous "Tarski–Sher thesis" for logicality (invariance under arbitrary permutations of the domain).

**Assadian's work:**
- **Bahram Assadian, "Mathematical Structuralism and Bundle Theory," *Ratio* 37(2–3), 2024, pp. 123–133.** Offers a bundle-theoretic account: a mathematical object is the mereological bundle of its structural properties (a form of mereological essentialism).
- Bahram Assadian, "The insubstantiality of mathematical objects as positions in structures," *Inquiry* 68(7):1626–1650 (received 14 Apr 2021, accepted 30 Aug 2021, published online 5 Mar 2022, assigned to the 2025 print volume; DOI 10.1080/0020174X.2021.1990120).
- Bahram Assadian, "In defence of utterly indiscernible entities," *Philosophical Studies* 176(10), 2019.
- Bahram Assadian, "The semantic plights of the ante-rem structuralist," *Philosophical Studies* 175(12):3195–3215, 2018 (first published online 2017; DOI 10.1007/s11098-017-1001-7).
- Bahram Assadian & Rob Fraser, "The individuation of mathematical objects," 2024.

**Recent frontier (2024–2026):**
- Korbmacher & Schiemer, "Logical Concepts and Structural Properties" — work-in-progress presented at the Workshop on Invariance, Objectivity, Symmetry, MCMP, LMU Munich, 9–10 October 2024. Argues logical concepts can be viewed as a limit case of structural properties via parallel invariance/definability criteria.

**Active researchers:** Johannes Korbmacher (Utrecht), Georg Schiemer (Vienna), Bahram Assadian (Leeds/Bristol), John Wigglesworth (co-author with Schiemer on "The Structuralist Thesis Reconsidered," *BJPS* 70(4), 2019).

## AREA 2: Categorical Foundations (things have no determinate make-up, only relations)

### Point 2.1 — "category theory foundations Lawvere ETCS structural set theory"

**Canonical/foundational sources:**
- **William Lawvere, "An Elementary Theory of the Category of Sets," *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (USA)* 52, 1964, pp. 1506–1511.** The origin of ETCS. (Grew out of Lawvere's 1963 teaching of undergraduate analysis foundations at Reed College.)
- Long version with commentary: William Lawvere & Colin McLarty, "An Elementary Theory of the Category of Sets (long version) with commentary," *Reprints in Theory and Applications of Categories* 11, 2005, pp. 1–35.
- William Lawvere & Robert Rosebrugh, *Sets for Mathematics*, Cambridge University Press, 2003.

**Technical definitions:**
- **ETCS**: a first-order axiomatization of the category of sets as a well-pointed topos with a natural numbers object satisfying the axiom of choice. It is the prototypical **structural set theory** (a.k.a. categorial set theory).
- Sets are "abstract structures": an element x∈S has no properties beyond being an element of S; membership is local and relative (meaningful only between an element and a subset of the same set), contrasting with ZFC's global ∈ relation. ETCS is weaker than ZFC.
- McLarty (1993) argues ETCS resolves Benacerraf's (1965) identification problem.

**Related/subsequent work:**
- Michael Shulman, "Comparing Material and Structural Set Theories," arXiv:1808.05204 — formal comparison of well-pointed (pre)topos theories with material/membership-based set theories.
- Erik Palmgren, "Constructivist and Structuralist Foundations: Bishop's and Lawvere's Theories of Sets" (CETCS), arXiv:1201.6272.
- Tom Leinster, "Rethinking Set Theory" — accessible exposition that ETCS axioms can be stated without explicit category-theoretic language.
- SEAR — a stronger alternative structural set theory.

**Active researchers / lineage:** F. William Lawvere (d. 2022), Colin McLarty, Saunders Mac Lane (advocated ETCS as alternative foundations), Michael Shulman, Tom Leinster, Steve Awodey.

### Point 2.2 — "ante rem structuralism mathematical objects relations Shapiro"

**Canonical/foundational source:**
- **Stewart Shapiro, *Philosophy of Mathematics: Structure and Ontology*, Oxford University Press, 1997.** The principal statement of ante rem (non-eliminative) structuralism.

**Technical definitions:**
- **Ante rem structuralism**: structures exist sui generis, ontologically prior to and independent of any system that instantiates them (terminology drawn from medieval debates on universals). Mathematical objects (e.g., natural numbers) are *places in structures* — "offices rather than officeholders."
- Slogan (Shapiro 1997, p. 9): "Structures are prior to places in the same sense that any organization is prior to the offices that constitute it." The natural number structure is prior to "6" as "baseball defense" is prior to "shortstop."
- Structures have a dual nature: universals (instantiable by many systems) but also particulars (named by singular terms, treated as objects). Shapiro develops an axiomatic **structure theory** specifying which structures exist.
- Contrasts with eliminative structuralism and modal structuralism (Hellman 1989, *Mathematics Without Numbers*), which are antirealist in ontology.

**Related/critical work:**
- Geoffrey Hellman, *Mathematics Without Numbers* (1989) — modal-structural (eliminative) alternative.
- Michael Resnik, *Mathematics as a Science of Patterns* (1997).
- Paul Benacerraf, "What Numbers Could Not Be," *Philosophical Review* 74, 1965 — the foundational problem motivating structuralism.
- Fraser MacBride, "Structuralism Reconsidered" (2005); Charles Parsons, *Mathematical Thought and Its Objects* (2008).

**Note (categorical/univalent crossover):** A 2024 frontier work connecting this area to univalent foundations is Lu Chen, "Univalence and Ontic Structuralism," *Foundations of Physics* 54(3), 2024 (listed for completeness; structuralist-foundations crossover).

**Active researchers:** Stewart Shapiro (Ohio State), Geoffrey Hellman, Øystein Linnebo, Hannes Leitgeb (non-eliminative structuralism, unlabeled graphs), Fraser MacBride.

### Point 2.3 — "identity of indiscernibles structuralism mathematics"

**Canonical/foundational sources (the indiscernibility / "Burgess–Keränen" objection):**
- Jukka Keränen, "The Identity Problem for Realist Structuralism," *Philosophia Mathematica* 9, 2001, pp. 308–330 — the founding statement of the objection.
- John Burgess, review of Shapiro, *Notre Dame Journal of Formal Logic* 40, 1999.
- The objection: ante rem structuralism is committed to the structural identity of patently non-identical objects in non-rigid structures (e.g., the complex roots i and −i, which are related by a non-trivial automorphism and so share all structural properties).

**Responses and developments:**
- **James Ladyman, "Mathematical Structuralism and the Identity of Indiscernibles," *Analysis* 65(3), 2005, pp. 218–221** — proposes **weak discernibility** (i and −i are weakly discernible via irreflexive relations).
- Jeffrey Ketland, "Structuralism and the Identity of Indiscernibles," *Analysis* 66(4), 2006, pp. 303–315 — counterexample to Ladyman's indiscernibility principle; on definability of identity in relational structures.
- Hannes Leitgeb & James Ladyman, "Criteria of Identity and Structuralist Ontology," *Philosophia Mathematica* 16(3), 2008, pp. 388–396 — argue identity in structures may be taken as primitive, not requiring a criterion.
- **Stewart Shapiro, "Identity, Indiscernibility, and Ante Rem Structuralism: The Tale of i and −i," *Philosophia Mathematica* 16(3), 2008, pp. 285–309.**
- Tim Button, "Realistic Structuralism's Identity Crisis: A Hybrid Solution," *Analysis* 66(3), 2006.
- Christopher Menzel, "Haecceities and Mathematical Structuralism," *Philosophia Mathematica* 26(1), 2018.
- Rafael de Clercq (2012); Callum Duguid, "Graph Theory and the Identity of Indiscernibles," *Dialectica* 70(3), 2016.

**Recent frontier (2024–2026):**
- Bahram Assadian, "Mathematical Structuralism and Bundle Theory," *Ratio* 37, 2024 — discussed in the *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* "Identity of Indiscernibles" entry (Fall 2025 edition) as part of the recent debate.
- "Structuralism, indiscernibility, and physical computation," *Synthese* (2022) — extends the indiscernibility objection to computational structuralism (AND/OR gate duals).

**Active researchers:** James Ladyman, Stewart Shapiro, Jeffrey Ketland, Hannes Leitgeb, Fraser MacBride, Bahram Assadian, Simon Saunders, F.A. Muller.

**Cross-reference (physics analogue):** The weak-discernibility debate has a physics branch — Simon Saunders & F.A. Muller, "Discerning Fermions," *BJPS* 59(3), 2008; F.A. Muller & M.P. Seevinck, "Discerning Elementary Particles" (arXiv:0905.3273); Nick Huggett & Josh Norton, "Weak Discernibility for Quanta, the Right Way"; Adam Caulton, "Discerning 'Indistinguishable' Quantum Systems" (arXiv:1409.0249).

## AREA 3: Measurement Theory (what makes a quantity measurable; ratio vs. interval)

### Point 3.1 — "representational theory of measurement Krantz Luce Suppes"

**Canonical/foundational sources (the three-volume *Foundations of Measurement*):**
- **Vol. I: David H. Krantz, R. Duncan Luce, Patrick Suppes & Amos Tversky, *Foundations of Measurement, Vol. I: Additive and Polynomial Representations*, Academic Press, 1971.**
- **Vol. II: Suppes, Krantz, Luce & Tversky, *Foundations of Measurement, Vol. II: Geometrical, Threshold, and Probabilistic Representations*, Academic Press, 1989.**
- **Vol. III: Luce, Krantz, Suppes & Tversky, *Foundations of Measurement, Vol. III: Representation, Axiomatization, and Invariance*, Academic Press, 1990.**

**Earlier foundational papers:**
- Dana Scott & Patrick Suppes, "Foundational Aspects of Theories of Measurement," *Journal of Symbolic Logic* 23(2), 1958.
- Patrick Suppes & Joseph Zinnes, "Basic Measurement Theory," in *Handbook of Mathematical Psychology* Vol. I (eds. Luce, Bush, Galanter), Wiley, 1963.
- Otto Hölder, "Die Axiome der Quantität und die Lehre vom Mass," 1901 — foundational axiomatization of measurable (extensive) quantity; Hölder's theorem.

**Technical definitions:**
- **Representational Theory of Measurement (RTM)**: measurement is the construction of a homomorphism from an empirical relational structure (objects + qualitative empirical relations/operations) into a numerical relational structure; a representation theorem proves such a mapping exists, and a uniqueness theorem specifies how unique it is (determining the scale type).

**Critical/interpretive literature:**
- Louis Narens, *Abstract Measurement Theory*, MIT Press, 1985; *Theories of Meaningfulness*, 2002.
- Patrick Suppes, *Representation and Invariance of Scientific Structures*, CSLI, 2002.
- Joel Michell — realist critique of RTM (operationalism critique).
- Conrad Heilmann, "A New Interpretation of the Representational Theory of Measurement," *Philosophy of Science* 82(5), 2015 — proposes viewing RTM as "a library of theorems" about numerical representability of qualitative relations.
- Eran Tal, "Two Myths of Representational Measurement," *Perspectives on Science* 29(6), 2021.

**Recent frontier (2024–2026):**
- Davide Rizza (University of East Anglia), "Measurement theory in the context of scientific enquiry," *Synthese* vol. 206, art. 32, 1 July 2025, DOI 10.1007/s11229-025-05109-0 — argues recent evaluations of measurement theory, naming "Heilmann (Philosophy of Science 82:787–797, 2015); Philippi (Philosophy of Science 88:929-939, 2021); Tal (Perspectives on Science 29:701–741, 2021)," dislocate RTM from the context of scientific enquiry, and offers a mathematically more nuanced account of its results.
- Elina Vessonen, "Representation in Measurement," *European Journal for Philosophy of Science* 11(3), 2021 ("Representation Minimalism").

**Active researchers:** Louis Narens, Conrad Heilmann, Eran Tal, Elina Vessonen, J.E. Wolff, Davide Rizza.

### Point 3.2 — "ratio scale interval scale measurement invariance meaningfulness"

**Canonical/foundational sources:**
- **S.S. Stevens, "On the Theory of Scales of Measurement," *Science* 103, 1946, pp. 677–680.** The original nominal/ordinal/interval/ratio typology; the criterion was *invariance of a statistic under the scale's group of admissible transformations*.
- S.S. Stevens, "Mathematics, Measurement and Psychophysics," in *Handbook of Experimental Psychology*, Wiley, 1951.

**Technical definitions:**
- Scale types defined by their group of admissible transformations: **ratio scale** — similarity transformations x ↦ ax (a>0); **interval scale** — positive affine x ↦ ax+b; **ordinal** — monotone increasing; **nominal** — bijections; also **log-interval**.
- **Meaningfulness**: a statement involving scales is meaningful iff its truth value is invariant under admissible transformations of the scales (Narens, Luce).

**Rigorous development of scale types:**
- Theodore Alper (1985, 1987) — classification of scale types.
- **Louis Narens, "A General Theory of Ratio Scalability with Remarks About the Measurement-Theoretic Concept of Meaningfulness," *Theory and Decision* 13, 1981, pp. 1–70.**
- R. Duncan Luce (1978, "Dimensionally Invariant Numerical Laws Correspond to Meaningful Qualitative Relations," *Philosophy of Science* 45; 1986, 1987, 2001).
- Narens & Luce, "The Algebra of Measurement," *Journal of Pure and Applied Algebra* 8, 1976; "Meaningfulness and Invariance," in *The New Palgrave* (1990).
- Luce (1997) quote: "S.S. Stevens... claimed that what counted was having an interval or ratio scale. Subsequent research has given meaning to this assertion, but... it is doubtful if he understood it himself."

**Debates/criticism:**
- Ongoing dispute over Stevens' prescriptions for permissible statistics (e.g., the Chalmers debate, "A Measurement Is a Choice and Stevens' Scales of Measurement Do Not Help Make It," 2019).
- Wilfried Ellermeier & Louis Narens — "Stevens' Direct Scaling Methods and the Uniqueness Problem," *Psychometrika*, 2004.

**Active researchers:** Louis Narens, R. Duncan Luce (d. 2012), J.E. Wolff, Fred Roberts.

### Point 3.3 — "metrology foundations unit definition self-reference SI"

**VERIFICATION NOTE:** The seed "Paul Hölder" appears to be an error for **Otto Hölder** (1901), the foundational quantity-axiomatization author.

**Primary sources:**
- **BIPM, *The International System of Units (SI), 9th edition*, Bureau International des Poids et Mesures, 2019** — defines the seven base units via seven fixed defining constants (ΔνCs, c, h, e, k, N_A, K_cd), effective 20 May 2019. The second is defined via the cesium-133 hyperfine transition frequency; the metre via c and the second; the kilogram via the Planck constant h together with the metre and second (h = 6.62607015×10⁻³⁴ J·s exactly).
- **JCGM 200:2012, *International Vocabulary of Metrology (VIM), 3rd edition*** — defines "measurand," "definitional uncertainty," etc.

**The 2019 SI redefinition (background):**
- 26th CGPM, Versailles, 16 November 2018 — unanimous vote to redefine the kilogram, ampere, kelvin, and mole in terms of fixed fundamental constants; effective 20 May 2019. The kilogram was the last unit defined by a physical artifact (the IPK).

**Coordination problem and circularity (philosophy of metrology):**
- **Hasok Chang, *Inventing Temperature: Measurement and Scientific Progress*, Oxford University Press, 2004** — foundational source on the coordination problem and "epistemic iteration"; how to justify a measurement standard without circular reliance on the instruments themselves.
- **Eran Tal, "Making Time: A Study in the Epistemology of Measurement," *British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* 67(1), 2016, pp. 297–335** (online 2014) — model-based account of measurement standardization, central case study: standardization of atomic time.
- **Eran Tal, "Individuating Quantities," *Philosophical Studies* 176(4), 2019, pp. 853–878** — argues foundationalist criteria of quantity individuation are "either empty or circular," proposing a coherentist account.
- Eran Tal, "Old and New Problems in Philosophy of Measurement," *Philosophy Compass* 8(12), 2013; "Measurement in Science," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (2015, rev.).
- Eran Tal, "Naturalness and Convention in the International System of Units," *Measurement* 116, 2018, pp. 631–643.
- **Ingvar Johansson, "Constancy and Circularity in the SI," PhilSci-Archive, 2014** — the most on-point philosophical treatment of circularity: defining units via constants whose numerical values are expressed in those same units generates circularity.
- **Nadine de Courtenay, Olivier Darrigol & Oliver Schlaudt (eds.), *The Reform of the International System of Units (SI): Philosophical, Historical and Sociological Issues*, Routledge, 2019** — key edited volume (incl. Susan G. Sterrett, "Relations Between Units and Relations Between Quantities").
- Nadine de Courtenay, "On the Philosophical Significance of the Reform of the SI: A Double-Adjustment Account of Scientific Enquiry," *Perspectives on Science* 30(4), 2022.
- **Sally Riordan, "The Objectivity of Scientific Measures," *Studies in History and Philosophy of Science Part A* 50, 2015, pp. 38–47.**
- Wolfgang Pietsch, "A revolution without tooth and claw—Redefining the physical base units," *Studies in History and Philosophy of Science Part A*, vol. 46, June 2014, pp. 85–93, DOI 10.1016/j.shpsa.2014.02.004 — case study of the New SI arguing it exhibits confirmation holism and undercuts a sharp empirical–conventional distinction.
- Luca Mari, Mark Wilson & Andrew Maul, *Measurement Across the Sciences*, Springer, 2021 (2nd ed. 2023).
- Robert P. Crease, "New views on units," *Physics World*, 2018/2019 — notes the redefinition means phenomena tied to a constant end up measured by a unit defined via that same constant.

**Recent frontier (2021–2026):**
- Miguel Ohnesorge, "How Incoherent Measurement Succeeds: Coordination and Success in the Measurement of the Earth's Polar Flattening," *Studies in History and Philosophy of Science Part A* 88, 2021.
- Miguel Ohnesorge, "The Epistemic Privilege of Measurement: Motivating a Functionalist Account," *Philosophy of Science* 90(1), 2023.
- J.E. Wolff, "Coordination and Obsolescence: A Response on Behalf of Measurement Realism," *Synthese* 201(3), 2023.
- François Papale, "From the Philosophy of Measurement to the Philosophy of Classification: Generalizing the Problem of Coordination and Historical Coherentism," *Studies in History and Philosophy of Science Part A* 106, 2024.
- Mahmoud Jalloh, "Calibrating the Theory of Model Mediated Measurement," *European Journal for Philosophy of Science* 14(40), 2024.
- Alistair M.C. Isaac, "Epistemic Loops and Measurement Realism," *Philosophy of Science* 86(5), 2019.

**Relevant metaphysics-of-quantities work:**
- **J.E. Wolff, *The Metaphysics of Quantities*, Oxford University Press, 2020** — defends "substantival structuralism": quantitativeness is an irreducible feature of attributes; quantitative attributes are best understood as substantival structured spaces.

**Active researchers:** Eran Tal (McGill), Hasok Chang (Cambridge), Nadine de Courtenay, Oliver Schlaudt, Luca Mari, Sally Riordan, Miguel Ohnesorge (Toronto), J.E. Wolff (Edinburgh), Flavia Padovani, Mahmoud Jalloh.
