A contour integral counts how many zeros sit in the box; a root-finder locates them. Then you simply look at where they landed. Pan upward and keep looking.

What "derived" buys, and what it doesn't. The zeros here are found, not pasted: ζ is computed (accurate to ~10⁻¹³ across this whole window), the count comes from a contour integral with no line bias, and the locations come from a 2-D search that could land anywhere in the strip. That it keeps landing on β=½ is something you observe, not something assumed — try the off-line seeds and watch them fall back to the line. But this is still finite, and still computing ζ rather than predicting it from the primes, so it proves nothing about the infinitely many zeros above the window. It's not a proof; it's an honest place to look. The zeros it derives are exactly what the companion instrument loads and measures — the field view hands the point view its input.
