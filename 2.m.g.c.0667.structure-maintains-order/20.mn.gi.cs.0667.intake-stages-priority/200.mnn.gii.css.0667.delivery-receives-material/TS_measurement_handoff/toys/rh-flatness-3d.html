TS → RH · projection is interpretation

Orbit the zeros, and RH becomes a question about whether a ribbon lies flat.

Each zero gets lifted into three dimensions: its height γ goes up, the loudness of its wave (amplitude 2/|ρ|) gives the form a body in depth — and the one axis the Riemann Hypothesis is about, the real part β, runs left–right. RH says every β is ½, so the whole ribbon lies flat inside the critical plane. Drag to orbit. Then use the three views — because, exactly as TS warns, one of them hides the answer and another reveals it.

The TS lesson, made literal. Looking straight down the β axis, an RH-violating ribbon and a flat one look identical — the strays project right onto the plane and vanish. That projection is an interpretation that throws away the very thing you care about. Turn edge-on and the thickness is unmistakable. The off-plane extent (measured along β, so no viewing angle can fake it) is the honest invariant — it's exactly the quantity RH says is zero. These are the real first 30 zeros, loaded, not derived; the instrument can't move what arithmetic placed, only let you see whether it ever leaves the plane.
