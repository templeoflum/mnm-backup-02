TS → RH · the apparatus measures flatness

The Riemann Hypothesis is the claim that the zeros are perfectly flat.

Each zero is a point in the critical strip: horizontal = its real part β, vertical = its height γ. RH says every β equals exactly ½ — so the whole set has zero width. That's a flatness condition, and flatness is something TS already knows how to measure: the smallest PCA eigenvalue, the off-plane extent. Drag any zero off the line and watch the instrument catch it.

Flat. Every zero sits on the line. The form is a perfect needle — one PCA axis carries everything, the width axis is exactly zero.

What it can and can't do. These are the real first 30 zeros, loaded (computed, not derived from the primes) — so this instrument can't prove RH; it can't move a zero that arithmetic has already placed. What it does is make RH measurable: the off-line extent is the exact quantity RH says is zero, and the form's needle-vs-blade shape is the same FA/ellipsoid reading TS uses on any form. RH, in this frame, is simply: this form is degenerate in one dimension.
