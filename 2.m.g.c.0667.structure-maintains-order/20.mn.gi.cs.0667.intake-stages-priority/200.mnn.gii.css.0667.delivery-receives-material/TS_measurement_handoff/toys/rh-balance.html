TS → RH · β is the amplitude knob

Every wave the same loudness, or one runs away and drowns the rest.

The prime counting staircase is rebuilt as a sum of waves, one per zero — TS's epicycles, swept along log x. Each wave's loudness is set by its real part β: the amplitude grows like xβ. RH says every β is ½, so every wave grows like √x in lockstep — balanced. Push one β above ½ and that wave outgrows the others without limit, tearing the reconstruction off the true staircase.

Honest reading. The waves are the real zeros, loaded. At β=½ the reconstruction hugs the staircase and the error stays inside the √x envelope. As you raise β, you'll see the error visibly swell past √x and the reconstruction pull away — that's the runaway, and it's the verified behavior. What this can't do is read β back off the curve to decimals, or prove arithmetic keeps β at ½. It shows you what's at stake: RH is the statement that the error is as small as a sum of √x waves can be.
