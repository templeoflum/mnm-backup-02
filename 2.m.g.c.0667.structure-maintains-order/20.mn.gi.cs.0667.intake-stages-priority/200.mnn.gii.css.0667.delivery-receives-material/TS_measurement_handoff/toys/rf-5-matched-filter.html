The primes are the echo delays hidden in ζ.

The logarithmic derivative −ζ′/ζ(s) = Σ Λ(n)·n−s is, along the frequency axis, a sum of pure tones — one at each frequency log n, with weight only when n is a prime power. So if you transform it (a matched filter, correlating against a sweep of delays), the output spikes exactly at delay = log p. The primes announce themselves as the resonant delays of ζ.

What's identity, what's lens. The fact that −ζ′/ζ carries tones precisely at log of the primes is a genuine identity — the same primes↔zeros duality the explicit-formula toy is built on, just read in frequency. Calling the transform a "matched filter" is the interpretive part: a matched filter is technically the optimal detector for a known pulse in white noise, and there's no noise here — but the correlation-detects-a-known-comb picture is faithful, so I keep it as a lens, not a claim. Composite n stay dark because Λ(n)=0 there: only the atoms ring.
