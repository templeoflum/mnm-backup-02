The critical line is the one place a zero can be its own reflection.

The completed zeta function obeys a perfect mirror symmetry across β=½: ξ(s) = ξ(1−s) — verified here to machine precision. So zeros come in mirror pairs reflected across the line. A zero off the line drags a forced partner along with it; a zero on the line is its own mirror image, standing alone. Drag the amber point and watch.

Why this matters for RH. The functional equation forces the whole zero set to be symmetric across β=½ — that's a theorem, not a conjecture. The line is the symmetry's fixed set: the only locus where a zero needn't summon a partner on the far side.
