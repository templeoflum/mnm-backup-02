The Critical Instrument — load a function, choose how to see it, measure it against the unit.

One object can be embedded into space many ways and read in many coordinate systems; the instrument's job is to hold them all against a single ground truth — the unit of value 1 — so you can tell what each choice keeps and what it throws away. Loaded here: the Riemann zeros. The Hypothesis becomes one measurable question — do they lie flat in the critical plane? — and the answer must survive every way of looking.

What it is, and isn't. The zeros are loaded — computed, not derived from the primes — so the instrument can't prove RH or move what arithmetic placed; it lets you see whether the loaded zeros lie flat, under any projection. The embeddings and coordinate rulers are interpretations: each keeps a different body, and the fingerprint changes with them. The off-plane extent is measured along β specifically, so no viewing angle can fake it — it is the same number in every embedding, the ground-truth unit-1 invariant. The spacing lens reads the one thing gross shape is blind to: whether the zeros repel. The architecture is general — ζ is just the function in the chamber.

Built from first principles; the Trajectoscope was the reference for how it was made the first time.
