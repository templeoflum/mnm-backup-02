## the subject (precise now)

**The plane is a *geometry*** in the technical sense — and specifically the unit's **stage**. Two classical definitions, and we use both:
- **Klein (1872):** a geometry = the invariants of a chosen **transformation group** (what may move). Global, group-theoretic.
- **Riemann (1854):** a geometry = a **manifold with local structure** (a metric, curvature varying point to point). Local, structural.

Our plane is the toy where these two meet — which is **Cartan geometry (1920s):** a local Klein symmetry model varying over a Riemannian base, with **curvature = how far it deviates from the flat model.** That is the real name for "the bridge" (not "algebra↔geometry"). Our `C/flat` readout already measures exactly that deviation.

## the three axes (the field's, = our knobs)

| axis | source | our knob | status in the baseline |
|------|--------|----------|------------------------|
| **1 — transformation group** | Klein | **isometry** (the unit's motion) | the **strict end** of the rigidity spectrum; type follows κ |
| **2 — method / coordinate** | epistemic | **chart** (cartesian/polar) | **free** — it's gauge, relabels the same space, changes no invariant |
| **3 — local structure** | Riemann | **κ** (curvature continuum) | the **master dial** — everything geometric follows it |

**κ is the master.** Its *sign* fixes the **geometry-type** (the +/0/− trichotomy): spherical/**elliptic** (κ>0) · euclidean/**parabolic** (κ=0) · hyperbolic (κ<0). And the geometry-type determines two things that are NOT independent knobs:

- **the algebra** (Cayley–Klein): complex (i²=−1, κ>0) · dual (ε²=0, κ=0) · split (j²=+1, κ<0). It is a **face of the geometry-type, derived from κ** — shown in the readout, no longer a free button.
- **the isometry group available:** rotation at every κ; **horocycle & translate only at κ<0** (the richer SU(1,1) of the Poincaré disk). At κ≥0 only rotation is offered — invalid types are not selectable, not flagged.

## the rigidity spectrum (axis 1, where we sit and why)

Klein's axis 1 is an ordered chain — **isometry ⊂ similarity ⊂ affine ⊂ projective ⊂ topological** — looser groups, fewer invariants. We sit at the **strict (isometry) end on purpose:** that is exactly the rung where the **unit circle is preserved** (affine → ellipses, projective → conics — the unit unbinds). So **usable = bounded = isometry-preserving** is *why* the baseline lives here. Loosening toward affine/projective is "open it later," not a new axis.
