What kind of noise are the zeros?

Point an engineer's instruments at the zero heights as if they were a noise signal. Three standard tools answer "what kind of noise is this": its autocorrelation, its power spectrum, and how its fluctuations grow. Compare the real zeros against the two reference noises — random (true white noise) and rigid (a crystal). The surprise: the zeros are neither. They are stiffer than white — and that stiffness is the stability.

Autocorrelation — pair correlation R₂(r)

"Given a fluctuation here, what's at distance r?" White noise is flat at 1 (no memory). A dip at small r means events avoid each other — repulsion.

Power spectrum — structure factor S(k)

The frequency content (Wiener–Khinchin: the Fourier pair of the autocorrelation). White noise is flat across all frequencies. Suppression at low k = the ramp; a spike = a pure tone (resonance).

Fluctuation growth — number variance Σ²(L)

Count events in a window of width L; how does the variance grow with L? White noise grows as Σ²=L (the diagonal). Flatter than the diagonal = stiffer than white — fluctuations suppressed.

what the three instruments agree on

White noise is the random surrogate — flat autocorrelation, flat spectrum, variance growing straight as the diagonal. That's the orange line, and it is what "random" means.

The zeros are not white. They forbid close neighbors (autocorrelation dips to zero), their low-frequency power is suppressed (the spectral ramp), and their long-range fluctuations are nearly frozen — the number variance barely moves while white noise's climbs without bound. The zeros sit on the ordered side of white, partway toward the crystal, but never reaching its resonant spikes.

That stiffness is the stabilizing structure. Because long-range fluctuations are suppressed, the count of zeros — and through the explicit formula, the count of primes — can't wander far from its trend. The primes are steady because their hidden spectrum is this particular stiff, repelling noise — not random, not crystalline, but the one texture in between. Figure (orderly primes) and ground (stiff noise) are the same fact seen from two sides.
