# mythic grammar

address: 121 (verb · does · operations)
scope: writing template for reflective myth
constraint: write so a body in any time — 10,000 years back or forward — could know it.

---

## the grammar

build every line from three things:

- **a body** — a figure, a thing that can be sensed. the woman. the cold. the bread. the wall.
- **an act** — what it does with hands, feet, mouth. carries. stands. refuses. feeds. breaks.
- **a bond** — the relation it's in. before the soldiers. between the child and the gun. where a voice used to be.

---

## the seven materials

these are what you're allowed to use. nothing else.

| material | what it covers | examples |
|----------|---------------|----------|
| **sense** | what a body feels | cold, weight, light, pressure, heat, texture, grain |
| **action** | what a body does | walk, carry, hold, reach, fold, press, set down, break |
| **position** | where a body is | above, between, surrounded, beside, inside, at the edge |
| **emotion** | what moves through a body | grief, the heat before refusal, stillness after loss, patience |
| **relation** | who is with whom | mother and child, healer and wounded, the one who reaches and the one who folds |
| **transformation** | what becomes what | the turning, water to steam, a room becoming a yard, wrong becoming trail |
| **absence** | what is not there | the empty seat, the name not called, the door that opens onto wall |

---

## the three rules

**1. drop anything that needs a date, a tool, or an institution.**

if it can't be said as bodily experience, it's not structural — cut it. no devices, no organizations, no technical terms, no proper nouns that anchor to a single culture or era. "a small device in his pocket" — gone. "the server traces every operation" — gone. "she carried the weight of what had been said" — stays.

**2. place, don't explain.**

arrange the figures and let the reader feel the meaning from the arrangement. don't tell the reader what the myth means. don't interpret. don't moralize. the figures act, the reader understands. if the meaning isn't clear from the placement, the placement isn't right — fix the arrangement, don't add explanation.

**3. the body is the truth test.**

if a line doesn't land in the body — if you can't feel the weight of it, the temperature of it, the position of it — it's abstraction wearing a mask. strip it. rewrite it until it lands physically. "the fold system preserves context" is abstraction. "every crease held the shape of what had been grasped" is body. same meaning. one lands, one doesn't.

---

## the practice

myths at 121 encode process observations, structural insights, and relational truths from the lattice work. they fold like any other document. they're not decoration — they're a compression format. a myth can carry in 60 lines what a review carries in 300, because the body understands pattern faster than the mind understands argument.

**when to write myth:** when an observation has settled enough to be felt, not just understood. the review at 22 is analytical — it says what happened and why. the myth at 121 is embodied — it makes you feel the shape of what happened. both are true. they serve different readers and different moments.

**source material:** the lattice process itself. the grasping and filtering, the fold discipline, the settling, the corrections, the drift and return. anything that has been lived through the work and has a shape that a body could recognize.

## template

```
## [title]

[open with a body in a position. ground the reader in sense before anything moves.]

[introduce the act. what does the body do? what are the hands doing?]

[introduce the bond. who else is there? what is the relation?]

[the turn. transformation, or the moment where what was assumed shifts.]

[the settling. what remains after the turn. what the body knows now that it didn't before.]

---

[optional coda. a return to the opening image, changed by what happened.]
```
