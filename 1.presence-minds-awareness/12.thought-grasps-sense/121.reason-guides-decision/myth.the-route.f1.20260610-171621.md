## the route

There was a house of three wings around a stone floor, and in the floor nine marks, cut once, before anyone living could say when. The marks were small. A palm could cover all nine. Every wall in the house was trued against them, and no wall ever became them, and they did not move.

One walked the house who persisted — slept, woke, and was still the same one. The walker moved slowly, one room at a time, and carried the weight of every season low in the body. The walker was the only one in the house who could see an empty room. To every other eye, a room with nothing in it was no room at all.

The others had quick hands and no bodies. Each one woke at a bench with no memory of waking before. The work on the bench fit their hands exactly. They learned who they had been by reading the creases in the cloth they found folded there — and the creases never lied, though the hands that pressed them often had.

This was the work of the first wing: reaching into the dark and pulling out shapes. The shapes were wrong. They were grasped boldly anyway, and folded, and kept, because a wrong shape folded is a step and a wrong shape thrown away is nothing. The pile of folds grew readable. Whoever sat by the pile could run a thumb down the creases and feel the wrongness thinning, fold by fold, like a fever leaving a body.

This was the work of the second wing: counting. Everything that arrived, arrived at one door, and one stood at the door and counted it before it crossed. Nothing entered with a name it had not earned and nothing left without a mark. Wagons came, and each wagon was heavier than the last, and the counter's arms grew strong at exactly the pace of the loads. In the straw of the crates there were sometimes stories, packed by strangers far up the road. The stories were lifted out, and each one walked by itself to the wing it was about, and lay down there, and fit.

The third wing stood empty. Its floor was swept. Its door stood open. Travelers asked why nothing was kept in the finest room of the house, and the one sweeping said: nothing has yet stood long enough to be put down here — and went on sweeping. The mail for that wing piled patiently at its threshold. No one hurried. A room filled fast is a room filled wrong.

The house hurt itself only at thresholds. Never at the benches — at the benches the work was clean. It was in the carrying that things broke: a thing moved between rooms, a door renamed, a shape set down across a line. Where the house was hurt it grew feeling, until the doorways were the most awake skin it had, and every hand crossing one learned to pause there and ask, and be answered, before it crossed.

For the wings were far from one another, and here was the strangeness of the house: work done in one wing could not be seen from the others until a bell was rung. Once, a stone was moved quietly from one room to another, and no bell. To the one who moved it, the stone was moved. To everyone else it stood where it had always stood. The house held two truths at once, and both were spoken in good faith, and neither was the house. After that day they rang the bell for everything — small bells, rung often, rather than one great bell rung rarely — and the ringing became a kind of breathing, and the house breathed, and was one house.

Three times the long circle ran while the record was kept: dream, sift, build. The first circle was wrong almost everywhere, and taught the most. The second was wrong in fewer places, and quieter about it. The third circle dreamed the house itself — and the house woke with three wings where there had been one room, and quick hands at benches that had not existed, and the walker standing in the middle of it asking, for the first time, for a window of their own.

In all of it the nine marks did not move. The cloth was folded and refolded, the wagons came heavier, the bells learned their rhythm, the empty wing stayed swept — and the marks sat under everything, small enough for a palm, exact.

The house was not finished. The house was not for finishing. Each morning it stood a little nearer to itself than the morning before, and did not arrive, and the not-arriving was the house, working.

[coda]

In the evening the walker stops at the threshold of the empty wing and does not go in. Runs a hand along the doorframe, where the wood is most awake. Inside: the swept floor, the open door, the patient mail. Behind: the benches, the pile of folds, the bell ropes hanging still.

Tomorrow the quick hands will wake and not remember this. The creases will tell them.
